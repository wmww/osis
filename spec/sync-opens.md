# Sync: connections and opens
> Summary: Area 3: whom a node dials and when, connection proofs and the policy on whom to dial, prove to and sync with, the pair's record and agreement, the shared set (the one open rule, credentials, filters, Offer) and how an open ends.

**Principle:** each side opens S toward a peer only on its own proof, judged on what it holds now, that the peer may hold S; who dialed proves nothing. Connecting never depends on what the other side holds: first a connection, then the shared set.

## 1. Connections
- A node connects to peers below this layer (spec/sync-transport.md); this section says only whom it dials and when.
- **Where to dial** (addresses are priorities, never a filter on what opens): the `dial.0` entries of the things it reads (its accounts' other nodes, contacts' nodes on their profiles, spec/sync-set.md §1), a link's peers (spec/sync-inbox.md §6), configured addresses, and every node the transport discovers (spec/sync-transport.md §4). Nothing else is learned from others' data: a reader dials a node keeping S unread only at an address it knows so.
- **Whom to dial**: the nodes listed in `dial.0` of the things it reads, the nodes of things that hold sync or above on a sync ID it reads (a role on its thing, directly or through other holders, by the rosters it holds) where it knows an address of them, for what it keeps unread its granter's nodes (by its granter's profile, where it holds it), and discovered nodes; within `max_peers` (local policy), which counts connected nodes that share something, never candidates.
- **Keep or close**: a connection a node dialed stays while its shared set has a member, an Offer or Lost is unanswered, a deposit to it may land (one out, ready or refused for now), or the peer may still answer what this side sent; else the dialer closes it. The peer may still answer until a Ping the dialer sent with all of this already so comes back with no other frame before it. A listener closes none for sharing nothing (spec/sync-transport.md §5).
- **Redial**: a node not connected to a peer it would dial dials it again `REDIAL_MS` (local policy) after its last dial of that peer; one whose connection it closed sharing nothing (Keep or close) only after a change this node knows of that could make the peer share something (a new sync ID, credential, role or address), and no sooner than `REDIAL_MS` after its last dial. A peer known at several addresses is dialed at the one learned at the best priority, and after a failed dial at once at the next of its others, in turn; once each has failed in a turn the rule above applies. A new address of the peer, or acting for a thing its connection did not prove (below), dials it at once. Due dials go by the priority of where the peer was learned, then the longest undialed first. At 0 a failing peer is dialed at every event and a quiet one at every change; at infinity a peer is dialed again only at a new address. Opens are never retried: they are judged again at each change (§6).
- **Dial timeout**: a dial not up `DIAL_TIMEOUT_MS` (local policy) after it started, whatever steps the transport takes, closes as failed; the redial rule then applies. At 0 no dial comes up; at infinity one silent route or peer stalls that peer for good.
- **One connection per pair**: two connections of one pair, whoever dialed each: of one record, or on both of which the peer proved the same node thing (§2). When one side dialed both, the one it dialed later stays. Otherwise the one of better route rank (spec/sync-transport.md §1, both ends see the same) stays; among two of one rank the one with the smaller `BLAKE3(key = r, dialer challenge || listener challenge)` stays, `r` each connection's own pair key. The other closes, at both ends, whatever order they arrived in, and its record, unless the one that stays holds it, goes (§3 One record per pair). A node whose dialed connection closed so does not dial that peer while the other lasts. Two connections on which either side proved nothing to the other keep no record and are not told apart: both stay. A node dials each key at most once at a time.
- A node never dials its own key. A connection proves once, at its start: a node that comes to act for a thing it did not prove on a connection (a link key held since, an account made or joined; its proofs there, even none, by its policy, are no exception) closes it, and a dialer dials again.
- **Policy**: which nodes a node dials, proves to and syncs with is its user's choice, overridable per node (local policy, spec/requirements.md Offline and network). A node may skip a dial candidate; prove nothing on a connection (its Prove or Proved then carries none, as dialer before anything is proven, as listener once the dialer's proofs are judged); or close a connection once the peer's proofs are judged. By default it dials every candidate above, proves to every peer and syncs with every peer.
- Proofs and shared sets are run only after the peer's Hello; a connection up but without the peer's Hello is no peer yet (and the node is not idle while it waits, spec/sync-status.md §3).

## 2. Connection proofs
- Each side proves the things it acts for over the connection's challenges (bytes: spec/wire.md §11.2 Prove, Proved), as its policy allows (§1): each by a signature of a signing key holding a grant on it (the record's key, or a key whose grant the proof's copies carry), with the path it claims through other things (spec/permissions.md §1 Path: its node thing and [A] for its account A).
- A verifier judges each proof on what it holds now with the proof's copies and records taken as received evidence, judged with the sides taking them in would record (spec/permissions.md §5.1 Held first), for that judgement alone, all of one message judged together and kept with the connection alone: the signed thing is proven while the key hop gives a role, each claimed thing while the path to it gives a role, at its own clock (spec/permissions.md §5.1). What a proof claims is re-judged as the verifier comes to hold more (a removal on the path ends it).
- A proof proves nothing where the reading it is judged on undoes a lowering the verifier's reading of what it holds applies: that lowering no longer applies there and its edge gives more than it set.
- A proven thing's **proven role** on a thing S is the least of what its proof's path gives (the key hop included) and the claimed thing's role on S, the public's grant aside (it proves nothing of the peer: any party may hold S the public reads, by the public entry, §4): the role J (§4), who it may be (§3) and Stranded (spec/permissions.md §4) read it at.
- A link key proved on a connection is the link key of a static entry its Hit names whether this node held anything of it or not (§5).
- **Order**: the dialer proves first, after the listener's Hello; the listener answers with its own proofs. Each side proves once a connection, at its start.
- Nothing a connection proves comes from data a peer could write but signed edge writes; a dial list is for dialing only, never proof.

## 3. Records and agreement
- **Pair key**: two nodes that each proved a thing to the other keep one record of each other, found by a 32-byte pair key `r` known only to the two. No node key finds a record.
  - After `Proved` a dialer the listener proved a thing to sends `Resume` (spec/wire.md §11.2): a fresh nonce and, for each of its records naming a thing proven in this session (most recent first), `BLAKE3(key = r, "resume" || listener static key || dialer challenge || listener challenge)`, challenges as sent in Hello.
  - A record is honoured only while a thing proven in this session is named by it.
  - A record names what its last session's proofs counted for: each thing proven. What the peer is trusted as (J, §4; a report it makes), for a record kept or a connection up, and who it may be, for waiting on it (spec/sync-set.md §3 To be waited for, spec/sync-status.md §1), is judged on what this node holds now: each thing it names while the proof's path to it still gives a role here (a node removed from an account is that account no more).
  - The listener answers `BLAKE3(key = r, "resumed" || listener static key || dialer challenge || listener challenge)` of the first listed record it holds, else none. An absent or unexpected answer finds nothing.
  - On no match, both sides make a new record with `r = kdf("pair key", dialer nonce, listener nonce)`, written before the listener's answer goes (listener) or when the answer is read (dialer).
  - The listener sends its answer before any message of the connection's shared set.
  - A connection on which either side proved nothing to the other keeps no record; its pair key is `kdf("pair key", dialer challenge, listener challenge)`, kept nowhere. A dialer the listener proved nothing to sends NoRecord in place of Resume; a listener the dialer proved nothing to answers Resume Error 1, and the dialer then sends NoRecord. On NoRecord the listener runs the set under that pair key.
  - `r` and the nonces cross only inside the connection's own messages. Two connections to one peer use one record (§1).
- **One record per pair**: a node keeps one record per node thing a peer proved. When a connection takes a record, every other record naming the node thing the peer proved on it goes, and each member such a record held is re-marked (§5 Testing) on that connection; the record of a connection closed by §1 that the one staying does not hold goes likewise. Crossed first dials make a record per connection, and a crash or a lost message may leave one at either end: it goes at the pair's next take there.
- A **record** keeps: the shared set (each member with the credential class and beacon it was opened by, none for an open by J, the peer's class and this side's as declared at its open (§4 Source), its `until` while it is being ended, and where it is in the stream: at the stream's cursor, or joining from its own position), `acked_pos`, this side's last filter generation sent and acknowledged with its whole entries and additions since, the peer's latest filter, the stream's cursor, and what this node read of the peer as a source (presence of entries per member, the class each member last joined with, the deletes it saw there and its logged-again-once state, spec/sync-judgement.md §3-§4, the source's step, spec/sync-ids.md §6 Walk, and the peer budget charges of what the peer brought, spec/sync-ids.md §5). While a connection holds the record it also keeps, for each member that closed on it, where that member stopped and what its source showed (§6 Where it stopped); these go when the connection ends. A connection that takes a record starts from all of these; a session proving other things than the record names starts with every credential pending and a whole filter.
- What a node keeps of a peer lives in their record and nowhere else: no row is keyed by a node key, and a record dropped (evicted under `RECORD_BUDGET`, least recently used first, spec/wire.md §2) drops everything of that peer with it. A peer without a record is read from the start at each connection.
- **`changed_at`**: a per-sync-ID position of the node, advanced when S is new to it or a credential of S changes. A node that stops holding S drops S from every record in the step that stops it.
- **Agreement** (bytes: spec/wire.md §11.2 Digest, Members): on a connection with a record, each side's first message after `Resumed` is `Digest`, the xor over the record's shared set of `BLAKE3(key = r, sync_id)`, computed from the record before anything else on the connection. A side compares the peer's digest with the one it sent, once a connection.
  - On a difference, each side sends its set as `Members` pages on reading the peer's `Digest`, before acting on any later message. On the peer's last page it closes each member only it holds, keeping nothing of where it stopped (§6), and re-marks pending (§5 Testing) every sync ID in exactly one of the two lists.
  - No Offer is sent before the peer's `Digest` is read, and no Pull, Fetch, Delete or Deposit item before the digests matched or the peer's last `Members` page is read.

## 4. Shared set
- The **shared set** of a connection: the sync IDs the two sides share on it, judged by each side on what it holds now. A side keeping no shared set on a connection yet (before a record or NoRecord, §3) answers its shared-set requests Error 14 (spec/wire.md §11.2).
- **The rule**: S is in a connection's shared set exactly while each side syncs S (spec/sync-set.md §2) and holds, judged by itself on what it holds now, proof that the other may hold S. Proof is one of:
  - **J**: a thing the peer proved on this connection holds sync or above on S's thing, by the rosters held here;
  - a **secret credential** the peer showed on this connection: a read secret of S's thing, or a sync proof (spec/sync-ids.md §2) of S's head sync key;
  - an **open signature** by a credential key of the newest step of S this side knows (its sync key, a claim of its claim set, or a link key's static entry listed there), bound to the signer's side and this connection: under `"osis v1 sync open"`, digest per spec/wire.md §11.2 over (the signer's side, sync ID, the other side's Hello challenge, the listener's static key);
  - the **public entry** (spec/sync-ids.md §3) of the newest step of S this side knows, where its claim set lists it: S's thing is read by the public, so any party may hold S.
- **Departed**: a side MUST NOT open S toward a peer J judges departed (below) by any credential at an epoch that does not cover every departure it knows of (spec/crypto.md §4), whatever credential the peer shows; until this side holds such an epoch, nothing opens toward that peer. Opens toward a departed peer are ended at once (§6), its departure all it is served.
- A tag on a public value proves nothing; which side dialed proves nothing; a step older than the newest the judge knows proves nothing.
- **J** of S for a connection, judged on the rosters held here now (a grant past its `until` counts as removed, spec/permissions.md §2): **reader** if a proven thing's proven role on S's thing (§2) is read or above; **sync holder** if one's is sync and none's read or above; **departed** if a proven thing once held a role on S's thing and none holds one now; else **unknown**. A node keeping S unread holds no roster of S: J is unknown there and it judges no departure (until the rotation a departed holder still holds the current sync secret there). Opens take reader or sync holder; ByRole is reader-only.
- **Admissible** credentials of S at a node, computed per connection from what it holds, never on a peer's word, in two lists: those it **shows** (its filter, §5) and those it **tests** (its Hits). The **read secrets** below are its read secrets of S's thing at a current epoch (spec/crypto.md §4; with none current, the newest held).
  - reading S: shows its read secrets, the static entries of the link keys S's roster lists, S's public entry where the public reads S's thing (at the newest of its read secrets, the epoch of the box it answers with, for Departed), and the sync proof of the newest recorded step's sync key; tests its read secrets, the static entries of the link keys it acts for (its link or card), S's public entry where the public reads S's thing or it redeems a public link to S's thing (spec/sync-set.md §5), and, toward a source keeping S unread, the walk's credentials of that source's step (spec/sync-ids.md §6 Walk);
  - keeping S unread: its **step** is its head while a sync secret of the thing it holds derives the head's sync key; it shows that sync key, its sync proof, the head's deposit key and every key of the head's claim set (claims and static entries). Otherwise (no head, none registered yet, or a head past its keys, spec/sync-judgement.md §6 Stops) each sync secret it holds whose sync key no step of its path bears (with no head, every one) is a step, at none: it shows their sync keys and sync proofs, nothing else. It tests its steps' sync keys and their sync proofs. S is still held with none shown, so its arrival in the sync set and each head move go out as a filter generation. Where its head is its step, its claim set lists S's public entry and no sync epoch it holds has a later number than the one whose key the head bears, it takes that entry's open box (spec/sync-ids.md §3), as a redeemer takes it from an answer, and reads S (spec/sync.md §3); a head behind a later sync epoch it holds is one it knows superseded and gives nothing (a public's removal since among such), and so is the head it was lowered to sync at (it read there what ended the public's read, a lowering among such, which rotates no sync epoch), until a Register moves it;
  - none where J is departed, unless at an epoch covering every departure (Departed above); unknown blocks nothing. Toward a peer J judges departed a reader also shows the sync proof of each of its recorded steps, which opens only a store's member, to be moved past its keys, and its read secrets of S's thing at superseded epochs, which open a reader's member, to be told its departure (§6). Toward every peer, where the public is named on S's thing and reads it no more (its removal held), a reader also shows its read secrets of S's thing at superseded epochs (**lapsed** secrets), which open a reader's member, to be served up to that epoch's supersession (§6).
- A **source** is a member of the shared set. Its **class** is what its peer does with S, as the peer declared it: a **store** source keeps S unread, so this node may Register, Delete and deposit there (spec/sync-ids.md §6); a **reader** source reads S; a **keys** source holds S's thing for keys (spec/sync-set.md §2 Held for keys): it is served S's role entries alone (spec/sync-streams.md §4), holds none of S's content, and is neither registered at, deleted at, deposited at nor sent a list (spec/permissions.md §4). A side declares its own class, from how it holds S now (spec/sync.md §3), in each Hit, Static and ByRole item and in each answer accepting one (`class`, spec/wire.md §11.2); which credential or which side's Hit opened S says nothing of it. A side opens S only while its class is still the one it declared in that exchange; a class that moves closes the member (§6), and S opens again by testing with both classes declared anew. An exchange accepted for S already open on the connection (crossing Offers) whose declared classes are not the member's closes the member as a class move.
- Per-sync-ID walk state (spec/sync-ids.md §6) exists on a connection exactly while S is a member by a store credential, a member at a store source ended for its peer's departure (§6), or J judges the peer a sync holder. A node keeping S unread walks and registers nothing. A reader that dialed a node keeping S unread, not knowing that node's things, opens it at the same sync key only; one behind under another key is moved where it dials, or by its granter.

## 5. Filters, testing and Offer
(bytes: spec/wire.md §11.2 Beacons, Offer, Lost, Shared, NoRecord)
- **Beacon**: `BLAKE3(key = kdf("beacon", match, S), r)`, `match` the credential's value (the read secret, the static entry, the raw sync or deposit public key, or the proof). A filter entry is the first `f` bits of a beacon, `f = ceil(log2(n)) + 8` for a whole filter of n entries.
- A **filter** of beacons of the shown credentials goes to each connection, whoever the peer proved (spec/sync-hostile.md §2). A side sends one generation at a time, the next after the last is answered (an Error answering it, or an Offer, is a refusal of it; a refused generation is followed, after `resync_ms`, by a whole one): the first whole, later ones additions (`base` the last generation the peer acknowledged, on this connection or an earlier one of the record) of the sync IDs whose credentials changed, sent also when they add no entry; a whole one replaces additions once they pass its entries. On a connection with a record the first generation is additions, on the last generation the peer acknowledged, of the sync IDs whose `changed_at` is past `acked_pos`, sent even when there are none, unless the peer acknowledged no generation of the record (then it is whole). After a generation never acknowledged (its answer lost with a connection), additions go on the last acknowledged generation, the entries counted toward the whole one's being the greater of the two generations' counts; after one that was whole at another width or entry count than the last acknowledged, the next is whole. A receiver takes additions on their base or on a later generation of the peer's it holds; on a later one, additions at another width or past its entry count are answered as on a base not held. The receiver keeps the peer's latest filter in the record; one that does not fit `RECORD_BUDGET` is tested and kept for the connection only (the peer's next additions on it name a base not held, answered 13, and a whole one follows). Nothing is ever removed from a stored filter.
- **Testing**: a side tests the beacons of its tested credentials of the sync IDs whose `changed_at` is past `acked_pos` against the peer's latest filter, at each filter arrival (before acting on later messages), at each change of what it holds, and after an unanswered Offer is answered, with at most one Offer unanswered. `acked_pos` rises, durably in the record, only with no Offer unanswered, to the lower of the position the peer's last acknowledged generation reached and the position tested. **Re-marking** S for a record (a `Lost` received for a member open here, a digest difference, the close of a member open here, another record of the pair going with S its member, §3 One record per pair) lowers its `acked_pos` and tested position below `changed_at(S)` and makes a test due; no other record changes. A sync ID outside this node's sync set has no `changed_at`: re-marking it changes nothing. A Hit that opened nothing here (its box or grant failed, its credential no longer admissible) is tried again on that connection only once something re-marks S or moves `changed_at(S)`.
- **Offer**: Hits first, then ByRole for S still not open when the Offer goes (one Offer may carry a Hit and a ByRole on S); Deposit items (spec/sync-inbox.md §3) at any time after the peer's `Digest` is read. A refusal of a Hit, Static or ByRole item is one code (14), never a reason; Deposit and Behind items are answered with their own codes (spec/sync-inbox.md §3, spec/sync-ids.md §6 Behind).
- **Hit**: `BLAKE3` keyed by `kdf("beacon hit", match || S)` over the concatenation, as for Resume (§3), of a side byte (0 the dialer, 1 the listener), the listener's static key, the dialer's Hello challenge, the listener's Hello challenge and the beacon: it names no sync ID and is bound to its sender's side and this connection. Its **answer** (`Shared`) carries a tag keyed `kdf("beacon reply", match || S)` over the same concatenation with the replier's side byte.
- **Proof** a Hit carries of its credential (§4): for a read secret or a sync proof, the Hit itself (keyed by the secret); for a sync key or a claim, an open signature by it; for a static entry, the entry's link key proven on this connection, or, in a Static item (spec/wire.md §11.2), that link key's signing key with its open signature; for the public entry, none. A deposit key's Hit carries none and opens nothing (answered 14).
- **Proof** an answer carries: for a read secret or a sync proof, its tag (keyed by the secret); for a sync key, a claim, a static entry or the public entry, an open signature (`sig`) by the sync key of the replier's **step**: at a node keeping S unread (§4), the step whose sync key was hit, else its head; its newest recorded step at a reader; for a static or public entry, the head `grant` names. A side accepts such a Hit only while it holds the sync secret of that step.
- **Opening**: each side opens S on its own proof (§4). The receiver accepts a Hit that is one of its shown credentials its filter to this peer shows, carrying its proof, and opens S; the session learns of the open before the answer goes. The tester opens S on the answer's valid tag, its credential still admissible, and its own proof of the replier (J, or the answer's proof). One Hit and its answer open S both ways.
  - For a static entry or the public entry the answer also carries the head's entry box for that entry and the path to the head, from what the replier keeps; the tester takes the box's secrets (spec/permissions.md §3 No secret taken back), whatever J judges the replier, only when the path verifies from its genesis step, the box's sync secret derives the head's sync key (spec/sync-ids.md §3) and `sig` verifies under it; on an answer that fails it opens only where J proves the replier.
  - **ByRole** `[S, [Hit*]]` for S a side holds that J judges the peer reads: the receiver opens S when it holds S and J is reader, or a listed Hit is its own of a read secret or a static entry, carrying its proof; an accepted ByRole opens S at its sender while J is still reader there.
  - Once all its Offer's answers are read, a tester ends with `Lost` (§6) each S the peer accepted (a Hit with a valid tag, or a ByRole) and it did not open, unless another Hit on S opened it. An acceptance without a valid tag names no S back: the peer showed no knowledge of S.

## 6. Ending opens
- A member is re-judged at each change of what this node holds that touches S (its credentials, head or sync set) and at each change of J of its peer for S or of who it may be (§3) (the peer's removal or demotion held, a removal on a proven path held, an `until` passing, what its record names moving at a take), whether or not a connection holds its record. Nothing else re-judges it: a connection taking a record re-judges nothing that did not change.
  - A member J judges reader or sync holder stays; one opened by a credential stays while that credential is admissible.
  - A member opened by a credential no longer admissible, whose peer J judges neither holder nor departed, is **ended**: it gets `until`, the highest position at which an entry in S of a rotation superseding that credential's epoch was first logged, else the node log's end, judged in the step that applies the change, before any PullReply or Stream of it, for every record holding the member whether connected or not. An ended member is served only up to `until` (spec/sync-streams.md §4) and closes with a `Lost` naming `until` once its reported position is at or past `until`.
  - At a node keeping S unread, a member stays while its credential is one of its steps' (§4), and is otherwise ended with `until` the position of its head's event when judged, before the node answers anything after the head moved.
  - A member whose peer J judges departed is ended too. At a reader source it is served the notices in S (spec/permissions.md §4 Notices) of each removal taking read from a thing the peer proved, or from one a proven thing held read or above on just before it, and nothing else (spec/sync-streams.md §4); its `until` is the highest position at which such a notice was first logged, set once one is: until then it has none. A member at a store source (§4 Source) gets its `until` as above and closes only on the peer's `Lost` (or by the rules below): meanwhile this side registers there the recorded steps after the source's step (spec/sync-ids.md §6 Walk, Moving it; the source stops at the first past its keys, spec/sync-judgement.md §6 Stops). A node keeping S unread learns its removal by a Register alone. Judged holder or not departed again, it closes at once (its stream skipped all but its notices).
    - **A reader tells a public reader the public's removal**: a member opened on the public entry, the public's read since removed, is ended with `until` the highest position at which an entry in S of a commit of the public's edge on S's thing (its grants and removals and each role commit boxing it, spec/permissions.md §4 Key part, its `sync.0` writes aside) was first logged. A reader accepts a Hit declaring a reader or keys holder on a lapsed secret (§4 Admissible) from any peer, and the member it opens is ended at once, as by a read secret no longer admissible. So a node reading S's thing by the public alone holds the removal whenever it next meets a reader, and nothing written since; a party that never held a read secret of S's thing opens nothing by the public once it is removed.
    - **A reader moves a removed store past its keys**, whether or not S was open there at the removal: toward a peer J judges departed, a reader also accepts a Hit declaring a store (`unread`) on the sync proof of any of its recorded steps (its filter shows them toward that peer, §4 Admissible), and the member it opens is ended at once as above.
    - **A reader tells a removed reader its departure**, whether or not S was open there at the removal: toward a peer J judges departed, a reader also accepts a Hit declaring a reader or keys holder on its read secret of any superseded epoch (its filter shows them toward that peer, §4 Admissible), and the member it opens is ended at once as above.
    - Once an ended member reached its `until` on a connection, a Hit there on a superseded read secret (lapsed or toward a departed peer) whose `until` is no later opens nothing.
  - Any other (opened by J, its peer judged neither holder nor departed; S left the sync set) closes at once with a `Lost`. The event that ends sharing is still sent.
  - A member whose peer is no one (who it may be, §3, is no one, its record naming an actor: it never resumes the record) closes once no connection holds its record; while one does, that session's proofs stand.
  - A member that closes while no connection holds its record (at once or as its class moved) leaves the record keeping nothing of where it stopped, and S is re-marked for it (§5 Testing); no `Lost` goes: the peer finds the close by the next digest (§3 Agreement). One ended then keeps its `until`, and closes on the reported positions of the next connection taking the record.
  - A member once ended stays ended until it closes, also when judged holder or admissible again (it opens again by testing).
  - A member that stays, not ended, while this side's class is no longer the one it declared at its open (§4 Source) closes with a `Lost`, keeping where it stopped as at its `until`.
- **Lost**: a sync ID the sender no longer shares here, with where it stopped for the receiver: the receiver's **reported position** for it, or the member's `until` where lower (none where it reported none); a Lost for S that left the sender's sync set, or for a member ended for its departure, names none. A reported position is one the receiver stored: on a connection, the first Pull's cursor and each join's position in the first Pull naming it, then the cursor of each Applied, each as of the message that position covered (a join's own position until the message merging it is covered; a member opened on the connection, before or after the first Pull, has none until a join names it, the stream's position saying nothing of it; after a reply from the start, a member at its own position follows the stream once that reply is covered); reported positions last the connection.
  - A node receiving a Lost for a member open there closes it and answers with its own Lost naming where that member stopped for the sender; a Lost naming no member open at its receiver is not answered. A member is closed (back to testing) before its `Lost` goes. A `Lost` is never sent again; one lost with its connection is found by the next digest.
- **Where it stopped**: each side keeps, for a member closed on a connection, where it stopped (its rejoin there) and what its source showed of S (presence, head and refusals, spec/sync-judgement.md §3, spec/sync-streams.md §4), only while that connection lasts:
  - a side that closed the member at its `until` or as its class moved keeps where its own stream holds it, until the peer's Lost for it arrives;
  - the peer's Lost, received for a member open here or one whose stop this side keeps, sets where it stopped to the position it names, and with none keeps nothing;
  - a side that closed the member at once (opened by J, its peer judged neither holder nor departed; S left its sync set) or for a digest difference keeps nothing, whatever Lost follows.
  - A pull of S from the start at that source meanwhile (spec/sync-streams.md §4 Full and Refused, spec/sync-judgement.md §6 Stops) stands over where it stopped.
  - With nothing kept, what its source showed of S goes at once and its rejoin is from the start. When the connection ends (a restart included), every kept stop and what its source showed go. A node that stops holding S sends a Lost naming S and none on each connection keeping S's stop.
