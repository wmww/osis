# Sync
> Summary: Blobs and their clear prefix; labels, their self-certifying IDs, registration steps and role keys; which blobs go under which label (closure, entitlement, projection, certificate and inbox blobs); blind-node state and the authorization of upload (writer stamps), deposit, download (sync key or claim key) and delete, timed deletes; inboxes and grant delivery; the per-label log protocol with range reconciliation fallback; label convergence and compaction; subscriptions; ephemeral commits held in memory, presence tied to its author's connection, and relay-only signals; transports and addresses; file bundles.

## 1. Blobs
- A **blob** is an encrypted commit (header plus unpurged deltas; spec/commits.md), an encrypted chunk, tree node or page (spec/data-model.md §6, spec/crdt.md §8, a header list spec/commits.md §1), or an inbox blob (§3). Blind nodes cannot tell them apart except as §4 states.
- Layout: **clear prefix** || ciphertext (spec/wire.md §5). Prefix: a random 16-byte salt (fresh per encryption), optional expiry `T`, the blob's **class**, the delete-key commitments, one per level (spec/crypto.md §7). Ciphertext: spec/crypto.md §5, plaintext padded with Padmé (Nikitin et al., PURBs).
- **Class**: durable, ephemeral, relay-only or presence. A commit blob takes its header's ephemeral marker (spec/commits.md §2): an ephemeral commit's blobs are ephemeral, a relay-only commit's relay-only, a presence commit's presence, every other blob durable. Blind nodes hold ephemeral and presence entries in memory only and relay-only ones not at all (§4, §5).
- **Blob ID** = BLAKE3 of prefix and ciphertext. A blob is at most `MAX_BLOB` bytes.
- One commit may travel as several blobs (projections, purge rewrites, certificate copies, concurrent re-encryptions): same header and commit ID, each with its own key, salt and ID. Every encryption of a commit computes its prefix from the header: expiry = the `expires` of the deltas it holds (§3: one class per blob); commitments to the delete keys its deltas list, blinded by the new salt (spec/crypto.md §7). A chunk reused by a later delta keeps its blob and prefix, unless its expiry is earlier than the new delta's or its commitments to the thing's delete keys (levels 1 and 3) are not exactly those of the keys the reusing delta lists; then it is re-encrypted. Author-chain commitments need not match. **Repair**: a chunk or tree node blob that a held, unexpired delta of a node's own commit links through its large values' trees, and that every server of a label it syncs has deleted (a purger that had not seen the reuse), is repaired by that node if it may write the aspect and holds the blob: it writes each large position of the aspect's current value whose tree holds the blob again, whole and without a reset (`put_new`, spec/crdt.md §5.6), reusing every other chunk and re-encrypting that blob, with that delta's retention and expiry; at most once per aspect per back-off interval (local policy, doubling).

## 2. Labels
- A synced group has a **label**: the unit a blind node stores. A label has an **index** i (0 for a group's first label) and Ed25519 role keys: **sync** `kdf("label sync", read secret, le64(i))`, **write** `kdf("label write", write secret, le64(i))` and **delete** `kdf("label delete", admin secret, le64(i))`, from the group thing's role secrets (explicit, or derived from its owner's by tid; spec/crypto.md §3), plus a **deposit** key `kdf("label deposit", deposit seed, label ID)` from the deposit seed of the group's actor (§3; spec/crypto.md §3), keyed by label ID so that labels of one actor register unrelated deposit keys.
- **Label ID** = first 16 bytes of BLAKE3(`"osis v1 label id"` || genesis delete public key). The genesis step derives from the admin secret current when the label is created.
- **Registration steps**: a tree. A step: label ID, `prev` (the step ID of its parent; none in the genesis step), the four public keys (sync, write, delete, deposit), a fresh X25519 **step key**, BLAKE3 of the **claim set** (which holds the **entry boxes**), and a signature under `"osis v1 label register"` by the parent's delete key (the genesis step by its own). Layout: spec/wire.md §8. **Step ID** = BLAKE3 of the step. Each step is carried with its claim set. A server holds one path from the genesis step to its **head** (§4); a new step is a child of the target server's head (§5), so after a fork (spec/crypto.md §4) servers may hold different branches, each signed through. An admin MAY issue a step without a rotation (same role keys, fresh step key) to list readers granted since its parent, key actors in particular.
- **Claim set** of a step: for each **reader** M of the registered read epoch (every actor holding its read secret as the registrar knows it at registration: the holders of boxes carrying it, the owner included; of a plain group, its owner; a member re-sealed later opens by sync key), the Ed25519 public key whose seed is `kdf("label claim", DH(step private key, M's X25519 public key), label ID || step key)`; M's X25519 key is the X25519 half of its read-role KEM key in the newest `/keys` of M the registrar holds (a key actor: its single KEM key); a non-contributory DH result omits M. A reader that is a key actor other than a node (a recovery key, a link key) is listed instead by its **static entry**, `BLAKE3("osis v1 static claim" || label ID || its signing public key)`, the same 32 bytes. Padded with random keys to a power of two, in random order. The registrar erases the step private key after signing. M derives its claim key from its own private key and the step key; it tries each keyset epoch it has held.
- **Entry boxes** of a step: for each reader that is a key actor other than a node (a recovery key, a link key), the epoch's read secret boxed to it (spec/crypto.md §4 forms; AD = (label ID, step key)), so a key actor holding no secret of the group reads the label from the current step. Other readers get none: they read the rotations they missed in order (spec/crypto.md §5), or a re-seal (§3).
- A node talks to a server with the role keys of the epochs the server's head registers; it derives them from the matching epochs it holds (every member holds all older secrets by the history boxes). A node whose epochs match no step the server holds opens under the sync key of the newest step it shares with the server and walks from there (§5).
- `/label` on the group thing: `map<label ID, { index, steps: map<step ID, step>, servers: list<address>, replaced: bool }>`, each step with its claim set and entry boxes, which an admin MAY drop once the step has been superseded for `MEMBERSHIP_KEEP`. Membership-class. An address is a transport URL plus the server's static public key (§10). Nodes upload under, and sync, every label of the group not marked replaced. A `/label` write on a group is also published under the home label of the nearest actor in the group's owner chain that has one, so that actor's devices find the group; one membership commit may carry the `/label` deltas of many groups.
- **Replacement** (a label lost at a server, e.g. to a removed admin re-registering first): an admin rotates the admin secret if the loss calls for it, creates label index `1 + max index in /label`, uploads the live blobs under it, marks the old label replaced, and at servers it still controls deletes the old label's content entries, keeping its membership-class commits and the `/label` commit that marks it replaced, so a node that knows only the old label finds the new one. No new step of a replaced label is issued; at a server that does not hold it, the admin MAY register its recorded path again to place that `/label` commit there. Once that commit is under the old label and its content entries are gone at a server (or the label cannot be opened there), the admin's work there is done.
- **Home label**: an actor with role secrets (not a key actor) MAY be synced as a group of itself; that label is its home label. It MUST have one when it names it as its inbox or when it owns labeled groups or receives grants (below). The home closure holds the actor thing and what its `/group` lists, nothing by ownership. A link's K label is K's home label.
- **Inbox**: the label an actor with role secrets names in `/keys` for inbox blobs (§3): a label of a group its admins administer (by default its home label). An actor that names none uses the inbox of the nearest actor above it in its owner chain. Key actors have none: a node receives a direct grant only over a direct connection to the granting device (pairing); a recovery key or link key holds its label from its card or link and claims the current step by its static entry (§5).
- A thing in no labeled group is not synced.

## 3. What goes under a label
- **Closure**: the blobs of group G are the commits touching any thing in G's transitive closure (through both sides of `/group`, spec/data-model.md §8: an ephemeral placement brings its item's ephemeral commits), plus the chunk, tree-node and page blobs they reference (every blob their values link, whether or not the linking commit's chunk list names it). Each node tags commits with their things' `/groups` and retags when group membership changes.
- **Entitlement**: a blob belongs under the label of a group G iff G's actor may read every delta in the blob. G's actor is the group thing if it has secrets of its own, else its owner. Only a writer of G stamps entries (§5); a node that is not one deposits what it must place there (its chain certificates and node card, its invite grant or self-removal, its heartbeats, and the revokes and roster snapshots it must place by spec/permissions.md §4) and relays stamped entries it holds.
- **Projection**: a commit is uploaded as one blob per distinct (readable subset, retention class) of its deltas, where the retention class is the retention and expiry (spec/commits.md §5, §6) and the delete keys they list (spec/crypto.md §7), each holding the deltas of that class and the commitments and expiry they call for, all with the same header and commit ID. A class whose blob would exceed `MAX_BLOB` is split into several blobs of that class holding disjoint delta sets, greedily in aspect-list order so every encryption of the class splits alike. So a delete key never deletes a delta it does not cover, an expired class is deleted rather than rewritten, and a blob expires whole.
- **Retag**: when a thing joins a group, any writer of the label that holds the thing's live blobs adds them under it; when it leaves, delete-key holders delete them under the old label as they see the change (§7).
- **Certificate blobs**: chain certificates, node cards, heartbeats, revokes and roster snapshots (spec/permissions.md §4) go under a label as a fresh encryption per label, never as a blob shared with another label; the chunks of a frontier likewise. A roster snapshot copied as a certificate carries the edge and ledger deltas the chain needs. A writer promoting a deposited commit completes its author's chain under the label from what it holds (§5).
- **Inbox blob** (spec/crypto.md §5): the parts about actor G of a membership commit (a grant with G's boxes, a change or removal of G's edge, a re-seal of G, a rotation boxing G where G held no box at the read epoch before it), boxed to G, with the label IDs and server addresses of the things it names, so G can reach them. Placed under G's inbox (§2) by deposit, or by upload when the sender holds the inbox label's write key; its prefix expiry is `INBOX_LIFETIME`, after which the sender re-sends if the recipient has not acted (a sender MAY bound the number of re-sends); the inbox group's admins may delete it earlier.
- A grantee device that accepts a grant for an actor (from the inbox, from K's label, or by pairing) places a certificate copy of the grant, with the `/label` commits of the labels it reached, under that actor's home label, so the actor's other and future devices find it; not when an entry there already holds the grant's parts, counted as for chain certificates (spec/permissions.md §4).
- **Contact card**: a link (spec/permissions.md §9) with read role to a person's profile thing P, a group of itself holding `/keys` and chain certificates only.
- A receiver keeps blobs a peer placed under a label even if its own closure disagrees.

## 4. Blind-node state
Per label: its registration path, the steps from the genesis step to its **head** (every step's role keys, step key, `prev` and signature; each step's claim set and entry boxes until it has been superseded for `MEMBERSHIP_KEEP`, longer by policy); a set of **entries** (blob ID, wrap or, for chunk/tree/page and inbox blobs, a random 49-byte filler, stamp or none, deposited flag, optional pending deletion time); **tombstones** (blob ID) of deleted entries, kept `TOMBSTONE_LIFETIME` (30 days); an append-only **log** of events (entries added, deletions, registrations) with sequence numbers. Blob bytes are stored once per node and deleted when no label references them. Ephemeral entries, their log events and their tombstones are held in memory only and lost at a restart; no sequence number handed out before a restart is handed out again. Relay-only blobs are never held.

## 5. Operations and authorization
Each operation names a label. Writes are signed with the role keys of the server's head; reads need the label opened on the connection:
- **Open** (sync key or claim key): a signature under `"osis v1 label open"` over (label ID, the server's connection challenge from the handshake, its static key). Opens the label on that connection for download, specific-blob fetch, reconciliation and subscription. A sync key is matched against the head first, then against the newest superseded step on the path bearing it (a step without a rotation shares its parent's keys); a signature under a superseded step's sync key is answered with that step's child on the path (without its claim set) and nothing else; the client verifies it under the step's delete key before using it.
- **Claim**: an open signature under a key V, with (step ID j, V). If V is in step j's claim set: j the head opens the label, j on the path is answered with its child as above. Otherwise refused like an unknown key. A key actor other than a node sends its signing public key with the open signature instead of (j, V), only to labels of things it is a member of or that it follows for itself (its link's or card's label); the server opens the label if its static entry is in the head's set, and that key reads the current read secret from the head's entry box. A member that missed n steps walks them in n round trips, verifying each step under its parent's delete key (the genesis step against the label ID), then downloads the rotations and derives the current keys; the claim is bootstrap only. A claim at a step whose set the server has dropped is refused; such a member places a stranded heartbeat (spec/permissions.md §4) and is reached only by re-seal (spec/permissions.md §3).
- **Register** (delete key of the parent): accept a genesis step only if its delete key hashes to the label ID and the label is new; accept a step whose `prev` is the head; answer anything else (a step with another `prev`, an operation under keys of a step it does not hold) with the head. A step is registered with its claim set when the registrar holds it (it MUST hash to the step's); a step held without its set serves no claims at it. Relayable by any node.
- **Stamp**: a signature under the label's write key, domain `"osis v1 upload"` (spec/crypto.md §2), over (label ID, blob ID, wrap). A server checks it against the write key of its head; stamps already accepted stay valid across re-registration.
- **Upload** (stamp; no open needed): add entries, each with wrap and stamp. Anyone holding a stamped entry may relay it, blind nodes included (replication between blind nodes is push only: download needs an open). Refused: a bad stamp; a blob whose prefix expiry has passed; a blob ID with a tombstone; an oversized blob; an ephemeral blob past the label's ephemeral cap (code 12). An upload of a held blob ID replaces its wrap and stamp and clears its deposited flag (rate-limited per entry). A **relay-only** blob is not stored: it is pushed to the label's subscribers on the server's other connections (spec/wire.md §11.2) and leaves no entry, log event, tombstone or expiry. It is refused without an expiry, past it, with one more than `2 × MAX_DRIFT` ahead (code 1), above `MAX_RELAY_BLOB` (code 3), or past the label's relay rate (code 4); a server pushes a blob ID once per label until its expiry (a repeat is answered OK and pushed to nobody), remembering a bounded number of them (past it the earliest-expiring is forgotten).
- **Presence entries**: a presence entry is held like an ephemeral one and tied to the connections that uploaded it. When every connection that uploaded it has closed, the server removes it with a log record and no tombstone; a deletion of one leaves no tombstone either. Blind nodes never replicate presence entries. Only its author uploads a presence entry (§9).
- **Deposit** (deposit key): add one entry with the given wrap (filler for an inbox blob) and no stamp, flagged deposited in the log. Refused like an upload, when the label's deposit budget is full, or for an ephemeral or relay-only blob (readers write no ephemeral data; code 1, whatever the store behind the server engine). The budget is server policy; recommended minimum `64 + 4 ×` (size of the current step's claim set) entries and that many × 32 KiB bytes; an entry leaves the budget when stamped, deleted or expired; the reply reports the remaining budget. Clients deposit certificates, membership writes and revokes before heartbeats and skip heartbeats when the budget is tight. A client sends a deposit of its own write (sealed, wrapped and signed when made) to each server of the label until that server answers it, also after losing read access to the label's actor (spec/permissions.md §3): other nodes may hold parts of that commit from elsewhere and wait for the rest. A writer that verifies a deposited commit the label must hold re-uploads it stamped (promotes it), only once its author's chain for the deposited deltas (spec/permissions.md §4) is under the label: a promoter completes the chain it holds, placing the missing deltas as its own certificate copies with the promotion; a writer that holds neither the commit applied nor the chain leaves the deposit unpromoted. A heartbeat (spec/permissions.md §4) is never promoted.
- **Order**: a node places a commit it made under the labels it syncs of the things the commit touches before any projection of it elsewhere (certificate copies, inbox blobs): a projection waits until each such label holds every body of the commit it must hold (§6) in an entry present at one of its servers or covered by a snapshot there (a label with no server: in an entry the node serves; skipped when the node has no `/reach`), and while a certificate copy of a node's chain under a label waits, so do its writes there that the chain verifies. Exempt: a commit writing `/keys` or carrying a frontier (spec/permissions.md §3: such copies go first), and the placements a membership operation makes when the commit is made (a removal's copies, a grantee's certificate copy, a deposit). The wait ends at the latest `PLACE_WAIT` (local policy; recommended 5 minutes) after the node, connected, first finds the commit not placed (a commit made offline waits from its first sync; a restart starts the wait again); the projections then go anyway and the node reports it.
- **Delete**: by the delete key, or of one entry by a level's delete key: a delete signature that verifies under the blob prefix's commitment at that level (spec/crypto.md §7), plus a signature under the head's write key for level 3 (thing write secret) or its sync key for level 1 or 2; any other is refused. Optionally **timed**: the entry is removed at the given time instead of now (one pending time per entry, the earliest request wins). Removal leaves a tombstone for `TOMBSTONE_LIFETIME`.
- **Expiry**: at the prefix's `T` the server deletes the entry outright: no tombstone.
- **Download**, specific-blob fetch, reconciliation (§6) and subscription (§8): only for labels opened on the connection; a blob is fetched by ID only as an entry of an opened label.
- Servers cap storage per label and the memory of ephemeral entries per label (and in total), counting each entry's, log record's and tombstone's overhead besides its blob; at the total cap the label using most gives way (its oldest log records, then its oldest entries, deleted with a log record and no tombstone: holders may bring them back). An ephemeral entry's tombstone lasts `MAX_DRIFT` (in memory). Servers may rate-limit deletes (not of presence entries), wrap replacements (ephemeral entries' included), deposits and relay-only uploads per label. A cursor below a trimmed ephemeral log gets the live ephemeral entries added since in place of their add events.
- A member peer serving a label applies the same rule (presence entries included: one a session uploaded goes when that session closes; relay-only uploads included: it passes a new one on to the label's servers it syncs with, §9), and may also serve a node of a current member: an open whose proof is a signature under `"osis v1 member open"` (spec/wire.md §11.2, proof 3) by the `dialable` key of a `/reach` it holds on a node thing whose actor currently reads the label, where no other node thing's `/reach` it holds names that key. Blind nodes refuse such proofs. A member peer that syncs a label with no server serves it as its own server: it issues a child step when it would re-register at a server (recorded in `/label`), holds the bodies it holds that the label must hold (§6) and the blobs they link, its certificates and heartbeats, and the inbox blobs it sends to an inbox label it syncs with no server, and drops what its sync would delete at a server (a body with live writes only once a carrier is under the label) or what expired. A body past its keep-at-most bound (spec/commits.md §5) is dropped all the same: that bound wins, and a member that may not write the aspect writes no carrier.
- **Re-registration**: an admin node that holds the epochs a server's head registers (the write and admin ones established) and, for any role, an established epoch newer than the head's (spec/crypto.md §4; its own rotation or another member's) issues a child of that head when it next syncs that label at that server: it first uploads there, under the head's keys, the membership commits of the actor the label's keys derive from (§2) that it holds and the server lacks (the rotation commits of the epochs it registers and of those they supersede in particular), then registers, then uploads anything else (a remover does so in the removal's sync, spec/permissions.md §3), and records the steps in `/label`. An admin that does not hold the head's epochs walks and reads first (§2) and registers nothing.
- **Deletion by delete key**: a node holding a delete key for a body the label must not hold (§6) deletes it under every label it syncs as soon as it sees the condition; a right holder that sees a body under a relative keep-at-most with no live write sends a delete timed at its bound (spec/commits.md §5). Whichever delete-key holder sees a valid purge executes it.

## 6. Protocol
- Framed messages over an ordered byte stream (§10); version negotiation, messages and limits: spec/wire.md §11. Every message has a fixed maximum size; every list is paged.
- Each side's handshake carries its wall clock (ms) and a random 32-byte challenge for opens (§5). A node SHOULD estimate its clock offset from peers' handshakes (round-trip corrected, robust to an outlier, ignoring samples whose round trip exceeds a few seconds) and use the corrected clock wherever wall time is used; it reports an offset of more than a few seconds as a diagnostic. A node SHOULD close a connection on which a request stays unanswered for minutes, and redial.
- **Cursor sync**: a client keeps a cursor per (peer, label) and sends: the cursor, its own new blobs inline, and the blob IDs it gained under that label from other sources since. The peer replies with its log after the cursor minus those IDs, blobs inline, and a want-list of the gained IDs it lacks, which the client then sends.
- **Range reconciliation** (fallback when a cursor is missing or invalid, or the gained list exceeds a message): range-based set reconciliation over the IDs of the live entries (additive fingerprints, 16-way splits; spec/wire.md §11.3).
- One connection may sync many labels; a blob under several of them crosses it once.
- **Convergence**: a label's **required set** is its closure blobs (§3) plus what is placed there from outside the closure: the certificate blobs of spec/permissions.md §4, the grantee's certificate copies and cross-published `/label` commits (§3), and unexpired inbox blobs. Per node, a body it holds is **must-hold** at a label (in the required set and, for a closure body, before its keep-at-least bound or established uncovered: the node can join it and no held snapshot of the aspect under the label covers it), **must-not-hold** (purged, expired, past keep-at-most, outside the required set, or a blob of commit C whose delta set is a strict subset of another held blob of C under that label that is not itself must-not-hold); a certificate blob outside the closure of a commit whose author no longer reads the label's actor and whose author's chain for it no entry under the label completes is outside the required set (judged per server by a node that applied the commit; a deposit is left to promotion, §5) or **may-hold** (the rest: covered, between bounds, parked, unfetched pages, a certificate blob whose author has no other blob under the label). A body meeting a must-not-hold condition is must-not-hold whatever else holds of it. After syncing, a writer uploads every must-hold body the label lacks (a blob of the commit that is not must-not-hold and holds every delta of the body that no snapshot under the label covers holds it), as a fresh encryption if the old blob ID is tombstoned; a reader relays the stamped must-hold entries it holds that the label lacks; neither uploads nor relays a body a delete removed from the label (an entry of it that is not a certificate copy, within `TOMBSTONE_LIFETIME`) while each of its live writes is carried there (it has none, or a snapshot under the label covers it): a right holder's keep bounds, dated by its own deaths (spec/commits.md §5), had passed; delete-key holders delete must-not-hold bodies. A node that dropped a body keeps knowing its entries (not its key or wrap); when the body counts as never received again (spec/permissions.md §5.2) it fetches them again where present, and otherwise takes the body from a holder's upload (it is must-hold there: no valid snapshot covers it). Nobody acts on may-hold bodies except a compactor dropping them once per body (commit and delta set, §7). A tombstone only keeps the identical blob out, against stale relays and bundles.
- A partial node repeats reconciliation until a pass changes nothing, since newly received data may grow its closure.
- **Ephemeral bodies** (§9) are not judged as above: a node holding an ephemeral body under a label uploads it (writer) or relays it (stamped) to each server and peer of the label that lacks it; a presence body only its author uploads, to every server and peer of the label it connects to, and nobody relays it. Its first Sync of a label on a connection lists the ephemeral entries it holds there as `gained` (when they fit the message), and it sends what the peer wants; an ephemeral entry received from one server or peer goes to the others it syncs the label with. A node's first sync of a label at a server after it starts reconciles the live sets first and keeps its cursor (the ephemeral entries the cursor passed are in no memory any more).
- Chunk and tree-node blobs of large atomic values may be fetched lazily, by local policy; pages of a delta are fetched before its commit is applied (spec/commits.md §4).

## 7. Compaction and deletion
- **Compact**: a node that may write an aspect uploads snapshot commits (spec/commits.md §2) for the aspects it compacts, then drops, and deletes at blind nodes, the bodies they cover that it may purge and whose keep-at-least bound (spec/commits.md §5) is `MAX_DRIFT` past on its clock, so that no node whose clock lags by up to `MAX_DRIFT` still judges them must-hold. Every right holder that holds a snapshot under the label, its author included whatever it wrote it for, does the same for its bodies, judging a body again while it is before that time or not established covered or uncovered. A body with live writes (in any of its deltas) is deleted at a label only while other entries under it carry each of them: a snapshot under the label covering it, or another blob of the commit holding it that is not must-not-hold and not an identical body with a higher blob ID (of two duplicates the higher goes without waiting). A right holder that must delete a body with live writes and finds no carrier uploads a covering snapshot first when it may write the aspect, unless it holds a blob of the commit that will carry them (a purge's rewrite); the snapshot-first order binds only drops that would leave a live write in no held blob (keep-at-most), not strict-subset or purge drops. A snapshot carries `/created` of each thing it covers. Chunk blobs still linked from a retained version are kept. Two snapshots covering each other are covered-equal; a compactor drops the one with the higher commit ID.
- Two blobs of one commit with the same delta set under one label: a delete-key holder deletes the one with the higher blob ID.
- A partial node may evict bodies it does not serve. Live state is never lost: a node keeps it in its own state (spec/crdt.md §4.6) and a label in an entry (above); a member serves current state from the bodies and snapshots it holds.

## 8. Subscriptions
A connection may subscribe to labels. The peer then pushes new log events (with blobs, wraps and stamps) as they arrive, in the cursor-reply format.

## 9. Ephemeral commits
- **Sending**: a node's own writes of ephemeral aspects and on ephemeral things (spec/data-model.md §1, §2) whose thing is in a labeled closure here go out as ephemeral commits (spec/commits.md §2): what was written since the last one, one commit per actor and expiry; a thing entering a labeled closure goes out whole (its ephemeral state without the values of writes that expire, and its record). Each ephemeral commit carries the `/created` of the ephemeral things it writes and of their ephemeral owner chains. Signal writes go as relay-only commits, each write batch's at once. They are placed under the labels whose closure holds their things (§3) and uploaded on every connection where the label is open. While every connection where a label is open has an upload of an own ephemeral (not relay-only) entry under it unanswered, the node's later writes of things that label's closure holds are not sent: they join what goes next, sent once one of those connections has none (answered, or the connection gone); likewise a presence commit for an actor is not built while every connection of the labels its previous one went under has an upload of an own presence entry for that actor unanswered. A covered entry a node deletes where it uploaded it, forgotten while that upload is unanswered, is deleted there once the upload is answered OK.
- **Holding**: every node holds the ephemeral commits it makes or receives, their blobs per label and their records, in memory only, never in its store or a bundle, until it restarts or a compaction covers them. A received one is verified as any commit (spec/commits.md §4) before its blob is kept, served or uploaded anywhere; one whose author's record is not held waits apart (bounded per label, neither applied, served nor uploaded) until it arrives; a part on a thing whose record is not held waits; parts are judged (spec/permissions.md §5.2); valid parts join the ephemeral state (spec/crdt.md §4.1); a part past its `expires` goes (spec/crdt.md §4.7). When a held part's validity changes, the aspect is rebuilt from the node's own writes and the valid held parts (spec/crdt.md §4.6). An ephemeral commit more than `MAX_DRIFT` ahead of the corrected clock is not held, nor one that valid held ephemeral commits list as absorbed on every aspect its parts are on (a writer deletes its entry where it found it). A node carrying others' writes in an ephemeral commit names in `Authors` the node a held valid part attributes the session to (a part naming its own author first) and leaves out writes of sessions it cannot attribute.
- **Compaction**: a node that may write an aspect compacts it when the held ephemeral bodies on it exceed twice its state and a floor (local policy), or when a server refuses an ephemeral upload of the label for its cap (local policy may skip a compaction that cannot shrink what the server holds): one ephemeral commit of the current states of such aspects whose held writes carry no expiry, together with every other such aspect of a held ephemeral commit on a compacted aspect when it may compact them all (repeated until none is added), each snapshot delta's `Absorbed` listing the held ephemeral commits all of whose parts are on compacted aspects. A node drops a held ephemeral commit once valid held ephemeral commits list it as absorbed on every aspect it touches; the compactor deletes the covered entries where it uploaded them (spec/crypto.md §7, level 3), before uploading the cover on the same connection.
- **Caps**: per label, a node holds at most a configured number of bytes of ephemeral bodies from others; further ephemeral commits of that label are not applied there (a diagnostic). A server's ephemeral cap: §5.
- **Presence commits**: a node's own writes of presence aspects (spec/data-model.md §2) go as presence commits: per actor, its whole own state of every presence aspect last written for that actor whose thing is in a labeled closure here, listing as absorbed every presence commit it authored for that actor before, after each such write, when such a thing enters a labeled closure, and every `KEEPALIVE` while any of those states is present. A presence commit absorbs only presence commits of its author, and is absorbed only by those; it is never compacted. A node holding another's presence commit drops it (un-merge, spec/crdt.md §4.6) as soon as no server or peer it syncs the commit's labels with holds an entry of it: a pushed deletion, the loss of its last connection to those (it reconciles the label on its next connection to that server, §6), or the close of the session it serves that uploaded it; and at `ts + MAX_LEASE`, its own included. One received past `ts + MAX_LEASE` is not held. A node that only bridges two servers or peers does not carry another's presence between them; a member peer serves what it holds to its sessions.
- **Relay-only commits**: a node drops a relay-only blob unopened when it is larger than `MAX_RELAY_BLOB`, its prefix expiry is passed or more than `2 × MAX_DRIFT` ahead of the corrected clock, or the node received that blob ID under that label before its expiry (remembered for at most a bounded number of blobs: past it the earliest-expiring is forgotten and its expiry becomes the label's floor, at or below which blobs are dropped). It verifies the rest like an ephemeral commit and delivers its valid signal parts to callbacks once; it holds and re-uploads nothing. It remembers delivered relay-only commit IDs until their `ts` is `MAX_DRIFT` old, at most 2^16 (past that the oldest is forgotten and its `ts` becomes a floor, at or below which relay-only commits are dropped), and drops relay-only commits whose `ts` is more than `MAX_DRIFT` from its corrected clock. A member peer serving the label passes a verified new one on, stamped as received: from a server to the sessions it serves, from a session to its other sessions and to the label's servers it syncs with. Within `MAX_DRIFT` of its `ts` a signal may arrive late, and a restarted node may deliver one again that a replayer uploads to a server that forgot it.

## 10. Transport and addresses
- Sync runs over any ordered, bidirectional byte stream with confidentiality and an authenticated peer key.
- A server address is a transport URL plus the server's static public key; a client MUST check the server's key against it.
- Clients connect to servers with an ephemeral transport key per connection. A node's dialable P2P key is separate from its signing key and published only in `/reach`, together with its preferred relays. Direct addresses are never published; they are exchanged with the peer at connection time (hole punching) or found by LAN discovery.
- **`quic://host:port`** server addresses (port required, an IPv6 host in brackets): QUIC v1 (RFC 9000, RFC 9001) with TLS 1.3 only, ALPN `osis`, 0-RTT MUST NOT be used. Both sides MUST authenticate (client authentication is mandatory) with RFC 7250 raw public keys: an RFC 8410 Ed25519 SubjectPublicKeyInfo, signature scheme `ed25519` only, no certificates. The server's key is the address key; a client's is fresh per connection, or a server's own address key when it dials another server. SNI is ignored. Each sync session (spec/wire.md §11.1) runs on a bidirectional stream the client opens; the dialer closes the connection.
- A node's dialable key and its LAN transport keys are Ed25519 public keys (RFC 8032), each with a 32-byte seed as its transport secret. A node dials co-readers with an ephemeral transport key, as servers; it proves itself a member node inside the session (open proof 3, §5).
- **LAN discovery** announces nothing stable across networks or meaningful to strangers: a tag `kdf("lan tag", lan secret, le64(floor(unix ms / 600000)))` (first 16 bytes) and a transport key fresh per network join, never the dialable key. The 32-byte random lan secret is in `/reach`; a co-reader matches tags of the nodes it knows for the current and adjacent windows. A node joins anew (a fresh transport key and listener, announced at once) whenever its network may have changed. Announcement (provisional): a UDP datagram to port 47380 on each address family the node has, as an IPv4 broadcast (255.255.255.255) and an IPv6 multicast to the link-local all-nodes group ff02::1 on each up interface with a link-local address, of exactly 124 bytes, `"osis lan 1"` (ASCII, 10 bytes) || tag (16) || transport key (32) || listening port (2, big-endian; the listener's port on the datagram's family) || sealed signature (64): the Ed25519 signature under the node's dialable key over domain `"osis v1 lan announce"` and digest BLAKE3(tag (16) || transport key (32) || be16(listening port)) (spec/crypto.md §2), XORed with the first 64 bytes of the XOF form of `kdf("lan sig", lan secret, le64(window) || digest)`, window as for the tag and digest the 32 bytes above; checking it needs both the lan secret and the dialable key. A receiver ignores any other datagram, one from a source address that cannot be on its LAN (only IPv4 loopback 127/8, private 10/8, 172.16/12, 192.168/16 and link-local 169.254/16, and IPv6 loopback, unique-local fc00::/7 and link-local fe80::/10 count; shared address space 100.64/10 does not), and one whose signature does not open and verify under the lan secret and dialable key of the node whose tag matched. It dials the announced key at the datagram's source address (an IPv6 link-local one on the interface it arrived on) and the announced port. That address is a candidate route to the node until a connection through it is up (the transport authenticated the announced key); a candidate never replaces a working route before that, and one whose connection fails is not dialed again for a while. A node connected to another off the LAN (through a relay) that hears it on the LAN dials the candidate beside that connection and, once it is up, closes the other: a proven LAN route is preferred.

## 11. File bundles
A bundle is a one-way log replay for a set of labels: registration paths, entries with wraps and stamps, blobs, deletions (spec/wire.md §12). An importer treats it as a peer with no cursor.
