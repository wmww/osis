# Sync
> Summary: Blobs and their clear prefix; labels, their self-certifying IDs, registration steps and role keys; which blobs go under which label (closure, entitlement, projection, certificate and grant blobs); blind-node state and the authorization of upload (writer stamps), deposit, download (sync key or claim key) and delete, timed deletes; inboxes and grant delivery; the per-label log protocol with range reconciliation fallback; label convergence and compaction; subscriptions and signals; transports and addresses; file bundles.

## 1. Blobs
- A **blob** is an encrypted commit (header plus unpurged deltas; spec/commits.md) or an encrypted chunk, tree node or page of a large value (spec/data-model.md §6, spec/crdt.md §8). Blind nodes cannot tell them apart except as §4 states.
- Layout: **clear prefix** || ciphertext (framing TBD). Prefix: a random 16-byte salt (fresh per encryption), optional expiry `T`, the token commitments (spec/crypto.md §7; list length capped). Ciphertext: spec/crypto.md §5, plaintext padded with Padmé (Nikitin et al., PURBs).
- **Blob ID** = BLAKE3 of prefix and ciphertext. A blob is at most `MAX_BLOB` bytes.
- One commit may travel as several blobs (projections, purge rewrites, certificate copies, concurrent re-encryptions): same header and commit ID, each with its own key, salt and ID. Every encryption of a commit computes its prefix from the header: expiry = the header's `expires`; commitments from the token hashes its deltas call for (spec/crypto.md §7) and the new salt. A chunk reused by a later commit keeps its blob and prefix, unless its expiry is earlier than the new commit's or its thing-secret tokens (spec/crypto.md §7) are not exactly those the reusing delta calls for; then it is re-encrypted. Author-chain tokens need not match.

## 2. Labels
- A synced group has a **label**: the unit a blind node stores. A label has an **index** i (0 for a group's first label) and Ed25519 role keys: **sync** `kdf("label sync", read secret, le64(i))`, **write** `kdf("label write", write secret, le64(i))` and **delete** `kdf("label delete", admin secret, le64(i))`, from the group thing's role secrets (explicit, or derived from its owner's by tid; spec/crypto.md §3), plus an optional **deposit** key `kdf("label deposit", deposit seed, le64(i))` from the deposit seed the actor publishes in `/keys` (spec/crypto.md §3).
- **Label ID** = first 16 bytes of BLAKE3(`"osis v1 label id"` || genesis delete public key). The genesis step derives from the admin secret current when the label is created.
- **Registration steps**: a tree. A step: label ID, `prev` (the step ID of its parent; none in the genesis step), the role public keys, a fresh X25519 **step key**, BLAKE3 of the **claim set**, the **entry boxes**, and a signature under `"osis v1 label register"` by the parent's delete key (the genesis step by its own). **Step ID** = BLAKE3 of the step. Each step is carried with its claim set. A server holds one path from the genesis step to its **head** (§4); a new step is a child of the target server's head (§5), so after a fork (spec/crypto.md §4) servers may hold different branches, each signed through. An admin MAY issue a step without a rotation (same role keys, fresh step key) to list readers granted since its parent.
- **Claim set** of a step: for each **reader** M of the registered read epoch (every actor holding its read secret as the registrar knows it: the holders of boxes carrying it, the owner included; of a plain group, its owner; of a link's actor before its first rotation, also the link key), the Ed25519 public key whose seed is `kdf("label claim", DH(step private key, M's X25519 public key), label ID || step key)`; M's X25519 key is the X25519 half of its read-role KEM key in the newest `/keys` of M the registrar holds (a key actor: its single KEM key; a link key: spec/crypto.md §3); a non-contributory DH result omits M. A reader that is a key actor other than a node (a recovery key, a link key) is listed instead by its **static entry**, `BLAKE3("osis v1 static claim" || label ID || its signing public key)`, the same 32 bytes. Padded with random keys to a power of two, in random order. The registrar erases the step private key after signing. M derives its claim key from its own private key and the step key; it tries each keyset epoch it has held.
- **Entry boxes** of a step: for each reader that is a key actor other than a node (a recovery key, a link key), the epoch's read secret boxed to it (spec/crypto.md §4 forms; AD = (label ID, step key)), so a key actor holding no secret of the group reads the label from the current step. Other readers get none: they read the rotations they missed in order (spec/crypto.md §5), or a re-seal (§3).
- A node talks to a server with the role keys of the epochs the server's head registers; it derives them from the matching epochs it holds (every member holds all older secrets by the history boxes). A node whose epochs match no step the server holds opens under the sync key of the newest step it shares with the server and walks from there (§5).
- `/label` on the group thing: `map<label ID, { index, steps: set<step>, servers: list<address>, replaced: bool }>`, each step with its claim set; an admin MAY drop the sets and entry boxes of steps superseded for `MEMBERSHIP_KEEP`. Membership-class. An address is a transport URL plus the server's static public key (§10). Nodes upload under, and sync, every label of the group not marked replaced.
- **Replacement** (a label lost at a server, e.g. to a removed admin re-registering first): an admin rotates the admin secret if the loss calls for it, creates label index `1 + max index in /label`, uploads the live blobs under it, marks the old label replaced, and at servers it still controls deletes the old label's content entries, keeping its membership-class commits and the `/label` commit that marks it replaced, so a node that knows only the old label finds the new one.
- **Home label**: an actor with role secrets (not a key actor) is synced as a group of itself; that label is its home label. The home closure holds the actor thing and what its `/group` lists, nothing by ownership. A link's K label is K's home label.
- **Inbox**: the label an actor with role secrets names in `/keys` for grants (§3), one of its labels (by default its home label), with servers and deposit seed. Key actors have none: a node receives its grant over a direct connection to the granting device (pairing) or as a grant blob under the target's label (§3); a recovery key or link key holds its label from its card or link and claims the current step by its static entry once a step lists it (§5).
- A thing in no labeled group is not synced.

## 3. What goes under a label
- **Closure**: the blobs of group G are the content commits touching any thing in G's transitive closure, plus the chunk, tree-node and page blobs they reference. Each node tags commits with their things' `/groups` and retags when group membership changes.
- **Entitlement**: a content blob belongs under the label of a group G iff G's actor may read every delta in the blob. G's actor is the group thing if it has secrets of its own, else its owner. Only a writer of G stamps entries (§5); a node that is not one deposits what it must place there (its chain certificates and node card, its invite grant or self-removal, its heartbeats, and the revokes and roster snapshots it must place by spec/permissions.md §4) and relays stamped entries it holds.
- **Projection**: a commit is uploaded as one blob per distinct (readable subset, token set) of its deltas (spec/crypto.md §7), each holding the deltas of that class and the commitments they call for, all with the same header and commit ID. So a token never deletes a delta it does not cover.
- **Retag**: when a thing joins a group, any writer of the label that holds the thing's live blobs adds them under it; when it leaves, token holders delete them under the old label as they see the change (§7).
- **Certificate blobs**: chain certificates, node cards, heartbeats, revokes, roster snapshots, and `/label` commits accompanying grants (spec/permissions.md §4) go under a label as a fresh encryption per label, never as a blob shared with another label. A roster snapshot copied as a certificate is projected to the edges and ledger entries the chain needs and omits `/secrets` and `/label`.
- **Grant blob**: a grant commit, projected to the grantee's boxes, boxed to the grantee (spec/crypto.md §5); other readers of the label cannot open it. Placed under the grantee's inbox by deposit, or by upload when the granter holds the inbox's write key (§5), together with the target's `/label` commit (spec/permissions.md §4). The inbox's writers delete consumed grant blobs. Rotations deliver nothing: a member that missed one reaches the rotation commit by claim (§5). A **re-seal** (spec/permissions.md §3) travels the same way, under the member's inbox or under the target's own label, which the member opens by claim; it is how a member that cannot reach the current epoch (its missed rotations compacted, its grant raced by a rotation, a fork branch it never received) gets back in.
- A grantee device that accepts a grant for its person actor (from its inbox, from K's label, or by pairing) places a certificate copy of the grant, with the `/label` commits it needed to reach the target, under the person's home label, so the person's other and future devices find it.
- **Contact card**: a link (spec/permissions.md §9) with read role to a person's profile thing P, a group of itself holding `/keys` and chain certificates only.
- A receiver keeps blobs a peer placed under a label even if its own closure disagrees.

## 4. Blind-node state
Per label: its registration path, the steps from the genesis step to its **head** (every step's role keys, step key, `prev` and signature; each step's claim set and entry boxes until it has been superseded for `MEMBERSHIP_KEEP`, longer by policy); a set of **entries** (blob ID, wrap or, for chunk/tree/page and grant blobs, a random 49-byte filler, stamp or none, deposited flag, optional pending deletion time); **tombstones** (blob ID) of deleted entries, kept `TOMBSTONE_LIFETIME` (30 days); an append-only **log** of events (entries added, deletions, registrations) with sequence numbers. Blob bytes are stored once per node and deleted when no label references them.

## 5. Operations and authorization
Each operation names a label. Writes are signed with the role keys of the server's head; reads need the label opened on the connection:
- **Open** (sync key or claim key): a signature under `"osis v1 label open"` over (label ID, the server's connection challenge from the handshake, its static key). Opens the label on that connection for download, specific-blob fetch, reconciliation, subscription and signal receipt. A sync key is matched against the head first, then against the newest superseded step on the path bearing it (a step without a rotation shares its parent's keys); a signature under a superseded step's sync key is answered with that step's child on the path (without its claim set) and nothing else; the client verifies it under the step's delete key before using it.
- **Claim**: an open signature under a key V, with (step ID j, V). If V is in step j's claim set: j the head opens the label, j on the path is answered with its child as above. Otherwise refused like an unknown key. A key actor other than a node sends its signing public key with the open signature instead of (j, V); the server opens the label if its static entry is in the head's set, and that key reads the current read secret from the head's entry box. A member that missed n steps walks them in n round trips, verifying each step under its parent's delete key (the genesis step against the label ID), then downloads the rotations and derives the current keys; the claim is bootstrap only.
- **Register** (delete key of the parent): accept a genesis step only if its delete key hashes to the label ID and the label is new; accept a step whose `prev` is the head; answer anything else (a step with another `prev`, an operation under keys of a step it does not hold) with the head. A step is registered with its claim set when the registrar holds it (it MUST hash to the step's); a step held without its set serves no claims at it. Relayable by any node.
- **Stamp**: a signature under the label's write key, domain `"osis v1 upload"` (spec/crypto.md §2), over (label ID, blob ID, wrap). A server checks it against the write key of its head; stamps already accepted stay valid across re-registration.
- **Upload** (label opened, stamp): add entries, each with wrap and stamp. Anyone holding a stamped entry may relay it. Refused: a bad stamp; a blob whose prefix expiry has passed; a blob ID with a tombstone; an oversized blob. An upload of a held blob ID replaces its wrap and stamp and clears its deposited flag (rate-limited per entry).
- **Deposit** (deposit key): add one entry with the given wrap (filler for a grant blob) and no stamp, flagged deposited in the log. Refused like an upload. Deposits are budgeted per label: at most `DEPOSIT_BASE` (64) + `DEPOSIT_PER_SLOT` (4) × (size of the current step's claim set) entries and that many × `DEPOSIT_ENTRY_BYTES` (32 KiB) bytes; an entry leaves the budget when stamped, deleted or expired. A writer that verifies a deposited commit the label must hold re-uploads it stamped; a heartbeat (spec/permissions.md §4) is never promoted.
- **Delete**: by the delete key, or of one entry by token: a token whose commitment is in the blob's prefix, plus a signature under the head's write key for a thing-write-secret token or its sync key for any other (spec/crypto.md §7). Optionally **timed**: the entry is removed at the given time instead of now (one pending time per entry, the latest request wins). Removal leaves a tombstone for `TOMBSTONE_LIFETIME`.
- **Expiry**: at the prefix's `T` the server deletes the entry outright: no tombstone.
- **Download**, specific-blob fetch, reconciliation (§6) and subscription (§8): only for labels opened on the connection; a blob is fetched by ID only as an entry of an opened label.
- Servers cap storage per label and may rate-limit deletes, wrap replacements and signals per label.
- A member peer serving a label applies the same rule, and may also serve a node it has authenticated (§10) as a node of a current member.
- **Re-registration**: an admin node that holds a verified epoch newer than the one a server's head registers (its own rotation or another member's) issues a child of that head at once, for every server in `/label`, and records the steps in `/label`.
- **Deletion by token**: a node holding a token for a body the label must not hold (§6) deletes it under every label it syncs as soon as it sees the condition; a node replacing a body under a relative keep-at-most sends the timed delete with the replacing upload (spec/commits.md §5). Whichever token holder sees a valid purge executes it.

## 6. Protocol
- Framed messages over an ordered byte stream (§10); version negotiation and message layout TBD. Every message has a fixed maximum size; every list is paged.
- Each side's handshake carries its wall clock (ms) and a random 32-byte challenge for opens (§5). A node SHOULD estimate its clock offset from peers' handshakes (round-trip corrected, robust to an outlier) and use the corrected clock wherever wall time is used; it reports an offset of more than a few seconds as a diagnostic.
- **Cursor sync**: a client keeps a cursor per (peer, label) and sends: the cursor, its own new blobs inline, and the blob IDs it gained under that label from other sources since. The peer replies with its log after the cursor minus those IDs, blobs inline, and a want-list of the gained IDs it lacks, which the client then sends.
- **Range reconciliation** (fallback when a cursor is missing or invalid, or the gained list exceeds a message): range-based set reconciliation over the IDs of the live entries (Negentropy/RBSR style; details TBD).
- One connection may sync many labels; a blob under several of them crosses it once.
- **Convergence**: per node, a body it holds is **must-hold** at a label (in the closure, before its keep-at-least bound or uncovered for this node), **must-not-hold** (purged, expired, past keep-at-most, outside the closure) or **may-hold** (the rest: covered, between bounds). After syncing, a writer uploads every must-hold body the label lacks, as a fresh encryption if the old blob ID is tombstoned; a reader relays the stamped entries it holds; token holders delete must-not-hold bodies. Nobody acts on may-hold bodies except a compactor dropping them once (§7). A tombstone only keeps the identical blob out, against stale relays and bundles.
- A partial node repeats reconciliation until a pass changes nothing, since newly received data may grow its closure.
- Chunk, tree-node and page blobs may be fetched lazily, by local policy.

## 7. Compaction and deletion
- **Compact**: a writer uploads snapshot commits (spec/commits.md §2) for the aspects it compacts, then drops, and deletes at blind nodes, the bodies they cover that it may purge and whose keep-at-least bound has passed (spec/commits.md §5). Every other right holder does the same for its bodies as it sees the snapshot. The same order, snapshot under the label first, applies to every deletion of a body with live writes. A snapshot carries `/created` of each thing it covers. Chunk blobs still linked from a retained version are kept.
- Two blobs of one commit with the same delta set under one label: a token holder deletes the one with the higher blob ID.
- A partial node may evict bodies it does not serve; a member MUST always be able to serve current state, from bodies or a snapshot.

## 8. Subscriptions
A connection may subscribe to labels. The peer then pushes new log events (with blobs, wraps and stamps) as they arrive, in the cursor-reply format.

## 9. Signals
- A signal is an envelope (spec/crypto.md §5) published under a label with a signature under its write key. Inside: sender node, actor, `ts`, tid, kind, optional target node, value. Sending needs write on the thing; receiving needs read; receivers verify the sender's signature and chain like a commit and drop signals whose chain they lack.
- A node receiving a signal forwards it to its other subscribers of that label and stores nothing. Clients do not re-forward. At most once, best effort, in order per sender per connection.
- A signal fires the sending node's own matching listeners. A node not named as the target drops a targeted signal.

## 10. Transport and addresses
- Sync runs over any ordered, bidirectional byte stream with confidentiality and an authenticated peer key.
- A server address is a transport URL plus the server's static public key; a client MUST check the server's key against it.
- Clients connect to servers with an ephemeral transport key per connection. A node's dialable P2P key is separate from its signing key and published only in `/reach`, together with its preferred relays. Direct addresses are never published; they are exchanged with the peer at connection time (hole punching) or found by LAN discovery.
- **LAN discovery** announces nothing stable across networks or meaningful to strangers: a tag `kdf("lan tag", lan secret, le64(floor(unix ms / 600000)))` (first 16 bytes) and a transport key fresh per network join, never the dialable key. The 32-byte random lan secret is in `/reach`; a co-reader matches tags of the nodes it knows for the current and adjacent windows and dials the announced transport key.

## 11. File bundles
A bundle is a one-way log replay for a set of labels: registration paths, entries with wraps and stamps, blobs, deletions (format TBD). An importer treats it as a peer with no cursor.
