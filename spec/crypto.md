# Cryptography
> Summary: Suite 1 primitives (Ed25519 strict, XChaCha20-Poly1305, BLAKE3, hybrid X25519 + ML-KEM-768 sealed box), signature domains, the KDF, keys per actor kind, `/keys`, `/secrets` boxes and history boxes, per-role epochs and the covers rule, blob encryption, wraps, inbox blobs, links, delete keys and blinded commitments, keyed hashes, algorithm agility.

## 1. Suite 1 primitives
- **Signature**: Ed25519 (RFC 8032) with strict verification: reject non-canonical encodings and small-order points (as ed25519-dalek `verify_strict`). Batch and single verification MUST agree.
- **AEAD**: XChaCha20-Poly1305: 32-byte key, 24-byte nonce, 16-byte tag.
- **Hash, KDF, MAC**: BLAKE3. `kdf(purpose, secret, input) = keyed_hash(derive_key("osis v1 " + purpose, secret), input)`; BLAKE3 XOF output where more than 32 bytes are needed.
- **Sealed box**: hybrid KEM X25519 + ML-KEM-768 (FIPS 203). Sender: X25519 ephemeral exchange (reject a non-contributory result) giving `ss_x`, `ct_x` (the ephemeral public key); ML-KEM encapsulation giving `ss_m`, `ct_m`. `K = derive_key("osis v1 hybrid kem", ss_x || ss_m || ct_x || ct_m || pk_x || pk_m)`. Box = `ct_x (32) || ct_m (1088) || AEAD_K(nonce 0, AD, payload)`. Public key `pk_x || pk_m`, 1216 bytes.
- **Randomness**: OS CSPRNG.
- Every hash osis computes itself is BLAKE3.

## 2. Signatures
- Signed input is `domain || digest`: an ASCII domain string, then the 32-byte BLAKE3 digest of the signed canonical bytes. Domains, one per signed object: `"osis v1 commit"` (input digest: the commit ID), `"osis v1 label register"`, `"osis v1 label open"`, `"osis v1 upload"` (stamps, spec/sync.md §5), `"osis v1 deposit"`, `"osis v1 delete"`, `"osis v1 lan announce"` (by a dialable key, spec/sync.md §10), `"osis v1 member open"` (by a dialable key, digest as `"osis v1 label open"`, spec/wire.md §11.2). Digests per object: spec/wire.md.
- Signers: node keys (commits by the node), recovery keys and link keys (commits signed as that key actor), label role keys (spec/sync.md §5), blinded delete keys (§7).

## 3. Keys per actor kind
- A `kdf` written with two arguments has empty input; a tid as input is its 15 bytes; an Ed25519 key "from" a kdf output is the key with that 32-byte seed (RFC 8032).
- **Role secrets**: 32 random bytes per role (read, write, admin) per epoch (§4).
- **Plain thing** (no non-owner member): role secret = `kdf("thing <role>", owner's <role> secret, tid)`, one per epoch of the owner's role (forks included), at the epoch with n = min(owner's n, 2^62 − 1) that the nonce rule (§4) gives the thing, role and derived secret; its **order n** (§4) is the owner epoch's order n while the thing is plain, its n once the thing has secrets of its own. Giving a thing secrets of its own (spec/permissions.md §3) is a rotation of all three roles from those derived epochs: the history boxes carry the derived secrets, the commit wraps under the derived read epoch, `/keys` is written. A node counts a thing as having secrets of its own iff it holds a membership-kind commit whose `/keys` part is valid (spec/permissions.md §5.2) and wrote a keyset in the body the node held (its body since dropped or missing counts); a thing whose only such part turns invalid is plain again (derived secrets), the secrets it learned from that commit kept for reading only (§4).
- **Key actor** (node, recovery key, link key): one 32-byte seed. Signing key from `kdf("sign", seed)`; KEM key pair from `kdf("kem", seed)` read as 96 XOF bytes: the first 64 the ML-KEM-768 keygen seed `d || z` (FIPS 203), the last 32 the X25519 private key; its X25519 half is also its claim key (spec/sync.md §2). Role secrets `kdf("role <role>", seed)`, all at the fixed epoch (0, 32 zero bytes), the one exception to the nonce rule (§4), never rotated: they exist so that things a key actor owns derive secrets like any plain thing. The creation record commits to the signing public key only. A link key's seed is in the link, a recovery key's in its card; the creation nonce of either is `kdf("creation nonce", seed, "")` (spec/wire.md §3).
- **Other actors**: one KEM key pair per role per epoch, `kdf("kem", role secret)`, so only holders of a role can open boxes sealed to that role. The read-role pair's X25519 half is the actor's claim key (spec/sync.md §2).
- **`/keys`**: a struct holding the current keyset only: per role `{ epoch, kem_key, delete_key }` (`delete_key`: §7; a key actor: one KEM key, no epoch), plus, for an actor other than a key actor, its **inbox** (label ID, servers; spec/sync.md §2) and **deposit seed** (32 random bytes, spec/sync.md §2). A role's entry is written by the node that rotates that role (spec/permissions.md §3); inbox and deposit seed by an admin, who MAY refresh the deposit seed at any time and MUST write a fresh one in each rotation of the read role it makes (a departed member can then no longer deposit under the actor's labels; neither can a current member that has not read the new `/keys`, so its stranded heartbeat, spec/permissions.md §4, is refused); a key actor's by its own key. A key actor's keyset is fixed by its seed and never rotates: a key actor changes keys only by being replaced (a new key actor added, the old one removed).
- **Dedup secret** of a thing = `derive_key("osis v1 dedup", creation nonce)`.

## 4. `/secrets` and epochs
- Each role secret of an actor has an **epoch** (n, nonce), independent per role. **Nonce rule**: the nonce of role r's secret s of actor A at n is `kdf("epoch", s, A's tid || role byte || le64(n))` (32 bytes; role byte 0 read, 1 write, 2 admin), so a secret names its epoch. A secret is the secret of the epoch its (actor, role, n) and the nonce rule name; a box, history box entry or entry box carrying one is read as naming that epoch. A rotation of a set of roles (spec/permissions.md §3) gives each a fresh random secret under epoch n+1 with the nonce the rule gives, n the greatest n of the epochs it supersedes (the role's current epochs at the rotator; epochs it has not established never count); the other roles keep their secret and epoch. Concurrent rotations of one role create several epochs with one n (a **fork**); nodes keep all of them. An epoch of r is **established** at a node when it is the node's own rotation, a key actor's fixed epoch or derived (§3) from an established epoch of the owner, when the node has applied its rotation commit (valid, spec/commits.md §4) and every epoch that rotation supersedes is established, or when a compaction snapshot of `/secrets` the node holds keys a box or history box with it; by rotation commit or snapshot only if its n is at most 2^62 or the node holds another established epoch of r with n at least its n − 1 (so n+1 stays within the int range). A node compacts `/secrets` only while every epoch its state keys is established. A role's **current** epochs are its established epochs that no established rotation superseded; its **newest** is the current one with the greatest order n (its n, a derived epoch's per §3), ties by lowest nonce; "newer" compares alike.
- `/secrets` = `{ boxes: map<(member tid, role, epoch), box>, history: map<(role, epoch), history box> }`. A rotation of role r writes r's boxes at the new epoch and removes the boxes keyed with role r at the epochs it supersedes (those it holds), so a concurrent rotation's boxes survive the merge; boxes keyed with other roles are untouched.
- A box seals one role's secret of one epoch to a member, AD = (actor tid, member tid, role, epoch, form fields). Forms (enum):
  - sealed: `{ recipient keyset epoch (none for a key actor), suite ID, sealed box }` to the member's KEM key for that role (§3);
  - symmetric: `{ epoch of s, wrap under kdf("box", s) }`, s the member's own secret of that role, used when the writer holds s (a thing's owner; a key actor by its seed holder).
- A key-actor member has one KEM key: a rotation or grant writes it one box, keyed (member, highest role among those written, the epoch written for that role), carrying every written role's secret with its epoch; a rotation's box also carries the newest current secret of each role below its key's role that the rotation does not write, since it replaces the box that carried them; a rotation that removes a key-actor member's box and writes it no role writes it one box carrying the newest current secret of each role it holds, keyed at the highest. A grant to a key actor carries each role's newest current epoch. Carrying a secret is encrypting under its epoch: a rotation also rotates a carried role whose epoch does not cover a known departure. For each role a member takes the secret from the box, of any key, carrying that role's newest epoch.
- A grant writes the grantee's boxes for every granted role at each of that role's current epochs. A rotation writes new boxes for every current member of each rotated role, the owner included, with the keysets and history boxes, in one membership commit (the **rotation commit**; its boxes page, spec/crdt.md §8). A commit rotating several actors together (spec/permissions.md §3) boxes each of them that is a member of another at its new epoch of that role (symmetric, under its new secret). A **re-seal** (spec/permissions.md §3) writes a current member's boxes for the current epochs of the roles it names.
- **History box** (role r, epoch e) = AEAD under `kdf("history", secret_r[e])` of every distinct earlier `secret_r` of an established epoch the rotator holds, forks included, written by the rotation of e; a grant or re-seal boxing e writes it again unchanged, so its recipient reads content under older epochs. A reader tries each live entry of a history key (spec/crdt.md §5.6), not only their merged read, with the secret of e it holds. A sealed box whose item for its key's role does not name the key's epoch, or a symmetric box whose secret does not name it, is ignored. `/keys` vouches for no secret: a node holding an epoch's secret derives its keyset itself (§3, §7).
- A **departure** from role r of actor A is a removal of a member M, a demotion of M below r, or M's edge passing its `until` (`until <= wall_ms` of the corrected clock at the time of encrypting). An epoch of role r **covers** a departure of M if it holds no box for M; it covers a departure inside a member M (a device of M, a member of an actor under M, and so on) if M's box in it names the newest keyset epoch of M for r (§3; the symmetric form's epoch of s) that the node holds and that epoch of M covers the departure (chains of members have no depth bound and may pass an actor more than once, A included (a membership cycle), but never pass the departed member (so a cycle is covered only by rotating it in one commit, spec/permissions.md §3); a node that holds no secret of M judges by M's newest `/keys` keyset it holds alone and by the boxes of M's `/secrets` it holds; and it counts that keyset's epoch as holding a box for a member that left M unless the keyset was set by the commit that made the departure, or the node holds the `/secrets` delta of the commit that set it and that delta boxes no such member; times decide nothing). An epoch whose rotation commit (the earliest valid commit writing its keyset in `/keys`) was written by a departed party (or a node acting through it) does not cover that party's departure; a node that may rotate r and holds no valid `/keys` delta writing the epoch's keyset (it may keep, for a delta it dropped, the epochs it keyed) counts the epoch as written by each departed party whose chain's first actor is A's owner or held an edge on A of write and at least r. A node that may rotate role r of an actor (spec/permissions.md §3) MUST NOT **encrypt under** an epoch of r that does not cover a departure it knows of; it rotates first. A departure whose newest edge write is the node's own self-removal (spec/permissions.md §2; signed by it or a key actor it holds) holds back none of the node's encrypting, here or under spec/permissions.md §4, until that commit is placed (spec/sync.md §5, Order); both judged from what the node stores, so a restart changes neither. Encrypting under an epoch means: wrapping under a label read key derived from its secret (§5), sealing or wrapping a box carrying its secret or a box under its secret or a KEM key derived from it, or signing a stamp, step or delete under a label key derived from it. Signing with a delete key (§7), opening a label and reading are not encrypting under. The one exception is the wrap of the rotation commit itself (§5). A node encrypts under established epochs only; the secret of any other epoch (one whose rotation commit it has not applied, or is pending or invalid, spec/commits.md §4) is kept for reading. A secret learned only from a pending or invalid commit (from its boxes, or from history boxes opened with such a secret) is kept for reading and never encrypted under until an applied valid box or a history box opened with a secret not so learned carries it, and such a commit's epochs count for nothing else (a box it holds that the node cannot open records nothing; what its history boxes list supersedes nothing).
- Each epoch names its suite ID.

## 5. Blobs, wraps, inbox blobs
- **Blob encryption**: a fresh random 32-byte key per encryption, nonce all zero, AD the clear prefix; nothing encrypts a blob under a caller-chosen key. Plaintext is Padmé-padded first (spec/wire.md §5). Blob = clear prefix || ciphertext (spec/sync.md §1). Blob ID = BLAKE3 of the whole blob.
- **Wrap** (per label, per blob): AEAD under `kdf("wrap", label read secret)`, nonce = first 24 bytes of the blob ID, plaintext = suite byte || blob key: 49 bytes. Deterministic. A wrap names no epoch: a reader tries the read secrets it holds, newest first. An uploader wraps under the newest current read epoch whose secret it holds (§4); a node holding no current secret places only its stranded heartbeat (spec/permissions.md §4), wrapped under the newest established secret it holds. Exception: a commit carrying boxes of a read epoch e (a rotation) is wrapped, under every label whose read secret derives from that actor's, under the read epoch e supersedes whose secret the rotator holds with the greatest n, ties by lowest nonce (n, not order n, §4), so the members it seals to can open it. Label role keys for a server are those of the epochs its current step registers (spec/sync.md §5).
- **Inbox blob** (spec/sync.md §3) = clear prefix || Padmé-padded sealed box to the recipient actor's read-role KEM key, AD = (recipient tid, `"inbox"`, clear prefix); payload = a membership commit (header and deltas) about the recipient, plus the label IDs and server addresses of the things it names (spec/wire.md §9). Its prefix expiry is a delivery lifetime (`INBOX_LIFETIME`, 90 days), not a commit expiry; it has no blob key and its entry's wrap is filler.

## 6. Hashes and links
- Keyed content hashes: delta hash `kdf("delta", dedup secret, delta bytes)`; link content hash `kdf("link", dedup secret, chunk or page bytes)`, for a tree node `kdf("link tree", dedup secret, le64(level) || for each child in order: content hash || le64(len))`, `len` the plaintext length under the child (spec/wire.md §6).
- Unkeyed BLAKE3: tids, commit IDs, blob IDs.
- **Link**: (link content hash 32, blob ID 32, suite byte || blob key 33).

## 7. Delete keys
- **Levels** of a delta, by its retention (spec/commits.md §5): `admin` and above, the admin secret of its thing (level 1); `author` and above, also the write secret of the actor directly above the author node in its chain (level 2; omitted for an imported delta, whose importer cannot derive it: its origin author purges at honest nodes by request and admins delete at blind nodes, spec/permissions.md §7); `writer`, also the write secret of its thing (level 3); `nobody`, none. A thing or actor without secrets of its own (§3) uses those of its owner, up its owner chain to the first actor with secrets of its own or a key actor (its **delete base**).
- **Delete key** of a role secret s: the Ed25519 key with seed `kdf("delete key", s)` (secret scalar a, public key A = a·B, RFC 8032); each keyset in `/keys` publishes its epoch's (§3). A delete base that is a key actor has its signing key (§3) as delete key at every level. A header lists, per level of each delta, the base's delete key of the newest current epoch of that level's secret the writer holds, else the `delete_key` of the base's `/keys` keyset of that role it holds, else the one an inbox payload named for the base (spec/wire.md §9), taken only once the carried commit applied valid, touches the base and is by an admin of it, the first kept until the base's `/keys` arrives; a writer holding none of these cannot write. Every delta lists exactly the levels its retention calls for (spec/wire.md §4).
- **Commitment** in a blob prefix = level byte || A' = h·A, h = `kdf("delete blind", A, prefix salt || blind)` (A as its 32-byte encoding, `blind` the 16 random bytes of the header that lists A, or for a chunk, tree node or page of the header that introduced it) read as 64 XOF bytes, little-endian, mod ℓ (the order of B). A blob commits to the delete keys of the deltas it serves (spec/wire.md §5); A' is computable only by readers of that header and unlinkable to A without it.
- **Delete signature** for a blob: Ed25519 (RFC 8032 signing) over `"osis v1 delete" || digest` (§2, spec/wire.md §11.2) with secret scalar h·a mod ℓ, nonce prefix `kdf("delete nonce", seed, prefix salt || blind)` (seed: the delete key's RFC 8032 seed), and public key A'; it verifies strictly under A' (§1). A server checks it against the blob prefix's commitment at its level and requires the label signature that level calls for (spec/sync.md §5).
- A holder tries every epoch it holds of the level's secret along the delete base's owner chain (a thing that got secrets of its own keeps its earlier content under its former base's keys) and signs with the one whose key the header lists.

## 8. Agility
- Keys, signatures, KEM public keys and sealed boxes are enums (`signing_key`, `signature`, `kem_key`, `sealed`) with one case today. Cases are never removed; a new primitive is a new case.
- AEAD and KDF choices ride on the suite byte of wraps and links and the suite ID of a secret epoch. Suite 1 is the only suite.
- An unknown case or suite parks (spec/versioning.md §2).
- Migrating an actor to a new suite is a new epoch under that suite; old content stays under its suite. A key actor migrates by being replaced (§3).
