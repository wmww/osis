# Permissions
> Summary: Actors, membership and ownership; role secrets, rotation and re-seal; chain certificates and what travels under a label; the two-layer validity rule (one membership timeline over all edge writes and per-delta content validity, both by the demoter's frontier); roster snapshots with their tenure ledger; moving a thing to a new owner (copy with imported history, `/moved` and `/was`); purge rights; share links.

## 1. Concepts
- **Actor**: a thing with role secrets (spec/crypto.md §3). A **key actor** (node, recovery key, link key) also has a signing key committed by its tid and owns itself. A **node** is a key actor whose seed never leaves its device. A **person actor** represents a user; its nodes are its members (by app convention its owner is a recovery key and its nodes are its admins).
- **Ownership**: every thing has exactly one owner actor, fixed by its creation record. An actor is a thing, so it has an owner too; owner chains end at a key actor, within `MAX_OWNER_DEPTH` (64) owner links: a delta written on a thing further below its key actor (judged from the creation records alone) is invalid, its `/created` excepted; the rest of its commit is judged as usual.
- **Membership**: an edge (member actor, target, role) with roles read < write < admin, stored in the target's `/members`. On an actor it grants the role on everything the actor owns; on a plain thing, on that thing only.
- **Effective role** of a node on a thing at time t: the maximum over paths from the node to the thing of the minimum role along the path, where a path climbs membership edges and owner links (an owner holds admin on what it owns). Evaluated with the timeline (§5.1) at t.
- **Admin** = write + change membership + purge (§8). An admin edge on a plain thing grants membership changes and purge on that thing.
- An actor's owner is implicitly its admin and cannot be removed by other admins. Other admins are equal and may remove each other.
- The membership-class aspects of a thing (spec/data-model.md §9) are written only by its admins, whatever other role a node holds, except invite grants and self-removals (§2), and what rotations and re-seals write into `/secrets` and `/keys` (§3).
- Group membership (`/group`) grants no rights.
- An ephemeral thing (spec/data-model.md §1) has no membership-class aspects: a node's role on it is its role on a new thing owned by the nearest durable thing on its owner chain. Ephemeral writes need the role the durable write would need.

## 2. Membership writes
- Grant, change and removal of an edge are writes to the target's `/members`, each in its own membership commit (spec/commits.md §2), by a node with admin on the target. A removal of an absent edge is an edge write too (a reset of the member's entry). A grant or change is remove + put: a reset of every entry of the member the writer holds, live or not, and a new entry holding the whole edge (spec/crdt.md §5.6 `remove`, then `put` of an absent key). A `/members` delta writing inside an entry it does not create is bad input (judged from the delta and its header: the entry's dot is outside the delta's own dots). A reset of an entry whose creation the reader does not hold names no member until that creation is held.
- An edge may carry `until: timestamp`: the edge counts as removed from `wall_ms(t) >= until`.
- An edge may carry `invite: role`, at most its role. A node's **effective invite** on T is the maximum over its paths to T of the minimum of the roles along the path before the last edge and that edge's invite. An **invite grant** adds an edge for an actor with no current edge on T (an expired edge counts as none), with role and invite at most the author's effective invite, and carries the grantee's boxes for those roles. Its `/secrets` aspect is one delta holding only new keyed entries (puts, spec/crdt.md §5.6: each entry's dot is created by that delta): boxes keyed to its grantees at or below each one's granted role and history boxes at or below the highest granted role, with no reset of the value, a field, a map or any entry. Judged from the commit alone; a `/secrets` delta that breaks this gets no invite exemption and is judged like any `/secrets` delta (the highest role it rotates or boxes, at least write, §3, §5.2); the grant's edge is judged on its own (§5.1). Invite holders cannot change or remove any other edge.
- **Self-removal**: a node with admin on actor X may always remove X's edge on any target, or lower its role, invite or `until`. Such a write is a self-removal when it lowers the author's own edge, or when admin on X is the author's only authority for it (no owner-chain or edge-admin authority on the target at that point, through X included); otherwise it is an explicit demotion (§5.1).
- A write that grants, changes or removes an edge whose member is actor G MUST also be delivered to G as an inbox blob (spec/sync.md §3), so G's admins know what G is a member of and erase what G lost.
- The roster apps read from `/members` is the membership timeline's roster (§5.1), not the CRDT join of the stored deltas.

## 3. Secrets and rotation
- Each role secret of an actor is sealed to every member holding that role or higher, every epoch (spec/crypto.md §4).
- A plain thing uses derived role secrets until it gets a non-owner member; it then gets random secrets of its own by a rotation from the derived epochs (spec/crypto.md §3), boxed to its owner and each member, and a `/keys`. A thing that is to be made a member of anything gets secrets of its own first.
- A departure (spec/crypto.md §4: removal, demotion, `until`) of member M from actor A (a device removal is one, on the person actor) requires rotating every role secret M could open through A: on A the roles M left; on every thing with secrets of its own that A's roles open (what A owns, the targets of A's edges, and so on transitively) the roles A holds there among those the roles M left open through A (a role secret opens only boxes of that role, spec/crypto.md §4). Content is not re-encrypted. The write by another node that removes or demotes M on A and the rotation of A's roles it requires are one membership commit (spec/commits.md §2), so no node holds that rotation without the departure; a self-removal (§2) carries no rotation, the departure stays uncovered until the next node that may rotate does so. Every other rotation is required of whoever may make it, when it next encrypts under the secret (spec/crypto.md §4); nobody is required to sync anything to find it.
- **Who rotates**: a node with effective role write or above on an actor may rotate its roles at or below that role (a writer rotates read and write, an admin all three). A rotation seals the new secrets to every current member of each rotated role, writes the history boxes and the rotated roles' keysets in `/keys` (spec/crypto.md §3, §4); the other roles keep their epochs. A rotation boxing a member that holds no box at a current read epoch before it, as the rotator's applied state stands (it cannot open the rotation from a label, where it is wrapped under that epoch) is also delivered to that member as an inbox blob (spec/sync.md §3; to a node, over a direct connection). Its validity is the author's role at its `ts` (§5.2).
- A node that may rotate a required rotation MUST do so before it next encrypts under a secret it would replace; it MAY rotate eagerly. A node rotating for a departure inside a member M of A (spec/crypto.md §4) first rotates, top-down along the chain from the departure, the roles of each intermediate actor whose epoch does not cover it, where it may; where it may not, it does not encrypt under A's epoch at all (it is stranded until that intermediate rotates), and it does not rotate A for a departure a fresh epoch of A would not cover either.
- **Re-seal**: a node that may rotate a role may write a current member's boxes for that role's current epochs, to the member's newest keyset it holds and with those epochs' history boxes, changing nothing else (spec/crypto.md §4), delivered as an inbox blob (spec/sync.md §3; to a node, over a direct connection, spec/sync.md §2) and placed under the actor's labels like any membership commit (later rotations remove its boxes, so every node must hold it). A node that may rotate r SHOULD re-seal a current member of r that it sees without a box at a current epoch of r, or whose heartbeat reports it stranded (§4), before it next writes under the actor. It decides at the end of a complete sync pass of the actor's labels (one that read the log of every server, and of every peer it received the label from, can reach and that still reads it, to its end; a node MAY stop waiting for peers a bounded time after the membership change it decides on, policy), never for a member whose edge's `until` is within `MAX_DRIFT`, and only at epochs established through their rotation commits (spec/crypto.md §4); at an epoch established by a snapshot alone it does not re-seal and MAY rotate instead.
- Label role keys (spec/sync.md §2) derive from the newest epoch of each role; only an admin registers them, and an admin that holds an epoch newer than a server's head, or whose actor's deposit seed derives a deposit key other than the head's (then only once it has opened every entry under the label it can open and applied every `/keys` write among them: else a newer seed may be on its way), issues the next step when it next syncs that label there, after uploading the rotation commits it registers (spec/sync.md §5).
- A blind node serves a label only to holders of its current sync key or of a claim key in its current step (spec/sync.md §5); a member that missed rotations claims its way through the steps it missed, then downloads them and opens each under the epoch before it (spec/crypto.md §5), or is re-sealed (a claim set dropped on the way, or a rotation compacted, strands it until then).
- A node that removes a member or device MUST, in the same sync and before any other upload, place the removal and the affected actors' new `/keys` under every label it tracks that the removed party could read (as certificates, §4); until the new `/keys` reaches a label, rotations there still seal to the old keyset.
- A node that learns it lost read access to an actor MUST drop those role secrets and every local unit it can no longer read by another path. A held pending membership commit boxing the node secrets of the actor (or of one a role on which gives one on it) is no loss of access only if the node holds no checked secret of the actor (only reading candidates, spec/crypto.md §4), or the commit is signed by its author, who holds admin or an invite on its actor as the node sees it now, and its `ts` is less than a day before now (and not more than `MAX_DRIFT` ahead). Whatever its `ts`, such a signed commit keeps its boxes through the drop (it may still apply and then gives the node the secrets with the role); the node reads nothing from them before it applies. A held redemption (a commit removing its signer's own edge on a thing beside a grant there) granting the node, that stands void only because an applied redemption by the same signer removed that edge first, with no explicit write on that edge since (an unfinalized single-use link race, §9), is no loss yet: the node keeps what that grant would give and keeps syncing its labels until such a write arrives.

## 4. Verifying authors: certificates
- A write's authority is checked through the chain from the signing node up: each link is a membership write or an owner link (tid commits to the owner), ending at a key actor whose tid commits to its public key. No secrets are needed.
- **Chain certificates** of a node: for each thing on its chain, the `/created` record, the epoch start (§6, projected to the entries the chain needs), the later membership writes that establish each edge (projected to their edge deltas), and the current `/keys` commit of each actor on it; closed under authors (the chain certificates of each such write's author at its time) and including every held demotion of those authors, with that author's chain certificates for the same thing at the demotion's point. Its **node card**: its `/keys`, `/node` and `/reach` commits (each a commit of its own touching nothing else).
- When a node first places anything under a label, and whenever they change, it places its chain certificates and node card under that label (spec/sync.md §3; a non-writer of the label by deposit), except the deltas an entry under the label already holds: a certificate copy or closure blob placed by any node counts once the node has opened it as the same commit and checked each of its deltas against the header's delta hash (spec/commits.md §4), while it is present at every server the node syncs the label with; a blob of a rotation commit of the label's actor (wrapped under the epoch it supersedes, spec/crypto.md §5) counts only if it opens under the newest read epoch. Deltas on things in the label's closure go as closure blobs, never as copies. A demotion or `/keys` write by another node is its author's to place (§3); a node MAY wait up to 30 s after receiving one before copying it. A promoter completes the chain it holds: a writer promotes a deposited commit only once its author's chain for it is under the label, placing the missing parts itself (spec/sync.md §5). `/reach` goes under every label by default; an app MAY exclude labels (e.g. public or bearer-read ones). Without it, co-readers of that label cannot dial the node off-LAN. A node that excludes a label already holding its current `/reach` SHOULD rotate it: a new dialable key and lan secret, no longer listening with the old key, the new `/reach` placed only where not excluded.
- **Heartbeat**: a node MAY place under a label it tracks a commit writing `/seen` on its node thing (`{ ts: the commit's ts, stranded: bool }`) with `expires` no earlier than `HEARTBEAT_MIN_LIFETIME` (1 day) and no later than `HEARTBEAT_MAX_LIFETIME` (14 days) after `ts`, at most once per `HEARTBEAT_MIN_INTERVAL` (6 hours) per label; a non-writer by deposit. It MUST place one with `stranded: true` when it cannot read the label's current epoch: it reached the head and after a complete pass there cannot open the epoch it registers, or a claim on the way was refused (spec/sync.md §5); it is wrapped under the newest established read secret the node holds (spec/crypto.md §5). The live heartbeat of a node under L (the newest `/seen` commit received under L, not the aspect's joined read) gives its last sync of L; absent means unknown. Heartbeats are never promoted (spec/sync.md §5). The node card itself is never re-placed unchanged.
- Any member that syncs a label and holds a revoke or roster snapshot about the author of an entry under that label that it judges invalid, or cannot judge yet, places it there (a non-writer by deposit), with the chain certificates of that commit's own author. A revoke changes no verdict on the writes its frontier covers, so it is not placed for those alone. Exception to spec/crypto.md §4: a member that may not rotate the label's actor A places, under a read epoch of A that does not cover a departure it knows of, only the revokes of the departures inside members of A that leave that epoch uncovered, so A's rotators learn of them: for a departure of X from actor M (a member of A, or the last actor on a chain of members below one), the newest applied write lowering X's edge on M, whole (its deltas on M, its rotation's boxes included), with its author's chain certificates (a departure by `until` has no such write); and the current `/keys` commit of each actor on that chain from A's member to M, projected to that `/keys` delta, with its author's chain certificates. Nothing else goes under that epoch, except what spec/crypto.md §4 exempts (its own self-removal until placed). The departed party can read these: those actors' new public keys and box keys, M's `/members` and `/tenures` deltas (its roster at that rotation) and the writes' authors' chain certificates, not the boxes.
- The grantee's current `/keys` commit goes under the target's label as a certificate, so every admin can seal and list the grantee.
- A commit whose author's chain has not arrived is pending (spec/commits.md §4).
- A node that does not sync a thing applies the certificate deltas it holds; its timeline for that thing runs over the entries it was given.

## 5. Validity
Validity is a deterministic function of the commits a node holds.

### 5.1 Layer 1: membership timeline
The timeline covers **edge writes**: writes to `/members` and roster snapshots. Every other membership-class write (`/secrets`, `/keys`, `/label`, `/moved`, `/was`) is judged like content (§5.2) by the role its aspect requires (§1, §3).
1. Order all held edge writes, over every thing, by (`ts`, author node, commit ID). For each thing, start at its epoch start (§6) if it has one, ignoring its edge writes other than snapshots ordered before it; else at its first held write.
2. Walk the writes, skipping the drop set D (initially empty). A write applies iff its author has the required authority at that point: admin on the thing; for an invite grant, the effective invite it needs; for a self-removal (§2), admin on the edge's member; a write lowering another member's edge by an author that also has owner-chain or edge-admin authority on the thing is an explicit write of that class (step 3). Authority at a point is evaluated over every thing's state at that point, so the walk is one joint computation.
3. Attribute each applied write by X on N to X's **authority class** on N at that point: owner chain, edge admin, or invite (with its level); owner chain before edge, admin before invite.
4. An applied write R at time T **demotes** X on N when X's effective class on N (the maximum over all its paths) falls: from the owner chain, from edge admin to below, or to a lower invite level. R then adds to D every earlier applied edge write by X on N, and, when N is an actor, on everything below N by ownership (transitively) where X's class also falls, attributed to a class X fell through (above its class after R, up to its class before R) and not covered by R's **frontier**, and the walk restarts. Losing a path while another keeps the class is no fall: removing X's admin edge on N while X keeps the owner chain audits nothing, and X's later removal from the chain audits its edge-admin writes too.
An explicit write on edge (M, T) judges X's class before it with the edge **as restored**: the join (componentwise maximum, alive) of the edge's values since the last explicit write on it, values taken away by `until` or self-removal included, plus, for X, the standing it kept as the author of an earlier explicit write on that edge (below).
R never demotes its own author: an author that is or reaches the member (by owner links and edges alive just before R) keeps the standing the edge as restored for it gave it, even when R writes it unchanged, until the next explicit write on that edge, which audits it against the edge as restored.
Self-removals are never added to D, except a single-use redemption's (§9), which is attributed like its commit's grant. Each edge write of a commit is judged on its own, against the state before the commit.
5. A walk that adds nothing to D has settled; the timeline is the settled walk of the final round of §6. D is rebuilt from scratch whenever the held set changes.
- **Frontier**: R embeds what A had received of X's writes as of T on the aspects R puts in question (membership-class aspects for any demotion; every aspect of the things the demoted authority gave write on, for a demotion below write): per (tid, ak, node of X) the last counter (inclusive) up to which A had received X's writes contiguously, plus the commit IDs of X's commits judged by commit ID (commit-form writes, imports, compaction snapshots) and of the membership commits and roster snapshots among A's chain certificates for R's target at R's point (§4), which A holds and so has received. A write is covered when its counters are or its commit ID is listed. Received means decrypted and verified, not stored or relayed; a frontier MUST NOT cover what A had not received as of T. Besides R itself, nothing but the frontier decides what X's writes before T survive: a write on A's chain that the frontier does not list is audited like any other. A frontier above `CHUNK_THRESHOLD` is a chunked large value referenced by link (spec/commits.md §1).
- A demotion by `until` or by self-removal audits nothing. The next explicit write on that edge, by any node, audits with its frontier against the edge as restored.
- The effective role of a chain at time t is the minimum over the timelines along it.

### 5.2 Layer 2: content
- A delta by actor X on thing N is valid if X held write or above on N at the commit's `ts` and no valid explicit demotion of X below write on N is ordered after it (a ledger entry with a frontier counts as an explicit demotion at its key, §6).
- Otherwise it is valid iff the frontier of X's newest valid explicit demotion below write on N (ledger entries included) covers its dot.
- A compaction snapshot S's delta of an aspect is judged as its author's delta of that aspect by the rules above, at S's key, by commit ID (a frontier covers it iff it lists S's ID), needing the aspect's role (§1: admin for `/label`, `/moved`, `/was`, else write); `/created` is always valid. An invalid S gives nothing: its body joins nothing, covers nothing (spec/commits.md §5), its frontiers and `Absorbed` count as not received (§5.1) while it is invalid, also when it was valid on arrival, its writes are no import origin (§7). A valid S vouches for every write it carries: no carried write is judged as its claimed author's.
- A write's own commit is the one holding it on that aspect: for a node-form write, the commit whose range holds its dot; for a `Commit(C)` source on thing N, C if it is a commit-form commit with a delta of that aspect, else an import on N with a delta of that aspect listing C among its origins (§7; C itself, on the moved thing, owns nothing on N). Where the receiver received that delta (held, or held and dropped; not a commit held without it), the write counts from the delta alone: a snapshot's records under the delta's dots (stamps, element creations with their chars and anchors, at any `ts`, value or position, and dots the delta does not use; an entry's key goes with the entry unless others' writes remain in it, whose records repeat it) count for nothing, in the snapshot body and in any state they reached before the delta was received, except a held valid snapshot that covered the delta's body when it dropped (spec/commits.md §5), or covered a snapshot that did when that one dropped, and so on: its writes under the delta's dots are the delta's and count (they give the write again where the state lost it to a write since turned invalid); the write is un-merged (spec/crdt.md §4.6) iff its delta is invalid and no held valid snapshot carries a write under its dot. Where the receiver never received that delta, the write counts as a valid snapshot gave it: when a held snapshot turns invalid, the writes it carries under such dots go from the state (exactly those writes; others' writes nested under them stay) and the other held valid snapshots give their copies under those dots again. A record carrying no `ts` (a tombstone, a bare anchor) carries no write. A valid snapshot's body dropped leaves what it gave in the state and vouches no more. When a held snapshot turns invalid, each body of that aspect dropped as covered (compaction, spec/sync.md §7; not for its keep bounds, expiry or a purge) whose covers (the held valid snapshots that covered it when it dropped) none carries any more (a coverer carries it if it is held with its body and valid, or dropped under covers one of which carries it) counts as never received from then on: a delta's own writes go from the state (exactly those writes; the held valid snapshots give their copies again), a snapshot gives nothing, and the body joins as a late part (spec/commits.md §4) when it arrives again.
- An actor holding the required role in the timeline's final state, every edge with an `until` counted as removed, is re-granted: each of its writes ordered at or after the first point it held that role is valid again, including those in the gap. Edge writes in the gap stay dropped.
- A revoked actor cannot reuse a covered dot: two commits by one node with overlapping node-form ranges on one aspect are both invalid (spec/commits.md §4).
- A delta that becomes invalid is un-merged (spec/crdt.md §4.6).
- A part of an ephemeral commit (spec/commits.md §2) is judged as a compaction snapshot's delta of its author: by commit ID at the commit's key, needing the aspect's role (§1; on an ephemeral thing, through its nearest durable owner); a part on a core aspect other than `/group` and `/created` (spec/data-model.md §9) is invalid. A part carries only its author's writes (a receiver joins no other write, spec/sync.md §9, spec/sync.md §9), so it stands for exactly its author's delta. A demotion's frontier lists no ephemeral commit, so an explicit demotion invalidates all the demoted node's ephemeral writes; what becomes invalid is un-merged from the ephemeral state (spec/sync.md §9).

### 5.3 Revoking (client behavior)
Before stamping a role-lowering membership write a client SHOULD sync every label of every thing the demoted authority gave write on (found by walking the edges and `/label` writes below), and take the write's `ts` after applying what it downloaded. When it cannot (a label unreachable, unopenable or too large, or the app will not wait) it SHOULD let the app choose between failing and stamping anyway; uncompacted writes by the demoted member under the labels not synced are then lost (spec/requirements.md, Permissions). An explicitly backdated revoke skips this.

## 6. Roster snapshots
- A **roster snapshot** of thing N is a membership commit with the roster-snapshot marker, by a node acting through N's owner chain (admin of N's owner actor or of an actor above it, or the key actor at the top; never by an edge on N). It carries reset-plus-full-state deltas of N's `/members` and `/tenures` as the author's timeline computes them, one delta per edge and per ledger entry plus one per aspect carrying the reset alone (spec/commits.md §2), an edge of role write or above also carrying `since`, the key of the write that started its tenure in force (with none in force, of the write that set the edge), absent if unknown. A write's **key** is its position (`ts`, author node, commit ID) in the order of §5.1. It has no frontier. N's other membership-class aspects are untouched: they compact like any aspect (spec/commits.md §2).
- An explicit write **audits** when the edge as restored (§5.1 step 4, a demoter's kept standing included) exceeds its new value: the new value is absent or expired when written, has a lower role or invite, or carries an `until`.
- **Tenure ledger** (`/tenures`): one entry per tenure of role write or above on N that ended before S: member, role, `since` as above, `until` = the key of the write that ended it (a demotion from admin to write ends an admin tenure and starts a write tenure; an expiry by `until` has the key (first `ts` at `until`, the all-zero tid, the all-zero commit ID)), and, when that write was an explicit demotion that audited (§5.1 step 4), its frontier (inline or by link; empty if it carried none), else none. An explicit write on the member's edge that audits while no tenure of it is in force (after an end by `until` or self-removal, or the standing its demoter kept, §5.1) is an entry of its own: `since` = `until` = its key, the highest role it audits, its frontier; one that audits from above the role of the tenure in force ends that tenure, which goes on from its key. An entry with a frontier stands for an explicit demotion by `until`'s node at that key. The previous epoch start's ledger is carried forward whole; entries are never dropped.
- **Epoch starts** are chosen for all things together, in rounds. Let T be the number of things with a held roster snapshot that takes part in the walk (not one withheld as bad input or awaiting its frontier blobs). The first round, and the first after each restart, has no starts. A round walks the timeline (§5.1, D from scratch) under the current starts until D settles. In that walk every held snapshot S of N is judged at its position (its key), whether or not it is N's start and whether or not it is ordered before N's start: S applies iff it is not in D and its author has owner-chain authority on N at that point; an applied S is an edge write on N attributed to the owner-chain class, so a later demotion of its author may add it to D (§5.1 step 4). After the round, N's new start is its newest snapshot (by key) that applied in the settled walk and is not excluded; N has none if no such snapshot exists. If the new starts equal the current ones for every thing, they are the epoch starts and that walk is the timeline. Otherwise, if this round is round T+1 since the last restart (or since the first round), every snapshot that is a start in exactly one of the current and the new starts is excluded and the rounds restart; else the next round runs under the new starts. An excluded snapshot is never chosen as a start again in this evaluation; it is still judged, attributed and audited in every walk.
- **Under an epoch start S of N**: N's edge writes ordered before S are ignored (N's snapshots are not). At S's position N's edges become S's roster, whether or not S itself applies. In the walk, a value of S's roster or ledger takes effect at its key, just after the commit that key names (whose edge writes on N are ignored). S's roster and ledger answer what role anyone held on N before S: a member held a role at a point inside one of its ledger tenures of that role, or after its roster edge's `since` and after every ledger tenure of its own; a roster edge without `since` counts from the beginning. Each ledger entry with a frontier takes part in the walk (§5.1) as the explicit demotion of the member's edge it records, so edge writes made through N before it are audited as that demotion audited them. A demotion of X after S audits X's writes before S too, including those under tenures without a frontier.
- A linked frontier's tree blobs MUST be in the commit's chunk list, its value MUST be a `frontier`, and the distinct linked frontiers of one snapshot (by content hash) MUST total at most `MAX_LINKED_FRONTIERS` canonical bytes; a snapshot breaking these, or naming one member in two `/members` deltas, is bad input. A writer refuses a snapshot that would.
- A certificate copy of S carries the edge and ledger deltas the verifier needs and the reset-only deltas (spec/sync.md §3); its signed header still shows a co-reader the roster's and ledger's sizes (one delta hash per edge and entry) and the number of linked frontier chunks.
- S replaces N's earlier `/members` and `/tenures` deltas (their writes die at S's `ts`); their retention keeps them at least `MEMBERSHIP_KEEP` after that (spec/commits.md §5).

## 7. Moving a thing
- A thing never changes owner. Moving thing T to a new owner X is a **copy**: a node with rights on X creates a thing M (owner X, fresh nonce, creator the node, spec/wire.md §3) and imports T's history into it. Membership on M is X's to grant; nothing carries over by itself.
- `/was` on M (register, tid): the thing M continues. `/moved` on T (register, tid): the thing T continued as. Both are membership-class, written by the thing's admins.
- **Import commit**: a content commit on M with the import marker, every delta on M (an import with deltas on several things is bad input). It carries no own writes (empty counter ranges). Its deltas carry writes taken from commits of other things (the **origin commits**: ordinary commits, whose own-source writes are imported, or snapshots, whose carried writes are imported), re-sourced: a write of origin commit C gets the dot (`Commit(C)`, k), k its counter in C for an own-source write, else its rank (from 1) by (source, counter) among the dots of the writes C carried on that aspect (stamps and element identities; a `Derived` dot as its underlying dot), and keeps its `ts` (an import carrying a stamp whose `ts` is above its own is bad input); dots the imported writes reference (anchors, reset stamps, sibling keys) are mapped the same way. A write is imported from one origin: a valid snapshot (§5.2) that covers it (its frontier holds the write's counter; a commit-form write: it carries writes of that commit) and carries it, where the importer holds one, else its own commit where the importer holds it valid and not dropped, else the snapshot carrying it; a snapshot copying a write of a delta the importer holds otherwise than that delta holds it (§5.2) is no origin. Native writes never use another commit's ID as a source, so imported dots collide with nothing and importing C twice yields the same dots. The header's **origins** table gives, per origin commit, its ID, author node, actor and `ts`. The import may be split into several import commits, one per origin commit, written in one local transaction. Each delta carries its origin delta's retention. Writes with an expiry, writes purged at the importer, and writes invalid at the importer are not imported. Of a sequence, an import carries only the writes on the elements the importer's state shows and on those their creation and position writes anchor at, transitively (spec/crdt.md §6), the latter without content (as a snapshot carries deleted elements, spec/crdt.md §3.3 rule 5).
- An import on a thing whose creation record does not name the import's author as its creator is bad input; a receiver judges this once it holds that record, whether or not it holds or syncs the import's deltas (spec/commits.md §4). An import is valid iff it is valid as the importer's content write on M (§5.2); its elements are the importer's writes on M (re-sourced dots) and are judged by the import's validity alone. For §8 and delete keys (spec/crypto.md §7) the author of an imported delta is its origin commit's author.
- A move is one import by one node; a client MUST NOT import t while it holds a thing with `/was = t` (two imports from different held sets duplicate elements).
- A receiver holding several imports on thing N that list one origin C (all by N's creator, which so alone decides what C's writes on N are: an invalid one strips them from every body, as its overlapping counters would void its own commits, spec/commits.md §4) keeps them all: each owns C's writes on its own aspects (§5.2).
- **Resolution** (read-time; stored values are never rewritten): a tid t held in a value of thing R resolves to the held thing M with `/was = t` in which R's owner chain holds rights (owns M, or holds an edge on M or on an actor that owns M), if exactly one; else to the tid t's `/moved` names, if the node holds t and it names one; else to t. Resolution repeats from the result, at most `RESOLUTION_HOPS` (8) times in all. A commit names things by the tid its writer resolved; an app pins a reference by writing it.
- Moving a thing between actors of one tree needs no copy: rights are edges (§1), only the owner is fixed.

## 8. Purge rights
A delta's retention names who may purge it (spec/commits.md §5), judged at the request's time: `admin` the thing's current admins; `author` also the actor directly above the author node in its chain while it holds write on the thing, acting through any of its nodes; `writer` also any current writer of the thing; `nobody` no one. A purge request drops a named delta iff its author held that right at the request's time. At blind nodes a delete key proves the right as of the delta and the label key current membership (spec/sync.md §5).

## 9. Links
- A link consists of K, an actor owned by the sharer with secrets of its own, made a member of the target with the link's role, and L, a key actor whose seed is in the link, with edge (role r, invite r) on K for the link's role r. The sharer's actor is K's only admin. The link encodes: L's seed, the target tid, K's tid, K's label ID and server addresses, and optionally the sharer node's dialable key and relays, so a redeemer sharing no server with the sharer dials the sharer's node, which serves K's label as a member peer (spec/wire.md §10). A link carries only that key and those relays: holding it gives nobody the sharer's `/reach` (the lan secret goes only to co-readers of labels its `/reach` goes under, §4), and a sharer does not place its `/reach` under K for the link's sake. The redeemer dials that key with an ephemeral transport key and no member proof (spec/sync.md §5, §10), opens K's label there as at a server, and MAY keep dialing it, from the link it followed, for K's labels and the target's, nothing else. Putting the key in a link is the sharer's explicit choice, and a node without servers can make only such links (one naming no server and no node reaches nobody); it serves K's label and the target's complete (spec/sync.md §5). When a link ends by revocation (of L, or with a joiner's), by expiry, or a multi-use link otherwise, the sharer SHOULD rotate its dialable key and lan secret, since the link text may be in strangers' hands; a single-use link finalized by its redemption does not (its one possible user is now a member and keeps the direct path).
- L is listed in every step of K's label by its static entry with an entry box (spec/sync.md §2), and holds boxes in K's `/secrets` like any key-actor member (sealed to L's KEM key, spec/crypto.md §3).
- **Redeem**: derive L's keys from the seed; claim K's label by L's static entry, take K's read secret from the head's entry box, download K's label and L's boxes; then, signing as L, invite-grant the redeemer's actor (or a fresh node) to K with role r and seal K's secrets to it. L writes in commit form and deposits the commit under K's label (spec/sync.md §5) unless r is write or above. A redeemer SHOULD discard the seed afterwards.
- `until` on L's edge expires the link; single use (the link's `flags` bit 0, spec/wire.md §10) = the redemption also removes L's own edge in the same commit (a self-removal). Both are finalized by a sharer device writing a plain removal of L (§5.1): a node that holds the link's seed thing and admin on K MUST write it once L's edge expired or was self-removed with no explicit write on it since, after a complete pass of K's labels (§3) that ended, for an expiry, after `until` (when K's labels have no server, at once and only by the node that wrote the seed thing; with servers it waits for that pass however long it takes). An invite grant as L that the removal's frontier does not cover is dropped (§5.1 step 4); one received before it stands.
- The sharer's link seed thing is placed in the sharer's home group, so every device of the sharer holds it.
- **Bearer use**: act as L without writing anything. Read only.
- A joiner that may still hold the seed is revoked only together with L (it could otherwise go on reading as L), both removals in one commit with one rotation of K (spec/commits.md §2); a new link is a new L.
- The sharer keeps the seed on a thing only its own actor reads (the seed thing: aspect `/link/seed`, a map {0: K's tid, 1: the seed}) and writes L's `/keys` and K's deposit seed.
- **Recovery card**: recovery key seed, person actor tid, home label, several server addresses (spec/wire.md §10). The recovery key owns the person actor, so every step of the home label lists it with an entry box; a device holding the card claims the home label's current step by the key's static entry (spec/sync.md §5), takes the read secret from the entry box, then its boxes, `/label` and `/created`.
