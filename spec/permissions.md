# Permissions
> Summary: The rules of actors, role grants and first admins, role secrets and rotation, certificates, validity (role timeline, content, an account's signing chain and epochs), purge rights and links.

## 1. Concepts
- **Terms**:
  - Rights are **roles** (sync < read < write < admin) given by **role grants** (**grants**; "edge" in rule text means a grant). An actor with a role on a thing is a **holder** of that role on it.
  - **Sync** gives the sync secret (§3) and nothing to read: its holder derives the thing's sync key (spec/sync-ids.md §2), so it keeps and serves the thing's sync IDs unread (spec/sync.md §3), and opens no section, roster or content of it (an inbox blob gives it the box and the sync IDs only, spec/wire.md §9).
  - A **role commit** (spec/commits.md §2) carries writes of **role-class aspects** (spec/data-model.md §9).
  - The **role timeline** (§5.1) orders edge writes.
  - **Member**, **membership** and **member of a group** mean only being listed in a group (`group.0`, spec/data-model.md §8), which grants no rights.
  - A value in a CRDT set is an **element**.
- **Actor**: a thing that can hold roles and act. Thing, group and actor are overlapping capabilities of one object, not kinds. A thing has this capability by having role secrets (spec/crypto.md §3).
  - A **key actor** (account, node, link key) also has a signing key committed by its tid and holds admin on itself. No edge write targets a key actor: it has no roster.
  - An **account** P is a key actor with **epochs**, its creation record marked so (spec/wire.md §3): its keys change by rotations of its **signing chain** (spec/crypto.md §3, §9; §5.4, §5.5). Only accounts and key actors other than nodes author commits; every commit a node makes outside its own node thing's aspects (`keys.0`, `node.0`, `reach.0`, §4) is authored by its account.
  - A **node** is a key actor whose seed never leaves it. It is a **node of P** while it holds read or above on P's devices actor D (a sync grant on D, which keeps P's inbox, makes no node). A node acts only as an account it is a node of: it signs as P (spec/crypto.md §3), and every role and authority this spec gives "a node" is its account's. A node that is a node of several accounts signs each commit as one of them.
  - **Devices actor** D of P: an actor with secrets of its own whose first admin is P, created with P. Its holders of read or above are P's node list; P is its only admin. Its `account.0` holds P's epoch seeds (spec/data-model.md §9), so D's read role opens P's epochs. D's boxes of read and above go to its nodes only, never to P: a box to P on D would open with the seeds D holds, and every holder of them is a node on D's roster. A node takes up P's seeds, and acts as P, only while it is a node of P. D's home sync ID is P's **home**: its inbox (spec/sync-inbox.md §1) and the sync ID of what only P's nodes share. Only P's nodes (and a recovery key, §7) read D, and nothing outside D is judged by D: a node's identity appears only in D's roster and sync IDs, in the inner signature (§5.5) and on connections (spec/sync-opens.md §2).
  - **Profile** of P: an ordinary thing whose first admin is P, created with P, read by P's contacts (contact card, spec/sync-inbox.md §5). The dial list on it is for dialing only (spec/sync-set.md §1).
  - Teams, bots and shared identities are ordinary actors with rosters; an account holding a role on one acts for it (§5.2, Acting).
  - A **link's actor** K (§7) is an actor its creation record marks as a link's.
- **First admin**: every thing but a key actor has a creation record naming its **first admin**, an actor, fixed by the tid (spec/wire.md §3). A key actor holds admin on itself.
  - **Record edge**: the record gives the first admin an admin edge on the thing from before every edge write (the walk's initial state, §5.1; not an edge write: it applies by the tid alone and audits nothing). It stores nothing; edge writes change or remove it like any edge, a removal resetting no entry of it (§2). The first admin is history: once its edge is gone it has nothing on the thing but what grants give it.
  - The **record chain** of a thing: its first admin, and so on up to a key actor, within `MAX_CHAIN_DEPTH` links. A delta written on a thing further down (judged from the creation records alone) is invalid, its `created.0` excepted; the rest of its commit is judged as usual.
  - With no admin left, a thing's edges, secrets and keys change no more (holders keep reading and writing as granted) and nothing recovers it.
  - A node MUST NOT remove or lower the last admin edge of a plain thing (§3); the write is refused locally. Limit: signed anyway it applies, the thing stays plain, its content stays readable through derivation by the first admin's holders, and nobody writes it.
  - A record needs no authority. A node names as first admin only its account or an actor its account holds write on. A thing may be created by a commit carrying its `created.0` alone (a role commit, spec/commits.md §2).
- **Role grant**: a record (holder actor, target, role), stored in the target's `roles.0`, on the target; what it reaches beyond follows from paths.
- **Effective role** of an actor on a thing at time t: the maximum over paths from the actor to the thing of the minimum role along the path, evaluated with the timeline (§5.1) at t. A path climbs role grants, record edges included while they stand; an actor holds admin on itself.
- **Admin** = write + change role grants + purge (§6) + write admin-only (`^`) aspects (spec/data-model.md §2; §5.2). An admin grant on a plain thing grants changing its role grants and purge on that thing.
- Admins are equal and may remove each other, the first admin included.
- An edge write whose holder is its target is not applied (§5.1).
- The role-class aspects of a thing (spec/data-model.md §9) are written only by its admins, whatever other role a node holds, except invite grants, self-removals and finalizes (§2), what rotations and re-seals write into `secrets.0` and `keys.0` (§3), and listings (§5.5).
- Ephemeral writes (spec/data-model.md §2) need the role the durable write would need.

## 2. Role grant writes
- **Edge writes**: granting, changing and removing a grant are writes to the target's `roles.0`, each in its own role commit (spec/commits.md §2), by a node with admin on the target (a finalize, below: with write).
  - Every edge write resets the holder's **current entry**: the entry created by the edge write in force on that grant in its writer's walk (§5.1: the last applied write on it, when it put an entry), whatever the `roles.0` state holds of it.
  - A removal of a holder with no current entry (a record edge, §1, or a grant already removed) resets an entry of its own delta: a record keyed by the holder at a fresh counter of the delta, holding the reset alone (spec/crdt.md §5.6). Its creation is that delta, so it is never waited for. A removal of an absent grant is an edge write too.
  - A grant or change also writes a new entry holding the whole grant (spec/crdt.md §5.6 `put_new`).
  - A `roles.0` delta writing inside an entry it does not create is bad input (judged from the delta and its header: the entry's dot is outside the delta's own dots).
  - A reset names the write it ends: its entry's creation. A role commit with a removal (a write leaving the holder no grant for keys: none, or `until` 0) resetting an entry whose creation the reader does not hold applies none of its edge writes until that creation is held; a commit's edge writes apply together or not at all. A reset beside a put of a live grant for its key decides nothing (the put sets the grant) and is not waited for. Limit: one whose creation the reader never gets never applies there. A reset carrying another key than its entry's creation removes no grant (spec/crdt.md §5.6).
- A grant may carry `until: timestamp`: the grant counts as removed from `wall_ms(t) >= until`.
- A grant **without history** carries `floor`: the n of the read rotation its commit makes (the cut, spec/crypto.md §4 Floors); its holder reads the current state (snapshots, spec/crypto.md §4 State at a cut) and is handed no commit written before it. A change of the grant keeps its `floor` unless its writer shows history (a grant with history, at its author's floor); a grant without history is written by an admin of the target, to a holder holding no role on it.
- **An `until` passing** is a loss (§3) the node judges by its own clock, with no role commit behind it. A node whose read access only an `until` ended (walked with every grant's time ahead, it has a role):
  - First hears out the sync IDs it tracks whose closure holds a thing it would forget. For each such sync ID, one of these holds:
    - with a source: each of its own commits with an entry under it is held elsewhere (spec/sync-status.md §2) and, a complete pass (spec/sync-status.md §1) begun since it found the role gone ended; or
    - a source refuses it: on a connection whose shared set settled without the sync ID, the peer proved an actor it judges a holder of the sync ID (spec/sync-opens.md §4), or the peer keeps the sync ID unread and refused it (stranded). Limit: a peer that proves nothing to it (one that judges it departed and knows it by nothing else) is no refusal, so with only such peers reachable it keeps what it lost until `LOST_HEARD_WAIT_MS`.
  - Meanwhile its writes from before the `until` go out (a peer that judges it departed, or that keeps the sync ID unread under a later sync key, shares nothing with it), and a renewal held by then keeps its role.
  - Then it drops.
  - It drops anyway `LOST_HEARD_WAIT_MS` after it first found the role gone.
  - A role commit received beside the ended grant that does not itself end the role changes nothing of this. A removal, demotion or a grant written with `until` 0 (below) ends it at once; a finalize (below) counts as the `until` it finalizes.
- **Finalize**: a removal of another holder's grant carrying no frontier that is not a self-removal (below). It applies (§5.1) iff the grant is past its `until` at the removal's `ts` (as the timeline orders it: a renewal ordered before it counts, and the finalize then ends a superseded write and applies nothing) and its author holds write on the target and a role there at least the grant's (a timed admin grant needs an admin); else it applies nothing, whoever wrote it.
  - It is no explicit write: it audits nothing, so the removed holder's own timestamps still decide its writes (§5.1, §5.2).
  - It is the grant's departure for keys (spec/crypto.md §4) and carries its rotation (§3) like any removal; one finalize may remove several ended grants of one target (spec/commits.md §2).
  - It says retain or erase as its `until` would (§3, Retain: the default in force at the expiry's key).
  - A node with write on actor T holding a grant on T it may finalize, past its `until` by its own clock, MUST write the finalize before its next commit encrypting under T other than by sealing a box to T (spec/crypto.md §4), and boxes that holder nothing meanwhile (a grant renewing it excepted).
- A removal says **retain or erase** (§3, Retain).
  - Its plain entry reset says erase.
  - Its entry reset with the grant written anew with `until` 0 (role read, no invite, no `tied`), gone at every clock, says retain, and is a removal all the same (§5.1), in one commit with its rotation. A client writes `until` 0 for nothing else.
  - It says what its remover wants, or else what the thing's default says: `retain` of the `retention^.0` setting in force (spec/commits.md §5), none erasing.
  - A self-removal says what its author wants, whatever the default.
  - A client removing a node from its account's D writes erase unless its user says otherwise, whatever the default.
  - A receiver reads what the removal says, never the default.
  - An invite grant gone when written does not apply (§5.1).
- A grant may carry `invite: role`, at most its role. A `roles.0` delta putting a value that breaks this or reads as no grant is bad by itself (spec/commits.md §4), and so applies nothing, its entry resets included.
- A node's **effective invite** on T is the maximum over its paths to T of the minimum of the roles along the path before the last grant and that grant's invite.
- An **invite grant** adds a grant for an actor with no current grant on T (an expired grant counts as none), with role and invite at most the author's effective invite, and carries the grantee's boxes for those roles. Over an expired grant still in the state at a higher role it is that grant's departure for keys, which carries its rotation (§3): a client writes it only once the grant is finalized.
  - Its `secrets.0` aspect is one delta holding only new keyed entries (puts, spec/crdt.md §5.6: each entry's dot is created by that delta): boxes keyed to its grantees at or below each one's granted role and history boxes at or below the highest granted role, with no reset of the value, a field, a map or any entry; its `keys.0` aspect, if any, likewise only the records of those boxes (`sealed`, spec/crypto.md §4 Stale) and no other field. This is judged from the commit alone.
  - The exemption holds only where the commit puts a grant on that thing and every such put applies as an invite grant (§5.1; another write of the commit applying, such as a self-removal, gives none).
  - A `secrets.0` or `keys.0` delta that breaks this gets no invite exemption and is judged like any such delta (the highest role it rotates, boxes or records, at least write, §3, §5.2).
  - The grant's write is judged on its own (§5.1).
  - Invite holders cannot change or remove any other edge.
- **Self-removal**: a node with admin on actor X may always remove X's grant on any target, or lower its role, invite or `until`. Such a write is a self-removal when either holds; otherwise it is an explicit demotion (§5.1):
  - it lowers the author's own grant. Lowers: removes it, or sets no component of role, invite and `until` above the grant's value and no `floor` below it. An unchanged rewrite and a removal of an absent grant lower; `tied` is ignored.
  - admin on X is the author's only authority for it: with X's grant on the target disregarded, the author has no edge-admin authority on the target at that point (authority through X does not count: an account leaving a group a team it holds admin on administers, or the admin of a group leaving a thing the group administers, leaves by self-removal; so does an admin of a thing's first admin removing the record edge).
- A self-removal needs no authority on the target and carries no rotation (§3); otherwise it is judged and audited like any edge write (§5.1).
- **Tied grants**: a grant whose holder is a group G (a thing with `group.0`) may carry `tied`: the `group.0` element of G listing the target T (as an item or subgroup) that the grant goes with, named by its creation stamp (spec/crdt.md §2.2) and its set.
  - The tie is no authority: edge writes are judged as without it (§5.1), a change of the grant other than a new one keeps it.
  - A tied grant is written by a node that may make the grant and write on G, in the same local operation as the membership and after it, naming a live element listing T there.
  - A node that holds a current tied grant (G, T) and holds its element dead in G's `group.0` (an element record without a live value, or none under an effective reset above its creation stamp, spec/crdt.md §4.2, §5.5) MUST remove the edge when it may: as an explicit demotion when it has admin on T, after a complete pass (spec/sync-status.md §1) of every sync ID whose bodies carry a thing of the demotion's pre-sync scope (§5.3; spec/sync-judgement.md §1: the thing in its closure and read by its actor) that ended after the last role commit of G or T it applied from elsewhere; else as a self-removal when it has admin on G.
  - An element it does not hold is dead only when it holds the element's own commit (§5.2) invalid for that delta; otherwise it is no reason to remove.
  - Only that direction: an untied grant stays when the membership goes, and a membership without a grant re-grants nothing.
- **Logging a lowered grant**:
  - A removal of another actor G's grant, and a self-removal lowering it, is logged by its author, as a certificate copy (§4) of every delta but its `secrets.0` and `keys.0` deltas, under every sync ID whose bodies carry G (spec/sync-judgement.md §1: G in its closure and read by its actor) that the author syncs.
  - A node that applied a write lowering G's grant on a thing in the closure of G's sync ID, which G's grant there has not been raised again since, logs it under that sync ID as a certificate copy of its `roles.0` deltas, with its author's chain, unless entries there hold them. Another node's write received less than `SETTLE_MS` ago is its author's to log (§4).
- A write that grants, changes or removes a grant whose holder is actor G MUST also be delivered to G as an inbox blob (spec/sync-inbox.md §2), so G's admins know what G holds roles on and erase what G lost.
- The roster apps read from `roles.0` is the role timeline's roster (§5.1), not the CRDT join of the stored deltas.

## 3. Secrets and rotation
- Each role secret of an actor is sealed to every holder of that role or higher, every epoch (spec/crypto.md §4).
- A **plain thing** (no holder for keys but its first admin, and no edge write on it) uses derived role secrets (spec/crypto.md §3). Before any grant on it, it gets random secrets of its own by a rotation from the derived epochs, boxed to its current holders for keys, and a `keys.0` (any other edge write on it lowers its first admin's edge, which §1 refuses).
  - A thing that is to be granted a role on anything gets secrets of its own first; so does a plain holder for keys of a thing getting secrets of its own (its first admin), where the node administers it: a plain thing has no keyset to be sealed at (spec/crypto.md §4, Holder actors).
  - An admin node that sees a current holder of a plain thing other than its first admin, or a plain thing holding a role with key access on an actor with secrets of its own (left so by its only keyset part turning invalid, spec/crypto.md §3), SHOULD give it secrets of its own the same way, boxing every current holder but one whose grant it may finalize (§2), deciding at the end of a complete pass (below) of the sync IDs of the actor its secrets derive from; it MAY first wait up to `SETTLE_MS` after receiving another node's newest edge write on the thing, leaving it to that write's author. Concurrent ones fork (spec/crypto.md §4).
- **Required rotation**: a departure (spec/crypto.md §4: removal, demotion, finalize) of holder M from actor A (a node's removal from its account's D is one, on D) requires rotating every role secret M could open through A:
  - on A, the roles M left;
  - on every thing with secrets of its own that A's roles open (the targets of A's grants, record edges included, and so on transitively), the roles A holds there among those the roles M left open through A (a role secret opens only boxes of that role, spec/crypto.md §4). Those rotations are required through A's keyset: A's rotation lists the keyset M could open, so each box to A at it is stale (spec/crypto.md §4, Holder actors), and so on up.
  - Content is not re-encrypted.
  - A node's removal from D (with D's rotation, below) is a role commit of P signed at P's current epoch, logged under D's sync IDs only. It requires rotating P's epoch, which is P's own role commit, made by the remover right after it and logged in the same sync: signed at the same epoch, it writes only the new seed into D's `account.0` (one entry, spec/data-model.md §9) and P's `keys.0` (spec/crypto.md §3), and its thing list for D names the removal (§5.5), so the removal counts wherever the rotation does. The seed is encrypted under D's read epoch the removal mints, so the removed node cannot open it and a node of P opens the new epoch only once it holds the removal. Any node of P holding a counting removal from D at an open epoch that no counting rotation on the open epoch's line (spec/crypto.md §9) names in its list for D, among those D's roster authorizes (minted, by inner signature, by a node D holds as current), makes that rotation. A removal whose rotation the bound refuses (§5.5) is not made. P's keysets are its epochs (spec/crypto.md §4, Holder actors), so every box to P at a closed epoch is stale and things P holds roles on rotate as for any holder actor's new keyset. A node of P decides the rotation a held removal requires at the end of a complete pass of D's sync IDs (below), so a removal and its rotation that arrive apart are both in.
- The write by another node that removes or demotes M on A and the rotation of A's roles it requires are one role commit (spec/commits.md §2). A node may hold its parts at different times (spec/commits.md §4).
  - The removal's rotation is the remover's one rotation of those roles of A in that act: the rotations it makes first (below; a finalize's on A included, its departure then covered by the removal's rotation) rotate none of them, so none boxes a party at a role it is leaving.
  - The removal applies at once, whatever the remover holds (no secret of A is needed for the rotation, spec/crypto.md §4, Boxes). It carries the rotation when its author may rotate those roles; otherwise the write goes alone, a finalize included: its departure stays uncovered, so A's rotation is due and no node knowing it encrypts under A until one is made (spec/crypto.md §4, Encrypting under); a node knowing it that may rotate a thing whose epoch boxes A at A's uncovered keyset rotates that thing first, leaving A out (Seal), and A is re-sealed to it once A has rotated.
  - A removal or demotion taking a party below write or below admin carries itself in the keysets of A's rotation in the same commit, and every later rotation of A carries it (spec/crypto.md §4, Carried demotions).
- A self-removal (§2) carries no rotation (a rotation by the leaver covers nothing of its own departure, spec/crypto.md §4): the departure stays uncovered until the next node that may rotate does so. Things above the actor leave it out meanwhile (spec/crypto.md §4, Seal), so a node that may rotate it SHOULD do so at the end of its next complete pass (below) of the actor's sync IDs or of a thing it holds a role on, not only before its next write.
- Every other rotation is required of whoever may make it, when it next encrypts under the secret (spec/crypto.md §4). Nobody is required to sync anything to find it.
- **Who rotates**: a node with effective role write or above on an actor may rotate its roles at or below that role (a writer rotates sync, read and write, an admin all four). A role through a keyless grant (spec/crypto.md §4, Key access) is no right to rotate: wherever a rule reads "may rotate", the role counts over grants with key access only. Validity counts every grant (§5.2).
  - A rotation seals the new secrets to every current holder of each rotated role, writes the history boxes and the rotated roles' keysets in `keys.0` (spec/crypto.md §3, §4). The other roles keep their epochs.
  - A rotation boxing a holder that holds no box at the read epoch its commit is wrapped under (spec/crypto.md §5; with none, at a current read epoch), as the rotator's applied state stands, is also delivered to that holder as an inbox blob (spec/sync-inbox.md §2; to a node of D, over a direct connection).
  - Its validity is the author's role at its key (§5.2), for its `keys.0` and `secrets.0` deltas alike: a rotation invalid there is invalid whole, its keysets included.
  - A `secrets.0` delta needs, per entry record it carries (a put, a write into an entry, a removal), the role its key names (each record names its key, spec/crdt.md §5.6, so this is judged from the delta alone), and at least write unless it puts history boxes only (a history add, spec/crypto.md §4). A key-erased record, a key that is no box or history key, and a write or reset of the whole value or of a field need admin.
  - A `keys.0` delta needs write when it touches only the `read` and `write` fields and box records (`sealed`, spec/crypto.md §4 Stale), each record (a put, a write into an entry, a removal) needing the role its key names as a box does, and admin when it touches any other field, writes or resets the whole value or the `sealed` field, or carries a key-erased record or a key that is no box key.
- A node that may rotate a required rotation MUST do so before it next encrypts under a secret it would replace; it MAY rotate eagerly.
- Sealing to a holder actor M's keyset is encrypting under M's epoch (spec/crypto.md §4): a node about to rotate, grant or re-seal A first makes the rotations of its holder actors it is required to make, so A's boxes name their new keysets.
- **Holder actors** (spec/crypto.md §4): a rotation of A (a removal's included), a grant on A or a re-seal boxes a holder actor M at the keysets Seal gives, per role (jointly while M's anchors fork), or leaves it out of that role and the roles above until one exists (then re-sealed, below); none waits for a rotation of M. A departure from M or below it is M's to rotate for: it reaches A's boxes only as M's new keyset, which makes them stale, and a demotion of a writer or admin there travels with that keyset (spec/crypto.md §4, Carried demotions).
  - A grant that would leave a holder actor with key access out of every role is refused.
- **Role cycles**: actors each holding a role on the next, back to the first, form a cycle. No key cycle forms: the grant that would close one is keyless (spec/crypto.md §4, Key access), so its holder reads nothing of the target through it.
  - A node that lacks the earlier grant takes the later one as giving key access and boxes into a cycle. Its box is stale (tainted) at every node that holds both grants, which rotates; until the writer holds the grants and the commits of the cycle, a party removed from one actor on it may read the other's new content (spec/requirements.md, Permissions).
- **Re-seal**: a node that may rotate a role may write a current holder's boxes for that role's current epochs, to the holder's newest keyset it holds (a holder actor: the keyset Seal gives, spec/crypto.md §4) and with those epochs' history boxes, changing nothing else (spec/crypto.md §4).
  - It is delivered as an inbox blob (spec/sync-inbox.md §2; to a node of D, over a direct connection, spec/sync-inbox.md §1) and logged under the actor's sync IDs like any role commit.
  - A node that may rotate r SHOULD re-seal a current holder of r that it sees without a box at a current epoch of r, or that a stranded notice names (§4), before it next writes under the actor.
  - **Complete pass**: it decides at the end of a complete pass (spec/sync-status.md §1) of the actor's sync IDs. A Pull sent before a role commit of the actor applied ends no pass after it. Once it applies a role commit of the actor from elsewhere, a node begins a pass of each of the actor's sync IDs (a Pull from its stream's cursor, spec/sync-streams.md §4, at each source whose last pass of it ended before the commit, once any Pull under way there ends).
  - It re-seals no holder past its key access (none without it, none above the role it has it at), no holder actor Seal leaves out (above) or a holder whose grant it may finalize (§2), and only at established epochs (spec/crypto.md §4).
  - It re-seals for a role only when holding the secret of such a current epoch the holder lacks a box at. It boxes the ones it holds and replaces no box the holder has; another holder boxes the rest, and where none that may rotate r holds one, the epoch is orphaned and rotated (spec/crypto.md §4). A re-seal a stranded notice asks for writes the holder's boxes at every current epoch held.
  - A grant or re-seal boxes the holder at the current epochs held whether or not an epoch is blocked at the node (spec/crypto.md §4, Encrypting under: a box carrying a secret is encrypted under its recipient's key), after the rotations the node may make.
- **History add** (spec/crypto.md §4, History box): a node holding the secret of a current epoch of r, established and not blocked at it, that holds an earlier superseded established secret of r no live history entry of that epoch lists, writes an entry carrying what it holds, whatever its role on the actor, deciding at the end of a complete pass; logged under the actor's sync IDs like any role commit (a non-writer by deposit, spec/sync-inbox.md §3).
- **Sync ID role keys** (spec/sync-ids.md §2) derive from the newest epoch of sync, write and admin; a step's claim set from the newest read epoch; only an admin issues a step (any reader registers it, spec/sync-ids.md §6). An admin issues the next step when it next syncs that sync ID with a peer that keeps it unread, after logging the rotation commits it registers (spec/sync-ids.md §6), when either holds:
  - it holds a sync, write or admin epoch newer than the one that peer's head registers;
  - its actor's deposit seed derives a deposit key other than the head's, and it has opened every entry it can open under the sync ID at the sources it reaches and holds no unapplied `keys.0` write of the actor signed by a current admin of it.
- A node that keeps S unread opens S only toward holders of its head's sync key, claim keys or static entries (spec/sync-opens.md §4). A holder that missed rotations claims its way through the steps it missed, then pulls them and opens each under the epoch before it (spec/crypto.md §5), or is re-sealed (a claim set dropped on the way, or a rotation compacted, strands it until then).
- A node that removes a holder MUST, in the same sync and before anything else it logs, log the removal and the affected actors' new `keys.0` under every sync ID it tracks that the removed party could read (as certificates, §4); a node's removal from D goes under D's sync IDs only, and the rotation of P following it with its lists is logged as §5.5 says.
- **Lost access**: a node that learns it lost read access to an actor MUST drop those role secrets and every local unit it can no longer read by another path, except what it retains (Retain, below). A node holding sync on the actor still keeps its sync secrets and its store of the actor's sync IDs: they become kept unread (spec/sync.md §3).
  - Reading-only secrets of the actor (or of one a role on which gives one on it) whose source commit is unjudged or pending (spec/crypto.md §4) are no loss of access while the node holds no other secret of it.
  - A held pending role commit signed by its author that boxes the node a read epoch newer than every one it held keeps its boxes through the drop (it may still apply and then gives the node the secrets with the role). The node reads nothing from them before it applies. Boxes only at epochs it held are dropped.
  - A held redemption (a commit removing its signer's own grant on a thing beside a grant there) granting the node, that stands void only because an applied redemption by the same signer removed that grant first, with no explicit write on that grant since (an unfinalized single-use link race, §7), is no loss yet: the node keeps what that grant would give and keeps syncing its sync IDs until such a write arrives.
  - A node removed from its account's D (a counting removal of it held) is no node of P: it drops P's seeds and loses everything it read as P (an account's retain default does not reach its nodes, §2).
  - A node that lost read access through a self-removal (§2) it made, or one by itself, its account or a key actor it holds, whichever node of the account made it, drops once that commit is held elsewhere (spec/sync-status.md §2), so it can log it (above).
  - One that lost it by an `until` passing alone (a finalize counting as its `until`) drops as §2 says.
  - **No secret taken back**: a node takes a role secret of an actor from an applied `secrets.0` box (wherever and whenever it opens, a holder actor's box among them) only when its roles on the actor now give that role's secrets (read or above: every role's; sync alone: the sync secret) or an unfinalized link race (above) keeps what reaches the actor; a box held before its roles give them (a rotation applied before the grant it follows) gives them once they do. From an entry box (spec/sync-ids.md §3) likewise, unless no ended edge on the actor of the node, a key actor it holds or an actor it reads is held (a link's redeemer opens the sync ID before it holds the grant to its key); the actor is judged as above once role commits on it apply. Reading-only secrets (spec/crypto.md §4) are as above.
  - Of a copy (spec/sync-judgement.md §1) opened under a sync ID, a node takes no content part (spec/data-model.md §9: not role-class, `addenda.0`, `created.0` or `retention^.0`) on a thing under an actor with secrets of its own that the sync ID's group actor may not read now, and keeps none of the copy's bytes; it fetches the copy again once it holds secrets of that actor.
- **Retain**: a node that lost read access to actor A retains a read-only copy of A's things when one of its ways to A retains.
  - A **way** is a holder H of A, in one of these forms (a record edge is a grant here):
    - a grant of H on A at read or above that ended saying retain (its role the last value's before a removal), the node holding read or above on H just before the write that last changed it;
    - a grant of H on A at read or above alive.
  - An ended grant says what the last write on it on the timeline (§5.1, the writes that applied) says:
    - a removal, for itself (§2);
    - a value with another `until`, passed: the thing's default in force at its key (first `ts` at `until`, the zero tid and commit ID, or the write's key if later).
  - It retains only while the node has H: it is H, holds read or above on H now, retains H itself, or holds H's secrets above sync not dropped in the same judging (a loss of H not yet carried out; the copy is judged again when it is) (least fixpoint, so holders cannot retain each other).
  - Everything else erases: a removal saying so, no default, a write that did not apply. A default set later changes no removal or ending before it.
  - **The copy** is what the node held when it dropped: state, bodies and their timers.
    - Its secrets and `secrets.0` go as above, and so does what it held of the things' sync IDs: it syncs none of them, logs and serves nothing of it, and cannot write it.
    - Its pending parts on the things' content go. A later part on it is not joined, so a purge request arriving later reaches the copy only in a commit the node takes for another part, and need not.
    - Certificate material stays and updates as on any forgotten thing (§4). Validity still follows what the node holds (§5.2).
    - Expiry and retention (spec/commits.md §5) run as on any held body. No body of a copy is must-hold, or known to anyone (spec/sync-judgement.md §2).
    - An actor the node acts as for a client (spec/sync-transport.md §6) reads the copy while it is a holder the copy hangs on, or holds a role now both on its first admin and on such a holder, a holder that is itself a copy counting by its own holders. It writes nothing there.
  - The node records per copy the holders it retains through, durably.
    - A holder leaves that record when the timeline shows its way no longer retains (as above) or the node no longer has it. A copy with none is erased as lost.
    - A default arriving late may end a copy whose way is an `until` ending, once the node next judges that way and it then says erase; it brings none back.
    - A role on A with a secret of it again ends the copy: the node is a holder with old data, nothing more.
    - The node's user may erase a copy at any time, with what is kept only through it.

## 4. Verifying authors: certificates
- A write's authority is checked through the chain from its author up: each link is an edge write or a record edge (the tid commits to the first admin), ending at a key actor whose tid commits to its public key; an account's commit also through its signing chain to the epoch it names (§5.4, §5.5). No secrets are needed.
- **Chain certificates** of an actor for a thing are built on the things on any path from the actor to the thing over record edges and every edge any held edge write sets, at any time, applied or not. They are:
  - for each such thing, its creation record (and those up its record chain), raw in a record blob (spec/wire.md §3), never a copy of the commit that minted it: a record outlives that commit's retention;
  - for each such actor, its **current `keys.0` commits**: its newest `keys.0` commit writing more than box records, the rotation commits of its newest keyset of each role, forks included, and the commits holding box records at those keysets' epochs (spec/crypto.md §4, Stale), each projected to `keys.0` (the newest may write no keyset, and a rotation of what the actor holds names its keyset, spec/crypto.md §4), with the `roles.0` deltas of the commits those keysets carry and their authors' chain certificates (spec/crypto.md §4, Carried demotions);
  - for each such account, the rotations of its signing chain the node holds, every line, as header-only copies (§5.5, Chains travel), and their thing lists for things under the sync ID (§5.5);
  - every held edge write on a grant between two of those things (projected to its grant deltas);
  - closed under authors: for each such write, its author's chain certificates for its target and for its holder.
- **Nothing of a node goes under another sync ID than its account's D's**: a node's `keys.0`, `node.0` and `reach.0` commits go under D's home sync ID only, as D's holder card, each a commit of its own touching nothing else, never logged again unchanged. No group sync ID holds a node's certificate, card or tid.
- **Logging certificates**: when a node first logs anything under a sync ID, and whenever they change, it logs its account's chain certificates (for the sync ID's group and for each actor it has acted for) and the cards (record, `keys.0`) of the key actors other than accounts holding the sync ID's group (spec/sync-judgement.md §1; a non-writer of the sync ID by deposit), and, for each destroyed thing (spec/commits.md §5) a live thing of the sync ID's closure needs (a destroyed actor on an authority path into it, named first admin or holder on a thing of the closure that is not destroyed or on such a destroyed actor; or a destroyed thing a live thing of the closure lists in `retainers^.0` or references in a live durable value other than `group.0`), the records up its record chain and every held edge write on it with that write's certificates (as copies where the sync ID's closure does not hold the destroyed actor; its own bodies go with its destruction), and, for every thing whose record any of these carries, its witness as a header-only copy and, destroyed, the requests destroying it (spec/commits.md §5, A thing's mintings travel with its record), except the deltas and records an entry under the sync ID already holds:
  - the records missing go as one record blob of the logging node's; a record blob logged by any node counts once opened, while it is held in the node's own store and at every source of the sync ID;
  - a certificate copy or closure blob logged by any node counts once the node has opened it as the same commit and checked each of its deltas against the header's delta hash (spec/commits.md §4), while it is held in the node's own store and at every source of the sync ID;
  - a blob of a rotation commit of the sync ID's actor (wrapped under the epoch it supersedes, spec/crypto.md §5) counts only if it opens under the newest read epoch.
- Deltas a sync ID's bodies carry (on things of its closure its actor may read, spec/sync-judgement.md §1) go as closure blobs, never as copies; every other delta of a chain goes as a copy.
- A demotion or `keys.0` write by another node is its author's to log (§3); a node MAY wait up to `SETTLE_MS` after receiving one before copying it.
- A promoter completes the chain it holds: a writer promotes a deposited commit only once its author's chain for it is under the sync ID, logging the missing parts itself (spec/sync-inbox.md §4).
- **Stranded**: a node is stranded on a sync ID when it cannot read the sync ID's current epoch by any key it holds: it reached the head and after a complete pass there cannot open the epoch it registers, or every source refused every open it had to try (spec/sync-ids.md §6: its sync keys, its claims at each step it reached, its static entries), wherever its walk stopped, a claim tried or not. A stranded node MUST send a Stranded message (spec/wire.md §11.2) naming the sync ID and the holder to each connected peer that proved an account holding write or above on the sync ID's actor, as the node judges it. With none connected, it deposits a stranded notice (spec/wire.md §9) under the inbox of each admin of the sync ID's actor it holds. It sends each, per peer and per admin, once per sync key of the newest step it reached (the head, or where its walk stopped). Either names a current holder to re-seal (§3), which the receiver checks itself (nothing of the sender: a re-seal of a current holder at the current epochs is due whoever asks), at the current epochs whatever step the node stopped at.
  - Each carries the **holder keys** of the holder the node reads the actor through as it judges it (its account, or an actor holding the actor that its account holds a role on): that holder's current `keys.0` commits, each projected to its `keys.0` delta, with their authors' chain certificates (spec/wire.md §9). A receiver that may re-seal the actor takes them, when they name a current holder of it, as chain certificates before it decides, and takes nothing of them off the holder's **authority chain** (no keyset but the holder's and the cards of those on the chain who wrote it, no record or edge write of anything else): the holder, then each one's first admin and current holders, transitively, as the receiver judges it, judged again as what it takes extends it, nor any such commit that it does not judge valid with every edge write it makes applied (spec/wire.md §9). A node gone from the holder learns the keyset and deposit seed it re-seals the holder with only so (spec/crypto.md §4, Reported). A notice whose keys do not fit the blob goes without them.
- **Suspect**: a node flags an aspect suspect when:
  - a body of it dropped as covered counts as never received (§5.2);
  - a part of it pending at its expiry goes (spec/commits.md §5).
  - A flag names the bodies it is about. An entry goes as its body is held again (or with its thing).
  - An entry not about a part that went pending at its expiry is dormant while a held compaction snapshot of the aspect valid at the node is stamped after the body's commit, judged from what the node holds when it reports (a snapshot turned invalid gives nothing, §5.2, so what it had settled is suspect again). An entry about such a part is never dormant.
- **Suspect list**: a node sends the flagged, not dormant aspects of things in L's closure to each connected peer it shares L with (spec/sync-opens.md §4), as Suspect messages (spec/wire.md §11.2), the most recently flagged first, on sharing L and whenever its flags change. Each entry is `{ thing, ak, digest }`.
  - `digest` is the state digest (spec/crypto.md §6) under the thing's dedup secret.
  - The state bytes are the canonical CBOR array `[snapshot delta bytes, [page*]]` of the aspect's state encoded as a snapshot delta (spec/crdt.md §8) with an all-zero nonce, no frontiers or `Absorbed`, its pages in the order of their hashes, or no bytes for an aspect holding no state.
  - An aspect with no known creation record is left out until it has one.
  - The repair a list asks for is spec/sync-compaction.md §1.
- **Logging revokes**: any node that syncs a sync ID and holds a revoke about the author of an entry under that sync ID that it judges invalid, or cannot judge yet, logs it there (a non-writer by deposit), with the chain certificates of that commit's own author, of the `keys.0` commits only those of actors that read the sync ID's actor. A revoke changes no verdict on the writes its frontier covers, so it is not logged for those alone.
- The grantee's current `keys.0` commits (an account's: with its rotations, §5.5) go under the target's sync ID as a certificate, so every admin can seal and list the grantee.
- A commit's **author's chain** is its author's chain certificates for each thing it has a delta on and for its actor (§5.2, Acting). A commit whose author's chain to the things it writes has not arrived is pending (spec/commits.md §4); one whose chain to its actor has not is invalid until it arrives. An account's commit at an epoch whose minting rotation the node does not hold is pending (§5.5).
- A node that does not sync a thing applies the certificate deltas it holds; its timeline for that thing runs over the entries it was given.

## 5. Validity
Validity is a deterministic function of the commits a node holds and its trust set (§5.1, Trust set) and side (§5.4). An account's commit first passes the epoch rule (§5.5); one that does not count there is judged by nothing below.

### 5.1 Layer 1: role timeline
The timeline covers **edge writes**: writes to `roles.0`. Every other role-class write (`secrets.0`, `keys.0`, `sync.0`) is judged like content (§5.2) by the role its aspect requires (§1, §3).
1. Order all held edge writes, over every thing, by (`ts`, author, commit ID): a write's **key** is its position in this order. Start before every write, with each thing's record edge in force (§1).
2. Walk the writes, skipping the drop set D (initially empty). A write applies iff its author has the required authority at that point:
   - admin on the thing;
   - for an invite grant, the effective invite it needs;
   - for a self-removal (§2), admin on the grant's holder;
   - for a finalize (§2), its own rule there;
   - a write lowering another holder's edge by an author that also has edge-admin authority on the thing is an explicit write of that class (step 3).
   - An **explicit write** is an applied edge write attributed to edge-admin authority (step 3), grants and raises included; self-removals, finalizes and invite grants are not.
   - Authority at a point is evaluated over every thing's state at that point, so the walk is one joint computation.
3. Attribute each applied write by X on N to X's **authority class** on N at that point: edge admin, or invite (with its level); admin before invite. A finalize is attributed to no class. A self-removal (§2) through admin on actor M ≠ X counts as a write by X on M, attributed to X's class on M; one lowering X's own edge is attributed to X's class on its target (none, for an author with no class there).
4. An applied write R at time T **demotes** X on N when X's effective class on N (the maximum over all its paths) falls: from edge admin to below, or to a lower invite level.
   - R then adds to D every earlier applied edge write by X on N, and, when N is an actor, on every thing N reaches by edges standing at that point (grants and record edges, transitively) where X's class also falls, attributed to a class X fell through (above its class after R, up to its class before R) and not covered by R's **frontier**, and the walk restarts once R's commit has taken effect (every applied write of it audits first).
   - An explicit write **audits** when the edge as restored (below, a demoter's kept standing included) exceeds its new value: the new value is absent or expired when written, or has a lower role or invite (an `until` alive at R takes nothing there: its passing audits nothing, below). It carries its author's frontier (below), whichever of grant, change or removal it is; so does every explicit removal (one without is a finalize, §2), and no other write.
   - Losing a path while another keeps the class is no fall: removing X's admin edge on N while X keeps another admin path audits nothing, and the removal that takes X's last admin path audits its edge-admin writes.
   - **Edge as restored**: an explicit write on grant (M, T) judges X's class before it with the edge as restored: the join (componentwise maximum, alive) of the grant's values since the last explicit write on it, values taken away by `until`, self-removal or finalize included, plus, for X, the standing it kept as the author of an earlier explicit write on that grant (below).
   - R never demotes its own author: an author that is or reaches the holder (by edges alive just before R) keeps the standing the edge as restored for it gave it, even when R writes it unchanged, until the next explicit write on that edge, which audits it against the edge as restored.
   - Each edge write of a commit is judged on its own, against the state before the commit. The commit's applied edge writes then take effect one at a time in order (the header's aspect order, then by holder tid, bytewise); each write's demotions and whether its author reaches the holder just before it are judged with the earlier ones in effect.
5. A walk that adds nothing to D has settled; the timeline is the settled walk. D is rebuilt from scratch whenever the held set or the trust set changes.

- **Frontier**: R embeds what its author A had received of X's writes as of T on the aspects R puts in question in the settled walk with R and that frontier, as commit IDs, never counters. What an account received is what the node writing R received.
  - **Aspects in question**:
    - for any demotion, role-class and admin-only aspects (spec/data-model.md §2), also of the targets of X's self-removals through things where X's class falls;
    - for a demotion below write, every aspect of the things the demoted authority gave write on.
  - **Listed**, each only as A had received it as of T:
    - X's commits with a delta on those aspects;
    - for a demotion below write, also X's held commits acting for one of those things (§5.2, Acting);
    - the role commits among A's chain certificates for R's target (§4).
  - A commit is listed whether A still holds its body, dropped it or compacted it.
  - **Purge requests**: for any demotion A also lists every commit whose purge request's right may rest on X's standing there (§6), received as of T. Such a commit is both:
    - signed, as author or as the header's actor, by X or by a holder X's possible admins named with write or above on X;
    - naming a delta on a thing whose role-class aspects R puts in question for X or on which such a thing may hold write.
  - Where a frontier would pass the list bound a node resolves (`MAX_LIST_BYTES`), A leaves out, as few as fit: first the plain-content commits (§5.2, Addenda) it received last, which its own addenda list instead; then the purge-request commits it received last, each losing its right. A frontier over the bound without them is refused. Limit: a removal whose role-class, admin-only and chain-certificate commits alone pass the bound cannot be written.
  - Ephemeral commits are listed as durable ones (spec/commits.md §2); one A restarted since receiving, or forgot (spec/sync-compaction.md §2), is not received.
  - A write is covered iff its commit's ID is listed.
  - A commit forgotten at A (spec/commits.md §5 Forgetting) is not received: it is not listed, and the covers it dropped under stand for it: X's own cover by being listed, another writer's as that writer's valid snapshot (§5.2, Copies). A removal thus lists what came after the removed writer's newest cover and names that cover.
  - **Received**: a commit counts as received once A decrypted its header and verified its signature (the header pins its deltas by hash), whether or not A holds, applies or stores its deltas; relaying is not receiving.
  - A frontier MUST NOT list a commit A had not received as of T.
  - Besides R itself, nothing but the frontier decides what X's writes before T survive: a write on A's chain that the frontier does not list is audited like any other.
  - A frontier above `CHUNK_THRESHOLD` is a chunked large value referenced by link (spec/commits.md §1).
- A demotion by `until`, self-removal or finalize audits nothing. The next explicit write on that grant, by any node, audits with its frontier against the edge as restored.
- **Trust set**: a node's walk takes one more input, its trust set: held edge writes it sides with, each carrying a frontier and lowering or setting the edge of a holder other than its author (and other than its target). Nodes holding the same commits under the same trust set read alike. Which writes a node trusts is its own, kept across restarts, carried by nothing on the wire; how it chooses (defaults, a user's choice) is local behaviour.
  - **Seeds** of a trusted write R by A on edge (M, T): every held edge write ordered before R that R's frontier does not list, by M when M is a key actor or by any author that reaches M by admin edges at any time (edges any held write sets to admin, record edges included; A excepted), on T or on anything T reaches by edges at any time (as its target or its holder), except a write lowering its author's own edge: below the value the last held write on that edge ordered before it set (else the edge's record value) in every component (role, invite, `until`) and not equal to it.
  - D starts at the seeds of every trusted write. A trusted write the settled walk does not apply is **void**: every void write loses its seeds at once and the walk is redone from scratch, until none is void.
  - A **dispute** is a write R that may be trusted, which the settled walk does not apply and no seed dropped, and which the walk with D starting at the trusted writes' seeds plus R's own applies as an explicit write with a value below the edge's value just before it (the walk's last change to that edge, else its record value) in every component and not equal to it. Disputes are keyed by (R, M) and reported; the walk acts on the trust set alone.

### 5.2 Layer 2: content
- A delta is judged by its author's authority, whatever its header actor (Acting, below, adds a condition). A delta by X on thing N is valid if X held write or above on N just before the commit's key (§5.1; grants' `until` judged at its `ts`) and no valid explicit demotion of X below write on N is ordered after it.
  - An **explicit demotion of X below write on N** is an applied explicit write (§5.1 step 2) by another author that demotes X on N (§5.1 step 4) and takes X's role on N, with the edge as restored, from write or above to below write.
- Otherwise it is valid iff the frontier of a valid explicit demotion of X below write on N lists its commit, whatever their order, or a counting addendum vouches for it (Addenda, below).
- **Addenda**: a write of `addenda.0` on N (spec/data-model.md §9): an entry its delta creates (spec/crdt.md §5.6 `put_new`; a write into an entry it did not create says nothing), keyed by the commit ID of a removal R, holding a `frontier` listing commit IDs. An addendum A by writer W **vouches for** a delta of X on N (or the Acting check of X's commit for N, below) when all of:
  - R is an applied explicit write that demotes X below write on N (§5.1 step 4), ordered after the delta's commit and before A, and A's entry lists the delta's commit;
  - W held write on N just before A's key;
  - the delta is plain content: its commit is a content commit (not a compaction snapshot) carrying no purge request, and its aspect is neither role-class nor admin-only;
  - A is valid: it is judged as W's plain delta on N by the rules above and the epoch rule (§5.5), itself vouched for by other addenda or not, against a removal or a rotation. Validity with addenda is the least fixpoint over every author at once: the addenda valid without any addendum count first, then those they vouch for, until nothing changes.
  - A's list counts while its body is held; without the body (dropped, purged) A is still W's write at its key, listing nothing, so a later addendum may vouch for it and its body is fetched again once it is valid. An addendum written by a commit acting for another actor, or by a role commit, is no addendum. Addenda are kept as role commits are (fixed retention, spec/commits.md §5) and never compacted.
  - Every removal's frontier counts for every commit it lists, and every addendum for the commits ordered before its removal, however many removals and re-grants of X follow.
  - Limit: nothing checks when W received what it lists, so a writer that lists commits it had not received by R's `ts` makes them count.
  - **Writing**: a writer node that applies an explicit demotion R, for every (X, N) R takes below write where it holds write, writes one addendum on N listing X's plain-content commits with a delta on N or acting for N that it had received by R's `ts` (§5.1, Received; as of its wall clock) and R's frontier does not list; one addendum per thing, so none goes under a sync ID of another thing. R's author does the same for what its frontier left out (§5.1). A node that cannot write on N yet (its delete keys not here) writes it once it can.
- A delta of an **admin-only** aspect (AK marker `^`, spec/data-model.md §2) is judged by the two rules above with admin in place of write: X held admin on N, demotions below admin. A writer that does not hold admin on N MUST NOT write one; every node judges it alike from the AK alone, also on an ephemeral commit's part and a compaction snapshot's delta.
- A `retention^.0` delta (spec/commits.md §5 The setting) is judged by those rules as an admin-only delta; no other delta's validity depends on it.
- **Compaction snapshot**: a snapshot S's delta of an aspect is judged as its author's delta of that aspect by the rules above, at S's key, needing the strictest role a write it could carry needs (admin for `sync.0`, `keys.0`, `secrets.0` and admin-only aspects, else write). `created.0` is always valid.
  - An invalid S gives nothing: its body joins nothing, covers nothing (spec/commits.md §5).
  - A valid S vouches for every write it carries: no carried write is judged as its claimed author's.
- **A write's own commit** is the one holding it on that aspect: for a session-form write, the commit whose range holds its dot; for a commit-form one (source `Commit(C)`), C.
- **Copies**: a valid snapshot speaks for current state alone. Its writes join like any write (spec/crdt.md §4.1), wherever held, also where the receiver holds the write's own delta: a copy differing from the delta's is never compared with it; the copies join by the copy order. A node attributes a write's author and time only to a commit it holds and verified.
- A delta's writes count while it is valid. An invalid delta's writes are un-merged (spec/crdt.md §4.6), and the held valid snapshots' copies of them stay, as the snapshots' writes with the payload they carry (a copy of a deleted text element's creation without its char never gives the char). Every read is a function of the valid writes held: an invalid delta influences nothing, a sequence's order included (an element it created does not exist for ordering, spec/crdt.md §6.1), so holding its body or not changes no read.
- A record carrying no `ts` (a tombstone) carries no write.
- **A snapshot that turns invalid, or whose body drops**, gives nothing and vouches no more: the state is the join of the bodies held (spec/crdt.md §4.6).
  - Each body of that aspect dropped as covered (compaction or purge on replace, spec/commits.md §5, spec/sync-compaction.md §1) whose covers (the held valid snapshots that covered it when it dropped) none carries any more counts as never received from then on (spec/commits.md §5 Never received) and joins as a late part (spec/commits.md §4) when it arrives again. A coverer carries it if it is held with its body and valid, or dropped as covered under covers one of which carries it; a forgotten one (spec/commits.md §5 Forgetting) as the covers it dropped under do.
  - Limit: a cover is its author's edit. A demotion of its author whose frontier does not list it makes it invalid (addenda vouch for no snapshot, Addenda above), and the live writes only it carried are lost wherever no node still holds their bodies; a re-grant brings them back only from a node holding them (§6 Invalid deltas).
- An actor holding the required role in the timeline's final state, every grant with an `until` counted as removed, is re-granted: each of its writes ordered at or after the first point it ever held that role is valid again, including those in the gap and those a demotion's frontier left out. Edge writes in the gap stay dropped.
- **Acting**: a commit whose header actor (spec/wire.md §4) is not its author is invalid, every delta and ephemeral part of it but `created.0` (whose record holds by its tid), unless a delta of its author on that actor needing write, at the commit's key, would be valid by the rules above (its demotions, frontiers, re-grant and addenda alike: an addendum on the actor listing the commit vouches for this check).
  - Role commits act for their author, so the role timeline never depends on this.
- One dot names one write: two commits of one session with overlapping ranges on one aspect are both invalid (spec/commits.md §4).
- A delta that becomes invalid is un-merged (spec/crdt.md §4.6). A signal's deltas join no state, so a validity change on one changes only which of them count (spec/commits.md §5 Signals).
- **Ephemeral parts**: a part of an ephemeral commit (spec/commits.md §2) is judged as the durable part of its kind would be, at the commit's key, needing the aspect's role (§1):
  - a session-form one as its author's content delta;
  - a snapshot one as its author's compaction snapshot delta.
  - A part on a core aspect (spec/data-model.md §9) is invalid.
  - Frontiers, addenda, rotation lists and listings name ephemeral commits as durable ones. What a valid ephemeral snapshot carries it vouches for, as any snapshot does; one turned invalid gives nothing, and a body dropped as covered by it counts as never received, as for a durable snapshot. What becomes invalid is un-merged (spec/crdt.md §4.6).

### 5.3 Revoking (client behavior)
- A role-lowering edge write is stamped at once with what its author had received. Writes of the demoted holder it leaves out survive where another writer had received them by its `ts` (§5.2, Addenda); those no writer had received are lost: every writer drops them as invalid (§6 Invalid deltas), so a re-grant brings back only what a node still holds, such as a peer that kept them unread while valid. Writes of it that a valid snapshot by another writer carries stay wherever that snapshot is held (§5.2, Copies), until an admin's reset or revert supersedes them.
- A client that wants to keep more MAY sync the sync IDs of the things the demoted authority gave write on first, for as long as it chooses, and stamp regardless after.

### 5.4 Signing chain
Which of an account's rotations count and which epochs are open (chain per spec/crypto.md §9), as a deterministic function of the held rotations, the node's side and the order it received them in. A rotation is held here only with its chain back to the first epoch (through the epoch it rotates; a merge accepts only named forks held so); before that it is pending (§5.5) and rejects nothing, is no party, head or side.
- **Rejects**: rotation m, signed at epoch e (the one it rotates), rejects held rotation r iff m supersedes r's epoch and no held rotation signed at e, m included, is on a line with r (on r's line, or r on its line). A line is contested only from below: a rotation signed at e rejects nothing on a line through e, its own line (the forks it accepts included) among them.
- **Counting**: with a side s (a held rotation), every rotation rejecting one on s's line is void. Then, until nothing changes, an undecided rotation is decided once its parent (the rotation minting the epoch it is signed at; the first epoch counts) and every held rotation rejecting it are: void if the parent is void, not held, or a counting rotation rejects it; counting if the parent counts and every rotation rejecting it is void. A rotation still undecided counts for nothing.
- An epoch **counts** iff it is the first epoch or its minting rotation counts. A counting rotation **closes** every epoch that does not descend from it: those are the epoch's **closers**.
- **Heads**: the counting epochs no counting rotation descends from (the first epoch when no rotation counts). More than one is a **fork**. Forks stand: no rule waits for one to end, and none is owed a rotation for being one.
- **Preferred**: of two held rotations' lines, the one holding the earliest-received rotation of those on exactly one of them (by the node's receipt order, ties by commit ID). Of a set of candidates, the one preferred to every other.
- **Open**: every head is **open**; every other held epoch is closed. A head's closers (the other heads, in a fork) bound only what a list may name of it once it closes (§5.5, Cutoff). The **preferred head**, of the heads whose line holds the side (every head when none does) the preferred one, is where a node of P signs (§5.5, Honest nodes of P); it decides nothing that counts.
- **Dispute**: rotations a and b are parties iff each rejects a rotation on the other's line other than itself.
- **Side**: the rotation the node's user chose, which holds until chosen again, against a rotation rejecting its line too; else, for a node of P whose roster authorizes a held rotation, its roster's line (below); else, in a dispute, the preferred party.
- **Nodes of P** side with the line D's roster authorizes: of the held rotations it **authorizes**, those on no other one's line, the preferred one. D's roster authorizes rotation r iff r is minted by a node D holds as current (read from the inner signature, §5.5) and no rotation on r's line is **beside** a removal of its minter from D: for each counting removal of P's of that node from D, at epoch e, the rotation is on e's line (made before the removal), or descends from the epoch of a counting grant of P's on D to that node signed at an epoch other than e descending from e (made after a re-add). A rotation names the heads by the same rule (§5.5, Honest nodes of P). A rotation **stands** iff no rotation on its line is beside a removal of its minter, whether D holds the minter now or not: a removed node's word from before its removal.
- **Doubt**: a node other than one of P reads the chain in doubt in a dispute. A fork is no doubt (a node's concurrent rotation, two nodes' rotations made at once, or a removed node's fork, which the next rotation of P rejects). A node tells its user when its chain comes to be read in doubt and lists it among its disputes while it is, or while the user's side holds; the user may choose any held rotation as the side.

### 5.5 Account epochs
A commit of account P names the epoch it is signed at (spec/wire.md §4); §5.4 decides which epochs are open.
- **Epoch rule**: commit c of P at epoch e counts at a node iff e counts and is open there, or e is held, counting or not, and c is **named**: listed by a counting rotation's list, named by a counting listing, or vouched for by a counting addendum (below). A thing list, listing entry or addendum on thing T names only commits with a delta on or acting for T. A commit named by a list or listing counts whole; an addendum saves only the plain-content deltas on its thing (§5.2). A commit that does not count is invalid whole but for `created.0` (whose record holds by its tid, as for Acting, §5.2): its edge writes apply nothing (the timeline never reads them), its deltas are un-merged (spec/crdt.md §4.6), and it counts again once it does. A commit at an epoch whose minting rotation the node does not hold is pending (spec/commits.md §4). Rotations count by §5.4 alone. Judging reads no `ts` or counter, and no inner signature but at P's own nodes, whose side it decides (§5.4); it also guides what they write (Cutoff, Listings).
- **Closers** of e at a node: the counting rotations it holds that close e (§5.4). A closer c **ends** e at a lister unless the lister can show it does not: a node of P reads D's thing lists, and there c ends e iff c descends from e, or a rotation D's roster authorizes on c's line names in its thing list for D a counting removal of a node D does not hold again that no such rotation on e's line names a removal of (e may be boxed to that node); a held rotation beside e naming no one more (a rotation of the same heads made at once) ends nothing, so a fork standing costs no write. At any other node every closer ends e, and every closer of an epoch not held. The **earliest closer** of e at a node is the first it received of those ending e.
- **Cutoff** (one rule for every lister: rotator, listing node, witness): a list, listing or addendum names a commit of P at epoch e only if its writing node received it (§5.1, Received) before the earliest closer of e it holds, or made it itself (read from the inner signature). A node lists a commit only once it holds the chain of the commit's epoch, and acts on a rotation (answers it, merges it, sides with it) only once it holds its chain.
- **Rotation's list**: a rotation r lists the commits of P at the epochs r closes that its rotator holds and names by the cutoff (any kind, covered and ephemeral ones included).
  - It is cut into **thing lists**: for each thing T under a sync ID the rotator opens that a listed commit has a delta on or acts for, one naming those commits. Each is a leaf of r's list tree, whose root r's header carries (spec/wire.md §4, List tree), at a random index.
  - A thing list is a chain certificate of T (§4): it goes under every sync ID holding T, and under every sync ID where a commit it names is logged as a certificate. The rotator logs, with r, the thing lists of the things under each sync ID it opens (spec/sync-judgement.md §1).
  - Nothing waits for a thing list: a commit whose thing list has not arrived reads as unlisted until it does. A thing under no sync ID the rotator opens gets none; P's commits on it count only once named otherwise.
  - **Bound**: a thing list's role-class and purge-request commits stay within `MAX_LIST_BYTES` (a rotation whose role and purge commits alone pass it on some thing is refused at the writer); plain content past it goes into the rotator's later listings (target, issues/linked-thing-lists.md: thing lists are written inline only, so the bound is `CHUNK_THRESHOLD`).
- **Chains travel**: every node of P logs under each sync ID it opens the rotations of P it holds (header-only certificate copies, every line) and their thing lists for things there.
- **Listings**: after each counting rotation r of P it holds with its chain that D's roster authorizes (one it does not, a removed node's fork, is answered by the rotation superseding it, which closes all r closes), a node of P writes a role commit at its preferred head whose `listing.0` entry keyed by r on each thing T names the commits of P on T at the closed epochs r closes (void ones included; an open one's commits are named once it closes) that no list, listing or addendum it holds names: its own (by inner signature), others' by the cutoff (its own rotations' overflow included), and every commit by a node that was removed from D and is a node of P again.
  - A listing at a closed epoch counts only once it is itself named.
  - A `listing.0` entry names commits of its own author only and needs no role on T; anything else in it is bad input. Listings compose by union, and the next rotation lists a listing by ID like any commit.
  - Bound: as a rotation's list, plain content past it in later listings.
- **Addenda against a rotation**: an addendum (§5.2) keyed by the commit ID of a rotation r of P vouches for a delta of P on N when r counts and closes the delta's commit's epoch, A's entry lists that commit, W held write on N just before A's key, the delta is plain content (§5.2), and A is valid (the same least fixpoint).
  - **Writing**: every writer node of N, of any account, that holds a rotation r of P with its chain writes one addendum on N keyed by r, listing P's plain-content commits on N at epochs r closes that it names by the cutoff and that no list or listing it holds names (target, issues/account-signing.md: again whenever what it holds changes).
  - Limit (§5.2): nothing checks when W received what it lists.
- **Demoting an account** on a thing is §5.1 and §5.2 with the account as author, independent of the epoch rule.
- **Honest nodes of P** sign at the preferred head. Removing a node X from D is two role commits signed at P's current epoch (§3): the removal with D's rotation boxing every node but X, under D's sync IDs only, then P's rotation (spec/crypto.md §9) writing only its seed into D's `account.0` and P's `keys.0`, its thing list for D naming the removal (the rotator's own commit, listed by the cutoff). No reader outside P receives the removal: the rotation alone closes X's epochs there, and its header shows neither D's roster nor its boxes. Any node of P rotates, deciding at the end of a complete pass of D's sync IDs, when it holds (each with its chain):
  - a rotation minted by a node D no longer holds, beside that node's removal, that no counting rotation D's roster authorizes supersedes, once a rotation so authorized names that removal (a removed node's fork);
  - a head whose line lacks a counting removal from D that a rotation D's roster authorizes names (the head may be boxed to the removed node); or
  - a counting removal from D no rotation D's roster authorizes names that it made, received by the cutoff, or that a rotation that stands names (§5.4, Nodes of P); the rotation's thing list for D names it. A removal named only by a rotation beside its maker's removal falls with it: a removed node removes no one.

  No node is designated: forks stand, so a fork alone is owed nothing, and rotations two nodes make at once fork and stand. The rotation supersedes every head and every such removed node's fork and names, signed at each one's epoch (spec/crypto.md §9), only the heads D's roster authorizes (§5.4, Nodes of P), so a fork by any other node, or a line through a removed node's rotation beside its removal (honest or not), is superseded unnamed and rejected; what P's honest nodes signed at its epochs counts by their own listings (Listings: those epochs are closed by it).
  - Limit: a removed node's commits at a head beside its removal (its own fork, or a sibling's epoch boxed to it) count at every node holding that head, whatever it received first, until a rotation of P superseding it arrives there; every node of P that holds the head owes that rotation at the end of its next complete pass of D's sync IDs, so with none of them online, or none holding the head, they count until one does.

## 6. Purge rights
- **The right**: a delta's retention names who may purge it (spec/commits.md §5), judged at the request's time. Each member of the named set holds the right on its own:
  - **admin**: the thing's current admins (in every set but `nobody`);
  - **author** (in `author_or_admin` and `writer`): the delta's header actor while it holds write on the thing: the actor itself and any author with write on that actor;
  - **writer**: any current writer of the thing.
  - An actor that lost write on the thing gives no author's right until it regains it.
- A role counts toward the right exactly when a delta of its holder needing that role at the request's key would be valid (§5.2): a demotion whose frontier does not list the request takes the right however early its `ts`; one that lists it leaves the purge standing; a re-grant gives the right back to a request made in the gap, as to its writes.
- **A purge request** drops a named delta iff its author held that right at the request's time and, when it acts for another actor (its header actor), so did that actor. Of a delta whose header the node does not hold, the class and actor are those the held snapshots carrying it state (spec/commits.md §5 Purge, Without the header). Each delta of a request by whole aspect or by author is judged alone, so the class of one never widens the others (a writer's request by author leaves that author's `admin` deltas).
- **Judgment** follows the role timeline the node holds, in any arrival order:
  - A named delta a request comes to drop is dropped then.
  - A delta held as its leftover for a request when no held request drops it any more counts as never received (spec/commits.md §5): its leftover is set aside and it is no longer held value-less, the node fetches it again where present and pulls it when a holder logs it again (spec/sync-judgement.md §3).
  - A held body stripped for carrying a purged value (a snapshot, a delta carrying a copy, spec/crdt.md §4.7) stays held as its leftover with any such fall: its value-less copies yield to the valued copy wherever one joins (spec/crdt.md §4.7 Join), and a copy of it that arrives replaces it, stripped again if it carries another's.
  - A body no node holds stays gone; the delta then reads as never received.
  - Held value-less again before a copy comes, it holds the leftover it set aside, as at a node that never saw the fall.
- **Destruction** (spec/commits.md §5): a request, a release request included, holds the right over a `created.0` delta as over any delta, by the set the delta's retention names; a key actor's, by the key actor itself (admin on itself). Its right is judged without the edge writes on the destroyed thing stamped after the request, so a demotion of its author on that thing stamped after it does not undo it whatever its frontier; one on another thing does, as above.
- **Who may undo a purge so**: the author of a demotion, whose frontier omits the request. A frontier may list a message and omit its purge, and the message is back wherever a copy is left. An addendum vouches for no purge request (§5.2).
- A node deletes at peers that keep a sync ID unread on its judgment after syncing, with no waiting time (between readers each judges its own store).
- The same right decides whether a compaction snapshot may drop a body it covers (spec/commits.md §5); a body a right holder dropped under a snapshot whose right later falls stays dropped.
- At a peer that keeps a sync ID unread a delete key proves the right as of the delta and the sync ID's current role keys (spec/sync-ids.md §6). Limit: so a holder demoted since can still delete there what its old role allowed, until a writer holding the body logs it again (spec/sync-judgement.md §3; a reader's relay of the deleted blob meets its tombstone).
- **Invalid deltas**: a node holds no delta it judges invalid past the transaction that judged it (spec/commits.md §5 Invalid). It keeps only:
  - a delta of a role commit: the timeline and the possible holders read every held edge write, of any validity (§5.1, spec/commits.md §4), and a sync ID holds the commit whole (a deposit is promoted whole, spec/sync-inbox.md §4);
  - a delta of its own commit: its user is told the write was refused and may write it again from it.
  - An ephemeral delta goes alike. A commit with no part held left and a part so dropped stays known only while an entry of it is (spec/commits.md §5 Forgetting, spec/sync-compaction.md §2); unstamped such entries count against the node's bounds on unvouched input (spec/commits.md §4 Pending storage).
  - A delta judged invalid drops as invalid even where a held snapshot covers it, never as covered.
  - A delta dropped so that turns valid again counts as never received and is fetched from any holder; with none it stays gone, at every node alike. The drop is no right's: no node deletes it at peers for it (spec/sync-judgement.md §2).
- What the right promises is that honest nodes keep the body. Which nodes carry it follows the thing's sync IDs: a thing leaving a group leaves that sync ID's stores whatever its retention (spec/sync-judgement.md §2), and holders keep their copies.

## 7. Links
- A **link** consists of K, an actor whose first admin is the sharer's actor, with secrets of its own and the `link` field in its creation record naming the target (spec/wire.md §3; every holder of the target holds that record among the chain certificates of K's grant, §4; the field is its creator's word: no node checks it and no authority depends on it; only a joiner's own leave reads it, below), granted the link's role on the target, and L, a key actor whose seed is in the link, with a grant (role r, invite r) on K for the link's role r, read or above (redeeming reads K's roster).
  - The sharer's actor is K's only admin.
  - A client making the link writes the target's `retain` default then in force on K (§3) when it says retain.
- **Link text** encodes: L's seed, the target tid, K's tid, K's sync ID, and optionally the sharer node's dialable key and relays.
- **First contact**: the redeemer dials the sharer's node at the key from the link, which serves K's sync ID as a peer that reads it (spec/wire.md §10).
  - A link carries only that key and those relays.
  - The redeemer dials that key with an ephemeral transport key, finds K through its shared set (the link key's static entry, spec/sync-opens.md §4), opens K's sync ID there as at any peer, and MAY keep dialing it, from the link it followed, for K's sync IDs and the target's, nothing else.
  - Putting the key in a link is the sharer's explicit choice; a link without it reaches the redeemer only through peers it already shares K with. The sharer's node serves K's sync ID and the target's from its own store (spec/sync-ids.md §5).
- L is listed in every step of K's sync ID by its static entry with an entry box (spec/sync-ids.md §3), and holds boxes in K's `secrets.0` like any key-actor holder (sealed to L's KEM key, spec/crypto.md §3).
- **Redeem**:
  1. Derive L's keys from the seed.
  2. Claim K's sync ID by L's static entry, take K's read secrets from the head's entry box (spec/sync-ids.md §3), pull K's sync ID and L's boxes.
  3. Wait until the held role history shows K's grant on the target (a client making a link delivers that grant under K's sync ID), and grant nothing without it: the target is the link text's word, unsigned.
  4. Then, signing as L, invite-grant the redeemer's actor (its account, or a fresh account) to K with role r and seal K's secrets to it. L writes in commit form and deposits the commit under K's sync ID (spec/sync-inbox.md §3) unless r is write or above.
  - A redeemer keeps the seed until its grant is at a source of K's sync ID (a peer holds it, or the sharer's node applied it), proving L on every connection to the sharer's node until then (that node opens K toward the redeemer by L until it holds the grant), and SHOULD discard it then.
  - A rotation of K by a holder's departure (a joiner's leave, a node removal) changes nothing for the link: L stays a holder and the next step lists it.
  - The link has ended, and the redeemer stops there, when either holds:
    - every route of K's sync ID (the link's node and the peers it shares K with, as known) has failed to open it, at least one by refusing L's static entry;
    - a held role history shows L's grant, or K's on the target, removed or past `until`.
  - A redeemer that stops, for any reason, forgets the seed and so loses read access to K and what K holds (§3).
- **Joiner's leave**: an account other than K's first admin holding K by a grant (a thing whose creation record has the `link` field) leaves K (a self-removal, §2) once a held role history shows K's edge on the target that field names removed or past `until`; K's edge on any other thing ends nothing. A node of the account MUST write it once it holds that history. A grant of K again brings back no joiner.
- `until` on L's grant expires the link. **Single use** (the link's `flags` bit 0, spec/wire.md §10) = the redemption also removes L's own grant in the same commit (a self-removal).
- **Finalized**: L's grant past `until` is finalized like any ended grant (§2), by any writer of K: that removal rotates K and audits nothing. Besides, an expired or self-removed L is finalized by a sharer node writing an explicit removal of L (§5.1), which audits L's invite grants: a node that holds the link's seed thing and admin on K MUST write it once L's edge expired or was self-removed (or finalized) with no explicit write on it since, after a complete pass of K's sync IDs (spec/sync-status.md §1) that ended, for an expiry, after `until` (it waits for that pass however long it takes).
  - An invite grant as L that the removal's frontier does not cover is dropped (§5.1 step 4); one received before it stands.
- The sharer's link seed thing is an item of the sharer's D, so every node of the sharer holds it. A node's removal from D does not end a link: the removed node reads through L until L is removed from K. An implementation MUST tell the one removing a node which of the account's links still work, and let any node of the sharer remove their L.
- **Bearer use**: act as L without writing anything. Read only. A node that read as L may forget the seed and keep what L reads and follow it (a contact card, spec/sync-inbox.md §5): it counts as read access while L holds a role on it (§3).
- A joiner that may still hold the seed is revoked only together with L, both removals in one commit with one rotation of K (spec/commits.md §2); a new link is a new L.
- The sharer keeps the seed on a thing only its own actor reads (the seed thing: aspect `link:seed.0`, a map {0: K's tid, 1: the seed, 2: the target's tid, 3: single use}) and writes L's `keys.0` and K's deposit seed.
