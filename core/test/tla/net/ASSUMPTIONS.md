# Networking rework model: assumptions
> Summary: What NetStream.tla, NetShared.tla, NetOpen.tla, NetHold.tla and NetKeep.tla abstract away from the sync spec (spec/sync*.md), and what is still stronger than reality.

- One node and one peer (b, or c, a copy of b's store at any instant); one sync ID in NetShared, one or two in NetStream. A store restored from a backup is modelled as c replacing b; two live copies as c beside b.
- A connection is an ordered pair of queues, reliable and private between the two nodes: the connection layer is encrypted end to end whatever carries it (spec/sync-transport.md). Each side proves actors over the connection's challenges; r and every Hit reach only the other end. A node between is not modelled, nor a choice of paths; nothing above the connection layer assumes a directly reachable peer.
- One outstanding Pull per direction. Beacon and Pull pages are bounded (one event per page) rather than sized. In NetStream `follow`/Push is a Pull plus an announcement flag (`dirty`, `Notify`); in NetShared a Rot is a Push.
- Events are entry adds and deletions; compaction keeps each held entry's latest event. Bodies, `inline_limit`, ephemeral entries and blob dedupe across connections are out: an event carries its entry.
- Rights are ghosts: in NetStream who may open is `rights`; in NetShared a credential is the sync ID's current epoch number, the one credential of each side. Covers across a departure, deposits and account proofs are not modelled.
- The adversary is an honest-looking peer answering Hits; forged proofs are not.
- `Synced` is per source; "holds what the other holds" is judged modulo entries deleted somewhere (`CaughtUpHolds`); `DeletionsArrive` then checks that the source's retained deletions do reach a node caught up with it. A deletion the log has dropped reaches nobody by the log; retention's judgment, not modelled in NetStream, handles the stale entry (NetHold shows a right holder's delete doing so at a store).
- Cost is modelled as: a from-the-start reply where the log could have answered from a position; no byte counts.
- Log and record are one durable unit: `Apply`, a copy and a restore never separate a stored cursor from what it covers.
- The digest is a flag of the member's openness; a difference sends a separate list (`List`, one message carrying one sync ID's flag) after the peer's digest is read, the order spec/sync-opens.md §3 states. Paged lists are one message; a hash collision is ignored. `RecvDig` compares the current open flag, equal to the connection-start flag because nothing opens or closes across the digest except a Judge whose Lost is in flight (idempotent).
- `Past` is the report of the requester's position: `Applied` when `Follow` (only after a Rot marked `rep`), a Pull's cursor otherwise and at the start of each connection (`PullCur`, the requester's durable `seen`, the cursor's coverage of the peer's rotation). A rotation already passed by that cursor is not sent again. It is sent also by a peer not in the member set, and by a removed peer that passes the event without applying it. Stronger than reality: `LearnView` reads the peer's true role (a node learns a role from the thing's role commits, later and through its own view); one Pull per direction; the `until` is one position, the rotation (an `until` over unrelated later events is not modelled); `T_untildrop`, `T_followremdrop`, `T_rot` and `T_rotdrop` are bounded to a drop after the rotation and 3 queued messages each way (`T_rotdrop` has no removal; `T_followremdrop` has rotation, removal and drop together, with `EpochReached` off because a removed rotator stops serving).
- `EpochReached` claims only a holder open on both sides when the rotation happened, no record lost: a one-sided open or a lost record may leave a holder a rotation behind, stranded until a node both can match (spec/sync-ids.md §6 Walk, Stranded there).
- Bounds that exclude classes of bug: one credential per side in NetShared, so additions equal the whole filter; `TN_genbase` fails RuleHolds through a copied store, which shows the base check is needed but not the two-credential case. Its argument: additions to a generation the receiver does not hold would be taken as the whole filter, the receiver would lack the sender's earlier credentials for its tests, and a pair is missed with nothing pending; code 13 and the whole filter close it.
- `Settles` is checked with bounded faults, so it says nothing of a connection that keeps dropping.
- NetOpen: one sync ID; steps a chain, one per sync epoch (no forks, no step without rotation); a fixed event order per instance (a grant, a removal of Adv, which rotates, a rotation), each event at any time, each honest node learning them in order at its own pace (a node learns a new head, its seed and its claims from the role commits, abstracted); Adv knows every event at once. Honest nodes are never removed. A holder granted later takes its claims (seeds and S's claim blocks) at once once it is open whole toward an honest peer; that peer registering the store's path (spec/sync-ids.md §6 Walk) and a store's head as the newest registered step are abstracted: a node's head is the newest step it learned. So a held sync secret newer than the head's (spec/sync-opens.md §4) never arises. Shown and tested lists as sync-opens §4 states them for a holder: the sync proof of each step on its path whose seed it holds, its head claim keys (every holder's claim key at its head, from the claim blocks it holds) and its head's deposit key; it tests its held seeds' proofs newest first and, while none derives the source's step's key, its own claim key at the source's head (learned by Head; Adv answers any step). Claim keys of holders it reaches X through (other things, the public) are its own here. An ended open is served up to its `until` and may close (a Lost) at any time after; the serve-through itself, reported positions, records, the pending set and Register are not modelled. A Hit's or tag's binding (side, both challenges, listener key) is modelled as a signature's (one challenge): weaker. Data order is outside the model: a node that has not learned a rotation holds nothing written after it, since the event comes first in S's stream. `Live` assumes stable credentials: every event known to both ends; fairness is per connection. Roles are fixed, so a class that moves is not modelled; core/sync tests it (`opens::a_sources_class_*`).
- NetHold: one sync ID, one aspect, three bodies (b, its cover c, a role commit x invalidating c); every reader a writer (no deposits, stamps or code 9: K takes any pulled entry not tombstoned). A reader's view of K is K's store at its last Sync (a stream read to its end, its Deleted events kept); Sync is strongly fair and a tombstone expires only once every reader read past the delete (a stream is read within `TOMBSTONE_LIFETIME`); with expiry before that, two readers' concurrent deletes of a duplicate and its lower copy remove the body and the stand keeps it out. b is past its keep bound; its author r2 never drops it (Own bodies) but, holding c valid, takes its entries out of its log (§3 Own store) and puts them back when c falls there; r1 drops it under c. No restarts: a reader's stand state and the deletes it saw at K never reset, the durable case of a peer with a record (§4); a peer with no record, whose stand lasts a connection, is not modelled. A fall is c judged invalid; the head moves freely (no rotation tied to x). H only deletes. A cap counts entries of b and c (x, a role entry, counts toward none); `full` is K's flag as last read. Ephemeral entries, caps giving way, purges, expiry of bodies and certificate copies are not modelled.
- NetKeep: sync epochs are steps one to one; the granter holds every epoch and registers its steps in order at K while K shows a key it holds; boxes and Registers arrive in any order and are not lost. A removed admin registering a sibling step (spec/sync-hostile.md §2) and an epoch no step bears are not modelled.
- NetStream presence (`TrackPres`): what a node holds as present at its peer lives in its record; events mark it, and a listing from the start, at its last page, replaces its members' presence by the server's holdings when it read that page (what the listing showed, given the horizon check: a position below the newest expired deletion event is answered from the start). The serving half of caught up takes the peer's Applied as read when the peer applied (no report in flight). `S_pres` has no drop (a restore reconnects) and two entries. `Echo` "applied": the requester's tells and Applied are one step with its apply; they reach the server after the server's reply in flight to it (sent before them), else at once, and are lost with the connection; what a node's peer counts it holding (`ctd`) is kept with the connection, an entry with the position of its event then.
- NetHold extensions: `KeepB` stands for any reason b is must-hold whatever covers it (a kept delta, a lasting header, a delta on another aspect no snapshot covers); x's fall (`XFall`) is learned by every reader at once and counts no fall of c; a restart changes nothing durable (`StandDurable`), the control resets the stand and the deletes seen. Without `Fixpoint` a reader's verdict on c lags until a Judge step; world writes still judge at once.
- NetKeep extensions: with `NoHead` K is granted at epoch 0 with the step and box both on their way; a hostile box names an epoch no step bears, which no reader holds.
- NetOpen junk: a junk filter entry matches any value a tester tests; Adv answers the Hit with any token, and its answer is valid only for a key it holds (a seed it was a holder at, its own claim key, a public value with `AdvPublic`).
