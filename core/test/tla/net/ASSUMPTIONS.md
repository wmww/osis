# Networking rework model: assumptions
> Summary: What NetStream.tla, NetShared.tla, NetBlind.tla, NetOpen.tla, NetHold.tla, NetDestroy.tla and NetKeep.tla abstract away from issues/sync-rewrite.md, and what is still stronger than reality.

- One node and one peer (b, or c, a copy of b's store at any instant); one sync ID in NetShared, one or two in NetStream. A store restored from a backup is modelled as c replacing b; two live copies as c beside b.
- A connection is an ordered pair of queues, reliable and private between the two nodes: the connection layer is encrypted end to end whatever carries it (issues/sync-rewrite.md, Reachable through anything). Each side proves actors over the connection's challenges; r and every Hit reach only the other end. A node between is not modelled, nor a choice of paths; nothing above the connection layer assumes a directly reachable peer.
- One outstanding Pull per direction. Beacon and Pull pages are bounded (one event per page) rather than sized. In NetStream `follow`/Push is a Pull plus an announcement flag (`dirty`, `Notify`); in NetShared a Rot is a Push.
- Events are entry adds and deletions; compaction keeps each held entry's latest event. Bodies, `inline_limit`, ephemeral entries and blob dedupe across connections are out: an event carries its entry.
- Rights are ghosts: in NetStream who may open is `rights`; in NetShared a credential is the sync ID's current epoch number and J is a view of one peer actor, learned at any time from group data. Key actors, covers across a departure, deposits and account proofs are not modelled. A peer that cannot read is NetBlind.tla: one reader, whose record of the peer lasts across connections (the case of a peer with a record; with none `known` and the cursor last one connection); entries are one body and its covering snapshot; the reader always holds the delete key.
- The adversary is an honest-looking peer answering Hits; forged proofs are not.
- `Synced` is per source; "holds what the other holds" is judged modulo entries deleted somewhere (`CaughtUpHolds`); `DeletionsArrive` then checks that the source's retained deletions do reach a node caught up with it. A deletion the log has dropped reaches nobody by the log; retention's judgment, not modelled in NetStream, handles the stale entry (NetBlind shows a right holder's delete doing so at a peer).
- Cost is modelled as: a from-the-start reply where the log could have answered from a position; no byte counts.
- Log and record are one durable unit: `Apply`, a copy and a restore never separate a stored cursor from what it covers.
- The digest is a flag of the member's openness; a difference sends a separate list (`List`, one message carrying one sync ID's flag) after the peer's digest is read, the order issues/sync-rewrite.md states. Paged lists are one message; a hash collision is ignored. `RecvDig` compares the current open flag, equal to the connection-start flag because nothing opens or closes across the digest except a Judge whose Lost is in flight (idempotent).
- `Past` is the report of the requester's position: `Applied` when `Follow` (only after a Rot marked `rep`), a Pull's cursor otherwise and at the start of each connection (`PullCur`, the requester's durable `seen`, the cursor's coverage of the peer's rotation). A rotation already passed by that cursor is not sent again. It is sent also by a peer not in the member set, and by a removed peer that passes the event without applying it. Stronger than reality: `LearnView` reads the peer's true role (a node learns a role from group data, later and through its own view); one Pull per direction; the `until` is one position, the rotation (an `until` over unrelated later events is not modelled); `T_untildrop`, `T_followremdrop`, `T_rot` and `T_rotdrop` are bounded to a drop after the rotation and 3 queued messages each way (`T_rotdrop` has ByRole on and no removal; `T_followremdrop` has rotation, removal and drop together, with `EpochReached` off because a removed rotator stops serving).
- `EpochReached` claims only a holder open on both sides when the rotation happened, no record lost: a one-sided open or a lost record may leave a holder a rotation behind, stranded until a node both can match, as issues/sync-rewrite.md accepts.
- Bounds that exclude classes of bug: one credential per side in NetShared, so additions equal the whole filter; `TN_genbase` fails RuleHolds through a copied store, which shows the base check is needed but not the two-credential case. Its argument: additions to a generation the receiver does not hold would be taken as the whole filter, the receiver would lack the sender's earlier credentials for its tests, and a pair is missed with nothing pending; code 13 and the whole filter close it.
- `Settles` is checked with bounded faults, so it says nothing of a connection that keeps dropping.
- NetOpen: one sync ID; a fixed event order per instance (a grant, a removal of Adv, a rotation), each event at any time, each honest node learning them in order at its own pace (a store learns a new head and seed from its granter, abstracted); Adv knows every event at once. A removal updates the roster and the listed static entries together, for stores too (a store reading the head's claim set). Honest nodes are never removed. Filters show only the shower's head values to honest testers; Adv may Hit anything a node ever showed (a stored filter keeps removed entries). An open ends at once when its node learns a change that voids its basis, with a Lost closing both sides; the `until` serve-through of the rotation event, records, Behind, registration, the pending set and read secrets (two readers, J only) are not modelled. A Hit's or tag's binding (side, both challenges, listener key) is modelled as a signature's (one challenge): weaker. An answer's signature is by the sync key of the replier's step (sync-opens §5); a link holder checks it against the steps it knows rather than the head its entry box names. Shown and tested lists are per holding as sync-opens §4 states: a store shows its head's sync key and proof and tests both, a reader shows its newest proof and tests sync keys (its walk); claims, deposit keys and read secrets are not modelled. A claim is as a static entry. Data order is outside the model: a node that has not learned a rotation holds nothing written after it, since the event comes first in S's stream. `Live` assumes stable credentials: every event known to both ends.
- NetHold: one sync ID, one aspect, three bodies (b, its cover c, a role commit x invalidating c); every reader a writer (no deposits, stamps or code 9: K takes any pulled entry not tombstoned). A reader's view of K is K's store at its last Sync (a stream read to its end, its Deleted events kept); Sync is strongly fair and a tombstone expires only once every reader read past the delete (a stream is read within `TOMBSTONE_LIFETIME`); with expiry before that, two readers' concurrent deletes of a duplicate and its lower copy remove the body and the stand keeps it out. b is past its keep bound; its author r2 never drops it (Own bodies) but, holding c valid, takes its entries out of its log (§3 Own store) and puts them back when c falls there; r1 drops it under c. No restarts: a reader's stand state and the deletes it saw at K never reset, the durable case of a peer with a record (§4); a peer with no record, whose stand lasts a connection, is not modelled. A fall is c judged invalid; the head moves freely (no rotation tied to x). H only deletes. A cap counts entries of b and c (x, a role entry, counts toward none); `full` is K's flag as last read. Ephemeral entries, caps giving way, purges, expiry of bodies and certificate copies are not modelled.
- NetDestroy: one content entry e and one release request q; q's expiry ends the window and comes only once every node not away was told (the window outlasts a connected node's reading). A node that forgot S syncs it no more, so the take-back of a released thing (§5) through S is not reached. Both readers hold the delete key.
- NetKeep: sync epochs are steps one to one; Registers are taken in order, and keep coming after K's removal (they need no open); boxes, removal notices and Registers arrive in any order and are not lost. A genuine notice is signed by the step before the removing rotation or by that rotation's own. The forger sends one notice signed by no delete key of S; a removed admin still holding the head's delete key is not modelled (§6 accepts its signature by design).
- NetStream presence (`TrackPres`): what a node holds as present at its peer lives in its record; events mark it, and a listing from the start, at its last page, replaces its members' presence by the server's holdings when it read that page (what the listing showed, given the horizon check: a position below the newest expired deletion event is answered from the start). The serving half of caught up takes the peer's Applied as read when the peer applied (no report in flight). `S_pres` has no drop (a restore reconnects) and two entries.
- NetHold extensions: `KeepB` stands for any reason b is must-hold whatever covers it (a kept delta, a lasting header, a delta on another aspect no snapshot covers); x's fall (`XFall`) is learned by every reader at once and counts no fall of c; a restart changes nothing durable (`StandDurable`), the control resets the stand and the deletes seen. Without `Fixpoint` a reader's verdict on c lags until a Judge step; world writes still judge at once.
- NetDestroy readers meeting: a reader's stream shows its store; no tombstone of a reader source is modelled; untold is triggered by meeting a delete it cannot explain or K still showing e (how it learns T is destroyed is abstracted).
- NetKeep extensions: with `NoHead` K is granted at epoch 0 with the step and box both on their way; a hostile box names one epoch past any real one, so it is never checkable (no step of it is ever registered), and K counts it only among the epochs a notice must bind past.
- NetOpen junk: a junk filter entry matches any value a tester tests; Adv answers the Hit with any token, and its tag is valid only on a match it holds (a public value with `AdvPublic`, or a seed or link key it holds).
