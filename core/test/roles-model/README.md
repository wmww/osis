# roles-model
> Summary: Rust reference models of the role rules (spec/permissions.md §5) and of account signing epochs (issues/account-signing.md): modules, searches with their sizes, and the latest results.

Independent of any osis crate: an oracle, never patched to match the implementation (osis-perm dev-depends on it for its differentials). Module map and API: `src/lib.rs`; findings behind the role timeline: notes/permissions.md; disputes: notes/disputes.md. Choices the spec or plan left open: AMBIGUITIES.md.

## Epochs (`src/epochs.rs`)
One account P (node 0 the remover, X = 1, Y = 2, maybe a sibling), witness writers of other accounts, an optional admin demoting P on thing 0, one outsider. A removal is two commits: the removal commit on the devices actor at the old epoch, received by P's nodes alone on its own schedule, and the rotation, whose devices-actor list names it; a sibling boxes X unless it holds the removal commit, and rotates from the new epoch only once it holds it (the seed sits under the devices actor's read epoch the removal mints). A rotation whose chain is not held is pending: it rejects nothing and is no side. Things sit in one or two labels (a thing may sit in both); every node opens some labels. Generated: content and snapshots by any node, X's removal and in some worlds Y's after it, X re-added, X's own rotation shortly before its removal (made offline: a sibling may write or rotate at its epoch first), a sibling's concurrent rotation (made before the removal reaches it, boxing X), removed nodes' forks, counter-merges, X forking plainly from its old epoch again after every rotation of P's (the world ends with one unrejected), node 0 gone for good after its removals (it acts and receives no more, still on the roster), backdated writes and own listings, witness snapshots that fall, part bounds of 1 or 2 IDs, covered commits omitted or listed; honest listings and addenda after every change. The rules under test: a counting rotation closes every epoch that does not descend from it (siblings' concurrent epochs included); each observer follows the rotations as `forks` places them (`World::follow`: outside P the line held first, at P's nodes the revocations first-seen as a consistent set), and any member of P rotates (superseding every head) when one is due from it (`Builder::due`: a removed node's rotation no authorized one supersedes, or a head whose line lacks a removal it holds with its rotation; in the settle also two heads it authorizes, `Builder::forked`, asked in turn as merges made at once fork again), none designated; a rotation is signed at each fork it names, and a rotation signed at e rejects nothing on a line through e; one cutoff for every lister (others' commits received before the earliest rotation ending their epoch, `World::ends`: one descending from it, or beside it naming a removal its line lacks; a witness counts every closer); a rotation names only the forks D's roster authorizes, judged from what the merger holds (no rotation on their line beside a removal of its minter, `World::stands`), and a commit at an epoch left void counts once named, as at a closed epoch (honest nodes list their own there). Checked at every observer and every prefix of its receipts:
- Safety: no write by a node while removed counts at an observer siding with an honest line no removal leaves beside it and holding that removal with its chain; counted apart: a removed node's fork counting and making it valid, or a head beside the removal (a sibling's concurrent rotation, or the removed node's own from before it) still open (the window until a rotation of P's arrives; the write reads invalid without those rotations), an unaware sibling's rotation or listing naming it (stated limit), a re-added node's later word reviving it, a witness's valid snapshot carrying it (stated limit). A write valid because of something signed at a sibling's concurrent epoch once that epoch is closed is a hard failure of its own.
- Re-forks hide nothing: at an owner-sided observer holding the forker's removal, once the honest answers are in (its last reading), dropping a removed node's plain forks (rejecting nothing, nothing built on them) and their lists shows no more honest content. Counted: observer-prefixes with a removed node's rotation on a head's line (and of those in a dispute, told), and those reading an honest fork.
- Removal commit: wherever the rotation following it counts, a held removal commit counts (it is the rotator's own commit, named by its devices-actor list).
- Removal stands: at an observer holding a removal with its chain where every removed node's rotation it holds is signed at an epoch node 0's line is signed at (the removal's own, or a sibling's fork node 0's merge named), and that held the removal before every rotation beside it no merge of node 0's line held before the removed node's forks takes in, the removal counts. This is the only check of the merge's fork signatures and of "rejects nothing on a line through e": without them the contest ends in a dispute, which the safety check (owner-sided observers only) does not see.
- Liveness, at observers holding everything an honest party holds under their labels: every legitimate content commit (at any epoch, void ones included) some party whose word stands received in time (oracle from the history alone: the receipt or authorship of a final member not gone, before the earliest honest rotation ending the commit's epoch, a witness's before the earliest rotation the epoch does not descend from, a rotator's list, a chain of honest listings; an epoch no honest rotation ends stays a head) is visible; nothing is visible beyond the upper bound (receipts before any closer's `ts`).
- Monotone: dropping an honest list, listing or addendum never makes more valid.
- Omit-covered loses nothing against listing every commit.

Hand tests (`cargo test -p roles-model --lib epochs`) state the properties of the build plan's steps 4 and 5 on small worlds; `removed_node_holding_a_sibling_epoch` is the minimal sibling-epoch world; `any_node_supersedes_a_sibling_epoch_with_the_remover_gone` and `a_standing_fork_loses_no_write_when_its_writer_goes` the minimal worlds for no designated rotator and for `World::ends`.

### Results
Judgment indexes each held set once (`Lines`: lines as membership rows, signed-at epochs, the minting rotation per epoch; `Roots`: `verified` memoized, as walking every merge's named forks again per call is exponential in merges), so a world costs polynomially in its items: the slowest of 20,000 random worlds (140 items) takes 0.6 s.

Oracle readings the first-seen sides need (each a stated limit, spec/permissions.md §5.5): an observer whose heads all lie beside a removal it holds followed a line it held first (an unaware sibling's rotation, or the removed node's own from before the removal), so a removed node's write counting there is the window until a merge of P joins them (counted with the beside-head window), not a safety failure; for "removal stands", a merge takes a rival in only if held before every rotation of the removed node's beside the removal, its own from before the removal included. Honest policy: a lister leaving out covered commits leaves out none a snapshot of P's covers (`covered_at`; hand test `a_cover_by_ps_own_snapshot_excuses_no_listing`), as P's snapshot counts by the lister's side of P's chain. A re-add comes first in its tick (`World::grant_of`; hand test `a_rotation_right_after_a_readd_stands_beside_the_removers`).

## Forks and sides (`src/forks.rs`)
The first-seen rule for an account's forked signing chain (spec/permissions.md §5.4, Placing and Following, at a node of P): rule, parties, honest policy and properties in the module doc. The thief X also seals inner signatures as Z, a key actor it makes up, edits D at any epoch it holds (backdated grants, a grant to itself at an unaware sibling's epoch, a commit creating D made again) and claims to take any revocation (A's removal of it among them) or one it keeps to itself for good. B's user, when B took X's side first, switches in settle round 0, 1 or 3, or never (a quarter of the worlds each); the settle runs `ROUNDS` rounds and fails if an honest node still rotates in the last. `cargo run --bin roles-model -- N forks` (random worlds); negative controls `forks-stand` (forks stand beside their removal), `forks-blind` (P's nodes judge as outsiders), `forks-anyone` (any key actor that signs is a node and D's roster reads every grant at a counting epoch: the rule before the node rule), `forks-first-grants` (a removed node's grant hit only when held after its removal or signed beside it), `forks-no-takes` (P's nodes read only the revocations on a rotation's line, not what it takes), `forks-every-answer` (a node answers every disregarded rotation no counting one rejects, not one per line); hand tests `cargo test -p roles-model --lib forks`. perm's `chain::forks_model_agrees` (and `_large`) is the differential of judgment, osis-engine's `rotation::tests_forks` that of real engines (judgment and the honest policy, `act`). A node of P opens an inner signature only with the seed of the epoch it is signed at (`signer_at`: a removed node's word at an epoch only it holds opens to no one); a merge names only heads D's roster authorizes (this rotation's removal applied: a removal never merges its target's earlier rotation, which the next rotation answers); a node not holding its preferred head's seed signs nothing; placing's last tiebreak reads `World::commit_ids` when a differential sets them (the engine's commit IDs).

Rejected for P's nodes (each failed here): judging as outsiders do (`forks-blind`); placing with a conflict relation (a later arrival in a line changes earlier decisions, and a plain fork held first outweighs its maker's removal: the honest devices split); revocations that drop earlier ones and revive (oscillates when a removed node's revocation hits its own line); first-seen grants (`forks-first-grants`); reading a device's side from what its rotations supersede (a line at odds with a taken revocation's, one disowning one on the other: a device that took X's revocation before it held A's removal disowns nothing of A's line, so A and T follow its rotations, boxed to X, round after round; at odds with any line followed before splits honest nodes once the device switches); a removal hitting its node's rotations off the removal epoch's line rather than off the line of the rotation carrying it (a removal merging its target's earlier rotation voids itself: `a_removal_merging_its_targets_earlier_rotation_stands`). Merge races converge once nodes act in turn (the settle); nothing bounds rounds of merges made at once.

## Custody (`src/custody.rs`)
notes/account-forks-design.md, greenfield (no code shared with `forks`): seed custody follows holding (a node opens every box to its key and derives every merge seed whose tips' seeds it holds, counting or not), the chain is D's roster (an epoch is its line of plain rotations; nodes on a line are the creation devices plus additions minus removals carried there; a removal hits its node's rotations off its line), a merge is only a join (its epoch the union of the joined lines, its seed a function of its tips'). Rule, parties, honest policy and properties in the module doc. Parties: A (removes X), B (offline, unaware: rotates boxing X), C (apart: removes T and rotates thing t at its own epoch, the listing-cycle shape), X (every header and old key: forks, revokes A, B or C, signs as X or as Z, adds Z or itself, takes A's removal or a revocation it withholds, answers honest rotations, certifies merges of epochs whose seeds it holds, re-seals its seeds to honest nodes, writes and lists), T (reads only), R1 (outside reader holding t), R2 (outside reader whose user may switch). Schedules random and lockstep; the removal reaching B and C only in the settle in half the worlds (they take X's revocation first); P's users on X's side switch to A's removal in the settle (B in round 0, 1 or 3, or never). Hand tests (`cargo test -p roles-model --lib custody`) are the minimal world of each property, each also asserting its negative control fails.

Searches: `cargo run --bin roles-model -- N custody` (random), `-- 0 custody-exh` (the small family: 32 setups (schedule, B switching in round 1 or never, B offline, C apart removing T, X's tick-0 rotation) × the removal late to B and C or not × one move of X at tick 2 from a menu of nine (revoking A, revoking B boxed to B, revoking A claiming A's removal, revoking A taking one withheld, signing as Z adding Z, answering an honest rotation, a write, a merge certificate, a re-seal; at each epoch whose seed it holds; delivered to everyone, to B, C and R1 only, or no one before the settle) × the honest move at tick 3); negative controls `custody-judged`, `custody-roster-bare`, `custody-merge-random`, `custody-stale-shut`, `custody-merge-carries` (random, N worlds). Default tests: 40 random worlds and every 40th world of two small-family setups; `random_worlds_hold_large` and `small_family_holds_large` the twins.

Counted, not failures (8,000 worlds): at P's nodes, X's write at an open epoch an unaware sibling boxed to X counting 2,030 node-prefixes (until the merge closes it); X holding seeds an unaware node minted (the unaware-sibling limit) 20,382 rotations. Not modelled: honest additions (pairings); X's additions and re-adds are.

Choices the model fixed in the design (notes/account-forks-design.md): a certificate's inner signature opens with any joined epoch's seed (else a node holding only some joined seeds reads certificates of one epoch otherwise); a plain rotation for a taken revocation is owed only while the node cannot merge (a taken revocation counts, so some head carries it); an answer is owed only to a rotation that does not stand or stands with a revocation not taken (not to one waiting on its parent epoch); D's roster removes only by taken revocations (a revocation held with its chain but not taken, its line merging a fork signed as a made-up key actor, removes no one: `a_held_revocation_not_taken_removes_no_one`). A certificate by a node a taken removal hits stands with nothing, so at a node on X's side an honest certificate may not stand while another of the same epoch does: certificates are compared among those standing at the node.
