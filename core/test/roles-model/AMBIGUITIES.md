# roles-model: ambiguities
> Summary: Where issues/account-signing.md (the decided plan) or the spec left a choice while extending the epochs model, the model's choice, and the open hole: a sibling's concurrent rotation boxes a removed node an epoch its removal does not close.

Each entry: the text, the problem, the model's choice (`src/epochs.rs`), and its status.

1. **A removed node keeps an epoch its removal does not close. Open: design hole.** Plan, Design: "Removal is the rotation: removing node X from D and rotating P's epoch are one role commit; X is not boxed the new seed"; Forks: "A line is contested only from below, so a removed node cannot void its removal, only fork." A sibling S that rotates before node 0's removal of X reaches it boxes X its new epoch e_S (X is on D's roster in S's view). The removal, signed at the epoch before, does not close e_S, so X, removed, still signs at e_S, and:
   - (a) X's fork signed at e_S supersedes the removal's epoch unnamed. The removal's line does not pass through e_S, so the fork rejects it: from beside, not below. Wherever node 0's merge is not held, the removal is void. Where it is held, the result is a dispute, or worse: if node 0 signs the merge at e_S as well, the merge cannot reject X's fork, and X voids the removal everywhere.
   - (b) Node 0's merge closes e_S and lists what node 0 received at e_S, which includes X's writes after its removal. So do S's listings (received before the earliest closer of e_S, that is the merge) and witnesses' addenda (received before the merge's `ts`). Those writes count for good.
   The member-level rotation rule has the read-side twin ("a rotation that missed a concurrent removal is rotated again before anyone encrypts under it", notes/permissions.md Forks), but a signing key is used by its holder, not by the honest side, so re-rotating does not undo what X signed meanwhile. Minimal reproducer: `removed_node_holding_a_sibling_epoch` (ignored). In random worlds the failure appears only when such a sibling rotation exists (15,072 safety and 42 liveness failures in 1M worlds, every one of them in the 103,299 worlds with one). The model makes the removed node sign at the newest epoch it was boxed (`Builder::epoch_known`), sibling epochs included.

2. **Parts and "applies once its parts for held labels are held".** Plan, Design (Rotation's list): "the copy under L carries L's part and applies once its parts for held labels are held (spec/commits.md §4)". But spec/commits.md §4 says nothing waits for a part. With Merkle-hidden parts (Settled items) a reader cannot tell which labels have a part, so it cannot know which parts to wait for. Choice: no gating. Each per-thing list is its own item and joins when it arrives; a rotation counts on its header. Counted: a listed write reads invalid while its rotation counts and its list has not arrived (`part_pending`, 959,720 observer-prefixes in 1M worlds). Nothing is lost by the end.

3. **Who places a rotation, and its per-thing lists, under a label the rotator does not open.** Plan: "A label the rotator did not open gets no part"; "per-thing lists are chain certificates and travel where their commits go". It does not say who carries them there. If no honest node of P syncs the label, nobody does. Choice: rotation headers reach every reader, and a rotation's list for a thing reaches every reader of that thing, under any of its labels (`Builder::recv`). Without this, a reader of an unopened label would never see the epoch close.

4. **Listings travel like rotation parts.** Plan: "per label it opens, a role commit at a counting epoch naming…". It does not say whether a listing's per-thing lists also travel as certificates into the thing's other labels. Choice: yes. A listing is per thing (`Kind::Listing`) and reaches every reader of the thing: one mechanism for lists.

5. **"Naming what nothing it holds names".** Choice: names in a counting list (a counting rotation's list, or a listing that counts as P's). A name in a void fork's list, or in a listing at a closed, unlisted epoch, does not count as naming.

6. **Cutoff for others' commits: "received before the earliest counting rotation it holds closing that epoch".** A member that has not yet received node 0's removal counts a removed node's fork as the closer. Its cutoff is then the fork's `ts`, which the removed node picks, so the member lists the removed node's writes up to it. Choice: literal. This is counted with the stated unaware-sibling limit (`listed_by_unaware_closer` includes listings). The oracle's upper bound accepts legitimate commits saved that way.

7. **Re-added node.** "Every commit of a re-added node" includes its writes from while it was removed: the re-grant rule (spec/permissions.md §5.2, "including those in the gap"). Choice: yes. Every member lists them, and the re-added node reads every rotation through D once back. Counted: such writes revived only by the post-re-add word (`hostile_revived_by_readd`).

8. **Bound.** Choice: the bound is IDs per label part. Anything but plain content is special, including listings (role commits) and snapshots. Specials past the bound refuse the rotation. Plain content past it, received last first, goes into the rotator's later listings (`World::overflow`). A per-thing list over the bound is bad input and lists nothing. Since listings count against the role bound, siblings' listings can refuse a removal or a merge. With the model's bounds of 1 or 2 that is common (17,511 worlds). A refused merge leaves a removed node's fork standing, and those worlds' final readings are not checked. At real sizes (`MAX_LIST_BYTES`) it needs one listing per node per label per rotation past the bound.

9. **P's side.** "P's nodes side with the line D's roster authorizes." Choice: node 0's newest rotation whose chain the node holds whole. Siding with a held rotation whose parent has not arrived seeds nothing, and a removed node's line can then void the removal in that node's view, so it lists by the wrong cutoff.

10. **Which epoch a merge signs at.** The plan does not say. Choice: the rotator's newest counting open epoch (`Builder::epoch_of`), which can be a sibling's (see 1a). A merge signed at a removed node's fork epoch can never reject that fork.
