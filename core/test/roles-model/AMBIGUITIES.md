# roles-model: ambiguities
> Summary: Where issues/account-signing.md (the decided plan) or the spec left a choice while extending the epochs model, and the model's choice: what closes a sibling's concurrent epoch, how parts and chains travel, cutoffs, listings, bound, side and merge signing.

Each entry: the text, the problem, the model's choice (`src/epochs.rs`), and its status.

1. **A sibling's concurrent rotation boxes a removed node.** A sibling S that rotates before node 0's removal of X reaches it boxes X its new epoch e_S (X is on D's roster in S's view). Closing only what a rotation supersedes leaves e_S open after the removal, so X signs there after its removal, forks from there, and node 0's merge, S's listings and witnesses' addenda list what X wrote there. Choice, in the plan: (a) a counting rotation closes every epoch that does not descend from it (through the epoch each rotation is signed at and the forks it names), so the removal closes e_S, and concurrent rotations close each other's epochs until a merge; (b) one cutoff for every lister: another party's commit at epoch e is listed only if received before the earliest rotation closing e (the removal, for e_S), so the merge's list, S's listings and witnesses' addenda leave out X's writes there; (c) a merge is signed at each fork it names, and a rotation signed at e rejects nothing on a line through e, so once node 0's merge (signed at e_S too) is held, X's fork from e_S cannot reject the removal, at every observer, outsiders included. Without (c) the post-merge contest is a dispute that outsiders on the default side may give to X (the removal-stands check fails in about 5,000 of 200k random worlds). Status: closed. Remaining, counted: before node 0's merge arrives, X's fork from e_S rejects the removal at that reader, as node 0's merge rejects a fork X makes from the removal's own epoch: the two DAGs are one with the roles swapped, so no rule outsiders can evaluate tells them apart (fork window). Readers that never place the removal count X's writes at e_S (unaware). Honest cost: writes on either side of two concurrent rotations read invalid at a reader holding both until the merge and their authors' listings at the merged epoch. Minimal reproducer: `removed_node_holding_a_sibling_epoch`; family C.

2. **Parts: "applies once its parts for held labels are held".** Text: "the copy under L carries L's part and applies once its parts for held labels are held (spec/commits.md §4)". With Merkle-hidden parts (Settled items) a reader cannot tell which labels have a part, so it cannot know which to wait for. Choice, in the plan: nothing waits on a part. The rotator uploads a label's part with the rotation; each per-thing list is its own item and joins when it arrives; a rotation counts on its header. Counted: a listed write reads invalid while its rotation counts and its list has not arrived (`part_pending`). Nothing is lost by the end.

3. **Who carries a rotation, and its per-thing lists, into a label the rotator does not open.** Plan: "A label the rotator did not open gets no part"; "per-thing lists are chain certificates and travel where their commits go", without saying who carries them. Choice, in the plan: every node of P places under each label it opens the rotations and per-thing lists it holds for things there, as the certificates its own commits and listings need (they are signed at epochs descending from the rotation). A label no honest node of P opens any more never learns the rotation: the unaware-reader limit. The model delivers rotation headers to every reader and a rotation's list for a thing to every reader of that thing (`Builder::recv`): the rule with some honest node of P opening every label.

4. **Listings travel like rotation parts.** Plan: "per label it opens, a role commit at a counting epoch naming…". It does not say whether a listing's per-thing lists also travel as certificates into the thing's other labels. Choice: yes. A listing is per thing (`Kind::Listing`) and reaches every reader of the thing: one mechanism for lists.

5. **"Naming what nothing it holds names".** Choice: names in a counting list (a counting rotation's list, or a listing that counts as P's). A name in a void fork's list, or in a listing at a closed, unlisted epoch, does not count as naming.

6. **Cutoff for others' commits.** One cutoff for every lister (entry 1b): "received before the earliest counting rotation it holds closing that epoch". A member that has not yet received node 0's removal counts a removed node's fork as the closer. Its cutoff is then the fork's `ts`, which the removed node picks, so the member lists the removed node's writes up to it. Choice: literal. This is counted with the stated unaware-sibling limit (`listed_by_unaware_closer` includes listings). The oracle's upper bound accepts legitimate commits saved that way. A witness answering rotation r takes the earliest closer on r's line, so a removed node's backdated fork cannot cut its receipts against node 0's rotations.

7. **Re-added node.** "Every commit of a re-added node" includes its writes from while it was removed: the re-grant rule (spec/permissions.md §5.2, "including those in the gap"). Choice: yes. Every member lists them, and the re-added node reads every rotation through D once back. Counted: such writes revived only by the post-re-add word (`hostile_revived_by_readd`).

8. **Bound.** Choice: the bound is IDs per label part. Anything but plain content is special, including listings (role commits) and snapshots. Specials past the bound refuse the rotation. Plain content past it, received last first, goes into the rotator's later listings by the ordinary cutoff (it was received before the rotation). A per-thing list over the bound is bad input and lists nothing. Since listings count against the role bound, siblings' listings can refuse a removal or a merge. With the model's bounds of 1 or 2 that is common (14,926 worlds in 1M). A refused merge leaves a fork standing (a removed node's, or an honest sibling's, with no epoch reopening), and those worlds' final readings are not checked. At real sizes (`MAX_LIST_BYTES`) it needs one listing per node per label per rotation past the bound.

9. **P's side.** "P's nodes side with the line D's roster authorizes." Choice: node 0's newest rotation whose chain the node holds whole. Siding with a held rotation whose parent has not arrived seeds nothing, and a removed node's line can then void the removal in that node's view, so it lists by the wrong cutoff.

10. **Which epoch a node signs at.** The plan does not say. Choice: the newest counting open epoch it holds; with none open (concurrent rotations close each other's), the tip of its side's line (`Builder::epoch_of`). A merge thus signs at node 0's own line, plus each named fork's epoch (entry 1c); signed at a removed node's fork epoch it could never reject that fork. A removed node signs at the newest epoch it was boxed, open or closed (`Builder::epoch_known`).

11. **Listings with no open epoch.** Plan: "a role commit at a counting epoch". A listing at a closed epoch counts only once a rotation lists it, and no merge's cutoff admits it (it postdates the closer), so such listings only pile up (each names the last, past the bound). Choice, in the plan: a node lists only at an open epoch and otherwise waits for the merge.

12. **Acting on a rotation without its chain.** Plan: "every writer of a thing answers every rotation of P it holds", "P's nodes merge out every fork they receive". A rotation whose parent a node lacks cannot be verified, and its line (hence the cutoff, and the epoch a merge must sign at) is unknown: a witness answering one listed a removed node's writes against a later rotation of node 0, and node 0 merging out a fork whose root (a sibling's epoch) had not arrived signed the merge without it, so outsiders read a dispute. Choice, in the plan: nobody answers, merges out or sides with a rotation before holding its chain (`World::verified`), and nobody lists a commit before holding its epoch's chain.

13. **When an observer knows a removal.** The safety check applies at observers holding the removal with its chain (a rotation without its parent is void, and a sibling's concurrent rotation is no sign of the removal). Others are unaware readers.
