# Rotation model: assumptions
> Summary: What Rotation.tla abstracts away from spec/crypto.md §3–§5, spec/sync.md §2/§5 and spec/permissions.md §3–§4, and why.

- **One actor, one role rotated: read.** Its label sits at one blind server. Write and admin secrets are never rotated, so step signatures (parent's delete key) and stamps always verify. Rotating read is what decides who opens the label (sync key, claim keys, wraps).
- **Crypto is access.** `sec[x]` = epochs whose read secret x holds from an applied valid box. A rotation's boxes are `ep[e].ob`; grant and re-seal commits are records in `bx` (member, epochs boxed, wrap epoch); history box = `ep[e].hist`, the established secrets the rotator held. Sealed vs symmetric box forms, KEM keysets and keyset epochs (departures *inside* a member) are not modeled: every member is a single node with one static key. So the departed-rotator rule (an epoch written by a departed party does not cover its departure) is implied (a rotator boxes itself) and the keyset judgement (a node holding no secret of M judges by M's newest `/keys`) does not arise.
- **Box knowledge is per node** (`Holders(x, e)`): the boxes of the rotation commits x applied and of the grant and re-seal commits x read. Coverage, the re-seal trigger, claim sets and the rotation-by-inbox test all use it.
- **Planted boxes** (crypto §4): an unfair, bounded adversary step `Plant` gives a member a secret of an existing epoch from a box a non-member sealed to it in a pending or invalid commit (`fsec`). Forged secrets open nothing (the real secret differs); the node may read with them but `Enc(x)`, the secrets it encrypts under, excludes them. `leakP` records any wrap, box, history box or step signed under a non-valid secret.
- **Established epochs** (crypto §4): `Est(x)` = epoch 0 plus every rotation x holds (`kn`) whose superseded epochs are all established; own rotations count at once. Current, newest, encrypting under, history boxes, re-registration and re-seal all range over it; opening and reading use any held secret. No `/secrets` compaction snapshots: every established epoch is chain-established, and the post-compaction residue (notes/sync.md Limits) is out of reach (AMBIGUITIES.md R12).
- **Epoch ids are nonces**: epoch `k` is the k-th rotation; ties in `n` break by lowest id. Forks arise from concurrent rotations.
- **Members** = `Admins ∪ Readers`; those not in `Late` are boxed at epoch 0, `Late` ones join by an unfair `Grant` (boxes at the granter's held current epochs, delivered by inbox; the grant commit goes under the label wrapped under the granter's newest epoch, and other nodes learn the member and its boxes from it). Only `Remover` removes, only members of `Departing`. Demotion and `until` are not modeled (`until` is out of re-seal scope by permissions §3).
- **Removal is a rotation** (commits §2): one commit, carrying the removal (`rem`), boxing the remover's known members minus the departed one, readable under `WrapOf(k)` = newest superseded epoch (crypto §5). Receiving it teaches the removal. The remover uploads it before any other rotation it holds.
- **Registration** (sync §5): an admin that holds the head's epoch and a newer established one first uploads every rotation commit it holds that the server lacks, then registers its newest epoch; its claim set is the epoch's box holders as the global `ep` shows. Steps never fork (one server). Steps without a rotation (the MAY in sync §2) are not modeled.
- **Open** is one atomic check (`CanOpen`): a sync key held for some step on the path, then for every later step either its sync key or a listed, not-dropped claim key. The n round trips and step verification are folded into that check.
- **Rotation by inbox** (permissions §3): a rotation is also sent by inbox to each member it boxes that, as the rotator knows, holds no box at any epoch the rotation supersedes.
- **Delivery**: `InOrder = TRUE` is one server read in log order (cursor sync). `InOrder = FALSE` allows any order and lets an online member peer serve the rotation commits it holds (range reconciliation, a second server, a peer).
- **Re-seal** (a SHOULD, treated as done: fair) only by an admin that has completed a full pass (R8): nothing readable left at the server (rotations and grants) and at every online member peer it has received from. A re-seal goes by inbox and under the label (wrapped under the re-sealer's newest epoch, so every reader learns its boxes; the re-sealed member usually cannot open it there). Re-seal and grant boxes, and their wraps, only at epochs covering every departure the node knows (boxing and wrapping are encrypting under); a needed re-seal at a non-covering epoch makes rotation forced. The manual re-share (an app action) is not modeled.
- **Inbox delivery** is reliable and needs no label access; it gives the boxed epochs' secrets and their history boxes.
- **Stranded heartbeat** is placed (by deposit, admins see it at once; wrapped under the newest established secret held from a valid box, none: no heartbeat) by a member that does not know its own removal when it reached the head but cannot open its epoch and can download nothing more, or when a claim on the way is refused (`~CanOpen`). With `Refresh = TRUE` every rotation refreshes the deposit seed and the deposit is refused unless the node holds the rotation commit the head registers (it has read that `/keys`).
- **Content** is one record per write: the epoch written under and the departures the writer knew. Writes happen only while the writer's newest epoch covers every departure it knows (otherwise `RotateForced` is enabled).
- **Claim-set drop** after `MEMBERSHIP_KEEP` is an unfair server step on any superseded step (time abstracted).
- **Liveness under weak fairness** of forced rotation, registration, upload, download, grant receipt, re-seal, heartbeat and inbox receipt. Eager rotation, removal, grants, writes, claim-set drops and going offline are unfair. `MaxE` bounds rotations, so a forced rotation past the bound cannot happen; no liveness instance needs one (their only departure is a removal, which is itself the rotation).
