# A commit's signer can delete its own blobs at stores whatever their purge-by
> Summary: No node checks *which* key a header lists at a delete level (spec/sync-hostile.md §2), so a non-admin signer puts its own key at level 1 and deletes its fixed-`admin` parts (a may-grant holder's grant, a `secrets.0` box, `created.0`, `addenda.0`) at nodes keeping S unread, also after demotion; junk there blocks foreign thing admins. Tess accepted the step-key design's side split (notes/write-path-design.md F6), but the design fails on its own: a thing made independent keeps derived-era content listing its former base's step keys, which its new readers cannot place (they do not read the former base). Needs a design that puts those keys in the thing's own unit, or the stated limit.
> Priority: low
> Tags: security, sync, tess-call

**What the hole is.** Levels already follow purge-by: a header lists exactly the levels a delta's purge-by calls for, and fixed aspects must carry their fixed purge-by (`Header::validate`, spec/wire.md §4). So "an author-level key on an admin-only aspect" is already refused. The hole is the key's identity: an `admin` part lists one level-1 key, and nobody checks that it is the thing's admin key. Listing its own key lets the signer delete the part at stores using only S's sync key. Linked blobs widen it: a link's delete keys are "its writer's claim, never checked when reading" (spec/data-model.md §6), and pages of role-class deltas are linked blobs. So a check on the header alone leaves pages and chunks open.

**Mizzen's two-part fix, judged:**
- Part 1 (readers judge levels against the aspect's purge-by setting): there is no purge-by setting. Purge-by is per delta, fixed for role-class, `created.0` and `addenda.0`, and the writer's choice elsewhere (spec/commits.md §5). Levels are already checked against it. So part 1 adds nothing.
- Part 2 (stores check admin-level deletes against S's step delete keys): a store cannot tell which thing a blob serves, so it cannot know when level 1 means S's actor. Level 1 is the delta's thing's delete base, which is often not S's actor (things in S's sync unit with their own admins, spec/sync-judgement.md §1). Checking only S's steps removes those admins' right at S's stores (requirements Deletion: "anyone who ever held the right"). Keeping the commitment route as well leaves the hole open. For S's actor, S's current admins can already delete any entry with the head's delete key (`DeleteItem::sign`). So part 2 adds only "any step's delete key", which broadens deletes to removed admins.

**The design that closes it** (worked through, not built):
- A role secret's delete key becomes its step key: level 1 = `kdf("sync delete", admin)`, level 3 = `kdf("sync write", write)`. Key-actor bases already reuse their signing key this way. `keys.0` keysets drop `delete_key`.
- A writer lists the matching key of the newest step its `sync.0` records for the level's base. Readers hold every step for good, so a reader checks each level-1/3 key against the steps that `sync.0` records for things up the delta's record chain. For a key-actor base it checks the signing key (an account: some epoch of its chain). A key it cannot place yet leaves the part invalid until the step arrives, as with a purge author's chain.
- Readers also check every link's delete keys against its delta's keys: levels 1 and 3 must be equal, and level 2 follows the reuse rule of spec/sync-ids.md §1. Pages are checked before apply. A chunk link that fails reads as absent.
- Stores do not change. A commit with a wrong key is invalid everywhere, so the signer deleting it loses nothing, and junk keys only fill caps (an existing accepted bound). Part 2 is not needed.
- Level 2 stays unchecked. It is the purge author's right, which the signer holds itself (it holds P's write secret to sign as P), so any key it lists only passes on its own right. Readers of t do not generally hold P's `sync.0`.

**Why it stops here (the tradeoff):**
- **Forks.** Whether a step counts depends on which `sync.0` writes are valid, and that depends on the trust set. In a permanent admin dispute (notes/principles.md, Forks are permanent), a writer on side X lists X's newest step. On side Y that step's `sync.0` write is a removed admin's, so it is invalid, and every commit the writer makes is invalid there. Today it is valid on both sides while the writer holds write on both. Accepting any step that verifies from genesis would avoid this, but readers would then judge by which steps they happened to see. So: either content splits by side in a dispute, or validity depends on what a node holds.
- Smaller costs: commit validity gains an input from other things (the bases' `sync.0` and account chains up the record chain, re-judged when they move). After a rotation, writes keep the previous step's keys until an admin issues the next step, which is no worse than today (the store's head is that step anyway). Every link and page gets a delete-key check.

**Decided (Tess, 2026-11-22):** the side split is intended: a party is on one side and its writes go to that side (notes/write-path-design.md §1 F6). So the fork cost above is no objection.

**Why the design still does not hold: derived-era content.** The design assumes every reader of a thing holds the `sync.0` of every thing up its record chain. False for a thing made independent:
- A dependent thing t uses its independent M's delete keys (spec/crypto.md §7 Delete base), so every delta written on t before it became independent lists M's step keys. Making t independent keeps that content as it is (spec/permissions.md §3 Own secrets add only; history boxes carry the derived secrets, spec/crypto.md §3).
- t becomes independent so that it can be granted on its own: a new holder B reads t through t's sync ID and is not entitled to M (spec/sync-judgement.md §1). M's `sync.0` goes only under M's sync IDs (spec/sync-ids.md §2), and B could not judge M's timeline anyway.
- So B cannot place M's keys: every derived-era delta of t is invalid at B and valid at M's readers. That is validity depending on what a node reads, the very thing the step check was meant to avoid, and B loses all of t's content from before it was shared (share one document out of a folder: the recipient sees none of its earlier writes).
- Also open in the design as written: a commit whose keys are placed by steps another of its own era records can support itself in a cycle (C2 lists s3, C3 lists s2, a third recording of s3 later invalid), so the check must read steps recorded at points before the commit (as roles are read at the commit's point), which the design did not say.

**What could close it** (not modelled): put the former base's keys in t's own unit when t becomes independent. The rotation making t independent records, by its admin's word, the steps of its delete base it holds (public parts only), and readers check level-1/3 keys against the steps of the delta's delete base alone (its own and those it took over), at points before the commit. Cost found so far: a writer that, concurrently with t's independence, lists a step of M that the rotator did not hold is invalid everywhere (an honest write lost), unless an admin of t that reads M adds later steps of M it sees listed: a duty, which is a carve-out smell. The other answer is the limit, with requirements Deletion reading "anyone who ever held the right, or the data's own signer"; it is bounded: only the signer, only over commits it signed, holders log each back once (spec/sync-judgement.md §4), and nothing a reader holds is lost.

**Designs that rely on a party's writes being valid on both sides** (to rewrite under F6 whichever way this goes): notes/disputes.md, Storage under a fork (model-checked, not built): "members carry clean bodies across" (a common member's later writes are fork-dependent under F6, so it carries none of them), "cover only under snapshots valid on both sides" (no snapshot made after the fork is), and the Testing oracle "Other things read the same on both sides" (a thing outside the dispute whose derived-era content lists the disputed thing's step keys splits by side under the step design).

**Overlap with issues/demoted-writer-deletes-at-stores.md:** part 2 does not close it. That issue is about a demoted writer's own level-2 key with S's sync key, and part 2 touches only level 1. The design above would remove that issue's stated residue (a writer that put its own key at level 1), so its option 1 (level 2 needs the head's write key) would then close it completely. Option 1 stays its own small change.

## Direction (coordinator)
The step a write lists is evidence for that write, so it rides with the write by A2's one mechanism (evidence per sync unit: what a reader needs to judge a held write rides down in the unit holding it), the way chain certificates do: the listed step with its path back to the delete base's first step (each step verifies under its prev's delete key, spec/sync-ids.md §6). A reader of t that never read M then holds the steps of M that t's derived-era content lists, judges them alike, and needs no copy duty at independence. Steps accepted only at points before the commit (no cycle). Built with A2 (notes/redesign-plan.md A2); the demoted-writer issue's option 1 then closes it.
