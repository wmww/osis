# Rethink "a group never looks inside its member groups" (depth 0)
> Summary: Tess doesn't recall choosing depth 0 and leaves it to us: remove it or replace it with whatever rule makes most sense. Today one rule bends it (a node holding a member group's keys and knowing someone left won't hand out that group's old key or write under a parent key passing it on; notes/crypto.md, Blocked through holder actors), and it still leaks when a second admin of the parent cannot read the member group (issues/blocked-walk-stops-at-readers.md, models `D0_readers_g2`, `D0_above_g2`; candidate "mark" needs a wire field). Fold into the keys redesign (issues/design-coherence.md B) where natural; state the principle first.
> Priority: normal
> Tags: keys, security

## What rotating-without-a-secret says (branch keys-rotate-free)
The principle behind the actor's own epochs: a node never waits for what it lacks; it supersedes what it cannot open, boxes who it can, and whoever holds the rest hands it on (history adds). Carried to holder actors it says:
- **Seal never waits.** The one rotation that still cannot be made now is one that would box a holder actor M whose anchors fork (`Seal::Wait`; a removal goes alone then). Its analogue of "supersede unread": leave M out and let M's standing boxes stand (a rotation already leaves standing the boxes at epochs it cannot open), so the anchors survive the rotation and M is re-sealed at a keyset descending from them once one exists. Anchors would then be read from every standing box to M, at current and superseded epochs alike; a rotation removes M's old boxes only when it boxes M again. Model: `D0_fork_out` failed because leaving M out dropped the anchors; with standing boxes it should pass. Not built here: it changes Anchors, Seal and the box-removal rule together (one change, three rules) and the fork-anchor instances are the slow ones.
- **The blocked-walk leak is not answered by it.** `D0_readers_g2` and `D0_above_g2` leak because a second admin of the parent that cannot judge the member's keyset re-seals the member at the keyset a knowing rotator left it out at; standing boxes make that worse (they are exactly the anchor the ignorant re-sealer takes). The fix that holds in the model is "mark" (the rotation records the keyset it left the member out at; Seal takes only a keyset listing it): a small wire field, and the natural reading of "left out" under this principle (a box that says "not at this keyset" rather than no box). If depth 0 stays, build mark; if depth 0 goes (a parent reads inside its members), the walk needs no mark but every admin of the parent must hold every member's keys, which is the cost Tess's study avoided.
- Keep depth 0 as the principle ("a departure below a holder actor reaches the actor only as that actor's new keyset") and state the two bends as consequences of what a node holds: it judges what it can open, never past that.
