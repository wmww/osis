# Storage audit: every stored row has an owner
> Summary: Input to issues/reference-graph.md (Storage), which owns the design: an audit, written without Tess's review, of the row families on main that outlive what the node syncs, and how each would hang off an owner (a thing the node closure holds, a store entry of a synced sync ID, a peer record, or the node root). S1's own-things and decline arms pass, the grantee checked only flat (items 15, 16); the grant arm is ignored for item 18 (its `ou/` bounded at a row per round, item 17). Check each row before relying on it.
> Priority: normal
> Tags: sync, engine, retention

Tess's principle: every stored row is attributable to what the node holds, and releasing everything drops storage to the node root and the account's own things. Nothing may wait for a release's writes to be held elsewhere (issues/reference-graph.md, Principles): a statement here that implies it is wrong.

## Owners
Every durable row and blob has an owner; a row lives exactly while its owner does and goes in the transaction its last owner goes. Owners:
- **Whole things**: the node closure (issues/reference-graph.md: what the node root reaches by strongs). Such a thing keeps all it holds: content, role-class aspects, secrets, derived rows.
- **Store entries**: the entries of each sync ID in the sync set (or leaving it), which the required-set judgement (spec/sync-judgement.md §2) already limits to what S justifies: sync unit bodies, lasting headers, certificates of the chains S's things need, deposits. Engine data taken from an entry that is about no whole thing (another account's chain, a destroyed dependency's record and requests) is owned by the entries carrying it.
- **Peer records**: what a connection proved (a Prove's rotations, presence, charges, filters), owned by the record, and the record by its members (the sync IDs both share) or by being a node of this account.
- **The node root**: identity rows (`seed`, `hlc`, `account`, `u/`, `d/`) and the account root, held unconditionally.

The links between owners are counts over acyclic structure, so no walk beyond the closure is needed:
- entry → blob: `X/ b s` (the store keeps a blob while an entry names it);
- blob → commit: `bp/ c b`, `cb/ s b` (a commit is held while a part of it is on a whole thing, or a held blob of it was taken from an entry, or it is an own commit not yet held elsewhere);
- commit → thing: a thing is **known** while a held commit has a part on it or a held record blob names it;
- thing or commit → derived rows: keyed owner-first, dropped with it.

Cycles exist only in the closure (strongs), which the node closure's walk handles. Authority needs (record chains, holders' and authors' chains) need no walk of their own: what a held thing needs to verify travels as certificates in the required set of the sync IDs carrying it (spec/permissions.md §4), so it is owned by those entries and goes with the last.

## Exceptions on main and how each would be covered
Checked on main (2b5ac0318) unless marked "to confirm".
1. **Role-class state of unsynced things.** `retention::forget` with `Scope::Local` keeps `created.0`, `retention^.0`, `roles.0`, `keys.0`, `sync.0` and `secrets.0` (`forget::KEPT`, `Fate::Local`), so their role commits, headers, `node/metas`, `node/edges`, `node/keysets`, `node/epochs`, `node/walks`, thing lists (`tl/`, `tn/`, `tb/`), `g/`, `dw/` and secrets (`k/`) stay. Role commits are forgotten only by destruction (`retention/destroy.rs`) or as covered (`retention/mod.rs`). Covered: the thing is no longer whole; its role commits stay only where an entry still carries them (a certificate another synced S needs), else they go with the content. Built (`retention/needed.rs`): a thing released or never in the closure, whoever's, is kept only while a held thing needs it, record and secrets included (engine `closure::own_things_are_released_whole_spec_sync_set_3`).
2. **Blob rows after their entries went.** A held commit's `bp/` rows (keyed commit then blob) outlive every entry of a blob. Closed for per-sync-ID copies: a copy is sealed for its sync ID's thing alone, so before S's shred the engine drops its notes of S's copies no other sync ID's entry or tombstone names (`Store::alone_under`, `Engine::shredding`; notes/sync-leave.md). A shared blob's note stays its held commit's (bounded by held commits): a peer still listing it is how a commit dropped as invalid is found again (sync `growth::a_write_valid_again_*`), so it cannot go with the local entries alone.
3. **Kept after a share ended**: gone with shared here (demolition); its `sh/` marks went with it.
4. **Plain things leaving every closure** (to confirm by the test below). `forget_local` names only the things of stopped sync IDs and those under their secrets, so a thing without secrets of its own that leaves the closure while the sync ID that carried it stays (its sync unit no longer holding it) loses its entries there but keeps its engine state and commits. Its cleanup today is release by lifetimes, which the plan removes. Covered: it left the node closure, so it is released (issues/reference-graph.md, Holding and release).
5. **Dispute sides** (`sides`: one record, entries for good). Covered: one row per disputed actor, owned by it.
6. **Purge marks** on role-class aspects and `z/` rows of a fallen purge. Content-aspect marks (`p/`, `pn/`, `v/`, `w/`, `ro/`, `f/`) already go with the aspect (`destroy::forget_aspect_rows`). Covered by the thing's ownership; `z/` is found by its commit (key commit-first, as today).
7. **Peer records** (`core/store/src/record.rs`): bounded by `RECORD_BUDGET` (256 MiB) and least-recently-used, not by sync; presence rows outlive membership (issues/presence-outlives-membership.md); a joined link's kept peers (`I/p/`). Covered: a record goes with its last member sync ID unless the peer is a node of this account; presence follows that issue's P1 (a member leaving ends its rows); `I/p/` is owned by the link's target thing.
8. **Suspect repair rows** `I/s/ reporter thing ak` (osis-sync `inbox/suspect.rs`): each goes only when an entry shows it stale, so rows of a thing forgotten here, of a shredded S and of a reporter that never reports again stay for the node's life. Covered: keyed thing first, owned by the thing; a reporter's rows also go when its role on the thing ends.
9. **Tickets an account holds**: one per thing granted to it, held while referenced from its root (issues/reference-graph.md).
10. **Whole-table scans in forgetting** (issues/forget-whole-scans.md: pending rows, `x/` timers, `z/`, `fl/`). Covered by owner-first keys: timers found per commit part (key from the part's bounds, or a per-commit row), pending commits owned by the entries carrying them, `fl/` keyed thing first, `z/` by commit.
11. **Lifetime work rows** (`lw/`, `lh/`, `lp/`): go with the plan.
12. **Set tombstones in the roots' `holds.0` and `tickets.0`**: accepted by spec/requirements.md Scale ("state grows with the number of edits and removals ever made, a few bytes each"). A removed set element stays as a tombstone until the set is reset (spec/crdt.md §5.5), so D's and the node root's snapshots carry one per strong or ticket ever removed: about 66 B per make-and-release round (three strongs and a ticket) in the snapshot records (`c/`) and about 50 B in their blobs. No cover can drop one (notes/crdt.md, No set/bag tombstone GC): it is what keeps a removal final against the add from a node that held it without the removal. A reset carries no value, so it keeps no forgotten name (`fn/`). S1 bounds them at 32 B per removal (`growth::TOMBSTONE_BYTES`).
14. **`cv/` after the trigger's snapshots**: a held part dropped as covered names a coverer later forgotten (the trigger's intermediate snapshot of D's `holds.0`), so `retention::handed` keeps its covers: one row, flat, where a fresh node's part is covered by the final snapshot directly. Bounded by held parts dropped as covered (notes on `handed`); S1 allows it.
15. **A need lost at quiet waits for a complete pass.** A thing that lost a need (a strong removed, `refs::note`) is judged only at the next complete pass's end (`Engine::settle_needs`, from status), and at quiet none comes: the grantee that dropped its accept after its removal keeps the thing's record, role commits and creation commit until something starts a pass. S1's grant arm (sync `growth::a_granted_and_revoked_thing_returns_storage_to_a_fresh_worlds_*`) keeps the last round's thing (flat: each later pass takes the earlier), so S1 checks the grantee only flat. The pass wait is for arrivals (a parent's record before what needs it); a lost need waits for nothing. Cover: judge a lost need at the transaction's end when no pass is in flight, or have status run a pass while the engine has things due (spec/commits.md §5 What a held thing needs says "judged once the pass ends").
16. **An own certificate blob in the grantee's inbox.** After a grant (accepted or declined) and the grantee's removal, the grantee keeps one own certificate blob (no commit) under its inbox's sync ID past `INBOX_LIFETIME`: `E/`, `X/`, `cb/` one row over a fresh world's, flat at 2 and 20 rounds (both grant arms: S1 checks the grantee only flat). What it certifies is to find (a held fire's chain, the removal notice's records?).
17. **Openings under a released thing on held commits.** `ou/` (`sync_ids::opened`) is keyed commit first; a header opening of a held certificate commit of the maker's chain under a released thing's secret (a header-only copy under that thing's sync ID, taken and not noted in `bp/`) stays once the thing is gone: one row per released thing per such commit at the grantee (S1's grant arm bounds it at one per round).
18. **A grantee keeps every thing it was granted and removed from.** The maker's creation commit of each such thing has a part on the maker's D, missing at the grantee, so `needed::needers`' `waiting` makes D (held: the maker's chain) need the thing: its record, role commits and creation commit stay for good, one thing per round in S1's grant arm (ignored for it). spec/commits.md §5 What a held thing needs counts a missing part only as "one the node fetches again"; filtering `waiting` to parts on things the node closure holds closes it (the grant arm then passes, the grantee flat) but fails `holding::a_grant_to_a_holder_its_granter_does_not_sync_reaches_its_readers_*` (Alice no longer holds T1's keys to seal the grant: the test leans on that need for them), so it waits on the holder-grant work: what keeps a holder's current keys at a granter that does not sync it. The shred drops a held commit's openings under things whose record is not held only for the copies it notes (`Engine::shredding`, `opened::forget_unheld`). Cover: openings keyed thing first (or indexed by thing), dropped with the thing (`needed::settle`, `forget`).


Closed by S1's own-things arm (sync `growth::releasing_own_things_returns_storage_to_a_fresh_nodes_*`, default): a content commit whose other parts are missing on things outside the node closure goes once its rest is covered (spec/commits.md §5 Forgetting; it stayed for good with a released thing's creation part); no `f/` row or fall mark (`fm/`) for a thing whose record is not held, and a fall mark goes with its aspect (`destroy::forget_aspect_rows`); a shredded sync ID's blobs of commits not held (forgotten with lost access, entries parked) take their notes (`Engine::shredding`).

Already conforming: own entries of commits forgotten other than by lost access (`CipherHost::forgot`); the sync store's per-sync-ID rows (`Store::shred`: entries, tombstones, path, usage, node log, `changed_at`, `N/`, `F/`, `R/`), `cp/` passes, content aspects and their rows, `hr/`/`hf/`, `fn/`, `cv/`, `ow/`, `ih/`, `T/`, `B/` (with its link), inbox fires (the inbox's history setting), `K/` (a kept-by-choice root).

## Purge tombstones
A lasting header is in the required set of each sync ID whose sync unit holds the purged thing (spec/commits.md §5), so it is kept and synced onward while any synced sync unit carries the thing, and goes with the last. Local purge marks are owned by the purged thing (whole) or the purge commit. A copy of the purged content arriving later through a peer that ignored the purge is accepted, as at a newcomer.

## Test
One count test: a node holding several things (shared with a peer, a purge, an admin dispute, certificates from other accounts, a retained copy, an ended share, an imported contact) releases everything; its store rows and bytes by family must equal those of a fresh node of the same account that synced nothing, and the work counted must be flat between n and 10n of what stays. Re-syncing must read as a node that never left.
