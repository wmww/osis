# read judges a body again after each delivery
> Summary: Node::handle settles every module after each input, and read's `judged` re-runs a body's judgement each time one of its reads moved: several judgements per (node, S, commit) in a run to quiet, about three in ten giving the answer already held. Work per round is flat in history (`growth::flat_work`) and a commit's meta and record are read part by part (`engine::commits`); what is left is re-runs on reads that move under the answer (timelines, peer record rows, own entry rows, heads), and a prune's stamp checks over every absent-row reader of its table.
> Priority: normal
> Tags: sync, performance, tests

## Measured
Scratch probe (an env-gated dump of each judgement's moved reads and whether its value changed) over `growth::a_granted_and_revoked_thing_returns_storage_to_a_fresh_worlds_spec_sync_set_3` (811 deliveries, both arms) and `read::a_start_after_a_session_finds_nothing_owed_at_n_and_10n_spec_sync_judgement_3` (9,166 deliveries):
- grant test: 3,709 judgements, 1,119 giving the answer held (30%); session-start test: 6,084, 1,302 (21%). The accepted grant arm judges about 430-530 bodies a round (both nodes).
- Causes of a no-change re-run, by read moved (a re-run may count under several), grant test: `timelines: into` 292, `store: record rows 0x58` (peer record index scans) 248, `read: blobs at S` 228 (its own row: an entry or presence of another blob of the commit), `store: local rows 0x45` (own entry rows `E/`) 163, heads `H/`/`P/` 119/99 (a head move judges each body under S twice by design: notes/sync-read.md), `index: commits` 100, `index: secrets` 88, `sync: read facts` 59, `index: closure held` 56. Session-start: `timelines: into` 343, record rows 0x58 341, local rows `X/` (by-blob) 198, `index: independent` 151, `streams: known` 101.
- What stays under `index: commits` is read on purpose: a blob carries every part it holds, so whether an entry of S strips (`blob_gone`, `strips`) reads each part the blob carries, S's body or not; a fresh encryption carries every held part of the commit, so `may_log`'s fresh-encryption branch reads them all (`hold_all`).
- cpuprof before this work (instruction-sampled, one thread): `Node::settle` 57% of `growth::`, 61% of `holding::`, 70% of the duties test; `Read::settle` 32-37% of each; the session-start test spends 42% in the per-delivery read step (/home/ai/osis-profiles/pertest-0d2778997).

- A prune of a store table's removal stamps (`memo::Table::prune`) moves no `Effects` reader of another absent row, but `Effects::due` still checks each such reader's stamps once per prune (no judgement): O(absent-row readers of the table) per prune, a prune per about as many removals as the table has stamped rows. Bounding removal stamps by the readers too (a prune per as many removals as there are readers at bucket stamps) would make it O(1) per removal.

## Direction
Take the next causes the same way: per cause, what the judgement reads of that container that moves without its answer (the probe names the key), and narrow the read to what decides the body (fast by construction), never settle less often (a node settles after every input by design: batching would hide the cost from tests, not remove it). Suspects: `timelines: into` (roles at now through `held::Timelines::role_now` stamp every holder's `Into` read: issues/test-time.md, Product levers 2), the peer record index scans (`record rows 0x58`, a prefix read per source), own entry rows read whole where the judgement uses a field. `growth::flat_work` guards history; a count of re-runs per key at quiet would guard this.
