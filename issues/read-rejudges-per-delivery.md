# read judges a body again with the answer it held
> Summary: A judgement re-runs whenever a read under it moves; in the grant rounds about a seventh of read's judgements give the value already held, mostly from `held_blobs`' scan of a commit logged under many sync IDs (`X/` rows), which grows slowly with the sync IDs holding it. Guarded by `read::a_new_sync_id_judges_nothing_again_under_the_others` and `growth::flat_work`'s third count.
> Priority: low
> Tags: sync, performance, tests

`Effects` counts a settle giving the value held as `"{name} unchanged"`, split by the container whose stamp moved under it (`"{name} unchanged <- {container}"`): `costs::take` after a run lists the causes. Guards: `read::a_new_sync_id_judges_nothing_again_under_the_others` (a granted thing's judgements and no-change re-runs the same at n and 10n other sync IDs) and `growth::flat_work` (judgements, store rows read, no-change re-runs per round no more late than early; its one caller is ignored on issues/storage-owners.md).

Grant rounds (`growth::grant_rounds`, held arm, both nodes, per round over ten rounds): 432 to 488 judgements, 60 to 85 giving the value held. What remains, per three rounds early and late:
- `store: local rows 0x58` 33 to 69, the one cause growing with rounds: `Live::held_blobs` (`may_log`'s fallback when S's own entries cannot be logged) scans each blob's `X/` rows, every sync ID naming it, so an account's certificate copy blob logged under every sync ID moves the fallback under each as any other logs or unlogs it. Tried and not landed: the fallback's blobs as a per-commit `Cutoff` row: it removes this cause but re-derives the row at each move for as long as the commit is held, though the judgements reading it take S's own entries by then (+39% store rows read in the expiry arm, +10% own arm). The shape that would hold: a store read of whether some sync ID carrying the body holds an entry of the blob, stamped so that another sync ID's entry coming beside a held one moves nothing.
- Flat: `index: keysets`/`sealed`/`secrets` 42-48 each, `sync: read facts` 36, `timelines: into` 34 (roles at now through `held::Timelines::role_now`), `store: record rows 0x58` (peer record index scans) 33. Reads under `index: commits` are on purpose: whether an entry strips reads each part its blob carries (`blob_gone`, `strips`), and a fresh encryption reads every held part (`may_log`).

Direction: per cause, narrow the read to what decides the body (fast by construction); never settle less often (batching would hide the cost from tests, not remove it).

A prune of a store table's removal stamps (`memo::Table::prune`) moves no `Effects` reader of another absent row, but `Effects::due` checks each such reader's stamps once per prune: O(absent-row readers) per prune. Bounding removal stamps by the readers too (a prune per as many removals as there are readers at bucket stamps) makes it O(1) per removal.

Tried and not landed: narrowing `role_now` to (node, thing) stamps written per edge change (nodes reaching the holder × things the target reaches) saved about 1% of osis-sync `it` but stamps grow with the whole role relation (147 stamps for one grant at 5 members × 20 things, 10,452 at 50 × 200; kept for good): rejected by fast by construction. The shape that would hold: stamp only pairs someone read (per thing, the nodes whose `role_now` on it was read; an edge change intersects them with the nodes reaching the holder). Low priority beside its gain.
