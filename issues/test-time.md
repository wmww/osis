# Test and gate time
> Summary: Targets (Tess): default tests 4x faster without losing coverage that matters (a test should cost milliseconds), and the whole gate in 10 minutes on ostrich, reached by fixing perf, not shrinking sizes. Both are about 2-3x over. Most of the cost is the engine's per-change work under every test and osis-sync's per-delivery settles; this lists where it goes, the levers left and the patterns behind them.

- Engine lib 1,823 G: rotation tests 2-4 G each, mostly their cached `synced`/`granted` fixtures; custody 73 G.
- The every-transaction debug checks (cpuprof of the whole binaries, share of samples; engine `it` / engine lib / osis-sync `it`): `apply::check_states` (the debug rebuild of the aspects a transaction touched) 6.2% / 4.9% / 1.2%; `flows::vouches::missed` (vouch and addendum triggers) 1.0% / 0.8% / 1.2%; `apply::check_rebuilt` 1.0% / 1.1% / 0.4%; `validity::misjudged_on` 0.9% / under 0.1% / 0.4%; `apply::paths::missed` (split-path waits) 0.2% / 0.2% / 0.3%; `check_walks` after a transaction (blob waits) under 0.1% everywhere. None is a large share at default sizes; two are super-linear in a test's length:
  - `vouches::missed` scans every held commit (`ix.commits`, filtered to role commits with a frontier not due) and gathers afresh for each such removal, after every durable transaction: O(transactions x commits), plus a gather per removal per transaction. Proposal for its owners (vouches flow): run it in full only after a transaction that changed a role edge, a role commit's validity or held parts, or passed a removal's window end (the inputs a gathering and `owed` read, independent of the trigger's own filing per thing, which a check filed the same way would share blind spots with), and in full at drop; and keep an index of role commits with a frontier for the scan (cost only). It catches the same misses as long as that input list is the whole of what `gather` and `owed` read: the owners check it against the inputs table (notes on the vouch trigger).
  - `check_walks(false)` rebuilds the whole blob-wait index and reads every lacking blob from the store after every durable transaction while any walk is kept: O(transactions x kept walks' missing pages). Under 0.1% today (few tests keep walks long); a trickling-server test at scale would pay it quadratically. Proposal (blob-wait owners): after a transaction, check the walks the transaction touched or whose blobs arrived in it, and the whole index at drop (`check_walks(true)` already); the lost-wake clause needs only the blobs stored in that transaction.
  - `paths::missed` is O(waiting split-path commits) per transaction: linear in what waits, empty in honest tests: leave it.
- The engine drop's index check (`Engine::check_index`, issues/index-check-rebuild-cost.md): about 23% of engine `it` (about half its load from the store, a third `checked_states`), 3-7% of a sync test.

## Budget
- Gate: 10 minutes wall but its lock wait (notes/time-limits.md, Limits, `gate`), about 8000 CPU-s on ostrich at its usual 85%; each stage's limit is its share (the hold 210 u: 483 s, 7590 CPU-s). Measured: the whole gate about 15 min (8000 CPU-s), the hold 11 min, every stage about 2x its share, builds after a rebase 7x. Other agents' unlocked builds take 25-35% of the box during the hold and count against its wall.
- Ostrich spends about 1.6x the CPU-s per u that fastbox-0 does (notes/time-limits.md, Investigations), so its u limits are tight by that factor until u is timed on real work.
- Default tests alone exceed the hold's budget even with properties and sim at zero.
- Shares moved to the work (Windlass, the coordinator's call; `gate.hold` and `gate` unchanged): `gate.sim` 410 u to 105 u (943 to 242 CPU-s on ostrich; it takes 135-150), `gate.properties` 530 u to 835 u (1,219 to 1,920; an engine change's take 1,880-2,050). Tess may undo it: it makes `gate.properties` hits rarer without making the properties cheaper.
- The default tests run their binaries in turn (cargo), each ending on its slowest tests: 170 s wall for 1,772 CPU-s on a quiet box, about 55 s above CPU/16. Running the binaries side by side (cargo's runner recording each run, as tools/heavy does, then a pool) would close it on a quiet box; under the usual load 20 other agents use those cores, so the gain is mostly theirs.
- Levers that need Tess: the four KNOWN story searches at a smaller fixed setting while the role-vouch decision waits (their `counts:` line can name `OSIS_ROLES_SEEDS`; the family runs have no size knob, a stride is the generator's), up to 1,700 CPU-s of every roles-model gate, at the cost of fewer worlds in which a branch's change to those rules shows as different counts; the drop's index check sampled (10-15% of every engine property), held-model judged after a sample of a clocked order's ingests instead of every one (most of the clocked orders' cost, a quarter to a third of the two order properties), smaller gate sizes (no property's gate size is shown to add nothing), u timed on real work (ostrich's 1.6x per u). And the default tests of only the crates a branch's paths select (the properties' rule, notes/gate.md, The selection): an engine change would skip perm, crdt, store, commit, base, blob, keys, crypto, schema and crdt-model (about 420 G, 135 CPU-s), a sync- or osis-only change also the engine's two binaries (about 3,160 G, 1,000 CPU-s, the gate then about 3,500). It changes CLAUDE.md's gate (every default test at every gate), and a test drawing fresh seeds per run (crdt-model's, osis-keys' proptests) would run only when its crate is selected.

## Levers left, biggest first
0. **Measured across the engine tests** (cpuprof of whole modules, one thread): the drop check `Engine::checked_index` is 17-23% of engine lib and `it` modules (rotation, validity, signing; 4% of osis-sync growth), two thirds of it `apply::load` (KEM opens in `ingest_secrets` and `observe_state` the most; item 4); `LocalPeer::packages_hashed` 9% of engine `it` `validity::` and lib `rotation::`, 4% of `tests_signing` (each changed sender re-asks every held commit: `sent_parts` two thirds, `sync_bodies`' memo gets and `follow_certificates`; `package_with_record` a quarter). Asking `sync_bodies` once per sender state for every receiver's sync IDs moved 0.1-0.2%: nearly every ask follows a move of the sender's `Engine::point` (any transaction or tick), one receiver per point. The lever is per-commit invalidation (a commit re-asked only when an input of its bodies or package moved), which needs the engine to name the commits a transaction or tick moved for `sync_bodies`: a product change, not the harness's; osis-sync's per-delivery `Read::settle` a third of growth and holding (item 2). Seed runners' samples are `_plain` properties, not default tests (notes/testing.md, When tests run): 31 tests, 690 G and 226 CPU-s of the profile (engine `it` 430 G, engine lib 153, perm 82, crdt 26), run only by a gate selecting their crate.
1. **A body's facts derived again per write on its aspect**: a part's date reads the aspect's state row, own covers read the author's newest own replace: O(n) per write. Narrow by stamping dates by the positions read (issues/derived-state-recomputed.md step 4) and checking own covers against the first two covering own replaces after the body.
2. **osis-sync per-delivery work**: a settle of every module per delivery; `follow_certificates` at every follow; a store transaction commit per opened entry. A 3-account `introduce` runs 4,024 judgements (`history::world_of` 2/3/4 accounts: 3.55/11.68/29.36 G). Most re-runs are what a judgement acts on (its blobs at S, presence, its own entries). Of the rest, `may_log`'s body derivation (`body_thing` -> `body_for`, `bodies_under`'s `readable_parts`, `epoch_under`, `thing_sync_now`) reads roles at now through `held::Timelines::role_now`, which stamps every holder's `Into` read, so a role change on any holder of a thing moves every `may_log` under its sync ID while the answer stands: narrow `role_now` to the edges the answer walked, or let `may_log` take read's settled body and body thing as `holds_body` takes the body. `body_things` derives `publisher` again instead of reading `Published`'s row (under 1% of the judgements).
3. **Memos re-run on unchanged answers**: `Core::bound_to_horizon` and `may_rotate` read a component's whole timeline (`Read::Whole`) though their answers rarely change; read the edges walked instead. `body_facts` tries every snapshot ever held of an aspect against each body.
4. **`check_index` has no incremental form** (22% of osis-engine `it`, 10-14% of the engine properties, a quarter of `covered_drops_move_nothing_large`): cheaper loads are the lever, and they are the product's open (issues/long-aspect-cost.md). Its sampling is a Tess call; the narrowest form is the order harness's alike tree ends (a node whose rows, pending, clock and reads equal one already judged: about a fifth of `held_set_decides_exhaustive`), which still differ in the path that built their live indexes.
5. `rotation_holds_large`, `deep_departure_holds_large`: ingest a third, the drop check 12-15%, fresh peers per seed (`LocalPeer::new`, key generation) 10%, the sender's packages rebuilt per moved sender and trimmed per receiver's sync IDs 14% (`sent_parts`, product memo reads after each change, 10%). 10 seeds at one thread: deep 55 G (51 before content waited outside the node closure), rotation 96 G (93). `incremental_equals_from_scratch_large` and perm `incremental::late_writes_across_kept_states`: oracles that replay from scratch per query.
6. Allocation: `Value` trees for epochs and schema reads, fragment clones where a join could consume its side, `kdf_key` formatting its purpose per call.

Rejected: `opt-level` 2 for the workspace (3-10%); fake or memoized crypto in tests (debug and release differ, every forgery test must opt out).

## Patterns
- **A harness loop that cannot tell progress from a repeat**: a pending commit handed again comes back `Pending` every round, so a hand-until-quiet loop counting non-duplicates ran its whole bound (osis-sync `history::reference`: sims 969 to 141 G, differential 683 to 174, flows 288 to 78).
- **Per-receiver work that is per-sender**: `LocalPeer` built every sender package once per receiver's sync-ID set, each decoded again to trim (40-50% of the two rotation properties); build once per sender state, trim per set.
- **A flip-prone input read before the answer needs it**: read it last, on the paths whose answer depends on it; a small map read whole where one key is asked: index it by what is asked.
- **Derived values whose inputs move far more often than their answers**: keep them as `Cutoff` rows or narrow their reads (n readers of an aggregate over n inputs record O(n²) reads per change).
- **Work done twice in one judgement**: one facade value asked from several calls.
- **A restart for a new session**: each restart is an end of history, checked whole; a test of what an operation loads cold drops the cached state instead (`Engine::forget_cached`) and restarts once at the end.
- **The forensic attack**: its cost is candidates (every random-looking window of what it recovered: hundreds of thousands in a sync world) times sealed sections and blobs; a test of what one node must not open runs it on an engine-level world (`flows::a_section_of_a_thing_not_held_leaves_nothing_on_disk`) and checks the sync world by what the node holds.
- **Worlds built from nothing per case**: copy them (notes/test-harnesses.md, Copied worlds); left: tests that close and reopen a node.
- **Width**: a covering set by default, full width through `testenv::large_n` and `_large` twins.
- **Real time waited out** in osis real-runtime tests (300 ms to 2 s timeouts in `runtime.rs`, `port.rs`); quadratic helpers (`apply::keys::tests::spread`).

## Method
Per test or per module: `tools/pertest.py` (`top`, `compare`; `run --modules` for what a gate pays). Whole binaries: `tools/icount <bin> -q`, under `tools/heavy`. Profile: `tools/cpuprof OUT <bin> -q`, or callgrind. Why a judgement re-ran: `osis_base::costs::take()` around the step, read the `"<effects> <- <cause>"` counts (notes/engine.md, Why a key went due). CPU seconds move 20-40% between identical runs: compare instructions.
