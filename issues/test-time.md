# Test and gate time
> Summary: Targets (Tess): default tests 4x cheaper than the 8,553 G per-test reference profile (about 2,140 G) without losing coverage that matters, and the whole gate in 10 minutes on ostrich, reached by fixing perf, not shrinking sizes. The per-test figure is about 6,500 G (1.3x); a third of it is fixtures rebuilt per process. A branch's gate fits the 598 s cap at the median (about 540 s); the full selection (every crate) is about 650 s, every test stage CPU-bound. The ranked levers, the patterns behind slow tests, and the method.
> Priority: normal
> Tags: tests, performance, sync, engine

Each binary's budget is its line in tools/test_cost.txt; each new default test needs a cut beside it. Profiles: `/home/ai/osis-profiles/` (newest `pertest-*`; `tools/pertest.py top` / `compare`).

## Where it goes
- **Per-process fixtures are a third of the per-test figure**: each test process of the three big binaries (osis-sync `it`, engine `it`, engine lib) builds its cached worlds again (`history::world_of`, `LocalPeer::cached`). A gate pays them once per binary, so caching across processes cuts nothing real; making a fixture cheaper cuts both.
- Most of the cost is in tests of 5-20 G (about 300 of them). Heaviest modules: engine lib `rotation`, `apply`; osis-sync `holding`, `deletes`, `growth`, `delete_keys`, `idle`; engine `it` `validity`, `order`, `scale`, `flows`, `shred`.
- **Gate**: the properties are the largest stage (Budget); the engine's rotation and order properties are the costliest. In the engine's lib tests the drop's `checked_index` is 21% and held-writes dating (`date_due`) 18%; both are in every engine property too.

## Levers, by evidence
1. **Cheaper fixtures**: osis-sync's `world_of` (nearly all `introduce`'s redeems over real sync, so mostly lever 2 seen through the fixtures) and engine lib rotation's cached setups; fewer distinct fixtures (tests sharing one world) also count.
2. **osis-sync per-delivery work** (issues/read-rejudges-per-delivery.md): a settle of every module per delivery (`Read::settle` about 40% of a world test after its fixture); `follow_certificates` at every follow; a store commit per opened entry. `may_log`'s body derivation reads roles at now through `held::Timelines::role_now`, which stamps every holder's read, so a role change on any holder moves every `may_log` under the sync ID: narrow `role_now` to the edges walked, or let `may_log` take read's settled body.
   - In osis-sync `it`: `Read::settle` about 27% (`may_log`, `wrap_secret`/`epoch_under`, `read_facts`), the drop's `check_index` 11%, `introduce` 7%, the quiet checks 6%, the differential's `model_now` handing the reference twins each new commit, from every node that holds it (about 5%: two thirds real applies, the rest each node's copy of a commit taken as a duplicate; none refused in the scripted histories).
3. **Debug end checks**: the drop's `check_index` is 17-23% of engine modules (issues/index-check-rebuild-cost.md); its loads are mostly KEM opens. Every-transaction checks with super-linear cost: `flows::addenda::missed` scans every held commit after each durable transaction (run it in full only after a transaction changing a role edge, a role commit's validity or held parts, or passing a removal's window end, and at drop); `check_walks(false)` rebuilds the whole blob-wait index per transaction (check the walks touched, the whole at drop).
4. **Held writes per join** (`osis_crdt::Held::change` over `PagedRows`): about 11% of engine lib `rotation::`, the product's cost of every superseding write. Row reads are about 4 per write, flat in the writes held (positions' tops per kind, read once per change: crdt `a_join_reads_rows_by_what_it_changes_spec_4_8`); what is left is `PagedRows` itself. Profiled over the engine lib's `rotation::tests::` (one thread): `PagedRows` frames are 14% of samples; page `decode` 19% of them (2.7% of the run, nearly all of `page_of`: a page is decoded again in every transaction reading it, since its `PageCache` lives in the transaction, `Tx::held_pages`), `encode` per page written 8% self. `page_of`'s hashing of level prefixes (`level_of`) is 0.13% of the run: no lever. A cache across transactions was rejected (issues/many-things-cost.md, Flat: it saves none of the growth and needs a cache equal to the store under rollback). Left: a cheaper `decode` (a `Vec` per key and value, a copy byte by byte in `unpack`), at most about 1.5% of rotation runs.
5. **`LocalPeer::packages_hashed`** (about 3.5% of rotation runs): a sender's commits are packaged once per change (`whole`: an `Effects` per commit, checked under `memo::check_effects`), but each move of its engine trims every held commit again: `sent_parts` per commit (`sync_bodies` misses as the secrets, sealed and timelines indexes move). Per-commit trimming needs the engine to name the commits a transaction or tick moved for `sync_bodies` (a product change).
6. **A body's facts derived again per write on its aspect**: a part's date reads the aspect's state row, own covers read the author's newest own replace: O(n) per write (issues/derived-state-recomputed.md item 2).
7. **Memos re-run on unchanged answers**: `Core::bound_to_horizon` and `may_rotate` read a component's whole timeline (issues/derived-state-recomputed.md item 3).
8. **The forensic attack** in a sync world (`deletes::a_purged_or_replaced_part_*`): the path it guards exists only in a sync world, so it stays; its cost is candidates times sealed sections (`Trial`'s section key derivation, `Keystream::new`, zeroizing).
9. **A large map written whole** (osis `api::values_past_limits_error`, `a_whole_read_loads_at_most_its_bound`): about 0.5 M instructions per entry of a 10-20k-entry map, half the drop's `checked_index` (`rows_exact_on` streams the part again), half the paged join (`join_part` per page: `set_state_touched`, `rewrite_set`, `Killed::note`). Count per page at n and 10n entries before changing it.
10. **Allocation**: glibc's `malloc`/`free`/`realloc` are 21% of self samples in the engine lib's `rotation::` tests, 10% in osis-sync `it`'s `holding::`/`deletes::`, 13-17% in the engine's top properties. Ours to cut: `Value` trees for epochs and schema reads, fragment clones where a join could consume its side, `kdf_key` formatting its purpose per call. Another allocator: Levers that need Tess.
11. **Each CLI call opens the store**: an open judges every held body again (`Read::settle`, about 40% of osis-cli `cli::named_keys_with_schema_plan_cli_1`) and its close runs the debug whole checks (about 25%), so a test of k calls on a growing store is O(k^2).

Rejected: `opt-level` 2 for the workspace (3-10%); `opt-level` 3 for dependencies (under 1% fewer default-test instructions than 2); fake or memoized crypto in tests (debug and release differ, every forgery test must opt out).

## Budget
- The gate: 10 minutes wall without its lock wait and `build` (notes/time-limits.md, Limits: 598 s, 8,188 CPU-s on ostrich). A branch's selection fits at the median: ostrich's 12 gates to e6647bc3f (load 37-76, none `--all`) took 383-737 s wall (median about 540 s) and 3,000-7,100 CPU-s (median about 5,600, 0.68x). Of the three over: two replayed a floor line that reran a whole property (547 CPU-s, 61-87 s on its own; the line went), one ran at load 76 beside another gate. The full selection (every crate) adds the model crates' properties (all of them about 4,700 CPU-s, `~/.cache/osis/prop-costs`): about 7,800 CPU-s, some 650 s at the 12-13 cores a gate gets, still over. Each test stage runs its targets side by side (`tools/targets.py`), so every stage is CPU-bound at the cores other work leaves; running side by side costs about 15% more CPU-s than one binary at a time (SMT siblings and caches shared), the same instructions. Left on the wall, by size:
  - **The hold** (75-90% of a gate): properties 2,700-3,400 CPU-s / 180-250 s, default tests about 2,400 CPU-s / 170-280 s, sim 150 CPU-s / 15-20 s, replays 5-30 s (the slowest, side by side). Costliest properties (CPU-s): `order::held_set_decides_plain` 619, `rotation_holds_large` 573, `held_set_decides_exhaustive` 502, `deep_departure_holds_large` 332, roles-model `small_family_holds_large` 301 and `random_worlds_hold_large` 219, crdt `stream_check_matches_whole_large` 187, `crdt_equals_model_large` 113.
  - **The drop's `checked_index`** is the largest single cost left (cpuprof): 28% of engine `it`, 21% of the engine lib's tests, 11% of osis-sync `it`, 13% of `held_set_decides_plain` and `rotation_holds_large`, 26% of `deep_departure_holds_large`. Making it cheaper is Tess's (Levers that need Tess, Properties and builds); below it the product's own work (ingest, `date_due` 16-21% of the rotation properties), then the held-model judging after every ingest (Tess's).
  - **Default tests** (5,335 G as one process per module, 2,109 CPU-s; 3,762 tests each alone 2,421 CPU-s, `tools/limits.py tests --part I/3`, one hold each): osis-sync `it` 1,660 G, engine `it` 1,499 G, engine lib 1,030 G, held-model 401 G, osis 382 G. Costliest modules: engine lib `rotation::` 523 G, osis-sync `differential::` 456 G (42 scripted histories on copied worlds, about 11 G each), engine `scale::` 297 G, engine lib `apply::` 295 G, osis-sync `growth::` 285 G, engine `order::` 262 G, osis-sync `holding::` 249 G. Over the `test` limit alone: osis-sync `growth::rotations_keep_keysets_flat_spec_commits_5` (117 G, 16 CPU-s) and `card_rewrites_grow_nothing_spec_commits_5` (60 G, 11 CPU-s), n against 10n growth checks whose every rotation or move is O(n) (Properties and builds).
  - **Outside the hold** (90-170 s): clippy 6-96 s (as much as the change recompiles), `gate.gui-tests` 14-105 s, the tools' tests and policy 14-29 s, `gate.unify` when the manifests changed, deny 3-12 s.
  - The full-size sync differential is not a gate stage: 5,300-5,700 CPU-s, about 8 min wall over four holds.
- A stage still ends on its slowest test: the properties' slowest (`held_set_decides_plain`, `rotation_holds_large`: 300-330 s wall each at the stage's oversubscription) end within the stage's CPU-bound wall; a default test over about 60 s would set the default stage's wall (osis-sync `growth::rotations_keep_keysets_flat_spec_commits_5`, 120 G, is the longest).
- Ostrich spends about 1.6x the CPU-s per u that fastbox-0 does, so its u limits are tight by that factor until u is timed on real work.

### Levers that need Tess
- **Another allocator** (Levers 10: glibc's malloc family is 10-21% of self samples). No well-maintained pure-Rust general-purpose allocator exists; the maintained ones (mimalloc, jemalloc: `mimalloc`, `tikv-jemallocator`) are C built by `cc`, MIT/BSD licensed. Unmeasured: none is on ostrich to preload, and measuring means adding the dependency (a global allocator set in each test binary's `main`, a dev-dependency: no new feature set). Typical gain in allocation-heavy code is a large part of the allocator's share, so about 5-10% of the heaviest binaries. Question: is a C allocator acceptable, for test binaries only or for every build?
- The drop's index check sampled (10-15% of every engine property), or checked whole at a cadence and incrementally between (Properties and builds, `checked_index` incremental).
- held-model judged after a sample of a clocked order's ingests instead of every one (a quarter to a third of the two order properties).
- Smaller gate sizes; u timed on real work.
- Default tests of only the crates a branch's paths select (the properties' rule, notes/gate.md, The selection): an engine change would skip the crates below it, a sync- or osis-only change also the engine's binaries. It changes CLAUDE.md's gate, and a test drawing fresh seeds per run would run only when its crate is selected.

## Properties and builds
- **Where the costliest properties go** (cpuprof): `held_set_decides_plain`: `node_checks` about 22% (`check_evidence` a third of it, `held::compare` judging held-model after every ingest 30%; half of a compare is `convert`, which reads every held body and commit through its own `Engine::read`), pending commits applied as an ingest wakes them (`retry_pending`, about 13%: real applies; `read_pending_boxes` about 2.5%, its `pending_deltas` decode 1.5%: skipping the actors already read for saves nothing, their sealed rows are rarely there), the drop's `checked_index` 15%, held-writes dating (`date_due`). `rotation_holds_large`: the drop's `checked_index` 14%, held-writes dating (`date_due`) 11%, `packages_hashed` 3.5% (Levers 5). `deep_departure_holds_large`: building each seed's world (`tests_deep::world`, its `sync_all`s) is over half, the drop's `checked_index` 17%; each seed draws its own depth, admins and members, so no world is shared. Roles- and held-model searches: set building and walking (commit sets are bitsets, `roles_model::idset`).
- **Held-writes dating** (`leaving::date_due`, 11-13% of rotation runs, 15% of the engine's lib tests) is per body joined or left, not per aspect: in rotation about two thirds is `Held::join` (a `secrets.0` body of many boxes: about 2 ms a join; its page flush and `encode` a quarter, `page_of` navigation 15%, page `decode` 9%) and the debug `whole_date` per row dated 18%. `built_fresh` (4% of lib, under 1% of rotation) is each aspect's first death building its held writes from its bodies (2-6 of them, counted over the lib tests), as cheap as joining them would have been; an aspect built and thrown away with nothing dead is a one-time event (turning dated, a rebuild: about 480 in the lib tests, of at most 4 bodies), never per change, since a dated aspect's dead write keeps its body held until after its dating. Flushing pages once per transaction instead of per change saves nothing measured (172 of 175 settles in three rotation seeds change one body). What is left is the per-row cost of `PagedRows` (Levers 4).
- **`checked_index` incremental** (for Tess; not built): 20% of the engine's lib tests, 14% of rotation. It runs at an engine's drop (and before a reload) when a transaction ran since the last check: O(the engine's state) once per engine that moved, each copied world's peer included. In the lib tests it is the rebuild-and-open loads (`apply::load` twice, 35% of it), the held writes built fresh per aspect touched (`rows_exact`/`index_exact`, 38%: O(the aspect's bodies) per touched aspect) and `check_states` (15%). Options, by what they keep:
  - Whole at a deterministic cadence, touched otherwise: the drops of engines whose transaction count since their last whole check is a power of two (or every 2^k-th drop in a process) run the whole check; the others run the open alone (cheap: no blob read) and `rows_exact` on the positions the transactions wrote. Keeps the missing-row class only at the cadence's drops: the planted missing-`originals` mutant is caught by 16 of 37 tests without the rebuild, so a test running one engine whose drop falls outside the cadence loses it. Saves about the rebuild's half on most drops.
  - Per-record derivation (issues/index-check-rebuild-cost.md): each record a transaction touched re-derives its own rows, compared both ways; the rebuild only at the cadence. Keeps direct-derivation misses at every drop; the arrival-order class at the cadence only. A design of its own (which rows derive from one record alone).
  - Per-aspect cadence for `rows_exact`: an aspect's held writes rebuilt whole at its 1st, 2nd, 4th, ... check, its touched positions compared otherwise. Keeps a wrong row at a touched position always; a stale row at an untouched one is found at the aspect's next whole check, or not if its engine drops first.
- **`LocalPeer::packages_hashed`** (8-12% of rotation and deep-departure runs, 4% of the engine's lib tests): a sender's whole package list is rebuilt at every move of its engine (`whole`: every held commit's `package_with_record`, then `sent_parts` per commit). An `Effects` per commit (as `Peer::offer_fires` keys fires) is the lever, sound only if every read there is stamped, blob reads (`Engine::blob`, `tx.blobs`) included: check under `memo::check_effects(true)` first.
- **A thing rotated n times costs O(n) per rotation in many reads** (osis-sync `growth::rotations_keep_keysets_flat_spec_commits_5`, 23 rotations of every role, 120 G, the default tests' longest). The store keeps each path parsed (`store::path::Paths`) and the engine every recorded step's needs and their inverse (`Engine::steps_needs`), so a path read and a role entry's `step_parent` are O(1). Left, each a derivation rebuilt whole per rotation (O(n) per rotation, O(n^2) per test), by share: read's re-judgement of every role body as the secrets and steps indexes move (`judge::body` 14%, `may_log`/`wrap_secret`/`epoch_under` 7-9%); `Core::unread_derived` 6% (every copy's boxes opened and steps verified per change); `path::earlier` 5.5% (a stamp under no head key checked against every earlier step's write key, both domains: O(n) verifies per refused stamp, each refusal shown again at a head move); `read_credentials_now` 5% and `head_keys_derived` 5% (every step's keys per change of the epochs, each deriving the deposit key from the deposit seed again: 3.5%, the same per sync ID); `verified_depths` 4% (every signature of a step tree per derivation reading it; a step verified once where it enters an index would make it O(change)).
- **The engine compiles twice**: every engine edit compiles osis-engine as a library and again with its 600-odd lib tests, the build's critical path (notes/time-limits.md, Patterns, An engine change). Moving them into `it` saves the lib tests' CPU and at most about 15 s of wall per edit, but is not mechanical: they reach about 1,500 `pub(crate)` items through `super::`/`crate::`, and a second feature set for tests is not allowed (notes/build.md). Tried on osis-crdt (blanket `pub`): no wall saved (crdt's lib tests build off the critical path), and blocked by tests building records field by field, `seq::LIN_MIN` as `cfg!(test)`, and `cfg(test)` checks in the library; each needs its own design. Count what the engine's tests reach before deciding.
- **Builds after a rebase**: main moving under a low crate rebuilds every dependent's test binaries (up to 1,700 CPU-s, where `build`'s limit sits).

## Patterns
- **Per-whole work redone per member**: a commit's reading built again for each part (`part_valid` in a loop instead of one `Judge`), a search from a root over every thing it minted per thing asked: linear at small n, 10-40x at a requirement's 2,000.
- **A harness loop that cannot tell progress from a repeat**: a pending commit handed again comes back `Pending` every round, so a hand-until-quiet loop runs its whole bound.
- **Per-receiver work that is per-sender**: build once per sender state, trim per receiver.
- **A harness reading a whole engine per step while it stands**: key what it read by `Engine::point` (rotation `Run::note`, `LocalPeer`'s `SyncMemo::needed`, `duplicate`); a copy keeps the points of what it copies.
- **The engine's fixed cost per `Engine::read`**: a mark and rollback of every journal (`Core::mark`, `rollback_to`, `walk_held`), about 2-3% of the order properties: a harness asking one aspect per read pays it per aspect (`order::shown`, `get_within`).
- **A flip-prone input read before the answer needs it**: read it last; a small map read whole where one key is asked: index it.
- **Derived values whose inputs move far more often than their answers**: `Cutoff` rows or narrower reads (n readers of an aggregate over n inputs record O(n²) reads per change).
- **A restart for a new session**: each restart is an end of history, checked whole; a test of what an operation loads cold uses `Engine::forget_cached` and restarts once at the end.
- **The forensic attack** per test: a test of what one node must not open runs it on an engine-level world (`flows::a_section_of_a_thing_not_held_leaves_nothing_on_disk`) and checks the sync world by what the node holds.
- **Worlds built from nothing per case**: copy them (notes/test-harnesses.md, Copied worlds).
- **Width**: a covering set by default, full width through `testenv::large_n` and `_large` twins.
- **Real time waited out** in osis real-runtime tests (`runtime.rs`, `port.rs` timeouts).
- **Controls run over the whole sample**: a control (a rule undone, which must fail its check) needs one failing world, not a tally: search it until one fails (held-model `par_any`, roles-model `family_any`); the design still runs every world.
- **One fixed world asked many alike questions**: a model search judging many views of one world judges each once (roles-model `judging_once`, around each world's checks).
- **A named history at a generator's size**: a hand-written order history checked over the property's shuffles, held sets, clocks and reopens (`ORDERS`) where its case is in the named orders or the partial held sets: `NAMED` plus what the case needs, chosen by taking its fix out and seeing which config still fails (order's two pend-again tests and the fallen-purge leftover: 12-16 CPU-s each to 1-2).
- **A floor test beside a scaling test of the same shape**: one test, its scaling check at a tenth of the floor and at the floor (osis `commits::changes_on_as_many_things_spec_requirements_decentralization`: 203 -> 99 G).
- **Large single tests**: split rather than slimmed when their work is spread evenly (the judgment vectors: one test per file; the named order checks).

## Method
Per test or module: `tools/pertest.py` (`top`, `compare`; `run --modules` for what a gate pays). Whole binaries: `tools/icount <bin> -q`, under `tools/heavy`. Profile: `tools/cpuprof OUT <bin> -q`, or callgrind. Why a judgement re-ran: `osis_base::costs::take()` around the step, reading the `"<effects> <- <cause>"` counts (notes/engine.md, Why a key went due). CPU seconds move 20-40% between identical runs: compare instructions.
