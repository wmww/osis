## Rules (spec/crypto.md §4 and spec/permissions.md §3 after the change)
1. **Seal**: a rotation, grant or re-seal of A seals holder actor M, per role, at the newest (n, nonce) valid keyset of M the node holds that is or lists (transitively, through held `keys.0` commits of keysets valid at the node: a forged keyset lists everything) every valid keyset the boxes to M at A's current epochs name (its anchors). None: M is left out of that role and re-sealed once one exists.
2. **Stale**: a box to M is stale when its keyset is held invalid or a held valid keyset of M lists it. A node MUST NOT encrypt under an epoch of A with a stale box; it rotates first. An unknown keyset is not stale. Not "any newer keyset": that moves A onto an ignorant fork (study §4).
3. **Merge**: a node that may rotate M rotates while any current epoch of M (not only the newest) misses a departure it knows. Without it an ignorant fork that loses the (n, nonce) tie stays current and A, anchored on it, never moves.
4. **Cycles**: no key cycles. Actor grants have an agreed order (total, consistent with causality). A node takes the grants it holds in that order and gives one key access only if the holder's key does not already yield the granting actor's over the keyed grants before it: the earlier grant wins, the later reads nothing. Keyless grants are not sealed to. A node that may rotate A rotates while a current epoch of A boxes an actor its held grants give no key access (the knowing re-key). The first admin's implicit grant (notes/owner-removal-plan.md step 2) counts as a grant at the thing's creation and so always wins (study §4).
   - **Hidden grant** (model-checked, study §4): a sealer missing the earlier grant takes the later one as keyed and seals into a key cycle; a device removed from X then reads X's new content through Y. Plain rule 4: until every admin of Y holds the grant and re-keys Y and X rotates onto it (a third party's sync; unbounded if a host withholds the grant).
   - **4b, taint (taken, Wren: an endless leak under a withholding host is worse than re-keying while it withholds)**: a keyset boxing an actor with no key access, in the node's view, is stale (rule 2) and never sealed to. Then the leak stops once the writer holds the grants and the cycle's commits ("until the next knowing re-key" with the writer as the knowing party). Cost: while a grant stays hidden, concurrent re-seals by the knowing and the ignorant side re-form the cycle between fresh keysets, one extra rotation per race, until the grant arrives. Recommendation: take 4b (same predicate as the knowing re-key; churn instead of leak under withholding), and word the requirement limit as in step 1.
5. Unchanged: A's own departures by frozen coverage at depth 0 (names of A's own removals, departs mark, establishment), forks, history boxes, claims, stranding, lockout recovery; the remover still places the removal and every affected actor's new `keys.0` under every label the removed party could read; a holder's `keys.0` goes under its targets' labels (the carrier for rule 2, and what lets a node judge a forged keyset invalid).
