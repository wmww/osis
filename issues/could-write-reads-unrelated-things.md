# "W could write c" reads things outside the vouch's thing
> Summary: spec/permissions.md §5.1 judges a vouch for c over every role-class part of c, so whether c counts on t depends on W's role on another thing S that c also writes; a holder of t that does not hold S (never synced it, or released it) reads W's role there as none, so t's holders split by whether they hold S, and whoever controls a node's holding of S (S's admin, by its grants) moves c's verdict on t on that node.
> Priority: normal
> Tags: perm, engine, security

Case (engine `apply::tests_vouches::a_vouched_commits_parts_read_alike_across_a_restart_spec_perm_5_1`, varied): X's commit c rotates keys on t and on S; H removes X from t without listing c; W vouches c on t. If W holds the role c's part on S needs, a node holding S counts the vouch (c's keys on t valid); a node holding t but not S, or one that released S, does not (c's keys on t invalid). Nodes holding different commits may differ, but these never converge unless every holder of t also holds S, and S's admin can make a node release S (revoke its read) and so turn c invalid on t there. The node judges an unknown thing's part as needing a role W is not shown to hold (`Held::parts_read`), so the split only ever withholds a vouch, never grants one W could not have written.

Why every part: a counting entry lists c whole for R's frontier, so a rule over the vouch's thing alone would let W keep c's parts on another thing N2 of t's walk that W could not write. A sounder rule judges "could write c" over c's parts on the things whose roles R's walk decides (R's component: the things its edge writes and the vouch link), which every node holding t holds: parts on unrelated things neither need W's role nor are kept by the listing (R's frontier audits nothing there). The spec has no notion of a component; stating it needs one (things reached from R's edge over grants and record edges). Roles-model has one thing per world and no non-edge parts, so it does not decide this.
