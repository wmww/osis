# One aspect with very many kept writes: what is left
> Summary: Engine cost at scale, measured on one long kept aspect (chat design E, release, by hand). At 10^6 a write takes 2.4 ms before its fsync (measured on tmpfs), a fall of 3*10^5 deltas 11 s, a rise 0.6-11 s; every flip still walks the thing's commits (0.6 s) and the first decodes all headers (+2 GB). A kick over about 1.9*10^6 commits fails at the author (a stated limit: kick frontiers are IDs only). Open takes 5 s and 2.6 GB. A new node reads the newest page after the listing (spec/sync-judgement.md §2), not the whole log.
> Priority: low
> Tags: engine, permissions, performance

Counted in the default tests: osis-engine `scale::long_aspect_writes_are_flat` (a write and its arrival flat, a writer's removal linear), `scale::purge_rejudge_is_linear_in_requests_spec_permissions_6` (a requester's flip rebuilds the aspect once). The numbers below are release measurements at 10^4..10^6 messages.

In order of damage:

1. **A kick over about 1.9*10^6 commits cannot be sent.** The frontier passes `MAX_LIST_BYTES`, so `demote` fails with `Commit(TooLarge("header list"))` and the member stays. Tess chose IDs only (spec/requirements.md Cost); ID batches are the fallback (notes/permissions.md, Frontier encoding).
2. **Every flip walks the thing's commits**, under the engine lock (`validity::rejudge_all`, its `judge` over `by_thing`): 0.6 s at 10^6 for one flipped write. The first after an open also decodes every header: 3 s more and resident from 2.6 to 4.7 GiB. 
3. **Open at 10^6**: 5 s CPU, 2.6 GiB resident (2.6 KB per message). Lazy headers: Engine levers below. The first open after a long fill also rewrites the index.
4. **An aspect written in parts still reads every body on a rise or a fallen winner** (a map's entries, a list's inserts; a whole-value write of any encoding stops it). A map's rebuild would need an index of which bodies hold what lies under each delta (Engine levers below).
5. **Ingest's CPU per message grows with the aspect during a fill**: 0.75 ms at 10^5, 3.4 at 6*10^5, 5.4 at 10^6, while a write after reopening at 10^6 takes 1.3-4 ms. Unexplained; not a budget break.
6. **Store writes per message grow**: 70 / 115 / 170 KB per batch at 10^4 / 10^5 / 10^6 (about 35 key-tree nodes of 10 KB each); store size stays 1.2-2 KB per message. A disk-wear problem (a 200 B message writes 800 times its size).
7. **Bulk sync**: one writer syncing N offline writes runs 30 engine transactions per message: 4.3 / 6.6 ms per message at 10^4 / 10^5. Only after long offline writing.
8. **Entry caps**: a node keeping the chat unread caps its entries per sync ID (spec/sync-ids.md §6 Caps), so a chat stops at its cap's message count unless that node raises it. Not measured past it.

Purge by author: about 1.4 ms per delta dropped at 10^6, all in one store transaction under the engine lock: 10^5 deltas 2.3 min on every node from one request, the hostile 10^7-message flood of notes/chat.md 3.9 h. Memory grows with it, 35-66 KB per delta held by the open transaction (1500 deltas +52 MiB, in-memory store, 0.22 ms each; 1040 at 10^6 +69 MiB), so 10^5 is 3.5-6.6 GB and the flood does not fit: it fails before it stalls. Chunking needs a persistent cursor per request, a job run from the tick, resuming after a restart and after every later delta (the request is already listed in `p/`), reads and the watch seeing it part done, and `lasting` and re-snapshots at its end; the order oracles judge the end state only.

A request by anyone with write walks the aspect before any right is judged, and clones two metas per delta: 2-3 us per delta of the named author with no right (1500 deltas, a whole aspect the same), so about 1 s per request for the 30% member at 10^6, on every node, against a 1 ms write. `MAX_AUTHOR_ITEMS` (32) stops one commit multiplying it. A cure: judge `holds_right` once per `(retention by, actor)` in a walk; the whole-aspect request has the same walk. A commit listener delivers one commit read per commit, so a fall of 3*10^5 deltas is that many callbacks on the dispatcher (the engine lock is taken per read).

**A hostile admin's flips (purge re-judge).** Each role change that makes R named requests rise costs every peer about 0.36 ms per part in one transaction, engine held (measured at 2*10^4: R=2000 0.74 s, R=10^4 3.6 s, so about 36 s at 10^5). Bounded per role commit, but an admin can repeat it. No narrower scope helps: the parts touched are the ones the flip affects. A plain writer cannot do this: its aspect-wide request costs a look per delta (21 ms at 2*10^4), its aspect's length, not its right check.

## Engine levers: re-judging, valid again, open
Granting someone new to a large tree still re-judges every commit in it (about 12 ms at 10^4 commits, linear); a commit valid again re-reads every held body of its aspect written in parts; opening scans every commit record, adapts every role commit and restores the walk over its whole input (O(commits), no bodies decoded).

Counted: osis-engine `scale::grant_is_flat_in_content_commits` (open reads every header: O(commits)); `scale::validity_flip_is_linear_and_invalid_arrival_flat_spec_permissions_5_2` (a rebuild from every body). What is built: notes/engine-validity.md, notes/engine-store.md Open.

- **Re-judging.** A commit perm evaluates anew (a holder new to the component joins it, an out-of-order or dropping write) visits every delta of the component: about 1 µs each, mostly the walk over `by_thing` and the commit metas plus perm's query for deltas before their author's standing. Skipping deltas whose answer cannot change needs perm to report, per evaluation, which (node, thing) answers moved: perm's timeline update, out of node's reach.
- **Valid again.** Rebuilding the aspect from held bodies stops at a whole-value write (`Fragment::settled_above`), but an aspect written in parts (a map's entries, a list's inserts) reads every body (O(aspect history), 60-170 µs per body), and so does a fallen write that superseded another in the state. A bounded re-merge needs an index of which bodies hold writes superseded by each delta.
- **Open is O(commits).** Release, 10^4 content commits: 59-67 ms; plus 10^4 edge writes: 190-200 ms (headers 25, metas 42, adaptation 50, walk restore 61-68). Each phase is linear. Steps toward O(changed):
  - Fetch a header from the store on first use instead of keeping its bytes (saves the record scan, the headers phase and the memory). Blocked on borrowing: store reads need `&mut Store` (a `Transaction`; the pager caches), while readers are `&self` (`Engine::header`, `LazyHeader`'s `Deref`, sync's direct `core.commits` reads, about 180 sites) or run inside a transaction already holding `&mut Store`. Shape: readers in a transaction go through `Tx::header(id)`; `&self` readers get a read-only handle on the last committed root (pager behind a lock) or become `&mut self`; `LazyHeader` keeps the view and an empty cell.
  - Load `by_aspect`, `ranges`, `invalid` per aspect on first touch from persisted rows (the metas phase).
  - Restore a held set and perm timeline per component without the whole input (walk restore: gathering the component's `Input` 15-27 ms, perm's partition and `Timeline::from_outcome` most of the rest; osis-perm).
  - Whole-map iterations over `Core::commits` on rare paths decode every header (`held_commits` without a scope, forget's lost aspects, debug checks).
  - Rejected: a walk tag over a persisted hash of its inputs (saves only the tag hash, 5-8 ms); persisting perm's adapted records instead of the rows (saves 40-50 of 76 ms at the risk of runtime and open adaptation disagreeing).
