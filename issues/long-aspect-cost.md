# One aspect with very many kept writes: what breaks
> Summary: Measured `scale::long_aspect` (chat design E). Every join scans the aspect's bodies, so a write or received commit costs 128 ms CPU at 10^5 and first sync is quadratic. Without that scan writes and first sync are linear and fine, but a fall is quadratic in fallen deltas, a rise or fallen winner rebuilds the aspect (about 3 min under the engine lock at 10^6), a kick listing over about 2.4*10^5 commits is refused by every receiver, and open takes 8 s CPU and 2.6 GB. Numbers in notes/testing.md.
> Tags: node, permissions, performance

Repro: `OSIS_SCALE_N=100000 OSIS_SCALE_DIR=<dir on disk> cargo test --release -p osis-node --test it -- --ignored --nocapture --test-threads 1 scale::long_aspect::cost`, `scale::long_aspect::first_sync`.

In order of damage:

1. **`any_bounded` scan on every valid join** (engine `apply/atomic.rs` `join_part`, `retention/deaths.rs`). Each join walks every held body of the aspect for a relative bound: about 1.3 us per body, so 128 ms per write or receive at 10^5, over 1 s at 10^6. First sync did not reach 3*10^4 in 11 min. Its comment said "only flips and un-merges ask", no longer true. Fix: an in-memory count of held bodies with a relative bound per aspect, kept where bodies come and go; the scan is the fallback in debug builds. `Engine::assume_unbounded` (bench only, `OSIS_SCALE_UNBOUNDED`) skips it to show what lies behind; delete it with the fix.
2. **A fall is quadratic in fallen deltas** (`unmerge_fallen` runs `Unmerge::of`, rebuilding the whole invalid list, per fallen part): 680 s at 3*10^4 fallen, over 26 min for 3.7*10^4 at 10^6, all one engine call. Fix: one `Unmerge` per `unmerge_fallen`, the fallen bodies removed in one pass.
3. **A rise or a fallen winner rebuilds the aspect from every body**, under the engine lock: 6 s at 10^5, about 3 min of CPU at 10^6, so worse than linear; see issues/node-core-gaps.md. Stored commits no longer re-check their delete keys, which took 10-14 s to 5.8 s at 10^5 and showed no clear gain at 10^6. For a register only the greatest valid stamp matters; a newest-first walk that stops there (and a rise comparing the risen writes with the state) would make both cheap on the shape chat uses. Other shapes need the rebuild.
4. **A kick over about 2.4*10^5 commits is refused by every receiver.** The author's `demote` succeeds (8.5 MB in 119 blobs at 3*10^5), then `ingest` fails `Commit(TooLarge("value"))` at the receiver: the header's frontier resolves past `MAX_LIST_BYTES`. The author is kicked here and nowhere else. notes/frontier-study.md's heads remove it; until then `demote` should fail rather than stamp what nobody can apply.
5. **Open at 10^6**: 8 s CPU, 2.6 GB resident (2.6 KB per message). Lazy headers: issues/node-core-gaps.md. The first open after a long fill also rewrites the index (135 MB in 8 batches at 10^6); later opens write nothing.
6. **Store writes per message grow**: 70 / 115 / 170-230 KB per batch at 10^4 / 10^5 / 10^6 (about 35 key-tree nodes of 10 KB each); store size stays 1.2-1.6 KB per message. Not a budget break, a disk-wear one (a 200 B message writes 1000 times its size).
7. **Bulk upload**: one writer uploading N offline writes runs 30 engine transactions per message: 2.5 / 6.5 ms per message at 10^4 / 10^5. Only after long offline writing.
8. **Host cap**: `cap_entries` 2^20 per label stops a chat at about 10^6 messages unless the host raises it. Not measured past it.
