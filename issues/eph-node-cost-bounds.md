# Ephemeral holder costs left
> Summary: the ephemeral pre-decode bound is loose per part (and the waiting share is charged by it); a replayed take still scans the held parts of its aspects; the CRDT's in-memory size per map entry (about 2 KiB) is what makes the decode ratio. Low.
> Tags: security, engine, node, crdt, ephemeral

Built (notes/ephemeral.md Bounds): the node cap counts decoded memory (`Engine::memory_charge`) and a commit is checked against the room before decoding (`Engine::ingest_memory(bytes, room)`, `memory_bound`), removed holders' commits are let go at once, pending blobs are charged by their bound and counted once promoted even past the cap, never-fitting blobs are remembered and not refetched (`Memory::unfit`). Measurements: `scale::eph_*` (release, by hand).

Left:
- Durable deltas: issues/durable-decode-bound.md.
- The ephemeral pre-decode bound (`osis_crdt::decoded_bound`) is loose per part (it cannot tell a map key from other values, so every map head and set tag pays a value node three times: about 3 times the charge for tiny map entries, 5.5 for a map of small structs, 13 for a small register part), so one part needs its bound in the room, not its charge: with the 16 MiB cap a single part of tiny entries past about 5 MiB decoded is refused, of small structs past about 3 MiB. A grammar-aware bound (key positions, struct maps) would relax that.
- The waiting share is charged by the same loose bound, so a big honest blob waiting needs its bound in the share: with the 16 MiB cap a string blob past about 680 KB that arrives before its record is refused, then fetched again at the next reconcile; one whose parts are all invalid (rights not here yet) comes back only when a waiting entry leaves the share or at a restart. A tighter bound shrinks this. A remembered blob is fetched again once per arrival of a record it lacks.
- Unverified since the lane moved onto the durable sync path (`sync/memory.rs`): a promoted waiting blob whose label this node no longer knows or whose key no longer unwraps was held uncounted (at most the label's share, once), and waiting entries and `Memory::unfit` of a left label were never cleared, so a left label's memory state stays until restart. Check by leaving a label with waiting memory entries and reading `Memory`'s per-label tables; a label-leave cleanup (memory state, and the engine's held commits of the label) would fix it.
- A waiting blob is retried within the room it was given at arrival, not its waiting charge: one that arrived while the label was nearly full may be refused at retry though its share was reserved.
- CRDT memory per element: a tiny map entry takes about 2 KiB decoded (`Eid` 120 B and its key index copy, `Entry` 56 B, a boxed `Fragment` 176 B per value, B-tree nodes), a small register part with its engine records about 2.3 KiB. That is what makes ephemeral (and durable, while loaded) states large; a leaner layout would shrink every cap's real meaning.
- Waiting entries that never resolve (a part on a thing whose record never comes, a demoted writer's commits) stay in the node's share and Core's pending until restart, taking the share from honest early arrivals. Bounded by `PENDING_SHARE`. Also, an admin demoting and re-promoting a writer makes holders drop and re-download that writer's entries once per cycle (up to the label's cap; cost to the admin: two durable commits with rotations).
