# Ephemeral holder costs left after the cost-bounds fixes
> Summary: a durable delta is decoded (about 200 times its size for tiny map entries) with no bound; the ephemeral pre-decode bound is loose per part; a promoted waiting blob can be held uncounted; a replayed take still scans the held parts of its aspects; the CRDT's in-memory size per map entry (about 2 KiB) is what makes the decode ratio. Low.
> Tags: security, engine, node, crdt, ephemeral

Fixed (notes/ephemeral.md Node cap): the node cap counts decoded memory (`Engine::ephemeral_charge`) and decodes a commit only within the room left, part by part (`ingest_ephemeral_within`, `osis_crdt::decoded_bound`), removed members' commits are let go at once, absorbed lookups are indexed (`Remote::absorbers`), a replay judged contained compares a piece of the cover (`Fragment::contains`). Measurements: `scale::eph_*` (release, by hand).

Left:
- Durable deltas decode before any bound: a writer's 8 MiB durable delta of tiny map entries decodes to about 1.7 GB at every reader (`verify_part`, apply), plus its CBOR value tree. The ephemeral fix (`osis_crdt::decoded_bound` checked against the cap's room part by part, notes/ephemeral.md Node cap) does not carry over: a durable commit must apply at every reader, so a local refusal diverges; a fix is a validity rule (a delta whose `decoded_bound` passes a protocol constant is invalid, writers split such writes), a spec change. A writer is trusted with write access, so this is a writer DoS on its readers.
- The ephemeral pre-decode bound (`osis_crdt::decoded_bound`) is loose per part (`ITEM_BYTES` per CBOR item whatever the item: about 3 times the charge for tiny map entries, 13 for a small register part), so one part needs its bound in the room, not its charge: with the 16 MiB cap a single part of tiny entries past about 5 MiB decoded is refused. A tighter bound (per item kind, from the grammar) would relax that.
- A blob kept apart is retried within the room it was given then, not the room left at retry; what it holds after is counted only when the node promotes it, and a promotion past the cap leaves it held by the engine and uncounted by the node (`eph_promote` then `eph_added` returns at the cap check while the engine holds the commit), each up to the room it was given (at most the cap); the waiting blobs are bounded by `PENDING_SHARE` in blob bytes.
- CRDT memory per element: a tiny map entry takes about 2 KiB decoded (`Eid` 120 B and its key index copy, `Entry` 56 B, a boxed `Fragment` 176 B per value, B-tree nodes), a small register part with its engine records about 2.3 KiB. That is what makes ephemeral (and durable, while loaded) states large; a leaner layout would shrink every cap's real meaning.
- `eph_containers` for a take (a commit listing nothing) still walks every held part on each of its aspects (cheap per part; `contains` only for later covers listing something there): a writer holding many covers on one aspect costs holders O(those covers) per replayed take. Bounded by the cap; an index of cover parts per (author, aspect) by `ts` would make it O(log).
- Waiting entries that never resolve (a part on a thing whose record never comes, a demoted writer's commits) stay in the node's share and the engine's `pending`/`invalid` until restart, taking the share from honest early arrivals. Bounded by `PENDING_SHARE`. Also, an admin demoting and re-promoting a writer makes holders drop and re-download that writer's entries once per cycle (up to the label's cap; cost to the admin: two durable commits with rotations).
