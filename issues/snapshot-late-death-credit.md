# A late-dated death is credited to a snapshot taken after the write's true death
> Summary: `deaths::carriers` credits a write's death to every bounded snapshot taken before the death's date whose frontier covers the write (`took`), without reading its body. Dating is late when a later superseder arrived first, so a snapshot taken after the true death gets a death it never carried and, under keep at most, drops early while holding live writes. Reads agree; bodies differ per order.
> Tags: engine, retention

Found in at-most-map-snapshot-drop's history (keep at most 0 forced on `t:map.0`, seed 83028, order `[.., 18, 17, 11, 15, 14, 16, 13, 10, 12, 9]`). Entry k0 is created at ts 5 (#9) and reset at ts 6 (#12, an undo); #17 writes field 1 of k0 at ts 196608. When #17 arrives before #12, the field-1 creation write (stamp (5, k0's dot)) dies at 196608 and stays dated there after #12 arrives (spec/crdt.md §4.8 says 6). Snapshot #15 (ts 131072, taken after the reset, carrying no write of that stamp) has `took` true and 131072 < 196608, so it gets first death 196608 and drops after `AT_MOST_GRACE` with live writes; as made it has no death. The oracle gives #15 none.

Not caught: `bound_kept_now` judges only bodies with own writes; the live writes stay in the state, so `live_write_lost` holds. Effect: a snapshot body gone early at one node; a later flip on the aspect then has one copy less there (the 2B exception covers the read). Options: in the `ts < d.at` branch also require `Parts::holds` (a body read per snapshot per step), or re-date a death when an earlier superseder joins.
