# One hub thread: cheap stranger messages stall the whole server
> Summary: the server runs every connection's input on one hub thread (driver `blocking`), so per-message work turns into a stall for every client. Strangers who know a label ID, or who register their own label, can force about 10^4 to 10^5 times their input in work: a claim-set load and parse per Open, up to 9 signature checks per bogus stamp with Sync entries uncapped in count, one fsync per stored entry, and a relay copied to every connection. They can also take every connection slot. High.
> Tags: security, server, proto

Each item costs a stranger nothing beyond a label of its own (anyone may register one) or a known label ID:
- **Claim-set parse per Open** (`evaluate_open`, core/label/src/auth.rs:105; `BackendStore::claim_set`, core/server/src/store.rs:1271): a claim proof (signed with any key the sender makes) loads and parses step j's whole claim set, and a static proof loads the head's. That is up to about 9 MiB read from redb and up to 65 536 keys put into a `BTreeSet`/`BTreeMap`, for a roughly 200-byte message. Nothing caches it and nothing limits Opens per connection. The head step ID is handed to anyone (issues/label-activity-to-strangers.md), or the attacker uses its own label with a 9 MiB set.
- **Stamp checks** (`check`, core/proto/src/server.rs:132): a bad stamp is tried against the head key and up to 8 older keys, so 9 Ed25519 verifications per entry. Upload allows 1024 entries (about 120 KB), giving roughly 0.3 s of CPU. Sync entries have no count cap (`"sync entries"`, core/proto/src/msg.rs:387; spec/wire.md §11.2 caps only replace items), so one 9 MiB Sync on an opened label of the attacker's own carries about 78 000 entries, or about 700 000 verifications (tens of seconds). Delete and replace items have the same 9× factor (1024 per message).
- **fsync per entry** (`durable_put` → `commit`, core/server/src/store.rs:862; redb immediate durability, core/store/src/file.rs:15): every new entry is its own durable commit. An Upload of 1024 tiny validly stamped entries under the attacker's label means 1024 fsyncs, and a Sync means tens of thousands, all on the hub thread. Deletes and Registers commit per item as well.
- **Relay fan-out** (`ServerOutput::Relay`, core/server/src/hub.rs:253): an accepted relay-only blob (up to 64 KiB) is cloned into `ServerInput::Relay` for every other served connection, subscribed or not. At `max_connections` 10 000 that is about 650 MB copied per relay. The relay rate is per label (`RelayLimits`), so an attacker with many labels has no bound. The node-wide `MAX_RELAY_SEEN` set is shared, so the same flood also evicts honest labels' replay memory.
- **Connection slots** (`Hub::accept`, core/server/src/hub.rs:85): past `max_connections` new connections are closed. A session that sent Hello and nothing else stays until the transport idle timeout, which QUIC keep-alive defeats. One QUIC connection carries 16 sessions, so about 625 connections lock out every honest client.
- Minor: `ServerConnection::synced` and `inline` (core/proto/src/server.rs:162) are never pruned when an open ends. A connection that opens its own labels and steps each to a new sync key grows them with every label (up to `max_labels`).

Fix direction: cache parsed claim sets (or index claim keys as rows, so `contains` is one lookup). Stop checking older keys after a few refused entries in one message, and cap Sync entries at 1024 like Upload (spec/wire.md §11.2). Batch one message's entries into one store commit. Fan a relay out only to the connections subscribed to its label, sharing the bytes (`Arc`), and add a node-wide relay rate. When slots are full, evict sessions with no open label (or the oldest idle one) instead of refusing, and consider a per-source-address cap (the server sees addresses anyway). Longer term, move per-connection decode and verification off the hub thread, keeping only store access serial.
