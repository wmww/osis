# History and expiry defaults are a thing's setting
> Summary: Design study (Tess's proposal): history is set per thing by its admins in one admin-only aspect `retention^.0` (per aspect key: keep window, keep-until, or purge on replace; plus the expiry default a writer fills in), judged from the settings held; per-write history, `required^.0` and schema history defaults go. Rules, costs, what goes, the model and its result, Tess's decisions, build steps. Go-ahead given.
> Priority: normal
> Tags: retention, spec, engine, sync, api, permissions

**Go-ahead given by Tess**: implement when ready (after expiry step 8 and the held_set_decides class study land, since both touch the same files). Halyard runs the lane, steps in order; Fathom reviews steps 1 and 3. Her decisions are below (Decisions).

## Principle
A thing's admins decide what replaced content is kept and shown, and for how long; the setting never decides who may drop a body (purgeable-by, per write) nor deletes anything promptly (purge, expiry). Every judgment reads only the settings and bodies a node holds.

## Rules
### The setting
- `retention^.0`, admin-only, on the thing it governs. Value: `rules` (AK to entry), `default` (an optional entry) and `retain` (the removal default, spec/permissions.md §2). An entry: `history` (absent: no history promised; `keep { after D, until T }`, at least one; `purge_on_replace`), `expires` (an optional duration, Expiry default) and `final` (edits off: only a writer reads it, Signal edits).
- The entry governing an AK is its own in `rules`, else `default`; neither: no history promised, no expiry default.
- Fixed retention: `nobody`, never out of history, never snapshotted, never undone, written whole, each delta read alone as the whole setting. A delta not reading as the type, with a key that is not an AK or past `MAX_RETENTION_ENTRIES` is no setting. It carries no `expires` and goes with a commit of any purgeable-by, as `required^.0` does.
- It governs every aspect of the thing but those with fixed retention (role-class, `addenda.0`, `purge.0`, itself), memory aspects included.
- A delta is judged as an admin-only delta at its key (spec/permissions.md §5.2). Validity of no other delta depends on a setting.
- **In force**: the held valid settings ordered by key are the thing's timeline. At wall time t the setting in force is the newest by key with `wall_ms(ts) <= t`; before the first, the empty setting. A setting is in force from its own commit on, so a creating commit's setting covers that commit's writes.
- **One way** (Decisions 3): where an earlier setting of the timeline governs an AK with purge on replace, a later `keep` entry for that AK reads as no history promised.

### History
- **Replaced at** r: unchanged (spec/commits.md §5 Bodies and deaths): a value body at the `ts` of the earliest held valid body holding a superseding write; a signal's body at its own `ts`; a purged part, for listing, at its request's `ts`. Holding more bodies dates no later.
- A setting **keeps** a body at time t while t < max(r + D, T) under a `keep` entry (a missing bound counts as 0); never under no entry or purge on replace.
- A body **leaves history** at the first t >= r at which the setting in force at t does not keep it, and is out from then on.
  - So a setting acts from its own stamp: it can shorten or extend the window of bodies still in history at that moment and reaches nothing that left before it. A body replaced before any setting, or under an entry with no history, is out when replaced.
  - Bodies of one aspect leave in the order they were replaced: one threshold per (thing, AK) at any moment.
- **In history**: a held valid replaced body that has not left, by the node's clock. That, and the parts live in the state, is kept history: what listings show (§7 Listed parts), what undo writes back from, and what `history()` reads. A body out of history is not listed, held or not.
- **Keep at least**: before a body leaves history (on the node's clock) compaction and own replaces MUST NOT drop it and it is kept for must-hold (spec/sync.md §6); right holders MUST NOT delete it at hosts before `MAX_DRIFT` past that (Dating, Margin). Purge requests and expiry act whatever the window; invalid bodies drop as today.
- **Out of history**: a value body may be dropped by every node once any held valid writer's cover covers it (a snapshot, or two own replaces of its author, spec/commits.md §5 Own replaces), the purge right or not (Decisions 4; Purge on replace, last rule); a signal's body may be dropped by any node. A node takes no copy of a body that is out, fetches none and holds none as must-hold.
- **Dropped stays dropped**: a body a node dropped as covered or as out of history is not missing when a setting that arrives later puts it in history. An extension reaches what a node still holds.
- Compaction snapshots carry `writer` and are judged by the setting like any body; replaced at the latest death among the writes they carry that no later held valid snapshot carries.

### Purge on replace
- A body is **under purge on replace** when the entry governing its AK in the setting in force at its replaced-at is `purge_on_replace` (never a signal's: there the entry reads as no history).
- The duty and the drop are spec/commits.md §5 Purge on replace with that test in place of the delta's own mode: a node that may write the aspect and holds such a body, replaced, that no held valid snapshot holding the right covers, writes a snapshot from its whole state in the transaction in which that comes to hold (the replacing write, the body, a validity or role change, or a setting arriving or changing validity); every node drops such a body once a held valid snapshot holding the right covers it.
- No writer is invalid for not knowing the setting: the duty falls on every writer that holds the replaced body and the setting.
- Content replaced before the setting's stamp is out of history from that stamp with no purge promise.
- A body out of history (under purge on replace once replaced) is dropped at every node by any held valid snapshot of a writer that carries what is still current, whether or not that writer holds the purge right over it: nothing promises to keep it, and dropping it is not a purge (no prompt-deletion promise). The purge promise of purge on replace is kept by right holders' covers as today.

### Signals
- A fire is history from its own `ts` under the entry governing its AK: no entry, no listing. A writer refuses a fire the setting it holds would not keep (`Invalid`).
- The wire carries no history, so the rule "a signal's retention is keep history" goes. `replaces`, honoring and listeners are unchanged; versions and their original share one window, each from its own `ts`.

### Listings, undo, permanence
- A purged part is listed while its value-less writes are in the state or a body replaced at its request's `ts` would be in history.
- Undo (§7): a part the undone part may have replaced is kept when it is in kept history, or the AK's bodies replaced at the undone part's `ts` are in history now (one check per aspect, not per part), or a kept part supersedes it.
- A delta is **permanent** when it is durable, not fixed-retention, without `expires`, and a body of its AK replaced now would never leave history (a forever entry). Lasting commits follow as today.
- `complete` is unchanged: it speaks of sync passes. History a node dropped before an extension reached it is absent there, not a gap.

### Expiry default (Tess decided)
- Expiry stays on the commit (§6). An entry's `expires` is the duration a writer fills `expires` from (`wall_ms(ts)` + duration), reading the newest valid setting it holds at write time; a schema default applies where the setting has none; a write may still set its own.
- A commit is valid with the expiry it carries, whatever the setting says or later says. A change reaches later writes only.
- One expiry per commit stands: a writer refuses a write whose parts would be filled with different expiries unless the write names one.

### Convergence
- Every judgment above is a function of the settings and bodies held and the clock; nothing is dated by arrival. Nodes holding the same bodies and settings show the same history.
- A late setting (stamped before ones already held) can move a body out of or back into history, where it is still held; a setting that turns invalid was never in force. Both converge: the timeline is the held valid settings.
- No resend loop: a body out of history is neither must-hold nor fetched; one in history at a node that dropped it is not fetched. Two nodes disagree only while one lacks a setting, and settings are must-hold for good under the thing's labels; node-sync places before it deletes, so a label a right holder deletes at holds the settings it judged by.
- **Disputes**: each side's timeline is the settings valid under its trust set. A side that does not count a setting keeps and shows what the other side may drop; right holders delete a covered body at hosts once per body as today, so the sides do not chase each other. Sides that later agree judge alike, with what is still held.

## Dating
When a body was replaced is a fact about the writes, read from what is held. A covered drop tells nothing new about it.
- **Invariant**: a valid covered drop changes no body's in-history status (no listing, undo answer, `history()` read or keep promise moves) and moves no replaced-at of a part in history. Only arrivals (bodies, settings, validity changes) and the clock move them. Every date read and its argument, with the tests: notes/engine-retention.md Replaced.
- **D1. A write dies at its superseder's own stamp**: at the earliest `ts` of a held valid write superseding it, read from any held body holding that write (its own commit, or a valid snapshot carrying it). That is spec/crdt.md §4.8 When as written. It is not the `ts` of the body that holds the write, also for a valid snapshot's copy of a write whose own delta is held invalid: the vouched copy counts as the write itself (Fathom; a writer's `ts` is its word, so this shortens history only, storage reclaim, notes/deletion.md). So a live body dropped under a cover redates nothing: the cover carries its writes with their stamps.
- **D2. A body is replaced at the latest death among its dead writes**, deltas included. A snapshot leaves out the dead writes that a held valid snapshot stamped later carries as winners, as today. So a body drops only after every dead write that it alone holds has left history, and a write replaced later than its commit's first change keeps its own window.
- **D3. Text**: a text element's creation dies at the earliest held delete stamped after the element's latest held undelete. A drop of the first delete's body then moves nothing.
- **D4. A covered drop needs the body out of history on the node's clock**: `out(r) <= now`. This covers snapshot covers, own replaces, purge on replace's cover and memory drops. It applies per aspect: only where some held setting keeps history for the aspect at some moment (a body spanning aspects: each part by its own aspect, D5, the body leaves when all may). Elsewhere there is no history to protect, so a covered body drops at once and nothing is dated, whatever the thing's other aspects keep (Fathom: thing level made an aspect wait on unrelated aspects). This replaces the clock rule and its `ahead` dating.
- **Why it holds**: supersession is transitive (D3 handles the one exception). Suppose B drops and held W had its earliest superseder in a dead write d of B, with d not carried by a later held snapshot. Then W redates to r' <= death(d) <= r_B (D2), and out(r') <= out(r_B) <= now, so W was out and stays out. A W in history has r_W <= B's stamp <= r_B, so it cannot depend on a droppable B. Live writes of B stay in the cover with their stamps (D1).
- **Margin** (Fathom): `MAX_DRIFT` applies only to host deletes: a host must not delete before other nodes' clocks agree, while a local drop only changes this node's reads, which follow its own clock. A right holder deletes a covered-dropped body at hosts `MAX_DRIFT` past its keep bound, recorded on the dropped record (`Dropped::hosts_from`; no timer or row of its own). No local margin, so no limit and no harness slack.
- **D5. Per part** (Fathom): each part of a body is dated only by its own writes' deaths and kept only by its own aspect's setting (where no setting keeps its aspect it adds no bound); the body stays while any part is current or inside its own keep, so until the latest per-part keep (`bounds::part_replaced_at`, `class_keep`). A setting governs its aspect's data, nothing else: what D4 says. So every date in "Why it holds" lives on one aspect's timeline. Rejected: a class dated by the latest death of all its parts and kept by each aspect's timeline at that one date (an undated body on an unkept aspect, dropped under a cover, redated the class's kept part: `setting.rs` `an_unkept_part_dates_no_class_*`, and `covered_drops_move_nothing_*` with mixed classes).
- **Audit of the invariant's reads**: D3 holds (an undelete's body drops only once a later delete supersedes it, which is no earlier than the creation's date, and dropping it only dates the creation earlier, so it stays out). Header reads hold for the fire's `ts`, the purge floor, a purge request's `ts` (never forgotten), `kept_whenever_replaced` and the settings, but for one gap: forgetting (spec/commits.md §5 Forgetting) drops the header of a commit whose parts all dropped under its author's own cover, and undo then counts the per-aspect forgotten `ts` instead (`undo::replaced_kept`): a part the undo excused before (a kept snapshot reaching it, or `keeps_now`: replaced earlier than the undone write) now refuses the undo unless a kept reset is stamped after it. Stated as a limit of forgetting (Fathom; spec/commits.md §5 Forgetting): forgetting may narrow what undo allows, never widens it and never brings back data. Local only: the undoing node's own refusal; a receiver judges an undo like any content commit (spec/crdt.md §4.6). Keeping extra to excuse the part would be a lasting trace (notes/deletion.md). Listing is per part too: a part is current by its own writes only, another live part of its body keeps the bytes, not its listing.
- **Late arrival**: a body that arrives after its earliest superseder dropped is dated by what is held, so later. It may show until that date leaves history where a node that held both would not. This is an arrival, as a late setting is (Decisions 1).
- **Rejected**: dating by headers (a header does not say which writes it superseded, and the seed's superseding write is held nowhere); per-node stored dates (dated by arrival); keeping B while it dates a held W (conflicts with purge on replace dropping B at once).
- **Built** (branch history-3, step 3): engine (`bounds::dated_replaced_at` per part, `class_keep`, `ByWrite::first_superseding`, D4 gated per aspect by `bounded`, `Dropped::hosts_from`), held-model (`died`, `Eager` by held, any-order property with the arrival class), held.rs judging every fresh drop with no slack; notes/engine-retention.md Replaced says the rules and tests.

## Security and promises
- Keep is a promise only by nodes that hold the setting: a node drops nothing in history by its own held set. Purge on replace keeps its property under late dating (model: content replaced under it is never history, however a node dates the replacement) because of the one-way rule.
- An admin can, beyond purge (which it holds over every write): shorten the window of every body, letting writers' compaction drop it and any node drop a signal's entries. It cannot touch live values or force a prompt deletion. Backdating gives nothing more: a setting stamped in the past ends at most what a setting stamped now would, and re-shows only bodies readers still hold. A removed admin's setting is invalid as any admin-only write is.
- A writer ignoring the setting can only give its own writes another expiry or purgeable-by.

## Cost
- A setting change: O(settings of the thing) to rebuild the timeline, plus what leaves or is owed. Nothing is re-judged for validity (today a `required^.0` change re-judges every later delta of the thing).
- Index: the timeline per thing (`ix.retention`, as `ix.required`); per (thing, AK) one timer at the next leaving, since bodies leave in replaced order. Signals need no rows (the listing is in `ts` order: the threshold is a range bound); value parts keep one row (thing, AK, replaced-at, commit) written where `T_KEPT` timers are armed today. A window passing costs the parts that leave, each once; reads and compaction O(log settings) on top of today's replaced-at.
- Turning purge on replace on: one snapshot per governed aspect holding replaced content, at each writer; then as today.
- Growth: one setting delta per change, for good (a few hundred bytes; replaces the `required^.0` row), and one per thing that wants history or undo, where a schema default cost nothing per thing. Per-write: the aspect entry loses its history field; a signal body's timer row goes.

## What goes, stays, becomes
- **Goes**: the wire's `history` in `Retention` (it is `by` alone) and the ephemeral marker's history clauses; `required^.0`, its validity rule (spec/permissions.md §5.2), `MAX_REQUIRED_ENTRIES`, `Required`/`RequiredRules`; schema `keep_history` and `purge_on_replace` on aspects; snapshot retention inherited from carried writes (`own_retentions`, `cannot_keep`, the "with at least its D" clause, mixed modes on one aspect); per-body signal timers; the door's per-entry source (`history::of` reading the aspect entry's wire mode); the zero-growth limit "a required rule invalidates both covers" (issues/zero-growth.md); issues/delete-required-rules.md (a delete answers to no rule); held-model `History`, `snapshot_retention`, `Delta::Required`.
- **Stays**: purgeable-by per write with schema defaults, its options `admin` (default), `author_or_admin`, `writer`: admins may always purge; `nobody` and `author` go as options for content (fixed retention keeps `nobody` for `created.0` and the setting itself); expiry per commit; replaced-at; covers, own replaces and forgetting (a commit goes once every part is out of history); purge, purge floor, leftovers; `ROLE_KEEP`; R1 and suspect repair.
- **Signal edits**: honoring and versions unchanged. `final` has no validity rule to live in: the default here is a flag on the entry that only the writer reads (`replace` refuses), Decisions 5. The expiry bound on a replacing part goes with `required^.0`: a writer gives an edit its original's `expires`, and no rule checks it. The limit "a purged edit with a shorter keep lets older versions show again" goes (one window per AK).
- **Chat**: `create` writes `retention^.0` (`chat:msg~0`: keep forever, `expires` the disappear timer, `retain`). The timer is a default: a message expires as its sender saw the setting; a stale or modified client's message keeps the expiry it carries. The purge option is filled per write by the chat library from its own setting; edits-off is a default the writer follows.
- **Purge on replace in requirements**: the promise and its cost line stand, "while the setting is in force" added, plus the one-way limit.
- **Ending** (notes/expiry-creation-design.md step 8): ending drops the thing's settings and history with every other part; role history kept for dependents is fixed retention and not governed. An expiring creation cannot carry the setting (it has no `expires`): two commits.
- **App layer**: kit and app write the setting where they create things that want undo (today's `keep_history(1d)` aspects); without it a replace is not undoable.

## Model
`core/test/held-model/src/thing_history.rs`, `tests/it/spec_thing_history.rs` (9 tests, pass, 2 s): over every timeline of up to three settings on stamps 0..7 and six options: bodies leave in replaced order; a setting stamped after a body left never brings it back; a later setting acts only from its stamp; a late setting can re-show a held body (stated behaviour); purge-on-replace content is never history however late dated, and is without the one-way rule (the counterexample is a test); a node dropping at the first allowed moment shows what a keep-everything node shows when nothing is late, a subset in every arrival order, and lacks only what it dropped itself.
Not modelled: covers and rights (unchanged rules), validity flips of settings (the timeline is "valid held"), host placement.
held-model lags spec/commits.md §5 Ending (rewrite it in step 3): `model.rs` `names` takes only deltas stamped before the request, so a later minting is never named (the spec names every minting of the thing); parts stamped after an ending are still held, and the ending's right is judged with the thing's later edge writes.

## Decisions (Tess)
1. "Never comes back" is a property of the timeline: a setting arriving late may re-show a body a node still holds. No per-node marks.
2. Admins may always purge: purgeable-by options are `admin`, `author_or_admin`, `writer`; `nobody` and `author` go for content (no writer can fill a thing with history admins cannot remove). No per-write keep-forever flag. Shortening needs only the admin right, over every body: no exception, bodies leave in replaced order, one threshold per (thing, AK).
3. Purge on replace is one way: once set for an aspect of a thing, a later keep there keeps nothing (model-checked).
4. Out-of-history bodies drop under any valid writer's cover (Purge on replace, last rule): data not promised is not kept for good.
5. Who-may-purge and edits-off are writer defaults only; no validity rule.
6. Requirements wording as proposed, with the purge list now "(admins, the author or admins, any writer)":
   - Deletion, first item: "Who may purge an edit is set per edit (admins, the author or admins, any writer): a default per aspect, overridable per write, immutable once written; admins may always purge. What happens to replaced content is set per thing and aspect by the thing's admins and may change at any time: by default nothing is promised; kept at least a while after it is replaced and/or until a time; or purged once replaced. A change reaches the history nodes still hold: what has left never returns, and a longer window brings back nothing a node already dropped. A default expiry is set the same way and is only what a writer fills in: an edit keeps the expiry it was written with."
   - In place of "A node without write on data deletes it on its own only at expiry...": "A node deletes data on its own only where nothing promises to keep it: at expiry, and history its thing's settings no longer keep. Purging is the act of a right holder, taking effect elsewhere when it arrives."
   - Purge on replace: "set per thing and aspect, for content replaced while it is set. Limit: an aspect that purged on replace keeps no history on that thing again."
