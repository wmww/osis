# A Prove's rotations are kept with no carrier or bound
> Summary: `Engine::take_rotations` keeps every valid rotation of any account whose record is held, signed at a held epoch; nothing carries it and no budget counts it, so a holder of an old epoch key (a removed device) stores fresh forks at every verifier on each connection, for good. And a verifier lacking more of the proving chain than one Prove fits never verifies the account by Prove.
> Priority: normal
> Tags: engine, sync, security

- Who writes the input: the dialer. Rotations are taken before the proofs are judged (they decide which key is open), so the account a Prove claims bounds nothing: a hostile dialer claims whichever account it likes. Keeping only "the claimed account's" would add a rule and close nothing.
- What it costs: a removed device X still holds its old epochs' keys and mints any number of forks there. Each applies at a verifier holding the account's record and that epoch (held, disregarded or followed by first-seen), and role commits stay for good (notes/growth.md rows 1, 58). Through sync the same forks need an entry per fork under a sync ID X may log to; through a Prove they need only a reconnection. Storage is then not attributable to the verifier's synced groups (unsync everything should leave zero).
- Candidate: take only the rotations the proof's key needs (the needs-closure of the rotation minting the signing key, what `AccountView::proving_chain` sends), and keep a disregarded one only while something carries it, as for pending commits (spec/commits.md §4 Pending storage). X still stores one chain per connection it proves on; whether that needs a per-peer budget is the open question.
- Cap: `prove_rotations` sends the proving chain each rotation after what it needs, cut to `MAX_ROTATIONS` and the message's room from the front (only a needs-closed prefix can apply). A verifier holding the record but lacking more than that never sees the newest rotations by Prove; it learns them only by syncing data the account's chain travels with. At infinity (long-lived accounts) Prove proves nothing to a fresh verifier. Candidate: the verifier names what it holds (heights per head) in its Hello, or the prover sends the chain from the newest epoch the verifier is known to hold (pair record).
