# Data that lives while its keepers are connected
> Summary: Tess's model for ephemeral data, replacing keep-alive and the "Superseded parts" rule (whose open problems are listed at the end): a thing may carry an aspect naming its keeper accounts (default its creator's); a node holds the thing (creation and all its content) only while it is connected, directly or indirectly, to at least one node of a keeper account, and drops it locally otherwise. Thing-level, not per commit: no wire change, one rule, and ephemeral state on a durable thing lives on a separate keeper-held thing. No periodic writes: a keeper leaving drops the data at once; a reconnecting keeper's node re-sends what it holds by ordinary sync. After the rebuilt sync (notes/sync.md) and issues/account-signing.md (accounts as the identity).
> Priority: normal
> Tags: sync, ephemeral, net, spec

- Connected set: each node holds the accounts it reaches through current connections. Indirect reach is learned from neighbours with the path (path-vector), event-driven (sent on change only), so a loop cannot keep a departed account alive; the connection layer's idle timeout bounds detection of an abrupt drop.
- Nodes may briefly disagree on who is connected during partitions; such data then shows at some nodes and not others until it settles (stated).
- Granularity: a whole thing (chosen over per commit: no wire field, the drop is a local held-set rule like any other, and a reconnecting keeper re-sends the thing by ordinary sync). Per-session churn of such things relies on creations being ordinary writes (no kept creation record, notes/expiry-creation-design.md).
- Listing other accounts as keepers only keeps one's own data alive longer while they are online; it grants nothing.
- Invalid memory parts: memory parts are the one non-edge, non-own class kept while invalid (spec/permissions.md §6; notes/ephemeral.md What follows from memory), since a memory commit forgotten whole cannot come back when valid again. This model should drop them like durable invalid bodies (a reconnecting keeper re-sends what turns valid) and remove that carve-out.

## Today's rule's open problems (they go with it)
- **Superseded map entries stay live**: an expiring delta is covered by the join like any body, which does not replace spec/sync-compaction.md §2 Superseded parts: a map key's rewrite (`KeepAlive`, a cursor map) is a `Put`, a fresh entry, and the old entry stays live in the CRDT (shadowed only in the read), so no cover drops it; without the rule a cursor holds every move to its expiry (60 moves: 60 commits and entries at peers, against 1). notes/ephemeral.md Superseded expiring writes.
- **Who deletes a superseded part** depends on whether another part of its commit is held: §2 says only the author deletes a superseded commit's entries at a peer keeping S unread, once a superseder is held there, but scopes that to forgotten commits; a commit still held with one part superseded falls under the general must-not-hold path, where any writer may delete it without waiting for the superseder. Under a permanent admin fork the superseder's validity can differ between nodes, a plausible reason for author-only; none is recorded.
