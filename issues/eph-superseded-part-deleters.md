# Who deletes a superseded part at a host
> Summary: spec/sync-compaction.md §2 (Superseded parts) says only the author deletes a superseded commit's entries, and only once a superseder is at the peer, but scopes that rule to forgotten commits. A commit still held with one part superseded falls under the general must-not-hold path, where any writer may delete it (logging a rewrite of the parts still held) without waiting for the superseder there. So who may delete a superseded part depends on whether another part of its commit is held. Pick one rule in the spec; the rebuilt sync follows it.
> Priority: low
> Tags: sync, spec, ephemeral

- Seen (in the old sync): a commit `{flag set, cursor put}` (both expiring), then the author's later cursor put. Every node drops the old cursor part and holds the flag part; author and holder both judge the two-part entry `MustNot(Dropped)` with a delete item ready, and a flag-only rewrite replaces it at the host. Reads converge (also across restarts); only the deleter rule differs.
- Superseding carries none of the old writes (unlike a coverer), so a deletion rests on the superseder being valid in the reader's view. Under a permanent admin fork that view can differ between nodes, which is a plausible reason for author-only; no reason is recorded.
- Options: (a) superseded deletes like covered: any writer, once a superseder is at the host (the forgotten path's `carriers_of_forgotten` already treats superseders as carriers); `MemoryDrop::Superseded` and `Covered` become one variant and judge_forgotten's author check goes. (b) keep author-only and make the held path follow it: a non-author leaves an entry holding a part with `Dropped::superseded_by` alone, and the author waits for a superseder at the host (carrier_of following `superseded_by`).
