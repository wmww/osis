# Who deletes a superseded part at a peer keeping it unread
> Summary: spec/sync-compaction.md §2 (Superseded parts) says only the author deletes a superseded commit's entries at a peer keeping S unread, and only once a superseder is held there, but scopes that rule to forgotten commits. A commit still held with one part superseded falls under the general must-not-hold path, where any writer may delete it (logging a rewrite of the parts still held) without waiting for the superseder there. So who may delete a superseded part depends on whether another part of its commit is held. Pick one rule in the spec; osis-sync follows it.
> Priority: low
> Tags: sync, spec, ephemeral

- Shape: a commit `{flag set, cursor put}` (both expiring), then the author's later cursor put. Every node drops the old cursor part and holds the flag part; author and holder both judge the two-part entry must-not-hold, and a flag-only rewrite replaces it. Reads converge; only the deleter rule differs.
- Superseding carries none of the old writes (unlike a coverer), so a deletion rests on the superseder being valid in the reader's view. Under a permanent admin fork that view can differ between nodes, which is a plausible reason for author-only; no reason is recorded.
- Options: (a) superseded deletes like covered: any writer, once a superseder is held at the peer (the superseder already stands for the snapshot there); the author-only clause goes. (b) keep author-only and make the held path follow it: a non-author leaves an entry holding a superseded part alone, and the author waits for a superseder held at the peer.
