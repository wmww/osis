# Expiry knocks out the commit, entries live while they hold content
> Summary: Tess's model of expiry (general, not driven by ephemeral data): at its expiry a commit's own values go and nothing else does; its removals stay; writes of other commits live by their own expiry. An entry (a slot a write created, e.g. a map entry or list element) lives while it holds live content from any commit, or while its creation is live: commit 1 (expiring) creates an entry with text, commit 2 (no expiry) adds text, at commit 1's expiry its text goes and the entry with commit 2's text stays; with no commit 2 the entry goes; with commit 2 expiring later, the entry goes then. Today an expiring commit's entry is its own and the expiry-copying rule forces later writes into it to share its expiry. Study first: does "entry present while its creation or any write inside it is live" fit the CRDT (map entries, list elements, text, nested containers, removals), what leftovers stay, what the copy-expiry rule shrinks to (undo copying back an expiring value), what goes. Then build.
