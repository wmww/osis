# The gate takes 30 minutes; Tess's limit is 5
> Summary: The whole gate (default tests, every selected property, sim at 15 seeds) is to take 5 minutes (Tess's cap), reached by fixing perf, not by shrinking sizes; each stage's limit is its share of the cap. On ostrich a gate takes about 15 min (8000 CPU-s), its hold 11 min; every stage is about 2x its share, the builds after a rebase 7x. Where the time goes and each remaining perf problem with what fixing it would save.
> Priority: normal
> Tags: tests, performance

## The budget
5 minutes wall for the whole gate but its lock wait (notes/build.md Time limits, `gate`), on ostrich (16 threads): at most about 16 x 300 = 4800 CPU-s if every thread is busy, about 4000 at the box's usual 85%. Each gate stage's limit is its share of that cap (notes/build.md Time limits, `share of`): the hold gets 105 u (241 s, 3795 CPU-s), the steps outside it 24 u (about 55 s), so an over-cap gate names the stages over their share. Breadth that leaves the gate waits for release checks (notes/coordination.md), so sizes stay; the work per seed has to fall about 3x.

## Where it goes (ostrich, main 7f0db4ff5; CPU-s, wall at load 12-30)
- Default tests: 4550-4730 CPU-s, 385-495 s (10,745 G instructions; 3479 tests, 35 binaries). CPU-bound: even alone on the box, at 16 threads it cannot go under about 290 s. Over the `test` limit alone (10 s CPU or 15 s wall): 5 tests, each multi-threaded, none over 11 s wall (notes/testing.md, Default-test cost); the biggest `order::held_set_decides` (144 CPU-s, 11 s wall). The long tail (3400 tests at 0.2-5 CPU-s) is most of it: per-change engine cost (issues/derived-state-recomputed.md) and the debug checks (notes/test-harnesses.md, Debug checks: index check about 10%, rebuild check 2.5% of osis-node `it`).
- Properties, every crate selected, each timed alone before later harness fixes (git log): 35 tests, 6095 CPU-s, 13,988 G. `held_set_decides_exhaustive` 3307 (54%), `rotation_holds_large` 418, `held_set_decides_plain` 426, `incremental_equals_from_scratch_large` 375, `epochs_model_agrees_large` 267, `paged_apply_matches_whole_large` 181 (100 s wall: 2 seeds of 90 s each), `random_churn_converges_large` 141, `stream_check_matches_whole_large` 107 (single-threaded, 107 s wall), `relay_differential_large` 109, `crdt_equals_model_large` 93; the other 25 under 75 each.
- Sim at 15 seeds (`--from 7000`, under the lock at load 15-20): 1840 CPU-s, 3472 G, 123-128 s wall, CPU-bound and balanced (wall within about 7% of CPU over 16 threads; longest-first order would win about 3 s). Flat: `member_churn` 208 (11%; 7-18 s a seed), `team_reads_member_thing` 123, `purge_tombstones`, `work_sets`, `grant_to_group`, `deposit_flood` 64-66 each; the other 46 scenarios 7-57 each.

## Remaining perf problems, with what each would save
1. Sim: what a seed costs is node-sync and ingest (`SyncEngine::handle` about 70%: `judge_label` 29% with `body_sends`, `judging`, `drop_covered`, `must_hold`/`part_keeps` under it; `log_events_from` 18% with ingest, signature checks and pager commits), the debug index check about 5%: issues/derived-state-recomputed.md. `deposit_flood`'s honest nodes judge each duplicate against every other copy of its commit (`stamped_parts`, `deletable`): quadratic in the copies a flood leaves.
2. `held_set_decides_exhaustive` (8 seeds 1250 G, 53-54 s wall alone): what is left is the property's own work per order (`node_checks`, `Engine::check_index`, held-model, the quarter of orders with late parts): no prefix to share (a setup copy for late-parts orders saved nothing), no check repeated. Lever, a Tess call: the index check's sampling (issues/tests-4x.md pattern 2: each order is a new engine sampled at its first and sixteenth transaction; about 120 CPU-s).
3. `paged_apply_matches_whole_large` (seed 3: 306 G): `World::seen`'s stored checks of the nodes a step changed, and the decoding and joins its readers do. A stored part is read decode-only (notes/engine-paged.md, Checked once); the full canonical check runs only where bytes enter. What is left is the property's own work.
4. A property's wall is its slowest seed when its seeds are single-threaded and few: `paged_apply_matches_whole_large` (2 seeds, 60-90 s each). `held_set_decides` spreads every seed's orders over every core (above), so its wall is about its CPU over the box's threads.
5. Profiled (one or two seeds, or the default sibling): `rotation_holds_large` is ingest and sync (`LocalPeer::sync_all` 75%). `incremental_equals_from_scratch_large`: the property's own oracles (`answers` before and after every arrival under `MOVES`, `Timeline::evaluate` from scratch each step), whose `Timeline::role` at past points walks again each query (`roles_walk`). `epochs_model_agrees_large`: `random_world` 26%, perm itself 26%, the model's judgment the rest. `relay_differential_large`, `random_churn_converges_large`: node-sync passes (`judge_label`, `must_hold`, `part_keeps`: issues/derived-state-recomputed.md) and `check_index` 17% in relay (each `Net::restart` is a new engine, so its first samples fire again: item 2's policy). No harness waste left found.
6. Default tests: nothing short of the engine work (issues/derived-state-recomputed.md: its `Measured` puts node-sync passes at about 70% of a typical osis test) moves the 4500 CPU-s much; the 5 tests over the `test` limit hold about 190 CPU-s, each multi-threaded and under 11 s wall. `shred::past_expiry_and_purge_alike` is 84% the forensic attack (`osis_store::attack`, every row ever written against every key found, twice per case for 105 cases): the property's definition, brute force by design; `net_records::no_connection_resumes_...` (12 seeds), `stream::stream_differential_paged` (3 seeds on the seed runner, one of them most of it): node-sync passes, `check_index` 11-16% and the rebuild check in `drop_body`.
7. Builds after a rebase: main moving under a low crate (osis-base, -crypto, -crdt, -commit, -store, -keys) rebuilds every dependent's test binaries, 5-17x the `build` share on both boxes (notes/time-limits.md, Patterns); a manifest or lock change also runs the unification check and its GUI rebuild. The shares assume a warm target.
8. `gate.gui-tests` compiles kit, app and masonry test binaries inside its stage (10-13 s of its 10-21 s wall on fastbox-0, share 3 s): the build stage prebuilds only core's (`tools/gate.sh` `builds` reads core's `build` lines). Prebuilding them there (`cargo test -p osis-kit -p osis-app -p osis-android --no-run`) puts the compile in `build`, where its share can count it.
9. Tiny wall shares (`gate.deny`, `gate.gui-lint`, `gate.checks`: 1-3 u, 2-5 s on fastbox-0) go over by wall at most gates under load with CPU within, so every gate log names them. Process start and load dominate at that size; one share for the small steps outside the hold would stop the noise without loosening the cap.

## The gap
An every-crate gate (load 17): unlocked steps 250 s (builds after a rebase 121 s, the unification check with a GUI rebuild 116 s; warm and without a manifest change about 15 s), then the hold 1048 s, 10,240 CPU-s: default tests 398 s / 4101, properties 352 s / 3600 (6095 CPU-s before the fixes, each alone), sim 297 s / about 2540 (the sim alone at 15 seeds: 120-160 s, about 1850 CPU-s). The 5-minute gate leaves the hold about 4 minutes and 3500 CPU-s: about 3x less work. Candidates: item 2's index check about 120; item 5 is engine work. That is a few hundred of the 6700 CPU-s to cut; the rest is the default tests' 4100 and the engine's per-change cost under every test (issues/derived-state-recomputed.md), which no harness fix reaches. Even with properties and sim at zero, the default tests alone are over the hold's budget (398 s wall, 4101 CPU-s).
