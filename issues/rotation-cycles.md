# Membership cycles keep a departed member reading
> Summary: with actors X and Y (secrets of their own) members of each other, a member D removed from X keeps reading X and everything above it after any sequence of single-actor rotations: Y's epoch boxes X's old keyset, which D holds, and X's new epoch boxes Y's. The covers rule follows only simple chains, so it calls this covered.
> Tags: security, engine, permissions, owner-call

D held X's old epoch kx. Y's current epoch ky has a box for X sealed to kx's keyset, so D opens ky. X rotates to kx', boxing Y at ky: D opens kx', and every epoch above X boxed to kx'. Rotating Y next (ky' boxed to X at kx') does not help, since D holds kx'; rotating X again repeats it. Every epoch of the cycle reachable from a key D held leaks. The chain X -> Y -> X is not simple, so `rotation::collect` (and the old path walk) never asks for it; asking for it would rotate without end. A fix rotates the whole strongly connected set at once (fresh secrets for every actor in it, each boxed to the others' new keysets) or forbids cycles of actors with secrets of their own (perm allows cycles; groups.rs also does). spec/crypto.md §4 is silent about cycles beyond "a chain never passes an actor twice".

The class merge in `rotation::collect` is exact only on acyclic graphs. In a cycle, a class kept at actor W through a chain that passes U (U above W, so U and W are on a cycle) is not extended to U, though another chain of the class avoiding U would be; so U, and A above it, can miss a class the simple-chain walk had (`walk_with_cycles_follows_simple_chains`: 16 of 1400 actors, uncovered roles equal in all). A decision can differ in principle (e.g. U rotated, W and the actors below it not, the node may rotate A but not U: the old walk strands A, the new one encrypts), but the departed member then sits below the U-W cycle and keeps reading through it anyway, as above. Exact simple-chain classes are a constrained simple-path search (not polynomial in general); the fix for cycles above replaces both.

Severity note (review): cycles of actors with secrets of their own are not an edge case under the tags design: notes/tags.md says tags are a graph with cycles allowed, a shared tag is an actor, and a sub-tag placed under a shared tag gets an edge for the parent's actor and becomes a shared actor itself, so two shared tags that each contain the other are exactly X and Y above. The first-party app's sharing model therefore produces the leaking shape by ordinary use (tag A in tag B, tag B in tag A); until fixed, apps should refuse to share a tag whose sub-tag graph reaches back to it.
