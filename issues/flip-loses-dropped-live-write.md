# A dropped body's live write lost across a validity flip
> Summary: a body dropped at its keep-at-most bound keeps its live write in the state; if its delta then turns invalid and valid again (role commits arriving late), the write is gone until a copy of the body comes, so reads differ by arrival order. spec/crdt.md §4.6 states it; the order oracle does not tolerate it yet.
> Tags: engine, retention, tests

Seen on a 33-step history of seed 733028 (shrunk by order-batch while hunting another failure; the 60-step default seed passes). The `then the rest` check: as made `t:reg.0` reads `also` (a peer-2 write under keep at most 0 whose body dropped at first death + `AT_MOST_GRACE`, its write live); held-then-rest reads `v1`: the same body dropped, then a role commit made the delta invalid (un-merge took the write from the state), another made it valid, and no body is held to give it back.

spec/crdt.md §4.6 now says what an un-merge takes of such a delta comes back only with a copy; spec/commits.md §5 already joins such a copy as a late part. Options: the oracle gives both nodes the bodies dropped at both on a flipped aspect where the states differ (as `alike_exchanged` does for bodies dropped at one node), or a rule keeping such a write (a residue of a body dropped live). The first is a harness change; the copy rarely exists in practice (hosts delete the body after the bound), so a node may read less for good.
