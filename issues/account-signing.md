# Sign as the account; no per-node data in groups
> Summary: Direction (Tess): outsiders verify a commit as from its account by an account signing key every node of it holds; the node's own signature travels encrypted to the account's own nodes, so only they see which node wrote it. Groups carry no per-node data. Outside, the account is its signing chain: a key actor with rotating epochs, its node list on a private devices actor. Removing a node closes the signing epoch, and what still counts of a closed epoch is decided as for any ended standing (the demoter-frontier rule with addenda, spec/permissions.md §5): the rotation's list, writers' addenda, and the account's own later listings. Modelled (`roles-model epochs`). Prerequisite of issues/net-rework.md.
> Priority: normal
> Tags: perm, crypto, sync, spec

## Today
- Every commit is signed by a node key. Readers check it against the node's chain to an account with rights, so each node places its chain certificates and node card (`keys.0`, `node.0`, `reach.0`) under every label it syncs, readers by deposit, plus `seen.0` heartbeats (spec/permissions.md §4, spec/sync.md §3). Per (node, label); about 13 KB once and 2.4 MB a day under a 1000-member label (notes/chat-cases.md, footnote 44), redone under every label when a node is added or its card changes.

## Principle
To everyone outside an account P, P is its **signing chain**: signing epochs, each a key every node of P holds, each rotation signed at the epoch it closes. A commit of P counts as P's while its epoch is open; closing an epoch ends the standing of everything signed at it, exactly as a demotion ends a holder's standing on a thing. What survives an ended standing is decided by one rule at both levels:
1. what the ender had received (a demotion's frontier; a rotation's list);
2. what other writers of the thing had received by the ender's `ts` (addenda, spec/permissions.md §5.2, as built);
3. the party's own later word where it stands again (a re-grant revives a holder's writes; a listing at a counting epoch revives the commits of P it names).

Nothing reads the author's timestamps. Nothing reads the inner (node) signature: it only guides what P's honest nodes list. Every rule below follows from this; node removal and account demotion are the same rule at the account and at the thing.

## Design
- **Signing**: each account has a signing key per epoch, held by every node that may act as it. A commit is signed with it; outside the account that is all there is. The commit also carries the node's own signature over the same digest, encrypted under a secret only the account's own nodes hold.
- **Validating** outside the account: the commit counts as P's (the epoch rule, Removal), and P's roles on the target, judged as any actor's (spec/permissions.md §5). No node list, no chain certificates of nodes. An author acting through other actors (an account holds write on a realm R, the group grants R) is validated through their rosters: every actor's roster is readable by readers of what it holds a role on (Tess; spec/requirements.md, Confidentiality between readers). An account has no such roster (Account shape).
- **Removing a node** closes the signing epoch (Removal).
- **Connections** prove the account by its signing key (issues/net-connect.md, net-shared-set.md); a node's identity is shown only to its own account's nodes.
- **Leaves groups**: chain certificates of nodes, node cards, reader deposits of them, heartbeats, `reach.0`. What each did, and where it goes:
  - authorization of a node: the account signature (above);
  - `reach.0`: own nodes' addresses under the account's own sync ID; other accounts' nodes are not found off the LAN for now (issues/net-connect.md);
  - heartbeat `stranded` (the re-seal trigger): exchanged with a connected holder that may re-seal, else a message to the group's admins' inboxes (design);
  - heartbeat suspect lists (repair, spec/sync.md §7): exchanged on a connection;
  - `last_sync`: the node's own record of its passes.
  - nodes that store a group without reading it (issues/net-one-peer.md): not listed in the group. An account lists the nodes that serve its data (key and addresses), written by its own nodes, on a thing its co-readers read (its profile); readers find them through the accounts the group names and open what they serve by key proof. Such a node is not one of the account's nodes (it holds no account key); the account administers it. Any account whose groups a node serves (its own box, or another's by that node's consent) may list it.
- **Stays**: write stamps for nodes that cannot read (per sync ID write keys); link keys sign as themselves.

## Account shape
Readers of what an account holds roles on may read the rosters of actors holding roles there, and an account's holders are its nodes; contacts also read the account thing (its profile). So the node list cannot live in the account's own `roles.0`.
- **Outside, the account is a key actor with epochs**: per epoch a signing key and a KEM key, minted by the signing chain from its tid (the creation record commits to the first signing key). It holds admin on itself and shows no roster. Boxes to it are sealed to its current KEM key. A node removal rotates both keys, so a removed node opens no later box to the account.
- **Inside**: the account's keys, its nodes' grants and the inner-signature secret live on a private thing only its own nodes read (its **devices actor** D). D's roster is the node list; nothing outside the account syncs it. D's role timeline is an ordinary one, every write on it signed as P and judged as P's (P is D's admin): the roster is what P's nodes read to decide what they write, never a judgment input.
- **Profile**: an ordinary thing the account is first admin of, read by contacts (name, the nodes that serve its data, issues/net-one-peer.md). Not an actor.
- **Teams, bots, shared identities** whose holders are accounts stay ordinary actors with readable rosters (like realms) and act through a member account's signature; only accounts sign. spec/permissions.md §1's "account" (anything apps treat as a user, team, bot or shared identity) splits accordingly.
- Key actors gain rotating keys; today's are fixed (spec/crypto.md §3). Link keys stay fixed.

## Removal
Outsiders on a group see no demotion of anyone when P removes node X, only P's signing rotation, and cannot tell X's commits from its siblings'. So the standing that ends is "P at epoch e", and the frontier's role moves to the rotation.
- **Removal is the rotation**: the edge write removing X from D and the rotation of P's signing and KEM epochs are one role commit. X is not boxed the new epoch. Rotating what P holds roles on (G's roles) is unchanged (spec/permissions.md §3).
- **The epoch rule**: a commit c signed by P at epoch e counts as P's while e is open at the reader. Once a counting rotation closes e, c counts iff a counting **listing** of P's chain names it, or an addendum on the thing vouches for it against the rotation (below). It filters before spec/permissions.md §5: a commit that does not count is invalid in both layers; one that counts is judged as P's there, as today. Never by `ts`, never by counters.
- **The rotation's list**: the rotator's receipts, as a demotion's frontier is the demoter's (spec/permissions.md §5.1): every commit of P at the epochs it closes that the rotator had received as of the rotation, of any kind (content, snapshots, role commits, listings), less covered commits (below), no ephemeral commits. Stored as one list part per label of P's the rotator opens, each part naming the commits with an entry under that label (parts on things in the label's closure, certificates placed there), pinned by the header like any part. The rotation's copy under L carries L's part. A node applies the rotation as any multi-part commit: once it holds the parts for the labels it holds, pending until then (spec/commits.md §4). So closing a label's commits and listing them are one event at the reader; nothing flickers in the common case.
  - Within a label part the list is a tree over per-thing lists; a per-thing list with its path is a chain certificate of the commits it names (spec/permissions.md §4) and goes where they go: a node that holds a thing's list and syncs another label whose closure holds the thing places it there. A thing in two groups, listed under one, reads alike at readers of either once anyone holding both has synced the other. No separate carrying rule.
  - A label the rotator did not open gets no part: the rotation applies there with nothing listed, and P's commits under it count only once listed or vouched (below). What a removed node wrote under such a label is void at once wherever the rotation is held, as today's removal voids a removed node's writes wherever it is held.
- **Listings** (P's later word): a role commit of P at a counting epoch whose list parts, in the rotation's form and placement, name commits of P at closed epochs. A node of P writes one, per label it opens, after each rotation it holds, naming what nothing it holds names:
  - its own commits at the closed epochs, of any kind (an offline sibling's late writes, role commits included, judged by the inner signature);
  - others' commits it received before the earliest counting rotation it holds closing that epoch (the same receipts rule as the rotator's; later concurrent closers do not widen its window);
  - every commit of a node re-added to P (today's re-grant revives all).
  - A listing is an ordinary commit at its epoch: when that epoch closes, the next rotation lists it (one ID for its whole list). Listings compose by union, so a long one is several commits.
- **Addenda** (spec/permissions.md §5.2, as built): a writer of a thing that holds a rotation of P's writes an addendum against it listing P's plain-content commits on the thing at the closed epochs it had received before the rotation's `ts` that the rotation's part for its label does not list; it answers every rotation it holds, whichever line it sides with (its receipts are facts; its readers may side otherwise), and again whenever what it holds changes. Judged as built, with the rotation in the removal's place: the commit ordered before the rotation and the addendum after, per part (a part on N by a writer of N, the Acting part by a writer of the actor; the commit counts all or nothing), plain content only (no snapshot, no purge request, no role-class or admin-only aspect). So an edit of P's survives wherever any writer of the thing received it in time, whether or not any node of P did; and the stated limit of spec/requirements.md Permissions holds here too: a writer lying about when it received an edit can make a removed node's edit count.
- **Covered commits**: a list or addendum leaves out a commit covered as the lister's own compaction would drop it (every part in scope dropped as covered or droppable, every such snapshot received as of the cut and judged valid by the lister then): a valid snapshot vouches for what it carries. Required by spec/requirements.md Cost and Scale: an epoch spans every commit of the account since its last node removal, and listing them compacted or not keeps 32 B per commit the account ever wrote at every reader of every label it is under (Rejected). This differs from the node-level frontier as built, which lists every received commit (the saving there is a few percent): at the account level it is the difference between O(uncompacted tail) and O(history). Residual, as for today's frontiers: a commit left out under a snapshot that later turns invalid loses its writes where the snapshot's copy is the only word for them, unless a party that still holds it answers again (the model's honest parties answer after every change and lose nothing; the addenda model, where they answer once, lost about 2 writes in a million worlds).
- **What stays lost** until listed: a removed node's edit no node of P and no writer of the thing had received by the rotation's `ts`; its purge requests and role writes the rotator had not received (no witness holds the right; listings only re-assert P's own nodes' writes). An unaware sibling that rotated before the removal reached it lists what it had received, the removed node's later writes included (today's concurrent-demoter limit).
- **Bound**: per rotation and label, one ID per uncompacted commit of P under that label at the closed epochs, plus the tree's paths; over the account's life one ID per commit, two when a listing saved it late. List parts are large values (chunked by link). The role-class and purge-request commits of a part keep today's list bound (spec/requirements.md Cost: a removal whose role part alone passes it cannot be written); plain content past the bound goes into the rotator's own later listings, as a demoter's overflow goes into its addenda. Retires issues/frontier-certificate-cost.md.
- **Demoting an account** (A, an admin of G, demotes P): today's frontier, addenda, trust set and disputes, with the author an account; A lists P's commits it received, at any epoch. The epoch rule and A's demotion are independent conjuncts (modelled).
- **Inside the account**: P's nodes judge P's commits by the same rule, so they read like outsiders; the node removal carries no frontier of its own. Inner signatures and D's roster decide only what a node of P writes: what it lists, which fork it merges out, which line it sides with.
- **Forks and a thief**: P's signing epochs form a DAG of rotations, each signed at an epoch it supersedes.
  - Concurrent rotations at one epoch fork; both count. A **merge** supersedes several epochs, naming the fork rotations it accepts; a rotation it supersedes without naming is **rejected**, with every epoch below it, provided the merge itself counts. A rotation rejects nothing on its own chain, and one signed at e rejects nothing on a line through e: a line is contested only from below, by a merge signed under the other fork, so a removed node holding the closed epoch cannot void its removal from above, only fork (an ill-formed supersedes entry is ignored, never an error). Counting is well founded: a rotation is decided once its parent and every rotation rejecting it are, void when the parent is void or a counting one rejects it. A node of P merges out every fork it receives, superseding the other line whole (every epoch of it its own line has not superseded), so a rejected line has no epoch left open.
  - Two lines each rejecting the other are an **account-level dispute**, settled per node by its side, as admin disputes are (notes/disputes.md, option F): the trust set names the line; the default is the line holding the first-received of the contested rotations, so a node that held the removal first cannot be taken over; P's own nodes side with the line D's roster authorizes. A thief acting first wins where its fork came first (accepted today). Until the owner's merge arrives, a thief's uncontested counter-merge voids the owner's line at nodes holding it (transient: P's nodes merge out every fork they receive, and a fork placed under P's labels reaches them when they sync).
  - A fork by a removed node is void once the owner's merge is held, at every node siding with the owner, as today's removal cuts a node that does not fight back.

### Model
`core/test/roles-model/src/epochs.rs` (`roles-model -- N epochs`, `-- 0 epochs-exh`): one account with two or three nodes (one removed), witness writers, an optional admin demoting the account, an outsider; every node an observer judged at every prefix of its receipts. Honest nodes list after every rotation they count and witnesses answer every rotation they hold (with what they received before its `ts`), both again after every change; X writes before and after its removal (backdated or forward-dated), forks, counter-merges, vouches at its fork; snapshots by P's nodes or a witness (some fallen); the rotator omits covered commits in half the worlds. Checked: no write by the removed node after its removal counts at an observer siding with the owner that holds a counting honest rotation (the stated exceptions counted apart: the fork window before the owner's merge, an unaware sibling's concurrent rotation, visibility through a witness's valid snapshot); every legitimate write counts in the end iff an honest standing party received it in time (the rotator before its rotation, a node of P before the earliest closer it holds, a writer of the thing before a closer's `ts`), or its author still stands; dropping any honest listing or addendum never makes a write valid; disputes read by side. Judgment is a pure function of the held set and the side, so arrival order cannot matter; the exhaustive family and the random worlds check it at every observer and prefix.
- **Results**: 1M random worlds and the exhaustive family (939,470 worlds): 0 failures of safety, liveness or monotonicity. Counted limits, random worlds: a thief's fork counts at an observer until the owner's merge arrives, 251k (observer, prefix) pairs; an unaware sibling's concurrent rotation or the addenda against it list the removed node's later writes, 6.0k; a hostile write visible through a witness's valid snapshot, 340k (today's stated limit). Disputes in 19% of worlds (half the worlds have a thief fork); 219k observer-prefixes side with the thief, every one where its fork arrived before the removal; the side changed the reading in 2.8M observer-prefixes. Leaving covered commits out lost no write (honest parties answer again after a snapshot falls) and cut listed IDs by 13% in these small worlds (42% of the lists carry a snapshot); in a long-lived account the cut is the whole compacted history.

### What else names a node
- Header `author`: becomes the account, plus the signing epoch; the inner signature (node tid, signature over the commit ID) sealed to P's nodes beside `signature`.
- The §5.1 order key `(ts, author node, commit ID)`: the account; the commit ID already breaks ties.
- Session dots `Session(tid, nonce)` (spec/crdt.md §2.2) name the node in every content write and snapshot copy: the account's tid, the nonce still random per node session. One session's commits stay linkable to each other (stated).
- Departs mark `(ts, signing node)` (spec/crypto.md §4, Names): `(ts, author, blind)`, since siblings may sign role commits at one `ts`; `blind` is 16 random header bytes.
- Trust-set seeds "by M when M is a node or by any node that reaches M": actors. The trust set also names signing-chain lines (Forks and a thief).
- An account's creation record names the node that made it as first admin (spec/permissions.md §1): the account's tid commits to its first signing key instead, and a node names its account, not itself, as first admin of what it makes (a node first admin is per-node data in every chain; a node delete base signs with the node key, spec/crypto.md §7).
- No node: delete keys (level 2 is the header actor's write secret), frontiers (commit IDs), possession, label and upload signatures; boxes to nodes exist only in D's `secrets.0`.
- Per node, outside groups: dialable keys (LAN announce, co-reader open: issues/net-connect.md), inbox blobs to a node over a direct connection.

### Rejected
- One account-wide received list in the rotation: shows every label's readers the IDs and count of P's commits in other labels.
- Lists as separate commits after the rotation: every reader reads P's content under a label as invalid until its list arrives, on every node removal; and each list needs listing again at every later rotation.
- Closing per label (a label's commits stay open until a list for it arrives): a removed node keeps writing as P under every label no remaining node of P has synced since the removal, and nobody but P's nodes can close it; today anyone carrying the removal cuts it.
- Carrying as re-vouching by P's nodes: needs a node of P on both labels; the certificate rule moves the per-thing list with anyone holding both.
- Listing every commit, compacted or not (the addenda decision for node frontiers, where it saved about 3%): with account-wide epochs it is O(history) at every reader.
- P's later word as addenda only (plain content, ordered before the rotation): an offline sibling's late role writes and post-closing content would have to be re-issued, a client-side rebase of its log; a listing re-asserts them by ID.
- Rotations rejecting siblings at their own epoch: a removed node holding the closed epoch could void the removal from above; made from below it is a fork the owner merges out.
- Revealing the removed node's marks (a tag key per node; verifier-local-revocation group signatures): an absent mark proves nothing unless every honest signature carries one, which shows every node; such group signatures are pairing crypto outside the suite.
- Per-node signing subkeys certified to readers: per-node data under every label.

## Open
- (Tess) The default side in an account-level dispute is the line holding the first-received contested rotation, and P's nodes follow D's roster; whether winning one should need something a thief lacks is the recovery redesign (issues/recovery-redesign.md). With no recovery key a thief holding a current node is a node of P and nothing in the data tells the lines apart.
- (Tess) Covered commits are left out of the rotation's lists and of addenda against a rotation (Covered commits above), where the node-level frontier as built lists every received commit. If both should read alike, the node-level rule is the one to change (it is O(history) at the account level either way).
- (Tess) spec/requirements.md: Cost on removal becomes "the account's writes since its last signing rotation that compaction may not drop yet, once per label they are under, plus its role-class and purge writes under the existing bound". Permissions: "a node's removal is the account's signing rotation; which of the account's edits at the closed epoch count is judged by what the rotator, the account's other nodes and the other writers had received, never by the author's own timestamps".
- (Tess) Witnesses across accounts for a closed epoch (writers of a thing add addenda against P's rotation as against a demotion). Proposed yes: it is the one rule, and it is what saves P's history in a group no remaining node of P had synced. Cost: every writer of every thing P wrote on compares its receipts with each rotation of P it holds.
- (design) Which role on the devices actor lets a node sign as its account; nodes acting for several accounts.
- (design) Session dots and the causal shape of commits (two concurrent commits of one account come from different nodes) still let readers group some commits by node; hosts and direct peers see which connection uploaded each. State the unlinkability guarantee at that scope in spec/requirements.md.
- (design) Inner-signature size per commit against spec/requirements.md Cost (a fixed overhead of a few hundred bytes).
- (design) List parts pinned flat in the header (reveals the label count to every reader of every label) or by a Merkle root with a path per part (about its log). Padding the path to a fixed depth is a picked number.

## Decided (Tess, coordinator)
- Account disputes: no special handling; the simplest rule to build (the line holding the first-received contested rotation, as the design says). The recovery redesign may revisit it later; not a goal now.
- Covered commits are left out of lists and addenda at both levels (the member-level addenda rule changes to match).
- Writers of other accounts may act as witnesses against an account's rotation (the one rule).
