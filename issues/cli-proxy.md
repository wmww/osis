## Follows from it
- **Thin clients' sharing and sync rows** (from (c)): `grant`, `change_edge`, `share_into`, `remove_edge_of`, `leave`, `make_link`, `sync`, `unsync`, `sync_now` and `wait_synced` are Owner only: the engine flows they call act with the node's authority and take no actor, so a client row would be a confused deputy. Giving them an actor is the rest of `Osis::act_as` (issues/grant-rename-multi-actor.md).
- **Thin clients' schemas** (from (c)): `register_schema_source` is Owner only (it stays in the engine's store, process-wide, keyed by version: a client's could retype the node's aspects). A client's typed write reaches the node untyped unless the node's owner registered the schema. Needs per-session schemas at the replay, or a decision that the owner registers them.
- **Transport slots are not tier-aware** (from (c), for (d)'s `client_listen`): `link::serve` caps connections at `max_served`, counting thin clients the engine counts apart, and a flood from many sources fills it before Auth: a new client is closed on arrival until `serve_idle`. Harmless on a dialable key known to co-readers; a public client listener needs slots of its own.
- **Port worker panic** (from (c)): a panic ends the one worker thread silently; `Port::kick` keeps sending to it, so every session's calls wait out the stall timeout (4 min measured), then fail, and the Owner CLI with them. No client input known to reach one. Respawning in `kick` and ending the held sessions would fix it.
- **A removed client's actor stays** (from (c)): placed in the home group with its secrets forever; one record per client ever added. Its commits' certificates may need it.
- **Stall bound for clients** (from (c)): a client's `commits` costs O(the store's changes), a `write` of `MAX_BATCH_OPS` about 1.8 s, as the Owner's; a client is less trusted than the Owner and these share one worker thread. Not bounded further.
- **One port worker per database** (first follow-up of (b)): every connection's requests run on one thread, so a slow call stalls every client; measured worst row `write` of 1024 sets about 1.8 s. Per-connection workers (bounded by `max_clients`) would not shorten that stall: the write's commit holds `eng` throughout (one engine transaction, notes/client-port.md Port stall bounds), which every other call, local or remote, and the sync driver also wait on. They would only overlap off-lock work (reply and Notify encoding, `settle` waiting on the node's dispatcher, which a slow app callback in the node process prolongs). Revisit with (c)/(d), when several remote clients share a node; the CLI daemon case has one client at a time.
- issues/cli-own-admission.md's last point (`hosting set` while serving) stays open: hosting calls are not port rows in (b) (`hosting set` through a daemon is Unsupported); it closes when they are.
- notes/arbitrary-values-table.md rows for the numbers listed in notes/client-port.md Picked numbers, with the branch that adds each.
