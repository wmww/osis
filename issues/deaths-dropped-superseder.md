# A body arriving after a residue went may die later
> Summary: Dropped bodies keep their writes value-less as residues (`retention/deaths.rs`) only while a held bounded body sorts at or before them, so a body arriving later, older than every held bounded body, can die later than at a node that held it before the drop (never earlier); keeping residues forever would be exact.
> Tags: retention, node, owner-call

Fixed: deaths are a function of the applied set while the residue lives (retention.rs `deaths_alike_after_*`; the order harness compares every order with a whole replay). A residue W dates only writes with a lower stamp, so it is dropped once no held bounded body sorts at or before it. A body V arriving after that, with W its earliest superseder, dies at a later superseder (bounded by what superseded W): kept longer, under keep at most past its bound by up to that gap.

Owner question: keep residues forever (exact; about 110 bytes per dropped body measured, 70 of them the record key, for a one-element set add, growth like tombstones, spec/requirements.md Scale; keeps the timing of every erased write under keep at most) or accept the window above (current)?

Growth under the current rule: residues stay while the oldest held bounded body does, so one long-held body (a live write never replaced, not yet covered by a compaction) keeps every later residue, and each whole replay reads them all. A dropped snapshot's residue keeps only copies whose own commit is neither held nor a residue (else it held the aspect's state per compaction).

Same family, in-order path (deaths-window-seed review; fixed in deaths-window-2): the replay plays a write dropped without a residue (or a residue-less dropped snapshot's copy) only with the state, so a body that arrived after that drop and died at it was dated there, while once a later in-order write killed it in the state a whole replay dates the body at a later superseder; `note_deaths` kept the earlier death, and a later window rested on records that were not the replay's (its debug check fired: strays seed 21812; end comparison: 1427). Now `note_deaths` replays the aspect whole when a death's write, whose own commit does not play it, is stamped at or after `first_bare`, or a held bounded snapshot or import after it lacks it in its frontier (strays seed 67421: such a write before the first held bounded body superseded a copy in that snapshot; once killed, a replay dates the copy at its killer) (`retention.rs late_deltas_windowed_found_histories_*`).

Same family, at the drop (fixed in deaths-window-2; strays seed 32432, window debug check; 2423 at the end, on main too): D supersedes a write W whose own commit does not play it, carried by a held bounded snapshot S after D that had not seen D (W died before S: no death for S). D drops without a residue (no held bounded body sorts at or before it): a whole replay plays W from S and dates it at a later superseder, after S, so S gained a death the records lacked. Now such a drop (a held bounded snapshot or import after it whose frontier lacks it, `uncovered_copies_after`) replays the aspect whole: the records follow the replay (S keeps the later death, the residue limit above). Keeping D's residue while S is held would be exact instead.
