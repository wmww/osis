# Connecting: which nodes connect, and the lower network layer
> Summary: The first of the two layers (Tess): which nodes a node connects to. Proposed: on a LAN, every osis node connects to every other (an announcement of a per-join key and port, nothing else; no tag, lan secret or signature), kept while issues/net-shared-set.md finds something shared or a delivery is pending; beyond a LAN, known addresses and own nodes only (groups carry no node addresses); one stable listening key, no automatic rotation; identity shown only to known actors (private recognition on the LAN), shared set only with them. Plus Android. One-way knowledge is a configuration choice the design keeps open (Tess). Open: meeting points for nodes that never met on different networks; a mesh without broadcast across hops is issues/net-mesh-discovery.md. Part of issues/net-rework.md.
> Priority: normal
> Tags: transport, node, spec

## Today (code as built)
- Every build runs the LAN loop on a node with direct sync and `Config::lan` on (the default); the CLI's short commands keep it off (notes/cli.md).
- Dialed (`conn.rs` `wanted_hosts`): config hosts, labels' and hints' non-`peer://` hosts, queued deposits' hosts, co-readers and a link's sharer (row 28). Co-readers (`peers.rs` `peers`): node things with a held `reach.0` that `may_read` a synced label's group, at most 8 per label and 64 in all, by node tid; an unreachable 8 hide a reachable 9th (row 36).
- `reach.0` (dialable key, global addresses, relays, lan secret) goes under every synced label.
- LAN: a 124-byte announcement of a tag (from the lan secret), a per-join key, a port and a signature by the dialable key sealed under the lan secret. A receiver matches it only against co-readers it knows from `reach.0`, only from a private source address (`lan.rs` `recv` and `peers.rs` `lan_seen`, row 30), within ±1 ten-minute window (row 34), and runs a candidate, heard-count, replay-binding and route-switching machine on the match (notes/peers.md LAN).
- Rotation (`rotate_reach`): only by hand (`rotate_peer_address`; notes/peers.md, No automatic rotation). It drops the old listener at once, so a node that knows only the old key and hears the new `reach.0` from nobody is cut off for good (rest of row 10).
- So a node connects only to nodes it already knows something of. Two nodes holding the same thing that never met (new devices on both sides; nodes granted by nodes now gone) never connect, on a LAN or off it.

## Rules
- **Connect, then recognise, then share.** A node connects to every osis node on its LAN. The two then find which known actors each is (issues/net-shared-set.md Words: own account, contacts, holders of anything it holds through the rosters it holds, and a key actor listed by static entry on a roster or in a head claim set it holds), proving the actors they act for (its account by the account signing key, issues/account-signing.md; a key actor such as a link or recovery key by its signing key, matched to its static entry). The shared set's entries for what a node reads go only to a node that proved a known actor; a node serving sync IDs it cannot read sends their entries to every connection, since they name no actor of its own (issues/net-shared-set.md, Peers that cannot read).
- **Identity only to known actors** (spec/requirements.md, Offline and network; Tess): a node must not show which node or account it is to a node that does not already know it. So the handshake is a private mutual recognition, not a plain exchange: each side proves an actor only to a side that already knows that actor. Sketch, unexamined: two actors that know each other's keys derive a pairwise secret (static DH of their KEM keys); each side sends a filter of beacons keyed by that secret and the connection's nonces, one per (own actor, known actor), the mechanism of issues/net-shared-set.md over pairwise secrets. A stranger sees random beacons; a match tells both who the other is, then the actors are proved. A key actor has no partner to pair with: it is recognised by its static entry, keyed by the connection's nonces, which a node holding the roster or head claim set listing it matches. Unsure (design): the cost, one beacon per known actor (a member of a huge group knows 10^5), and how a new node of a known account computes it (from the account's secrets). An account's KEM keys change with each rotation of its read role (spec/crypto.md §2), so a side holding an older keyset of the other account derives a different secret and the two don't recognise each other until it catches up: use a key that does not rotate, or a beacon per current epoch.
- **One-way knowledge (Tess).** Mutual recognition needs each side to hold the other's keys. Where only one does (the recipient of a share request has not added its sender; a member granted by a node that is now gone meets a node that never saw the grant), the two do not recognise each other and share nothing directly: the request or the data waits for a node that knows both, or a node serving the data without reading it. Design when one side knows the other's `keys.0`: it shows "I hold your keys" (a beacon of a fresh X25519 scalar against the other's KEM key, computable by the key's holder alone, under the connection's nonces), and a match lets the holder reveal itself and the two prove their actors; one proof per held `keys.0`, so one hash per contact and per holder of the node's groups. Whether a device answers someone holding its card is a product question tied to the sharing stories (accounts, adding people to chats, on and off the internet): the design must not constrain it, so either behaviour stays configurable, and the default does not matter yet (Tess). First build: no answer, the waiting shown as pending (issues/net-first-contact.md). Allowing it changes recognition here and nothing in issues/net-shared-set.md.
- A first build may exchange identities plainly on the LAN: that breaks the requirement and must be listed as a known gap until the private handshake lands. Known gap as built (issues/net-shared-set.md step 2): a dialer proves its node, account and key actors to any key it dials, a listener to any dialer proving an actor whose record it holds. Off the LAN the dialer proves first, only to the key it chose to dial; the listener proves only to a dialer that proved a known actor (issues/net-shared-set.md).
- Connecting never depends on what the other node holds; what opens is the shared set's job.

## On a LAN: connect to every osis node
- **Announcement**: `"osis lan 2"` || per-join transport key (32) || be16 port: 44 bytes, to the same targets as today. No tag, no signature, no lan secret: nothing stable across networks, nothing meaningful to anyone, and nothing to match.
- **Dialing**: a node dials every announcer it hears, at the source address and announced port, with an ephemeral key. Once per pair: of two nodes that hear each other only one connection stays (Unsure (implementor): e.g. the dialer names its own announced key in the session and the higher announced key closes its duplicate). Dials go out paced (a few at once), with no cap on how many are dialed in the end.
- **Keeping**: a connection stays while the shared set has something, an Offer or Lost of this node is unanswered, or a delivery of this node is pending that the peer may take (issues/net-shared-set.md Deposits: it proved an actor holding the inbox, or serves the inbox unread); one with none of these closes, and is dialed again when this node's set of sync IDs or pending deliveries grows (its own change, which it knows), or either side rejoins (a fresh key is a fresh announcement). The other side's growth reaches it the same way: the side that grew dials.
- **Preferring the LAN**: once a LAN connection's peer is the device of a record this node already has a connection with (issues/net-reconnect.md Resume: the same r), the other connections to it close and it is not dialed elsewhere while the LAN one lasts. This replaces the candidate, heard, bound and `lan_probe`/`lan_switched` machinery. A peer may recognise the same device across connections (Tess; spec/requirements.md), which is what tells two connections to one device from two devices of one account.
- **Strangers**: any node on the LAN can already dial an announced key today. Served sessions stay bounded by `max_served` (oldest that opened nothing evicted). Announcements are unauthenticated, so a forged one costs one dial; pacing and at most one pending dial per source address bound a flood.
- **What goes**: the lan secret (`reach.0` field 2), LAN tags and windows, the sealed signature (osis-crypto `seal_lan_sig`/`open_lan_sig`, `core/test/vectors/lan/announce.vec`), the source-address filter (row 30 falls out: routed meshes, CGNAT and global sources all work), the replay binding, and rows 34 and 36 for the LAN (nothing to match).
- A link's redeemer on a LAN then finds K with no special case: it connects to every LAN node, proves the link key (a known actor to every node holding K's roster or head claim set: the sharer's nodes, other readers, a box of K), and K's sync ID matches on the link key's static entry through the shared set (row 6, with issues/net-first-contact.md). The sharer need not be online.
- Two accounts that both hold T but neither holds the other's keys do not share over their connection; they sync through any node that knows both. Accepted for now, can change later (Tess).
- Unsure (design): a big LAN (hundreds of osis nodes) makes every pair connect once per join and run the shared set; check its cost at 10^3 nodes with issues/net-shared-set.md's numbers before shipping. Rejected: capping LAN connections (some pair that shares a group would never meet).

## Beyond a LAN: known addresses only
- Groups carry no node addresses (issues/account-signing.md). Off the LAN a node dials configured addresses, a held link's connect hints, its own account's nodes, whose addresses live under the account's own sync ID, and the serving nodes a contact lists on its profile (issues/net-one-peer.md: boxes that store data unread; the listing is read by contacts, so it is a way to dial, never the way a group's holders learn who serves it: that is the shared set, issues/net-shared-set.md Peers that cannot read). Other accounts' nodes are found only through these, on a LAN, or once a lookup exists (Nodes that never met, below).
- Which to keep connected is a priority, never a filter on what may open. The cap counts connected nodes that share something, never candidates, so an unreachable few never hide a reachable one (row 36). Unsure (design): the target per group and the total (today 8 and 64), through notes/arbitrary-values.md.

## The listening key
- One listening key per node is a design choice, not a requirement: the dialable key, dialed by key from config, links and own account data, also its key as a peer of groups it cannot read (issues/net-one-peer.md). LAN listeners take a fresh key per network join, since an announcement is heard by strangers and a stable key in it would let anyone track the device across networks (spec/sync.md §10). The dialable listener shows its stable key in TLS to any dialer, scanners included: tracking of a node reachable at global addresses; a later fix makes the dialer prove it knows the key first (issues/privacy.md).
- One key for dialing, serving and the client port (issues/net-one-peer.md step 2).
- **No automatic rotation** (Built; notes/peers.md, with the why): the key stays put; `rotate_peer_address` is the only rotation, an explicit user act; ending a direct link rotates nothing.
- Not built, for `rotate_peer_address`: keep the old listener until the new key reached every own node and connected peer, or a bound passes (Unsure (design)), so connected peers learn the new key before the old one goes.

## Nodes that never met, on different networks
- Two nodes of different accounts on different networks with no common node online and no known address cannot connect: no relay or default meeting point is in builds (decided). Off a LAN that is the usual case for phones and home machines, whose addresses change and who are not reachable. On a LAN or with any common node online they meet as above.
- Needed: a meeting point both can name without knowing the other node. Per-sync-ID announcement is rejected (Tess). What both do know is the other's account (roster, contacts).
- Not part of this rework (it is done without it); a later job. Design when needed: a lookup by account: each node registers once, under a beacon of its own account's key, at the meeting points it uses; a node looking for co-holders looks up the accounts of its groups' holders. Meeting points are relays (any node relaying, listed in `reach.0` as relays are) or an opt-in DHT (notes/networking.md decision 3). One record per node, not per sync ID. It must keep identity to known actors (above).
- A mesh with no broadcast across hops (row 31) is this case unless extra announcement targets are configured: the target is issues/net-mesh-discovery.md. Rejected here: every node re-announcing the (address, key, port) it hears one hop on (n² datagrams per announcement round on a segment of n nodes, reaches two hops and not three, and carries addresses onto segments their owners never announced on).

## Android and client isolation
- Android: `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`, `ACCESS_WIFI_STATE`, `CHANGE_WIFI_MULTICAST_STATE` and a held multicast lock over JNI; `/proc/net/if_inet6` may be unreadable on newer APIs. Unverified, needs a real phone (issues/no-server-audit.md, Builds).
- Wi-Fi client isolation blocks LAN discovery entirely; nothing to do but fall back to the above.
- Sharing the announcement port needs `SO_REUSEADDR` semantics of Linux and Android. On BSD and macOS a second node on one machine fails the bind (logged, no LAN for it): `SO_REUSEPORT` there, untested.
