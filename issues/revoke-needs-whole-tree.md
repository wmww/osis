# Safe revocation needs one node holding everything the member could write
> Summary: a demotion's frontier is the revoker's own receipts (spec/permissions.md §5.1), and writes it had not received are lost until re-grant (spec/requirements.md Permissions), so the pre-sync (§5.3) must download, decrypt and verify every label of every thing the member could write before stamping. spec/requirements.md also says total data may exceed what any node tracks and removal cost is "never to content or history". Both cannot hold: in a tree larger than any one node, every removal loses some of the member's data, or the removal is made from a node sized to the tree. The design states the rule and the pre-sync, not this consequence.
> Tags: permissions, spec, owner-call

Scale: removing a device of a person who writes to 500 groups syncs 500 labels first; removing a member from a 100 GB organisation from a phone that holds 2 GB of it loses that member's unseen edits in the other 98 GB (`WaitForSync::Required` fails, `Try`/`Skip` lose). Organisations are told to demote from a node that tracks the tree (notes/api.md Demotion), which reintroduces a node that holds everything.

## Promising direction (discussed with the owner; not a fixed decision, the implementer may change it or surface problems)
**Receipts-only sync.** The frontier needs, per (aspect, Bob), the last edit number the remover has seen; it never needs the content. A commit's signed header pins its deltas by hash, so verifying the header is enough evidence that the write existed (Bob cannot swap content under it later). Large values are chunked out, large deltas are paged out, and compaction deletes covered commits at servers, so the commit blobs of a group are small and few relative to its data (roughly the uncompacted commits at about a kilobyte each). So: a per-group sync mode that opens the label, lists entries, fetches commit blobs only (chunk and page entries carry filler wraps and are told apart without opening), decrypts, checks the signature, records receipts per (tid, ak, author node) in the existing receipt record, and keeps and applies nothing. `demote` uses it for every label of the member's reach the device does not hold, so a phone can remove a member without loss while it can reach the servers.

Spec: spec/permissions.md §5.1 "Received means decrypted and verified" becomes header-level: a commit counts as received once its header is decrypted and its signature verified; the frontier still lists counters contiguously received. No wire change.

Risk to watch: the convergence passes. A device in receipts-only mode on a group is an admin that reads the log but must not act as a member there (no uploads, deletes, certificate placements, heartbeats, promotions, compaction). The passes already tell synced labels from server-less served ones; this is a third mode and needs the same gating and an equivalence-harness check (notes/sync.md Work sets), else the device "repairs" a group it does not hold.

Remaining gap after this: servers the remover cannot reach at removal time, and edits that reached another member only over a peer path and never a server. If that matters later, **writer witnesses** close most of it: every writer's ordinary delta carries the per-author received frontier its compaction snapshots already carry (spec/crdt.md §8 `Frontiers`), and a removed member's write counts if the remover's frontier covers it or a valid writer's delta stamped before the cutoff shows it received it, witnesses counted outward from the remover's record (a least fixpoint, so a later-removed witness bootstraps nothing). Writers only: a reader witness would let a reader and an ex-writer combine into write power. Membership writes keep today's rule (that is where the self-witness hole and the non-settling histories of notes/permissions.md live). The receipt record is shared, so nothing from the first step is wasted. Inherent floor for either: Bob's trailing edits that no writer saw before the cutoff look exactly like a backdated forgery and are hidden until re-grant.

Other uses of the receipts-only mode: activity and history views over groups a device does not hold (headers carry author, time, aspects), the own-counter recovery a restored node needs (issues/restored-node-counter-reuse.md), lightweight admin devices (an org root that holds nothing), header-level forensics of rejected writes.

Rejected along the way: trusting the author's timestamp for content (a removed writer plus a colluding reader could inject backdated edits that honest writers then re-stamp and spread: "readers only read" breaks); scoped removals via `until` (works today with no new rule, but needs a device holding the tree to finalize, which cannot be relied on).
