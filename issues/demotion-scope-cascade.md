# A demotion's frontier misses falls its own audit causes
> Summary: `Timeline::demotion_scope` reads falls from the walk before the demotion R; R's audit can drop a write that held up another standing, widening the fall in the settled walk, so the writer's frontier leaves out aspects R puts in question and those writes drop needlessly.
> Tags: permissions

spec/permissions.md §5.1 "Frontier": R embeds what its author had received of X's writes on the aspects R puts in question, i.e. per the settled walk with R. `perm/src/scope.rs` judges standings in the timeline without R. When R's audit drops an earlier write (an empty or partial frontier), the settled walk can differ before R: the dropped write may have been what kept another node's standing (a grant by the demoted node, or the node's own self-grant), so the fall at R is wider than the scope. Example (joint model, `OSIS_PERM_SEED=3`, `queries::demotion_scope_covers_audits_spec_permissions_5_1` with an empty frontier in place of the full one): n0 admin on A0 only through A1, and granted itself write on A0; removing A1's admin edge on A0 drops that self-grant, so n0 falls below write on A0, but the scope lists only A0's role-class aspects for n0. Further failing seeds there: 1 (n0's grant of admin to n2 dropped, n2 falls), 2.

Convergence is unaffected (the frontier is on the wire, all nodes judge alike); writes the author had received are lost. Possible simple fix: compute the scope by appending R with an empty frontier to the timeline and reading its falls and demotions at R (the walk is the oracle; no separate standing computation), then iterate since a wider frontier can narrow the fall again; check the cost against notes/testing.md budgets.
