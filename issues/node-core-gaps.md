# Node-core residue: membership-heavy changes
> Summary: Granting someone new to a large tree still re-judges every change in it (about 12 ms at 10^4 changes, linear); a change becoming valid again re-reads every held change of its aspect; opening still reads every commit header, re-adapts every membership change from the index and restores the walk over its whole input (O(commits), no bodies decoded).
> Tags: node, permissions, performance

Repro: `cargo test --release --workspace --test it scale::membership_change_and_open_cost -- --ignored --nocapture`; flips: `scale::validity_flip_cost_spec_permissions_5_2`.

Done (notes/node.md: Validity, Snapshots vouch for nothing, Snapshots, Import, Open, Rollback): saved walks, the persisted index, journaled rollback, re-judging per changed timeline, the `node/edges` rows, the pass work sets of re-examination; validity that only falls un-merges in place (O(touched pages), no body read: a revoke flip 719 -> 28-40 ms, an invalid delta arriving 704 -> 4-6 ms at 10^4); the re-judge looks things up once per thing and asks perm once per (thing, author, role) (`Timeline::valid_from`): one grant at 10^4 26 -> 12 ms.

Remaining:
- A change that perm evaluates anew (a member new to the component joins it, an out-of-order or dropping write, an epoch start change) still visits every delta of the component: about 1 µs each, now mostly the walk over `by_thing` and the commit metas (memory-bound lookups) plus perm's query for deltas before their author's standing tenure. Skipping deltas whose answer cannot change needs perm to report, per evaluation, which (node, thing) answers moved (e.g. comparing each author's role history and demotions on the thing before and after): perm's timeline update, out of node's reach.
- Validity becoming valid again (a part or a carried write) rebuilds the aspect from every held body (O(aspect history)): re-merging needs them (a register's earlier value, another's write nested under a revalidated creation come back only from the bodies). So does a fallen write that superseded another in the state (a register, reset, flag or move of the invalid delta). A bounded re-merge would need an index of which bodies hold writes nested under or superseded by each delta.
- Open is still O(commits), not O(what changed): at 10^4 content plus 10^4 edge writes 0.35-0.39 s at load 25 (notes/node.md Open; was 0.83-0.85 s before stored headers skipped the delete-key point checks and `node/edges` rows held parsed edges). Each phase is linear: every commit record scanned and its header decoded into `Core::commits` (106 ms; store scan and generic CBOR decoding), the metas and per-aspect indexes (57 ms), every membership commit adapted from its row (76 ms), the saved walk restored (90 ms). Measured and set aside: a walk tag over a persisted hash of its inputs saves only the tag hash (5-8 ms); restoring still gathers the component's `Input` (the timeline holds it) and perm's partition and `Timeline::from_outcome` (items, checkpoints) take most of the rest, so a cheaper restore needs those saved or lazy (osis-perm internals). Persisting perm's adapted records instead of the rows saves about the adaptation's own work (40-50 ms of 76) at the risk of runtime and open adaptation disagreeing; not done. O(changed) needs `Core::commits`, the held set and the per-aspect indexes loaded lazily (every reader takes `&Meta` from an in-memory map today: about 110 call sites) and a perm timeline restorable without its whole input: a redesign of the engine's in-memory indexes.
