# A writer's own rotation lists a chain that skips the removal
> Summary: Settled (spec/crypto.md §4) trusts every rotator's list along the chain, transitively. A writer W (may rotate read) that saw R_s (M's removal) rotates read to e1 with a keyset listing nothing, or s: {F} (W's forged valid claim of s); its history box still lists s, so at owner A e1 supersedes s and is current. A removes W, rotating to e (listing e1: {R_e1}). A node X withheld R_s settles e and re-seals it to M, though both M and W departed before e.
> Tags: security, keys

Not a regression of forged-valid-claim: main accepts the same shape. "A malicious valid rotator can leak e1 anyway" covers e1 only; e here postdates W's departure, and W could not grant M. Reproduced (review of forged-valid-claim, osis-engine `rotation::tests`, setup as `valid_forged_claim_withheld_rotation_spec_crypto_4`): after `lp.sync(0, 1)`, peer 1 calls `osis_keys::rotate` on its held epochs, replaces the read keyset by `keyset(&r.epoch, &r.secret, &list)` (list empty, or `{s: {forge_claim(..)}}`), commits it with `put_secrets`/`put_keysets` and `record`; peer 0 ingests it (current = [e1]) and demotes peer 1; peer 3 ingests all but R_s: e is established and `reseal_candidates` lists M.

Fix directions (none tried): an epoch supersedes s at the node only if its list names every held valid claim of s carrying a departure (A then keeps s current and lists R_s); or supersession from the list alone (drop history-box supersession), which closes only the empty-list variant; or rotations list the departure commits they cover.
