# A snapshot's last death may be dated early
> Summary: Two ways a compaction snapshot's last death can come out earlier than the deaths of the writes it carries, shortening its keep at least: a recompute drops its copy of a never-held entry creation (suspected, not reproduced), and later deaths of commit-form writes it carries are never attributed to it.
> Tags: retention, crdt

Deaths are dated per node by joins (notes/node.md, Deaths dated per node); both cases are in the paths that attribute deaths to snapshots, not in the joins.

1. Recompute (`retention/deaths.rs` `replay_all`, run on validity flips and un-merges only): it removes a snapshot's copies of writes whose own commit is held (`Fragment::remove_writes`). Shape: B (never held here) creates entry k; held P overwrites every field of k; held snapshot S (bounded map) carries k with B's `created` and P's content; held R removes k later. By joins, S's arrival or R's join dates B's creation dead at R on S. The recompute plays S without P's writes, k is left without content, so `remove_writes` drops it, `created` included (a creation stamp lives only with content, `prune_entry`): S's last death comes from P's superseder, possibly before R. Not reproducible with the test schema (no bounded map: `/t/gonemap` drops bodies at their first death, `/t/map` snapshots are unbounded). The crdt property `remove_writes_rejoins_spec_4_8` compares reads, not records, for this reason.
2. `carriers` attributes a death to a held snapshot by its frontier, which covers node-form writes only: a commit-form write (an import's, a commit-form commit's) a snapshot carries that dies after the snapshot arrived gives the snapshot no death (`note_copies` dates those already dead on arrival). Fix sketch: attribute by the snapshot's carried writes per commit-form source (an index like `Core::carried`, for held sources too).
