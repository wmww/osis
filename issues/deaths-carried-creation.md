# A snapshot's last death may be dated early
> Summary: Two ways a compaction snapshot's last death can come out earlier than the deaths of the writes it carries (or missing), shortening its keep at least: later deaths of commit-form writes it carries are never attributed to it, and a recompute dates none for a carried write first superseded before the snapshot. (A third, a recompute dropping a never-held entry creation, went with the crdt fix keeping a stripped entry's creation.)
> Tags: retention, crdt

Deaths are dated per node by joins (notes/node.md, Deaths dated per node); both cases are in the paths that attribute deaths to snapshots, not in the joins.

1. (Addressed: `remove_writes` now keeps an entry's creation whose own dot stays, so the join below dates it; kept for context.) Recompute (`retention/deaths.rs` `replay_all`, run on validity flips and un-merges only): it removes a snapshot's copies of writes whose own commit is held (`Fragment::remove_writes`). Shape: B (never held here) creates entry k; held P overwrites every field of k; held snapshot S (bounded map) carries k with B's `created` and P's content; held R removes k later. By joins, S's arrival or R's join dates B's creation dead at R on S. The recompute plays S without P's writes, k is left without content, so `remove_writes` drops it, `created` included (a creation stamp lives only with content, `prune_entry`): S's last death comes from P's superseder, possibly before R. Not reproducible with the test schema (no bounded map: `/t/gonemap` drops bodies at their first death, `/t/map` snapshots are unbounded). The crdt property `remove_writes_rejoins_spec_4_8` compares reads, not records, for this reason.
2. `carriers` attributes a death to a held snapshot by its frontier, which covers node-form writes only: a commit-form write (an import's, a commit-form commit's) a snapshot carries that dies after the snapshot arrived gives the snapshot no death (`note_copies` dates those already dead on arrival). Fix sketch: attribute by the snapshot's carried writes per commit-form source (an index like `Core::carried`, for held sources too).
3. Recompute, a carried write whose earliest held superseder is before the snapshot's `ts`: `replay_all` kills it before the snapshot plays (no carrier: the snapshot is later), so the snapshot gets no death from it, while by joins `note_copies` or a later superseder's join dates it at the first superseder after the snapshot. A snapshot left with no death is past a relative keep at least, so after a flip or rebuild it may be compacted before the date the joins gave (keep at most only grows). Same as main's exact dating; the dating checks (retention.rs `deaths_never_early_random_*`) therefore accept a snapshot death the recompute lacks when it is after the snapshot's `ts` (e.g. `OSIS_STRAYS_START=20`).
