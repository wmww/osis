# A snapshot's copy of a never-held entry creation is lost in a replay
> Summary: A replay removes a snapshot's copies of played writes (`Fragment::remove_writes`); when every write in a map entry is played elsewhere, the entry goes and with it the creation stamp of a creator never held here, so the creation's later death (at a remove of the key) never reaches the snapshot's record; in order the join keeps it. Suspected, not reproduced.
> Tags: retention, crdt

Shape: B (never held here) creates entry k; held P overwrites every field of k; held snapshot S (bounded, a schema with a relative keep bound on a map) carries k with B's `created` and P's content; held R removes k later. In order, S's join puts B's creation into the state and R's join kills it, dated on S (its frontier covers B): S's last death is R. A replay plays S without P's writes, k is left without content, so `remove_writes` drops it, `created` included (a creation stamp lives only with content, `prune_entry`): S's last death is P's superseder instead. Not reproducible with the test schema (no bounded map: `/t/gonemap` drops bodies at their first death, `/t/map` snapshots are unbounded). Same under the old `remove_dots`. The crdt property `remove_writes_rejoins_spec_4_8` compares reads, not records, for this reason.
