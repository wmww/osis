# Evaluating the role timeline: what is still not incremental
> Summary: Appending to a thing's role history costs about a quarter of a millisecond at 10^4 role writes; a late write in the middle is placed without a re-walk unless something forces one (2-5 ms when nothing later reads it; 0.1-0.3 s when over a hundred later items must be re-judged, about what a re-walk costs); new holders and things named before their `created.0` join by going on from where the walk meets them. Still costly: the late writes that must re-walk, re-judging many dependents, and demotion frontiers that grow with the remover's chain.
> Priority: normal
> Tags: permissions, performance

Stage A1 (notes/redesign-plan.md): the walk's rebuild counts work per update at n and 10n edge writes with replay from a checkpoint (osis-perm `paths`: rebuild the walk; A1 brief 6); items 1 to 3 are of today's walk and are read again against the rebuild.

**Checkpoint replay is the walk (Tess).** Simplest correct wins; a very large number of role changes being fast is not a priority. spec/requirements.md, Cost: a role change arriving out of order may cost in proportion to the role history after it on that thing. The A1 rebuild is one walk per component with a checkpoint per restart point, an update replaying from the earliest key it touches (notes/redesign-plan.md, A1). osis-perm `paths::counts::an_update_costs_what_it_changes_at_n_and_10n` is restated to that bound: an appended write costs the same at n and 10n; a backdated write or a removal costs at most the role history after its key in its component.

Repro: osis-perm `scale::repro_late_write_rejudges_flat`, `scale::repro_evaluate_with_drops_linear` (`osis_perm::commits_walked` at 10^2 and 10^3 edge writes; notes/test-scale.md Scale checks).

Done: notes/perm-impl.md (`Timelines::append`, the found-order rule for D, placed late writes and joining nodes, shared kept states) and "Walk cost" (numbers).

Remaining, in order of expected payoff:
1. Late writes that still re-walk (`Walked::insert`, notes/perm-impl.md): one with a drop of D found at or after it (found by an earlier walk, whose state there is not kept); one a later item writes the same edge of (the later write's restore reads the new value; the difference runs along that edge's later writes, so exactness needs those re-judged with a changed edge record, untried); two ops on one edge; one whose own audit drops something new (D grows: unavoidable). A later item that does otherwise with it only moves the re-walk to that item. Re-judging costs about 0.5 ms per dependent item at 10^4 (they are the costly items to walk: mostly explicit writes on edges of an organization whose audits read many nodes), so it pays only for few dependents; past an eighth of a long rest the write re-walks (`SHADOW_SHARE`). Counted (perm `scale::late_write_placed_without_rewalk`, `repro_late_write_rejudges_flat`): a read grant nothing later reads walks nothing; a removal, an admin grant and a write grant on an organization re-judge their dependents, growing with the history (13, 39 commits walked at 10^3 against 1, 8 at 10^2). Cheaper next: a narrower static dependence (most dependents are flagged because a node that may reach a holder of an organization could be audited on what the new edge reaches; the dynamic reach check skips none of them in the bench), or re-judging only the audit pairs (x, n) the new edges touch. The plain placement is about 2.1 ms at 10^4 at low load: the later-item scan about 0.75 ms, the log splice about 0.55 ms (undo entries are 152 bytes; boxing the applied records would cut it a fifth), item and kept-state shifts about 0.35 ms.
2. A new node that is mint admin of a thing walked before, or two components merging, still evaluate anew.
3. Walk residue from scratch: 178M instructions at 3000 writes (callgrind, `--toggle-collect=osis_perm::walk::drive`); not profiled at 10^4. Certificates at 3000 writes 116M; admins' deltas at 10^4 3.5-6 ms (point overlays).
4. Demotion frontiers grow with the remover's chain (spec/permissions.md §5.1): they list by commit ID (32 bytes each) every role commit among the remover's chain certificates, and, since role commits use commit form, every role commit of the demoted node it received on the things in scope; a long-serving admin's frontier can list thousands. Computing the list (`Timeline::certificates`) takes 7-12 ms at 10^4 (an evaluate 0.27 s). Role history is never compacted (spec/commits.md §5), so nothing bounds the list but the chain's history.
5. Compaction snapshots list every absorbed commit-form commit with its author (spec/crdt.md §8, 47 bytes each), so a `secrets.0` snapshot grows with the thing's rotation count.
6. Carrying frontiers: a demotion on a chain is a certificate of every later grant along it, so its frontier chunks go under every sync ID the chain's holders write to, and role history is never compacted. Not measured. Candidate if it measures large: split the frontier into `chain` (the demoted holder's role commits and the demoter's chain certificates, all a verifier of a later grant asks about) and `content` (the rest), both covered by the signed header; certificates carry `chain` only, `content` goes only where the demoted holder's content is; a projected copy marks `content` "not carried", never "empty" (a header wire change, spec/wire.md §4). Rejected: sharing frontier chunk blobs across sync IDs (links every sync ID of one account at a node keeping them unread).
