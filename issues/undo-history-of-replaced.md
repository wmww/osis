# Undo judges the undone part's history, not the replaced parts'
> Summary: `undo::keeps_now` asks whether the undone part keeps history, but what an undo writes back is the content of the parts it replaced, kept by their own retention. Where the two differ (per-write `retention`, a write untyped by a node that registered a keeping schema, a schema-less writer) an undo either refuses content that is kept or goes through writing back less than the state before (silently).
> Priority: normal
> Tags: engine, undo, design

notes/listener-design.md (Undo writes back only kept history) says a restoring undo is `NotUndoable{ContentGone}` "unless the replaced parts are kept"; spec/crdt.md §4.6 and the code judge the undone delta's own retention (`keeps_now(h, a, now)` in `undone_parts`), its bound from its own `ts`. With one schema-wide history per aspect the two agree. They differ when retention differs between writes of one aspect:
- **Undone part keeps, replaced part does not**: the check passes, `kept_before` leaves the replaced part out (`body_keep` passed), and the inverse writes back as if the edit were a first set: the field reads absent after the undo, not as before. Seen: an undone creation's redo (`t:made.0` written untyped, the undo commit typed by the registered keeping schema) redid without the thing's content.
- **Undone part keeps none, replaced parts keep**: `ContentGone` while the content is kept. Every writer that does not attach the aspect's schema hits it: osis `delete` did (fixed: its removals are now typed by `aspect_key`), the engine undo's own creation reset did (fixed: `undo::schema_of`); untyped app writes (`DynamicAspectKey::untyped`), the CLI and a node without the schema still do.

Both are a function of kept history, so deterministic across nodes; the first is a wrong result. A rule that matches the note: a restoring part is undoable iff every part whose writes it killed is in kept history (`kept_before`'s set), else `ContentGone`. Needs: which held parts the undone delta's resets and kills reach (`replaced_at`/`deaths` already judge a part's killer), and what a missing (dropped, not fetched) killed part means there.
