# Anyone holding a record can keep its thing from being destroyed
> Summary: A minting (`created.0`) needs no authority, so anyone holding a thing's record (every reader of a dependent's label, by record blobs) can mint it again at any `ts`: a durable minting keeps an expiring thing existing or brings it back, and a `nobody` (or another's `author`) one keeps it from being destroyed by purge. It carries no content. Stated as a limit in spec/commits.md §5 Destruction; Accepted as a limit (Tess: an unavoidable consequence of leave-no-trace deletion).
> Priority: low
> Tags: engine, permissions, security

- What users see: a destroyed thing (a disappearing message, a purged item) can read again as existing; its content still expires and is purged as before, and only copies still held come back. Back, it is live: its roster stands and its writers write it again, writes stamped after the destruction included, alike at every node (`destruction::a_minting_again_the_request_cannot_drop_brings_back_a_live_thing_alike_spec_commits_5`). So any reader of a dependent's label can undo an admin's destruction by purge.
- Option for the purge half: a destroying request covers every minting whatever its retention (equivalently `created.0` retentions all include `admin`). Small, but it drops the `nobody` option for things (a permanent chat's messages become destroyable by admins); principled now that `nobody` is gone for content (notes/engine-retention-history.md Tess's decision 2). The expiry half needs another rule (the record carrying the expiry is rejected: `Write::create` returns the tid first).
- Past a purge request's window the node keeps the verdict (destroyed for good, spec/commits.md §5) and takes no minting; this issue is the window itself and expiry.
- Not the purge mark for expiry: an expiry has no verdict over other mintings to keep, and a mark set at `T` would take or refuse a durable minting by its arrival around `T`, so honest nodes split for good on any minting in transit at its expiry (the purge mark has that edge only 90 days out, where role history ends anyway). The rule belongs at the root: who may mint again.
- Why mintings escape "nobody writes after its destruction": the destruction is where every minting was dropped, so a later one would decide it by arrival, and an order-free cut lets a backdated minting expiring early destroy a thing before its real creation (data loss). notes/expiry-creation-design.md (Limit).
- Rejected: a minting judged as a write on its thing. It loses a removed writer's creation its removal's frontier omits (addenda vouch for content, not `created.0`; letting them vouch for it looped, `osis` `sync::removal_from_partial_node_keeps_unheld_edits_spec_permissions_5_2`), and still leaves writers able to do the same.
