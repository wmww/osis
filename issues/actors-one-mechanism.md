# Actors, signing and forks as one mechanism
> Summary: An account is a special kind of thing (members by signing chain, no grants on it, its own fork and merge rules, an account root standing in for it); target: an ordinary independent thing whose members are signers. Membership, removal and forks are designed in notes/account-membership-design.md (visible mode first, hidden as a design); open here: questions for Tess, the nestable hiding rule, the write path (explicit paths, evidence in the sync unit, profiles) and the model.
> Priority: normal
> Tags: tess-call, perm, crypto, keys, spec

## What is special today
An account's members are its nodes, kept by its signing chain (spec/permissions.md §5.4-§5.5, spec/crypto.md §9, notes/engine-validity-epochs.md), not by role grants: no edge write targets it, nothing derives from its epochs (things minted with it as admin are independent at birth), and the account root stands in for it wherever a thing is needed. So an account cannot hold a role on another account's account. It has its own fork rules (sides by first revocation seen; merges of concurrent rotations) beside the admin-dispute rules and the role-key rule (forks stand).

## Direction (Tess)
- **One nestable rule for who sees who acted**: those inside an actor (holders of some role on it) see which member acted; those outside see the actor acting. The same for nodes in an account, accounts in an org, and any other nesting.
- **Hiding is not welded to the actor**: an account may act through an org and stay visible as itself, or show its node. Showing more must be easy. The existing "acting for" header field is part of this and gets rethought with it. Sketch (Claude): a commit carries its signatures from the node up, each layer sealed to the next layer's insiders or left clear, the outermost being the public signer; above it, plain grants. One choice per write: where hiding stops; default the account.
- **Attribution**: the API gives the public signer and what it acted for, favouring neither; the grants above are a separate question with possibly several answers; whose face shows is app policy.
- **Accounts on accounts**: an actor being its own only admin is one arrangement of roles, never a kind of actor or its definition.
- **Forks**: two members removing each other has no right answer inside osis, whatever the members are: both sides go on, others pick a side by a heuristic, warn, and may switch. Never a required merge, never a global winner. With members as signers, account forks are admin disputes and concurrent rotations are key forks that stand (notes/account-membership-design.md §1).
- **Hiding wherever offered pays hiding's cost** (an org's anonymous voice as much as an account's nodes): with a shared key a removal cannot tell members' writes apart, so it vouches for everything (notes/account-membership-design.md §3, §7). The nestable hiding rule above is decided with this: offered generally and paid for where used, or not at all.

## Membership, removal and forks: studied
Design: notes/account-membership-design.md (an account as an ordinary independent thing whose members are signers; the audit of every built mechanism against the stolen-node attacks; visible and hidden mode; the ordinary removal's fixes; why a signer is a node; sessions; node anonymity as built; the model plan). Settled there, with what is argued and what is checked marked: removal lists (one form, a leaf per thing, no size limit, nothing kept per commit), the shared-key sketch and hiding as a shared key under the ordinary rules (with corrections: a swap is two commits; two honest swaps at once dispute each other; a thief's swap reads as honest), why a signer is a node, sessions (keep them).
- **Decided (Tess)**: node anonymity was chosen before its removal cost was known; a pseudonym per node per thing is acceptable, and plainly visible nodes if that is significantly simpler. The study finds visible much simpler (the note, §1-§2) and a pseudonym per thing to cost what hidden mode costs (the note, §5).
- **Still open, from the study**:
  - The model (the note, §8), before anything is built: the new design replaces roles-model custody and epochs and Account.tla.
  - Ordinary-rule gaps the study found, to confirm by the model and then fix in spec/permissions.md for every holder: the frontier as a leaf per thing with no size limit; the withdraw rule possibly making both sides of any admin dispute rotate at every complete pass; a re-grant counting from the holder's first chained grant (the collateral grantee); the overlap rule counting only otherwise valid commits; pairing handing the new node its pairer's trust entries.
  - In the built design: session-form dots under an account's shared tid let a holder of its seed void a sibling's write (the note, §1, Found in passing). Moot if visible mode lands; an issue of its own otherwise.

## Plan (Tess)
- Two modes over the ordinary rules: **visible** (each device signs with its own key; probably the default) and **hidden** (devices share a key; hidden from everyone, their own account included). Each should cost little beyond what other features need anyway, both together far less than today's account system. The choice applies at any level of a chain of holders.
- Which is the default, whether the other is opt-in, a toggle, or unexposed to end users, follows from their trade-offs once known. Possibly only one is built and the other kept as a design.
- A key swap must not read as one person leaving and another joining: the account stays the identity, its keys are members. Decide how a write says so (the signer acting for the account, as the header's acting-for field does today) with the attribution rule in Direction.
- Most of what is missing is gaps in the ordinary rules (the study's list, above), to be fixed whatever is decided.
- Claude's suggestion: model both, build visible first (it needs nothing hidden does not), and keep hidden a checked design so nothing built rules it out (Questions for Tess, 2). spec/requirements.md already says a write hides who made it only by its author's choice.

## What should fall out (Tess): identities managed through another
Not features to build: a check on the design, and UI over ordinary features later. Each is an account thing with some arrangement of member keys.
- **Unlinked** (Tess's "sock puppet"; name open: persona, alias): an account whose one member is a shared key all the owner's devices hold. Nothing shows the owner's account, or that it differs from any single-device account. The key travels between the owner's devices as private data of the owner's account.
- **Shared or nested**: the same key also held by another account's devices; or a member that is itself such an account.
- **Provably linked**: an account whose admin is the owner's account by an ordinary grant, the chain visible.
- Why it matters: where privacy counts is often not where edits are many or their history audited, so choosing per identity softens the hidden mode's costs.
- **Checked against paths**: each holds with stated paths (notes/write-path-design.md §1): an unlinked identity writes with path [P] signed by the shared key, showing nothing of the owner; a shared or nested one likewise, its key held by several accounts' devices; a provably linked one writes [Owner, P], the chain visible. The purge author of a linked identity's write is the owner's account (the path's first thing), as proposed there.
- Limits for now (Tess: well-known problems, to be mitigated when privacy features are built, not part of this design): the same node serving both identities and both profiles naming the same addresses; every identity a stolen device held swapping keys at once; edits' timing.

## Signers, actors, profiles and accounts (working model; Tess and Claude)
- **Signer**: a key. A device's own (the node's key: its node root and settings stay its local things, signed by it alone), a key several devices share (hidden mode), a link's. A device holds its own and any shared keys it was given. A commit has exactly one signer: one signature.
- **Actor is a part played in a commit, not a kind of thing** (Tess): a commit has a chain of actors, starting with its signer, passing through the things it acts through, and ending at the things it acts on, which are not its actors. No thing "is" or "is not" an actor.
- **Explicit path, a full path per thing written, listing independent things only** (Tess, decided). Designed in notes/write-path-design.md (§1): what a path is (hops by direct edge or by descent down the mint chain, so a thing becoming independent changes no path), judging along it alone (validity, attribution, demotion, frontiers, re-grant per path; keys stay on every grant), what removal from a holder touches, osis-perm, where it sits (one path in the header when every thing uses it, else a hashed path per thing with the path in its section) and its bytes. Rejected: one path per commit with the last actor holding a direct role on everything written (a write across two authorities is refused or split, and apps would have to understand why); a set of actors from which paths are derived (the search again); a commit-wide default some things override (it shows their readers a path naming holders with no role on them).
- **Profile** (Tess): the public face of an independent thing, a name and a picture, what a write is shown as; a separate thing, generally not on the path; the thing names it by a reference, so holding the thing brings it in and a missing one shows as a broken reference; one profile per thing. Which face an app shows is its policy. A news outlet's public identity is a thing with members and a profile and nothing else.
  - Rejected: proving whose profile it is from the profile's side. By "minted with the actor and written through it": that leans on things being minted empty, which may change, and on the mint admin differing from other admins, which the API must never show (issues/behaviour-depends-on-own-keys.md). By "the actor is its admin": anyone can mint a thing, fill it and hand its admin to the actor.
  - Where the reference sits and how those who see the thing act read the profile, and where the dial list goes: notes/write-path-design.md §3 (open points in Questions for Tess).
- **Account**: more than a profile. An arrangement apps engage with from the inside: a profile, an inbox, private things its devices share (a home, the device list), members that are signers. A workspace or realm may have a like arrangement. A convention over ordinary things (a schema), technically possible to set up otherwise; nothing says it is a person. The account root goes once the account is an ordinary independent thing; home is the account thing; what must be written down (a role on it reaches all the account reaches): notes/write-path-design.md §5.
- An unlinked identity is an account whose member is a shared key; a linked one has no signer of its own, only the owner's account as admin, signed by the owner's devices.

## How a reader gets what it needs to judge a path
- **Goal (Tess): what a reader holds to judge is a function of the data, never of what was sent before.** Designed in notes/write-path-design.md §2: a unit's evidence is the closure over the paths its held commits state (records, every held edge write on their edges, recursively), in its required set as per-(commit, thing) copies, one blob wrapped per sync ID; the three points (same blob, work per roster change, size) settled there, with what becomes of pending, of nodes keeping the unit unread, of removals up a path, and of today's certificate machinery.
- It departs from Tess's proposal (evidence for every actor that could write in the unit): that costs an entry under every unit a holder reaches per roster change of that holder (a device added included), against spec/requirements.md Cost, and a key handed down to everyone above, which every removal would force to rotate. Question for Tess.
- To check: the frontier of a lowering lists commits on every thing the paths through it wrote, so its copy in one unit shows other things' commit IDs until removal lists take a per-thing form (Removal lists, above).

## Answers (Tess)
- **Who learns of a removal** (question 1): every sync unit the removed node could read. It costs about the same as only where it wrote for a node that writes widely, and leaves no window in which a stolen node writes somewhere new. So additions travel as their writes need them and removals travel at once.

- **Hidden mode** (question 2): build visible; keep hidden as a design in its plain form, a shared key with no sealed node signature on any commit, its costs against visible written down (every removal puts every node's writes and grants in question; unsent work falls until its node vouches; honest concurrent swaps dispute; a thief's writes before removal cannot be told from the owner's; no sessions), for identities where privacy is the point and edits are few.
- **A signer per node per account** (question 3): derived from the node's seed, so a node in two accounts links them for no reader.
- **Evidence** (question 4): a unit carries the evidence for the paths its held commits state, with every later change to those grants, not for every actor that could write there. Still a function of the data; nearer to what is built (certificate copies, with the set made exact and a copy leaving with the commit that needed it). It stands or falls with stated paths, which the model decides.
- **More planning before a coordinator takes this** (Tess): too many open questions for a build. Wanted first: the model, then a staged plan in which each stage leaves the tree working, starting with what is needed whatever else is decided (the ordinary removal's gaps, vouching for role writes). That it lands in sync, the least settled area, is in its favour (Tess): replacing code there is as likely to remove unknown bugs as to add them.
- **Purge author** (question 5): chosen at write time, one of the write's own actors, as who may purge an edit is already fixed when it is written. Core picks no position on the path ("the first thing after the signer" would build in one arrangement, nodes with a single account, because it happens to give the wanted answer there). The default is whatever the session was configured to act as; that configuration is written by the layer that understands the schema's account, never by core. To design: how a write names it (an index into its path, per commit or beside each aspect's purgeable-by class), what a purger must hold on the named thing, and what "purge everything by this author" matches.

## Open, from going through the answers with Tess
- **A remover that does not sync a thing the removed node wrote in** (not covered by notes/account-membership-design.md): nodes of one account hold different things, and a frontier lists only what the remover received, so the removed node's writes on such a thing fall at its readers. Another remaining writer that received them saves the content by an addendum; nothing saves its role-class writes there (grants are made again by an admin), nor content only readers hold. A commit counts as received once its header is read and its signature checked, bodies or not (spec/permissions.md §5.1 Received), and the removal is logged under every unit the node could read anyway, so the remover can take the node's headers there first and list them: say whether it must, what it does when a unit cannot be reached at removal time (remove now and lose them, or wait), and that whatever the thief wrote before the removal is blessed with the rest.
- **Role writes of a removed node must be kept on the same terms as its content** (Tess: removing a node from an account must not undo the membership of what was shared from it). Today a frontier lists the removed party's role-class commits only as the remover received them, and addenda vouch for plain content only (spec/permissions.md §5.2 Addenda), so where the remover does not hold a thing, the grants the node made there fall, and with them every write made under those grants: no list is made for parties removed that way, their writes simply lose their authority, and come back only with a re-grant counted from their first grant (notes/account-membership-design.md §1, the collateral grantee). Wanted: a remaining holder with the authority to make a role write itself (another node of the account, a co-admin) that had received it by the removal can vouch for it, as a writer does for content, without a role's validity coming to depend on addenda in a circle (the reason addenda save plain content only today). Design and model it with the unsynced-thing case above.
- **Core has no concept of an account** (Tess): core knows signers, things, roles, a write's stated path and named purge author, and each session's configured default path. An account is the schema's (an aspect on a realm, with conventions); making one and pairing a node are ordinary operations (mint a thing with these aspects, grant a signer a role on it) done by a library above core beside the first-party schema, not by the `osis` crate. To establish: what in core assumes an account today (`me()`, finding sibling nodes to sync with, inbox delivery are the likely places) and how each is said without it (siblings: the other members of the thing a node acts through).
- **Who may make a thing a holder without the external signal** (question 6, decided): write on the thing gives the right to grant to it, for a new thing and an existing one alike. Admins-only would leave minting with it as mint admin (which must stay open to writers, or members could not create things in a realm) as a case where the same admin edge is reached under a weaker rule: a mint-admin special the API would have to explain. Cost of write sufficing: a writer can attach outside things, adding to what members read (no new power) and to the keys that change when a member is removed (a bounded writer).
- **Thoughts, not for building (Tess)**: a writer minting a dependent of a realm is not its admin, so cannot set its admin-only aspects (its history setting, a chat's settings); minting it under one's own account, setting them, and handing admin to the realm gives the same result at an independent thing's cost. So perhaps creation carries arbitrary data, admin-only included, which only admins change afterwards; or perhaps that is undesirable. Likely the answer is per use: a member makes a sub-realm, grants the main realm a role on it, and mints documents with the sub-realm as admin, so the member administers them, the realm reaches them, and each document is dependent.
- **Profiles: public, and by ordinary sync** (question 7; Tess). A profile is a schema concept, not core's, and is publicly readable; a writer puts a strong reference to its own profile, as ordinary data, in the thing it writes in (an aspect the schema names). The write placing it states its actors, which is the actor naming its profile, checkable by any reader without reading the actor. Replaces notes/write-path-design.md §3 on this point: nothing in evidence, no rule that evidence pulls things in, no `public.0`.
  - **Publicly readable, and reached by the reference** (Tess): a ticket alone would leave a reader served only by a node keeping the referring thing unread with nowhere to fetch the profile once the writer is offline. With a sync unit being one independent thing and its dependents (notes/sync-unit-scope.md), the profile is a unit of its own that anyone may enter, and a node keeping the referring unit unread reaches it by that unit's list like any other member.
  - **How a thing is public** (Tess's suggestion): a well-known **public** key actor whose seed is a constant of the protocol; a thing is public by granting it read. One edge on the thing however many read it, nothing on it showing where its owner writes, and the box to it can take the symmetric form since every node holds the recipient's secret. The one new rule: anyone may enter a unit the public actor reads.
  - Rejected: granting the referring thing read on the profile (the profile's roster would list every thing its owner writes in, to every reader of the profile, and its role history would grow with them); a ticket or a link's secret in the reference (the profile stays outside the sync unit).
  - **The public actor is a special case, and may be one** (Tess: unlike most things): whatever a holder of a reader's key may do, anyone may do as public. It must not be able to leave (a self-removal signed with the well-known key would un-publish anything), and dropping reader deposits alone is not known to be enough: go through every act open to a reader or to a key actor (leaving, deposits, history adds, opening a sync ID at a node keeping it unread, external fires) and say what public may do. Expect more special cases.
  - To settle: whether a public thing's read epoch ever needs to change; who writes the entry for a member who has not written (the granter); weak references for very large channels; where a thing's inbox is named (core's, needed to grant to it; separate from this). One profile blob under every unit its owner writes in links those units at a node keeping them unread: issues/sync-units-linked-at-rest.md.
- **Public information and contact-only information** (question 8; Tess: both kinds will have more uses): the dial list leaves the public profile for a thing of its own read by those its owner shares with, as the profile is today. Both are the schema's.
- **Removing a thing's last admin** (question 9, decided): refused for every thing, dependent or independent, unless the write says explicitly that the thing may go adminless (for a thing meant to be frozen; holders keep reading and writing as granted, nothing recovers it). Wanted as a rule every node judges alike, not only a local refusal: an edge write lowering or removing an admin edge is invalid where, as the walk stands at it, it would leave the thing no admin edge, unless it carries the mark. To check in the model: that it is a function of the held writes like any validity (it flips when an earlier grant arrives, as validity already may; it is read per trust set under a dispute); which edges count (a timed admin grant that will end, an admin thing nobody can act as any more); that a thing can still end adminless by a timed grant ending, its admin being destroyed or every key of it lost, which no rule prevents, so that state is handled either way. A dependent thing becomes independent first, as for any role change on it. As apps see it (Tess): the API returns an error where the node can tell; a removal made on an incomplete view goes out, and once nodes hold the writes that make it the last admin's it is judged invalid and the admin stands again, as any write that turns out invalid is undone, with the role's listeners told.
- **Hidden mode is close to the built design** (Tess): what goes is the parallel layer (chain, epochs, custody, account sides); vouching for the whole account's history at each removal is inherent to hiding and stays, with the bugs that live there; merges and automatic settling are given up, not solved. Not known to be much simpler than what is built: do not plan on it.

## Questions for Tess
Each with the study's recommendation. From notes/account-membership-design.md:
1. **Who learns of a removal.** The remover logs a node's removal under every sync ID the node could read (spec/permissions.md §3), so readers of anything the account shares learn each removed node and its remover, even where it never wrote. The alternative (only where it wrote) leaves a window in which a removed node's first write on a thing counts at its readers until a node holding the removal syncs there. Recommendation: log it everywhere; removals are rare, and the window is a security hole.
2. **Hidden mode's costs.** Under the plain ordinary rules, insiders cannot tell nodes apart: two honest key swaps at once split the account until a user picks, a thief's swap reads as an honest one, and the user sees a fight between two keys naming no node. A sealed node signature on swap commits would fix that but brings back sides that differ inside and outside the account. Recommendation: accept those costs, build visible only, and keep hidden as a checked design (nothing visible builds rules it out, the note §3).
3. **One signer per node, or per node per account.** A node in two accounts signing with one key links the accounts for every reader; one signer per account (derived from the node's seed) costs nothing more, since a stolen node needs a removal per account either way. Recommendation: one per account.

From notes/write-path-design.md:
4. **Evidence for what was written, not for every actor that could write** (§2): recommended, for cost (no entry per unit per node change), size and what readers learn. It departs from Tess's proposal; question 1 is the one place where "every unit the node could read" is still paid, per removal.
5. **Purge author** (§1): the path's first thing after the signer (normally the account), else the signer; recommended (any node of the account can delete its writes, as with today's header actor).
6. **Granting to a thing not opened needs write on it, not any role** (§4): recommended (readers only read; a reader's grant would make the thing's writers store it). spec/requirements.md Permissions says "holding no role".
7. **Faces of writers held automatically** (§3): the evidence's profile reference enters the profile like a strong from the unit (recommended: a missing face shows as broken, never nothing; one sync ID per distinct face per reader), or the app holds the faces it shows.
8. **The dial list moves off the profile** to a thing of its own with today's profile readership (§3): recommended, since a profile is read by everyone who saw the account act.
9. **Removing a thing's last admin** (§5): allowed for every thing, freezing its roster (recommended; core permits, the API asks the app to confirm), or refused locally for every thing.
