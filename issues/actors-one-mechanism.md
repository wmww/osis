# Actors, signing and forks as one mechanism
> Summary: An account is a special kind of thing (members by signing chain, no grants on it, its own fork and merge rules, an account root standing in for it); target: an ordinary independent thing whose members are signers, with no concept of an account in core. Designed in notes/account-membership-design.md (membership, removal, hidden mode as a design) and notes/write-path-design.md (paths, purge author, evidence, the public actor, external grants, the last admin, core without accounts); here: Tess's direction and answers and what the design settled. Open questions and the staged build: notes/redesign-plan.md.
> Priority: normal
> Tags: perm, crypto, keys, spec

## What is special today
An account's members are its nodes, kept by its signing chain (spec/permissions.md §5.4-§5.5, spec/crypto.md §9, notes/engine-validity-epochs.md), not by role grants: no edge write targets it, nothing derives from its epochs (things minted with it as admin are independent at birth), and the account root stands in for it wherever a thing is needed. So an account cannot hold a role on another account's account. It has its own fork rules (sides by first revocation seen; merges of concurrent rotations) beside the admin-dispute rules and the role-key rule (forks stand).

## Direction (Tess)
- **One nestable rule for who sees who acted**: those inside an actor (holders of some role on it) see which member acted; those outside see the actor acting. The same for nodes in an account, accounts in an org, and any other nesting.
- **Hiding is not welded to the actor**: an account may act through an org and stay visible as itself, or show its node. Showing more must be easy. The existing "acting for" header field is part of this and gets rethought with it. Sketch (Claude): a commit carries its signatures from the node up, each layer sealed to the next layer's insiders or left clear, the outermost being the public signer; above it, plain grants. One choice per write: where hiding stops; default the account.
- **Attribution**: the API gives the public signer and what it acted for, favouring neither; the grants above are a separate question with possibly several answers; whose face shows is app policy.
- **Accounts on accounts**: an actor being its own only admin is one arrangement of roles, never a kind of actor or its definition.
- **Forks**: two members removing each other has no right answer inside osis, whatever the members are: both sides go on, others pick a side by a heuristic, warn, and may switch. Never a required merge, never a global winner. With members as signers, account forks are admin disputes and concurrent rotations are key forks that stand (notes/account-membership-design.md §1).
- **Hiding wherever offered pays hiding's cost** (an org's anonymous voice as much as an account's nodes): with a shared key a removal cannot tell members' writes apart, so it vouches for everything (notes/account-membership-design.md §3, §7). The nestable hiding rule above is decided with this: offered generally and paid for where used, or not at all.

## Membership, removal and forks: designed
notes/account-membership-design.md: an account as an ordinary independent thing whose members are signers; the audit of every built mechanism against the stolen-node attacks; visible mode; what a removal keeps (a leaf per thing, a remover that does not sync a thing, what the user is shown); hidden mode as a plain shared-key design with its costs; why a signer is a node; sessions (kept); node anonymity as built; the model plan.
- **Decided (Tess)**: node anonymity was chosen before its removal cost was known; a pseudonym per node per thing is acceptable, and plainly visible nodes if that is significantly simpler. Visible is much simpler, and a pseudonym per thing costs what hidden mode costs (the note, §5).
- In the built design: session-form dots under an account's shared tid let a holder of its seed void a sibling's write (the note, §1). Moot once visible mode lands; an issue of its own otherwise.

## Plan (Tess)
- Two modes over the ordinary rules: **visible** (each device signs with its own key; probably the default) and **hidden** (devices share a key; hidden from everyone, their own account included). Each should cost little beyond what other features need anyway, both together far less than today's account system. The choice applies at any level of a chain of holders.
- Which is the default, whether the other is opt-in, a toggle, or unexposed to end users, follows from their trade-offs once known. Possibly only one is built and the other kept as a design.
- A key swap must not read as one person leaving and another joining: the account stays the identity, its keys are members. Decide how a write says so (the signer acting for the account, as the header's acting-for field does today) with the attribution rule in Direction.
- Most of what is missing is gaps in the ordinary rules (the study's list, above), to be fixed whatever is decided.
- Decided: build visible; keep hidden a design so nothing built rules it out (Answers). spec/requirements.md already says a write hides who made it only by its author's choice.

## What should fall out (Tess): identities managed through another
Not features to build: a check on the design, and UI over ordinary features later. Each is an account thing with some arrangement of member keys.
- **Unlinked** (Tess's "sock puppet"; name open: persona, alias): an account whose one member is a shared key all the owner's devices hold. Nothing shows the owner's account, or that it differs from any single-device account. The key travels between the owner's devices as private data of the owner's account.
- **Shared or nested**: the same key also held by another account's devices; or a member that is itself such an account.
- **Provably linked**: an account whose admin is the owner's account by an ordinary grant, the chain visible.
- Why it matters: where privacy counts is often not where edits are many or their history audited, so choosing per identity softens the hidden mode's costs.
- **Checked against paths**: each holds with stated paths (notes/write-path-design.md §1): an unlinked identity writes with path [P] signed by the shared key, showing nothing of the owner; a shared or nested one likewise, its key held by several accounts' devices; a provably linked one writes [Owner, P], the chain visible. The purge author of each is whichever of its actors the write names (notes/write-path-design.md §1).
- Limits for now (Tess: well-known problems, to be mitigated when privacy features are built, not part of this design): the same node serving both identities and both profiles naming the same addresses; every identity a stolen device held swapping keys at once; edits' timing.

## Signers, actors, profiles and accounts (working model; Tess and Claude)
- **Signer**: a key. A device's own (the node's key: its node root and settings stay its local things, signed by it alone), a key several devices share (hidden mode), a link's. A device holds its own and any shared keys it was given. A commit has exactly one signer: one signature.
- **Actor is a part played in a commit, not a kind of thing** (Tess): a commit has a chain of actors, starting with its signer, passing through the things it acts through, and ending at the things it acts on, which are not its actors. No thing "is" or "is not" an actor.
- **Explicit path, a full path per thing written, listing independent things only** (Tess, decided). Designed in notes/write-path-design.md (§1): what a path is (hops by direct edge or by descent down the mint chain, so a thing becoming independent changes no path), judging along it alone (validity, attribution, demotion, frontiers, re-grant per path; keys stay on every grant), what removal from a holder touches, osis-perm, where it sits (one path in the header where it serves, else the split form: notes/path-form.md) and its bytes. Rejected: one path per commit with the last actor holding a direct role on everything written (a write across two authorities is refused or split, and apps would have to understand why); a set of actors from which paths are derived (the search again); a commit-wide default some things override (it shows their readers a path naming holders with no role on them).
- **Profile** (Tess): the public face of an independent thing, a name and a picture, what a write is shown as; a separate thing, generally not on the path; the thing names it by a reference, so holding the thing brings it in and a missing one shows as a broken reference; one profile per thing. Which face an app shows is its policy. A news outlet's public identity is a thing with members and a profile and nothing else.
  - Rejected: proving whose profile it is from the profile's side. By "minted with the actor and written through it": that leans on things being minted empty, which may change, and on the mint admin differing from other admins, which the API must never show (issues/behaviour-depends-on-own-keys.md). By "the actor is its admin": anyone can mint a thing, fill it and hand its admin to the actor.
  - A profile is the schema's, public, and referenced by an ordinary strong reference from what its owner writes in; the dial list is contact-only information in a thing of its own: notes/write-path-design.md §3.
- **Account**: more than a profile. An arrangement apps engage with from the inside: a profile, an inbox, private things its devices share (a home, the device list), members that are signers. A workspace or realm may have a like arrangement. A convention over ordinary things (a schema), technically possible to set up otherwise; nothing says it is a person. Core has no concept of it. The account root goes once the account is an ordinary independent thing; home is the account thing; what must be written down (a role on it reaches all the account reaches): notes/write-path-design.md §5.
- An unlinked identity is an account whose member is a shared key; a linked one has no signer of its own, only the owner's account as admin, signed by the owner's devices.

## How a reader gets what it needs to judge a path
- **Goal (Tess): what a reader holds to judge is a function of the data, never of what was sent before.** Designed in notes/write-path-design.md §2: a unit's evidence is the closure over the paths its held commits state (records, every held edge write on their edges, recursively), in its required set as copies; removals of a signer besides are logged under every unit where an act of it could be valid.

## Answers (Tess)
- **Who learns of a removal**: every sync unit where an act of the removed node could be valid (its path gave write, an invite, or an enabled reader-level act), kept there for good. Not only where it wrote: that leaves a window in which a stolen node writes somewhere new. Not where it could only read: nothing it signs is judged valid there, and the entry would name the account to that unit's readers. So additions travel as their writes need them and removals travel at once.
- **Nothing vouches for a removed node's role writes** (Tess, issues/role-vouch-contested.md): one the removal's list does not name is dropped, whoever received it; the removal's result lists the members the removed node had added.
- **Nothing nobody received is kept**: no vouch on a user's say-so; a removed node's unseen writes are untrusted with no exception; a node still in its owner's hands syncs before it is removed. spec/requirements.md keeps "vouches for having received".

- **Hidden mode** (question 2): build visible; keep hidden as a design in its plain form, a shared key with no sealed node signature on any commit, its costs against visible written down (every removal puts every node's writes and grants in question; unsent work falls until its node vouches; honest concurrent swaps dispute; a thief's writes before removal cannot be told from the owner's; no sessions), for identities where privacy is the point and edits are few.
- **A signer per node per account** (question 3): derived from the node's seed, so a node in two accounts links them for no reader.
- **Evidence** (question 4): a unit carries the evidence for the paths its held commits state, with every later change to those grants, not for every actor that could write there. Still a function of the data; nearer to what is built (certificate copies, with the set made exact and a copy leaving with the commit that needed it). It stands or falls with stated paths, which the model decides.
- **More planning before a coordinator takes this** (Tess): too many open questions for a build. Wanted first: the model, then a staged plan in which each stage leaves the tree working, starting with what is needed whatever else is decided (the ordinary removal's gaps). That it lands in sync, the least settled area, is in its favour (Tess): replacing code there is as likely to remove unknown bugs as to add them.
- **Purge author** (question 5): chosen at write time, one of the write's own actors, as who may purge an edit is already fixed when it is written. Core picks no position on the path ("the first thing after the signer" would build in one arrangement, nodes with a single account, because it happens to give the wanted answer there). The default is whatever the session was configured to act as; that configuration is written by the layer that understands the schema's account, never by core. To design: how a write names it (an index into its path, per commit or beside each aspect's purgeable-by class), what a purger must hold on the named thing, and what "purge everything by this author" matches.

- **Core has no concept of an account** (Tess): core knows signers, things, roles, a write's stated path and named purge author, and each session's configured default path. An account is the schema's (an aspect on a realm, with conventions); making one and pairing a node are ordinary operations (mint a thing with these aspects, grant a signer a role on it) done by a library above core beside the first-party schema, not by the `osis` crate.
- **Who may make a thing a holder without the external signal** (question 6, decided): write on the thing gives the right to grant to it, for a new thing and an existing one alike. Admins-only would leave minting with it as mint admin (which must stay open to writers, or members could not create things in a realm) as a case where the same admin edge is reached under a weaker rule: a mint-admin special the API would have to explain. Cost of write sufficing: a writer can attach outside things, adding to what members read (no new power) and to the keys that change when a member is removed (a bounded writer).
- **Profiles: public, and by ordinary sync** (question 7; Tess). A profile is a schema concept, not core's, and is publicly readable; a writer puts a strong reference to its own profile, as ordinary data, in the thing it writes in (an aspect the schema names). The write placing it states its actors, which is the actor naming its profile, checkable by any reader without reading the actor. Replaces notes/write-path-design.md §3 on this point: nothing in evidence, no rule that evidence pulls things in, no `public.0`.
  - **Publicly readable, so in the sync unit** (Tess): a ticket would leave the profile outside the referring thing's sync unit, so a reader served only by a node keeping that unit unread could not fetch it once the writer is offline. A public thing is one every thing's keys open, so a strong reference brings it into the referrer's sync unit, carried under its sync ID like the rest (one blob, a wrap per sync ID).
  - **How a thing is public** (Tess's suggestion): a well-known **public** key actor whose seed is a constant of the protocol; a thing is public by granting it read. One edge on the thing however many read it, nothing on it showing where its owner writes, and the box to it can take the symmetric form since every node holds the recipient's secret. The one new rule: every thing's keys count as opening what the public actor reads (spec/sync-set.md §2 T's keys open).
  - Rejected: granting the referring thing read on the profile (the profile's roster would list every thing its owner writes in, to every reader of the profile, and its role history would grow with them); a ticket or a link's secret in the reference (the profile stays outside the sync unit).
  - **The public actor is a special case, and may be one** (Tess: unlike most things): whatever a holder of a reader's key may do, anyone may do as public. It must not be able to leave (a self-removal signed with the well-known key would un-publish anything), and dropping reader deposits alone is not known to be enough: go through every act open to a reader or to a key actor (leaving, deposits, history adds, opening a sync ID at a node keeping it unread, external fires) and say what public may do. Expect more special cases.
- **Public information and contact-only information** (question 8; Tess: both kinds will have more uses): the dial list leaves the public profile for a thing of its own read by those its owner shares with, as the profile is today. Both are the schema's.
- **Removing a thing's last admin** (question 9, decided): refused for every thing, dependent or independent, unless the write says explicitly that the thing may go adminless (for a thing meant to be frozen; holders keep reading and writing as granted, nothing recovers it). Wanted as a rule every node judges alike, not only a local refusal: an edge write lowering or removing an admin edge is invalid where, as the walk stands at it, it would leave the thing no admin edge, unless it carries the mark. As apps see it (Tess): the API returns an error where the node can tell; a removal made on an incomplete view goes out, and once nodes hold the writes that make it the last admin's it is judged invalid and the admin stands again, as any write that turns out invalid is undone, with the role's listeners told.
- **Hidden mode is close to the built design** (Tess): what goes is the parallel layer (chain, epochs, custody, account sides); vouching for the whole account's history at each removal is inherent to hiding and stays, with the bugs that live there; merges and automatic settling are given up, not solved. Not known to be much simpler than what is built: do not plan on it.
- **Role writes of a removed node are kept on the same terms as its content** (Tess): removing a node from an account must not undo the membership of what was shared from it.

## Settled by design since the answers
Each argued from the spec and to be checked by the model; nothing here is built.
- **Role writes of a removed node kept by the list alone**: one the removal's list does not name falls, whoever else received it; the collateral grantee is this case, back once a member grants it again. notes/account-membership-design.md §4, Role writes.
- **A remover that does not sync a thing**: it may hear out the units the node could write first (holding each: no header-only fetch exists), never must, and core never waits; a unit out of reach is owed the removal, and any node holding the removal and a unit it is owed under logs it. What nobody received is discarded and shown to the user. notes/account-membership-design.md §4.
- **What the model found** (notes/path-model-findings.md, each with where the design answers it): a removal is judged in the world its list makes (nothing is exempt from its drops, no list entry is read as absent, a lowering that does not then apply is void and drops nothing; its writer and the witnesses leave out what it would not apply with); a removal's list has an entry per role commit, carried with the evidence that needs it; under a standing dispute the other side's epoch is read and never answered; the last-admin rule relies on one named remaining admin, so a reader judges it; nobody vouches for its own signer's commit.
- **The public actor**: five special cases, the first closing every act of its own (a signature by the public key counts for nothing); every act of a reader or key thing gone through. notes/write-path-design.md §3.
- **Where a thing's inbox is named**: an admin-only aspect of the thing names its inbox and its cards; a card (the keys, the inbox, its sync ID and deposit seed) sits on any thing its members choose and counts only where its write states the thing as an actor; who reads a card can grant. notes/write-path-design.md §3, The card.
- **The external-signal switch**, in spec-ready form: notes/write-path-design.md §4. Write on a thing gives the right to grant to it (decided).
- **The purge author**: an index into the stated path, one per path; a purger's own path must contain the named thing and give write; a request by author matches exactly that thing. notes/write-path-design.md §1, The purge author.
- **The last-admin rule**: a lowering relies on one remaining standing admin edge, named where it is not its own path's last; a standing admin edge is a direct admin edge with no `until`; two admins removing each other never leave a thing adminless under the existing rules; two leaving at once, the later-stamped leave is not applied. notes/write-path-design.md §5, The last admin.
- **Core without accounts**: what assumes one today, how each is said without it, and the functions leaving the `osis` crate: notes/write-path-design.md §5.
- **Readers hidden from each other** conflicts with nothing above; two points for its own design: issues/readers-see-readers.md.
- **Thoughts, not for building (Tess)**: a writer minting a dependent of a realm is not its admin, so cannot set its admin-only aspects; minting it with one's own account, setting them, and handing admin to the realm gives the same result at an independent thing's cost. So perhaps creation carries arbitrary data, admin-only included, which only admins change afterwards. Likely the answer is per use: a member makes a sub-realm, grants the main realm a role on it, and mints documents with the sub-realm as admin.
- **Second round**: core removes a node when told and never waits, with nothing tied to collecting first (a general wait until connected nodes are synced, behind a sync button, serves a node being retired: UX, later); a timed admin grant does not count as a standing admin for the last-admin rule; the dial aspect is core's, the schema deciding which thing carries it and who reads it; what nobody received is not kept, on anyone's say-so. A public thing's deposit budget stays open to everyone. Node signers showing on public things is accepted with visible mode; a shared signer for a profile comes if and when shared keys are built. Every special case of the public actor is listed in one place (notes/write-path-design.md, The public thing) in case they are regretted or public readers are redone.
