# Keys: key access and cycles
> Summary: Why role cycles stay allowed but no key cycle forms (rule 4, spec/crypto.md §4 Holder actors): keyless grants by an agreed order, removed actor edges, taint for hidden grants, and the cycle rules that failed in Depth0.tla (kept so nobody tries them again).

Principle: **no key cycles.** Role cycles are allowed (Tess: tags become members of each other by ordinary use; two people's shared tags have disjoint admins; nested shared tags can be one-way), but the grant closing a cycle is keyless and its holder boxed nothing, so for keys a cycle reduces to member chains. Tess left the cycle rule to the agents, with model-checking first.

## Why
X and Y readers of each other, D removed from X: Y's epoch boxes X's old keyset (D holds it), so X's new epoch boxed to Y leaks, and any order of lone rotations that box each other leaks. In a cycle, X's new secret is safe in Y's keyset k only if no box from k back to X's old keysets is ever added, and re-seals are added by nodes that may not know X rotated. Also rejected: joint rotation of the cycle in one commit; forbidding cycles.

## Key access
- **Key access** (rule 4, `key_access`): actor grants in an agreed order (a record edge, standing from creation, first; then by the key of the write starting the grant's standing, `Timeline::standing_since`; ties by target, holder). A grant whose target's key already yields its holder's over the earlier keyed grants is keyless and boxed nothing; only grants inside a strongly connected component are judged one by one. The knowing re-key: a current epoch boxing a holder whose grant is keyless rotates.
- A new grant is judged with the mint admins of both ends added: a thing whose mint admin is its target (a thin client minted by the account) closes a cycle through the implicit edge though it holds nothing yet, so it is keyless. The mint admin's record edge counts as a grant at creation (never keyless); its removal may make a later keyless grant keyed (a re-seal, as a new grant).
- Kept by the timelines (`held::Timelines::keyless`): a cycle lies in one component, so an update judges anew only the components where an actor grant or a record changed, and its readers move only when the set does.
- A keyless grant is a role for authority (who may write or administer, `may_rotate`) but reads nothing: every judgement of what a party holds for keys (departures' makers, a rotator's word in coverage) walks grants with key access only, past those a destroyed thing holds too (`members::key_skip`, `role_for_keys`; notes/holder-actors.md, Destroyed holder actors), and so does every read entitlement (`Core::keyed_role_now`, `reads_now`: what T's keys open, `may_read`, the node's own role for taking secrets, `role_here`). The rest read `role_now` as is: a cheap path while no keyless grant is held and nothing destroyed holds a grant.
- **Removed actor edges** (Depth0 `D0_fa_flip`, `D0_fa_both`): key access is judged over the held grants less the actor edges whose removal the node applied, so removing a mint admin F from T turns T's later keyless grant on F keyed, and F's admin re-seals F's epochs to T by the ordinary re-seal trigger. Ignoring removals fails liveness in the model (T never boxed F's epochs).
- **Taint** (rule 4b, the coordinator's call: churn while a peer withholds a grant beats an endless leak): a keyset of M boxing a holder past its key access to M (`members::keyless_holders`) is stale and never sealed to. A box to a departed holder actor, or above a holder's role, is a departure, not a taint (counting it locked a holder's thing out of A after the holder's self-removal from M). M's `secrets.0` is read for taint only when some holder of M lacks key access.
- **Hidden grants**: a grant the sealer does not hold leaks. Plain rule 4 leaks until every admin of Y holds the grant and re-keys (unbounded under a withholding peer); with taint the leak stops once the writer holds the grants and the cycle's commits, at one extra rotation per race while a grant stays hidden (`D0_nc_hide_taint_lean`). Refining taint to fire only on superseded keysets is the box-judging family that failed.

## Cycle rules that failed
In Depth0.tla every rule judging boxes failed:
- leaving the other out on a direct-departure rotation and re-sealing once the other's keyset boxes back: staleness rotations ping-pong (`Settles`); a departure below a cycle leaks; concurrent departures on both sides leave each out for good;
- every rotation leaving the other out and re-sealing when unreachable from superseded keysets over held boxes: leaks (`NoLeakDirect`: a check on what is held now cannot see a later ignorant re-seal);
- **Fresh** (seal Y at k only when every box of k to X names a current keyset of X and no superseded epoch of X boxes Y at k): fails with a second admin of Y; counting only k's own rotation commit closes that, but rejoin rotations ping-pong and chase round a 3-cycle;
- a rotation trigger for tainted or "dirty" keysets: ping-pongs or misses chains longer than 2.
Rule 4 passes every instance with every grant visible, the second-admin one included. Not modelled: the agreed order itself (assumed total and causal: if it is ever not causal, e.g. by claimed time, implicit mint-admin edges need an explicit "always keyed" exception); write and admin roles.
