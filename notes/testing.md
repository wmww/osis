# Testing
> Summary: How osis is tested and how to run each layer: principles, tiers and the per-branch oracles, what finds bugs, models, conformance vectors and their freeze policy, fuzzing, the simulator, the arrival-order differential, seed runners, size budgets and scale checks with measured numbers, the forensic attack, policy checks, the spec-to-test map, conventions every crate follows, security reviews' checked-sound lists, performance budgets. Build commands and costs: notes/build.md.

Strategy items not built (a 10^4-seed sweep, GUI smoke tests) are in issues/tests-unbuilt.md.

## Principles
- The spec is the oracle: every MUST has a test that fails without it, and every test cites the spec section it checks, so `grep` (or notes/testing-map.md) answers "what checks §X".
- Determinism: seeded randomness, injected clocks, in-process transport. A failure is a seed; a flake is a bug.
- Peer bytes never panic: every parser is fuzzed; decoders take limits.
- Models (core/test/crdt-model, core/test/roles-model, core/test/held-model, core/test/tla) are independent oracles; the implementation never depends on them and they are never patched to match it.

## What finds bugs
Over about 200 merged tasks: reading in review, audits and simplification passes found about 180 product bugs; a new targeted test or differential for the branch about 45; per-branch tier-2 oracles at default size (fresh offsets) about 25; larger ranges, periodic runs and wide sweeps 9-13; dogfooding about 10; tier 1 2; TLA none new. Re-running already-run ranges found about 2 (both regressions). Of about 38 seed-found bugs, about 14 were slips against a clear rule and about 24 had no rule yet: the fix wrote a new spec sentence, a stated limit or a redesign. They clustered (arrival order × retention × validity, epochs and rotation, ephemeral), and each chase ended with a study that found one root shape, never with more seeds. Hence (notes/coordination.md): per-branch oracle sizes a tenth of full, breadth only in periodic runs, never rerun old ranges, one all-core run at a time per box, a study after the second seed fix in an area, and time spent on reviews that read, audits, dogfooding and targeted differentials.

## Tiers
Everything runs locally; there is no CI service.

| tier | what | how | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | unit, small proptest (`PROPTEST_CASES=32`), vectors | `cargo test --workspace` (about 2.5 min warm on a quiet box); one area: `cargo test -p <crate> --test it <module>::[name]` | every gate |
| 2 | `#[ignore]` tests: oracles at size, large property runs; networking tests | by name: the oracles below, when the branch touches their area; the whole set `OSIS_FULL=1 PROPTEST_CASES=256 cargo test --workspace -- --include-ignored --skip scale:: --skip bench:: --skip budget --skip sweep --skip repro_ --skip bench_ --skip covered_text_cost --skip flows::scale_ --skip _probe --skip judgment_vectors_write` as a periodic check; networking: `cargo test-net -- --ignored` | reviewers per branch; periodic |
| 3 | sim seed sweeps, fuzz, big model instances, TLC, benches, coverage, timing budgets (`scale::`, `bench::`, `budget*`, release only) | the gate runs every sim scenario at 5 seeds for sim/engine/node/perm/crdt changes; the periodic checks below; the rest by hand | gate; periodic; by hand |

Sizes: a `_large` test's range or sample count below is its full size; a plain run takes a tenth (`osis_base::testenv::large`, at least one seed), the per-branch size; `OSIS_FULL=1` runs the full size (periodic checks only); explicit `OSIS_*_START`/`_SEEDS` override both. Rule: a per-branch oracle finishes in under 5 minutes on a quiet box; anything longer is a periodic check (notes/coordination.md) and gets an issue to be made shorter. All per-branch oracles together at plain size: about 2.5 min wall, 950 CPU-s quiet; at full size 13.4 min, 11,370 CPU-s.

Per-branch oracles, by area (full-size costs where measured):
- Apply, late parts: osis-node `parts::part_away_differential_large_spec_commits_4` (60 seeds; `OSIS_AWAY_START`/`_SEEDS`).
- Held-set rule, any node or perm change: `order::arrival_order_alike_large_spec_permissions_5_2` (60 seeds). Purge or retention: `order::arrival_order_purge_large_spec_commits_5` (60 seeds), `order::arrival_order_purge_rows_large_spec_perm_6` (a purge's fall; 60 seeds). Required retention: `order::arrival_order_required_large_spec_commits_5` (40 seeds). Deaths, retention or validity: `order::arrival_order_exhaustive_large_spec_crdt_4_8` (40 seeds, 24 clocked orders per held set). Kept copies: `keep::any_order_keeps_no_more_large` (200 seeds, about 60 CPU-s).
- Perm: `OSIS_PERM_SEED=<n> cargo test -p osis-perm --test it -- --ignored differential:: queries:: incremental::` (15 tests sharing a pool of a thread per core; the largest per-branch cost).
- Sequences or paging: osis-crdt `seq_differential_large_spec_5_7`, `hostile_moves_join_time_spec_6`, `paging_large_spec_8`, `model_order_independent_spec_7_large` (single-threaded); wire or decoder: `stream_check_matches_whole_large_spec_8` (600 seeds, the streaming check against the whole-delta oracle on mutated roots and pages); paging or decode memory: `stream_memory_big_map` (alone, `--exact`: one child process per step).
- Apply, paging or join: osis-engine `apply::tests_paged::paged_apply_matches_whole_large_spec_crdt_8` (streamed apply against the whole-part oracle, 24 seeds, `OSIS_PAGED_APPLY_START`/`_SEEDS`).
- Synced ephemeral data, its sync path or the host's ephemeral entries: sim-sweep `--seeds 30 --only ephemeral,ephemeral_capped`; osis-node `ephemeral::random_window_converges_large` (200 seeds; the `ephemeral::random_window` filter also runs the `_inflight` and `_one_capped_host` variants); `ephemeral::random_churn_converges_large` (superseded expiring writes, expiry, removals, undos, demotions, restarts; 300 seeds about 4 min on 8 threads; `OSIS_SEED_TIMEOUT_SECS=60` marks a hung seed early).
- Rotation, re-seal or departures: `cargo test -p osis-engine --lib rotation::tests:: -- --ignored --skip repro_` (`repro_` tests reproduce open issues and fail by design).
- Sync or retention: `cargo run --bin sim-sweep -- --seeds 30 --only <the scenarios the change touches>`. Named list **permission/compaction** (validity, revocation, receipts, snapshots, covers) = `revoke_while_writing,removed_writer,compaction,stranded_reseal,offline_rotation,work_sets,coverer_revoked,revocation_bob,revocation_carol,stolen_node,demotion_synced,demotion_unsynced,removed_replayer,equivocating_writer,keep_at_most`.
- Not per branch: osis-node `ephemeral::many_authors_under_host_rates_large` (a simulated minute of 10 authors: 12 min in dev), the sim `work_sets_seed_1` and `work_sets_match_full_passes` comparisons (use `OSIS_SIM_SEEDS=2` per branch), the perm `bench_10k_edges` (13 GB, alone), a 300-seed sweep. Timing budgets (`budget_*`, `scale::`, `bench::`) are meaningless in dev: release only, by hand.

**Periodic checks** (every tier-2 test with `OSIS_FULL=1` unless it names its own range; in the background in a spare worktree, offsets rotating, notes/coordination.md): `cargo run --bin sim-sweep -- --seeds 150 --from <F> --budget-secs 3600`; `OSIS_ORDER_START=<S> OSIS_ORDER_SEEDS=300 cargo test -p osis-node --test it -- --ignored order::arrival_order_alike_large_spec_permissions_5_2` and `OSIS_ORDER_START=<E> OSIS_ORDER_SEEDS=200 ... order::arrival_order_exhaustive_large_spec_crdt_4_8` (about 9 and 22 min at load 20); `OSIS_PERM_SEED=<P> cargo test -p osis-perm`; `OSIS_CRDT_SEED=<C> cargo test -p osis-crdt -- --include-ignored --skip repro_ --skip budget --skip bench:: --skip scale:: --skip hostile_moves --skip shrink_script` (without the skips `budget_big_text_spec_6` runs 30+ min in dev); `core/test/tla/run.sh small`. Proptest's seed cannot be rotated.

**Rebuild check** (debug builds, `apply::atomic::check_rebuilt`, run by `Engine::run` before the index persists): after every transaction, each touched aspect's state must equal `rebuilt` (the join of held bodies, leftovers among them, and the own state, invalid writes removed, spec/crdt.md §4.6), compared encoded, text chars §5.7 leaves to the node dropped from both. Every change but a valid join is that rebuild (issues/drop-rejoin-robust.md), so there it checks a fixpoint, and the incremental joins against it. Touched: an aspect where a held part's status (valid, dropped, missing) changed or a new part arrived not valid and whole, or whose state was written and no new commit names it, always; one only new valid whole parts joined, when its held commits pass a power of four in the transaction (a wrong state stays wrong, so a later sample still sees it; crossing, not landing on, so a batch arrival is not skipped). Gap: a write the state holds and no body gives passes (`rebuilt` starts from the own state); comparing with the bodies alone where every part is held whole is stronger (issues/misstated-strip-key-erased-remnant.md). Invisible to the tests: costs are paused (`costs::paused`) and the state cache restored. A test hook writing a state no body gives sets `Core::unchecked_tx` (`damage_index`, `free::memory_state_row_follows_key`). A failure panics with the differing Debug lines; a test it finds a bug in becomes `#[ignore] repro_...` naming the issue. Death dates are not checked (a node dates a death at the first superseder it met, spec/commits.md §5), nor indexes (`index_digest` tests compare a reopen). Cost: about +16% of tier 1's CPU (every touched aspect at every commit would be about +40%), mostly `rebuilt` decoding commit records.

**Store-contents differential** (a refactor that must not change stored bytes or write order): seeded `LocalPeer`/`MemoryBackend` stores are byte-deterministic, rng-sealed rows included, once the persisted index tag is pinned (`index.rs` `VERSION` folds in `OSIS_INDEX_RULES`, a hash of the sources). Throwaway patch, applied to main and the branch alike: in `MemoryBackend::commit` fold each batch's ops into a running hash, and in a `Drop for Data` append `thread-name trace-hash final-hash batches rows` to `$OSIS_DUMP_FILE` (add `OSIS_DUMP_FILE` to core/base/testenv-known.txt, or the harnesses refuse it); set `VERSION` to a constant; run tier 1 plus the order oracles on each, sort, diff. About 12,400 stores. Tests that differ run to run on one build (crash, store, runtime, driver, cli, some notes/sync/encodings tests, `apply::tests_paged`'s three paged-snapshot tests, osis `api::created_reads_through_run_time_key_*`: async, OS randomness) differ between builds too; rerun those on one build to rule them out. A store dropped on another thread (`osis-dispatch`) moves its line to that name between runs: compare the lines without names too.

Integration tests are one binary per crate: `tests/it/main.rs` with a module per file (`tests/it/<module>.rs`; notes/architecture.md, Conventions), so a test's name is `<module>::<fn>`. Single-file crates (osis-base, osis-vectors, crdt-model) keep `tests/<file>.rs` (`--test cbor`, `--test vectors`, `--test props`). Nothing requires nextest, nightly or cargo-fuzz. Tier 1 never sleeps or touches the network. Tier 3 by hand: `cargo +nightly fuzz run --fuzz-dir . <target> -- -max_total_time=300` per target in `core/test/fuzz/` (crash inputs land in `core/test/fuzz/artifacts/`), `sim-sweep` (`--out <dir>` writes failing seeds), `cargo run --bin roles-model` plus `-- 2000000 joint|edges|disputes`, `core/test/tla/run.sh small`, `cargo bench --workspace`, `cargo llvm-cov --workspace --lcov --output-path lcov.info`.

**Tier-1 cost** (quiet box, warm): `cargo test --workspace` 152 s wall, 1900 CPU-s, 2834 tests; osis-node `it` 65 s / 985 CPU-s (`order::` 42% of its thread time), osis `it` 27 s, engine lib 12 s. Under the parallel run single tests show 10-40 s by contention; alone the slowest are `order::arrival_order_found_histories_spec_crdt_4_6` (pinned histories, LARGE orders each) and the pinned `seed_<n>` order tests at 2-7 s.
- The run is CPU-bound, not tail-bound: the same tests take about 2.3x less CPU run one at a time (hyperthreads, all-core clocks, memory traffic). Measured not to help: every binary at once, osis-node `it` beside the others in series, harness inner threads at 1, opt-level 2 for the workspace crates (one-line edit builds 15-20% slower), selecting tier-1 crates by the diff (node and engine take most commits: 15% at best, opens a gap). Only less work shortens it.
- In place: osis-crypto at `opt-level = 2` in dev; smaller data where the property is relative; tier 2 for sizes; debug cache cross-checks compared once per `Engine::point` (`Engine::recheck`: `labels_of`, `labels`, `inbox_label`, `inbox_actors`, `holders_named`, `head_keys`, `check_unchanged`, `vet_inbox_hints`), since a fresh read repeats itself until a commit, the indexes or the clock move (`secrets_in` runs inside a transaction and still checks every hit); `LocalPeer::sync_all` stops after a round of duplicates (a fixpoint) and does not repeat a delivery that came back a duplicate and moved nothing; the forensic attack's last-byte key test (below). What is left is real work: ingest, decode and signatures (`CommitBlob::decode_as` 15% of node `it`, half of it delete-key point decompression at ingest). `fill.rs` `N` stays at 500 because `a_push_during_a_fill_comes_next` needs more than 3.5 batches.
- Per-test times: run a test binary with `RUSTC_BOOTSTRAP=1 <binary> -Z unstable-options --report-time` from its package dir; CPU per test with `/usr/bin/time <binary> --exact <name>`. Where a test's CPU goes: `tools/cpuprof OUT <test binary> --exact <name>` (an LD_PRELOAD SIGPROF sampler: no perf or gdb here; inclusive and self shares per function); over a whole binary (`--test-threads=8`) it shows what all tests share. A test binary run outside cargo runs proptests at 256 cases: set `PROPTEST_CASES=32` as `.cargo/config.toml` does.

osis-crdt's seeded random tests (every `testutil::rng(seed)`, fixed-seed loops and proptest cases alike) sample other runs with `OSIS_CRDT_SEED=<n>`: a splitmix hash of n xor'ed into each run's seed; unset, the committed seeds. A panicking test prints the effective seed. `fuzz_seq::random_scripts_replay_spec_5_7` replays random `seq_ops` scripts the same way; `OSIS_SHRINK=<loop seed> cargo test --workspace -- --ignored shrink_script` (with the same `OSIS_CRDT_SEED`) drops steps and ops of a failing one while it fails and prints it.

osis-perm's random runs (`differential`, `queries`, `incremental`) sample other histories with `OSIS_PERM_SEED=<n>`; each tier-2 run is `common::sharded`: 16 shards on scoped threads, shard i seeded `seed ^ i << 40` (fixed count, so a failing seed reproduces anywhere; the panic names the shard's seed). A shard runs only while it holds one of a process-wide pool of permits, a thread per core (`OSIS_PERM_THREADS` overrides), so the 15 concurrent tests keep about 16 busy threads instead of 240. Sample counts keep the counted paths (appended, placed, restored) well over their asserted floors; `append_shapes_*` costs about 1.2 CPU-s a sample, so it has 384.

How to replay a failing sim seed: `sim-sweep` prints `osis_sim::replay("<scenario>", <seed>)`; call it from a test (`cargo test -p osis-sim --test it`) or add it as an ignored `<scenario>_seed_<n>` test naming its issue (Phase C, Blocked). `OSIS_SIM_TRACE=1` prints every trace line. A "read differently" failure ends with a divergence dump: per node, every commit naming the differing aspect with its part's status (dropped, missing, valid, covered by) and the aspect's state (`Engine::aspect_dump`).

What nothing covers: the scale checks' "Not covered" list, the TLA+ "Not covered" list, the items of issues/tests-unbuilt.md, QUIC and iroh tests outside `cargo test-net`, browsers, real networks beyond localhost.

## Models (core/test/: crdt-model, roles-model, held-model, tla)
- `core/test/crdt-model` and `core/test/roles-model`: Rust reference models (notes/crdt.md, notes/permissions.md); the roles model's exhaustive tiers and random samples are tier 3.
- Roles model API (core/test/roles-model/src/lib.rs): per model `walk(h)` (pure), `random_hist`, `quiescent(h)` (each node's walk over the writes in its delivery order equals the canonical one; a search counter and a tier-1 test) and a `Display` trace; `shrink::shrink` reduces a failing history to a 1-minimal one (fewer writes, ops, nodes and actors, lower roles and edges, receipts dropped, times and commit IDs renumbered). The searches and osis-perm's differential runs print counterexamples shrunk, as traces. The joint walk's code follows §5.1 step by step. `roles-model -- N digest` hashes every verdict of N random histories per model (std `DefaultHasher`, stable within one toolchain); a refactor keeps it: at 1,000,000 joint c1e2a7ce5bfba6c7, edges 6dd857bba4cb80a4, edges-prov 42995b30bc031d58.
- `core/test/held-model`: what a node holding a body set reads, written from spec/ alone (retention, validity, apply); compared with the order harness (Arrival-order differential, held-model comparison). Its AMBIGUITIES.md is the rule backlog.
- Differentials against models: crdt join and wire (osis-crdt `tests_seq`, `tests_join`), the crdt decoder (osis-crdt `tests_decoder.rs`; notes/crdt.md, Reference model), perm (osis-perm `tests/it/differential.rs`).
- `core/test/tla/sync` and `core/test/tla/rotation`: TLA+ models written from spec/ alone, checked with TLC. Sync covers one label at one host: upload, download, deposit and promotion, deletes by delete key (blob-ID carrier tiebreak) and timed deletes (snapshot first when writes stay live), expiry, compaction, purge and rewrite, due snapshots (also before a live drop), tombstones, offline nodes, a dropping/replaying/reordering host; properties: no live write leaves the label uncarried, must-hold bodies converge to the host, must-not-hold bodies and duplicates do not persist, entries settle, deposits get promoted, purges reach every node, holders end up holding a carrier for their live state. Rotation covers read-role rotation of one actor: established epochs, forced and eager rotation, forks, removal carried in its rotation, grants, registration steps after the rotation chain is uploaded, claims and claim-set drops, stranded heartbeats (also on a refused claim), deposit-seed refresh, re-seal after a complete pass (by inbox and under the label), rotation by inbox to holders without a box at the epoch before, per-node box knowledge, co-reader peers, and an adversary planting boxes (secrets from pending or invalid commits are reading-only); properties: no departed holder reads content written with its departure known, no departed holder holds an epoch that did not box it, nothing is encrypted under a planted secret (a negative control with the rule removed fails in 4 states), every current holder eventually opens every current epoch and reads everything. `core/test/tla/roles` (written from spec/, checked against osis-perm where the text was subtle) covers the role walk (permissions §1–§2, §5.1, §5.2): owner chains, B as a holder on A, `until`, self-removals, explicit demotions with frontiers, the demoter's kept standing, several-grant removals, layer-2 content, the depth cap; a state is a history (any ts, any frontier subset); properties: drop order does not matter, a removed holder's later writes are invalid, demoters keep and are audited on their standing, owner-chain authority confers write within the depth cap. Snapshot neutrality is the costly check (`MC_snapA` 72k states 9 min, `MC_snapB` 73k 12 min, `MC_until` 108k 10 min at 1 worker). Each README gives the instances with results, state counts and times; each AMBIGUITIES.md the findings; open ones: `MC_drop_refresh` (a re-seal trigger residue), roles `MC_sticky` (sticky drops).
- TLC is tier 3, not part of the merge gate. `core/test/tla/run.sh small|all [filter]` downloads the jar to `~/.cache/tla` if missing and runs the listed instances with 1 worker (`TLC_WORKERS` overrides); `small` is `sync/deposit`, `sync/mostabs`, `rotation/MC_live`, `MC_grant`, `MC_plant`, `MC_safety`, `MC_fork`, `roles/MC_core`, `MC_multi`, the roles expected failures and the `sides` instances; `all` adds instances of up to about 20 minutes each, run by hand. Instances that reproduce an open finding (`MC_drop_refresh`, roles `MC_sticky`) and the negative control `MC_keepoff` are listed in the script as expected failures: the script exits non-zero on any other violation, on an expected failure that passes (update the docs), or on a TLC error.
- `core/test/tla/sides`: a design exploration (notes/ at hand, not written from spec/ alone) of hosts during an admin dispute, nodes on two sides of one label, step 6 candidates as modes (notes/owner-removal-plan.md step 6); `matrix.sh` runs every scenario under every mode.
- **Not covered by the TLA+ models**: ownerless things (the Rust `joint` model has them), several labels or hosts, sections and certificate copies (but a grant of a holder actor held as its grant alone, rotation `MC_c3_proj`), retagging, parked bodies and unfetched pages, clock skew, range reconciliation details, delete levels, purge classes, nodes offline past `ROLE_KEEP`, connections and timeouts, signals; write and admin rotation, keysets and departures inside a holder, demotion and `until` in rotation, step-tree branches, steps without rotation, entry boxes and key actors, `label.0` and `secrets.0` compaction (so snapshot-established epochs), the stale-view carrier race; in the roles model invites and links, more than two things and the grant `keyset`. A spec change that touches their sections updates and re-runs them.
- **Method for a new TLA+ target**: a fresh agent given spec/ and the target's sections only (never notes/ or code) writes the model under `core/test/tla/<target>/` with a README (instances, results, times), an assumptions list and an ambiguity list (the findings); crypto is access (secrets as set membership, boxes as edges); 2–3 nodes, 1–2 hosts, tiny bounds, symmetry sets; safety as invariants, liveness under weak fairness of honest steps; each property cites its spec sentence. Findings go to spec first, then the model is updated to the fixed text and rerun. TLA+ when notation independence matters and the state is small; stateright when an actor-shaped model would also serve the simulator.

**Confidentiality over `LocalPeer`**: `Package`/`deliver`/`sync` hand over plaintext commits, so a peer that lost access still reads whatever it is sent: no "who can read" claim holds there. Such claims use the sealed mode: `LocalPeer::sealed` (rotates first as node-sync's `uncovered` does, then `Engine::seal_package`: each body sealed and wrapped under its reader's secret, none under an uncovered read role) and `deliver_sealed` (`Engine::open_package`, as node-sync opens an entry; retried while one opens). flows.rs `sealed_write` asserts a peer that does not read the write opened none of its bodies; `spread_sealed` is `spread` sealed. Plaintext stays right for pairing/setup and for tests of what an engine does with what arrives (order, validity). Inbox blobs are real either way (`carry`, `accept_inbox`).

## Conformance vectors (core/test/vectors/, crate osis-vectors)
- Format, subjects and rule IDs: core/test/vectors/README.md (language-neutral, ships with the spec). `.vec` text files of `key: value` records; every case has `case` and `spec`; `pending: <reason>` marks a case not yet checked.
- `core/test/vectors/MANIFEST` lists every required file: `pending` (crate not landed), `provisional`, or `frozen <blake3> <FC-n>`. **Nothing is frozen before the first stable release (Tess): no format-change log is kept until then.** The freeze tooling stays for the release: a frozen file then changes only with a `## FC-n` entry in core/test/vectors/FORMAT_CHANGES.md naming it and `gen-vectors freeze <path> FC-n`; the `manifest_policy` test enforces this.
- Generated files (all but `cbor/` and `padme/`) must equal a fresh `cargo run --bin gen-vectors`; hand-written files have a checker that re-derives each case. Fixed inputs: seeds are 32 repeated bytes, randomness injected (each generator in `core/test/vectors/src/subjects/` states its inputs).
- State (MANIFEST is the source): all provisional. `cbor/values.vec` and `padme/padme.vec` are hand-written; `tid/thing.vec` and `chunker/gear.vec` boundaries were cross-checked by an independent Python implementation. Waiting on Tess's calls: `blob/blobs.vec` (the commit blob case), `inbox/inbox.vec`.
- Judgment vectors (`judgment/`, format in core/test/vectors/README.md): a bundle of the labels' bodies and referenced blobs, the read secrets, the clock and an arrival order in; per body validity, held/dropped/missing, covering snapshots and deaths, rosters, D, state and read digests out. Generated and replayed in osis-node `tests/it/judgment.rs` (histories by `order::play`, all with seed 1038 so a file's bundles share one `setup` header; `judgment_vectors_spec_permissions_5_2` replays every case through a fresh engine and checks the files equal a fresh generation, about 3 s; `OSIS_WRITE_VECTORS=1 ... --ignored judgment_vectors_write` rewrites them); osis-vectors checks their shape. Same freeze policy; a change to a judgment vector's expected output is a spec change. `models.vec` plays the roles model's reference scenarios; `rules.vec` one per family (frontier coverage, re-grant, cover drop and uncover, keep-at-most finality, un-merge, `until`, invite grant; `order::Step::Grant` makes `until`/invite grants, scripted only), then pinned order-harness histories (`judgment.rs` `found()`: each kept only if it judges alike under 1038 and shows a rule no other case does; its doc lists those left out and why). A file's header lists once the expected lines every case shares. A bundle record is about 1 KB per commit; each file's `setup` is 26 KB of hex.

## Fuzzing (core/test/fuzz/, outside the workspace)
core/test/fuzz/README.md: running, the target table (live and planned per crate), crash handling. `seq_ops` (the sequence differential: `arbitrary` op scripts replayed by osis-crdt's `fuzz_seq::replay`, built with `--cfg fuzzing`, which also turns on the crate's test-only checks; `crdt-model` is a `cfg(fuzzing)` dependency) has no committed corpus: libFuzzer grows one from nothing in seconds. Seeds come from the vectors via `gen-vectors corpus` (osis-vectors `corpus.rs`). Nightly fuzzes every target `cargo fuzz list` prints for `fuzz_seconds` (default 300). Never commit `core/test/fuzz/artifacts/` or run output; corpora stay small. Planned targets: `join` (needs `arbitrary`) and `page`.
- Panics: the workspace denies `unwrap_used`, `expect_used`, `panic` and `indexing_slicing` in every library crate (allowed in tests, the cli binary crate, and two commented one-line exceptions: the chunker's u8 index into its 256-entry table, a whole-slice zeroize). Casts on peer numbers checked by hand: every `as usize`/`as u32` in decoders widens or narrows a bounded local length; fuzzing runs with overflow checks on.

## Simulator (core/test/sim/, crate osis-sim)
- Shape: `Sim::new(name, seed, Config)` boots `nodes` real engines, each over its own `MemoryBackend` + `MemoryRoot` sharing one `osis_store::Faults` plan, a `ManualClock` and a seeded rng; all randomness comes from one `Dice` seeded by the run seed. `Config`: per-node `ClockSpec { skew_ms, drift_ppm }` (wall = T0 + elapsed + skew + elapsed·drift/10^6), `NetFaults { drop_pct, dup_pct, max_delay_ms, reorder }`, scheduler `Weights { write, gossip, deliver, tick, crash, restart }`, `max_tick_ms`, `check_every_step`.
- `step()` picks one event: a random write (1–3 random ops of `workload`, a schema with one aspect per encoding), gossip (node → random peer: packages of every applied commit the receiver lacks, an oracle standing in for reconciliation; each through drop/dup/delay), delivery of a due message (earliest, or any under reorder; to a down node it is lost), a tick, a crash (immediate, or at one of the node's next durable writes via `Faults::at(n, Crash)`), a restart (`Peer::restart` over the same backend). `run(n)`, `run_until_quiescent(max_rounds)`. Setup helpers: `create_thing`, `grant`, `revoke`, `commit`, `deliver`, `sync_all`, `advance`, `set_clock`, `read`, `engine`.
- Invariants (`check`): per step, every group of up nodes holding the same parts of the same applied commits reads the same values; per applied event, own `ts` strictly increasing across restarts and within `MAX_DRIFT` (+1 ms) of the wall clock, no remote commit applied more than `MAX_DRIFT` ahead, no counter range of one session overlapping another commit's on one aspect; at quiescence, every node holds the same parts and nothing pends. Engine errors on honest input and panics (caught per run) are failures.
- Failures carry scenario, seed, step and the last 40 trace lines, and print `osis_sim::replay("<scenario>", <seed>)`, which reruns it exactly. A failing sweep seed becomes a test with a one-line description.
- Phase A scenarios: `two_nodes`, `three_random` (all faults, skew ±5 min, drift ±200 ppm), `crash_restart`, `drift_parks` (a node jumps 2 h ahead: its commit pends with `Drift` until the receiver's clock is within `MAX_DRIFT`; needs hand delivery), `revoke_while_writing`, `grant_to_group` (a grant to a group with its admin away, a later reader, a removal, then a grant of an unlabeled thing: `Engine::unrouted` until `create_label`, spec/sync.md §2-§3). `OSIS_SIM_SEEDS=N [OSIS_SIM_ONLY=a,b] cargo test --workspace sweep -- --ignored` is a small sweep.

### API world (core/test/sim/src/world.rs)
`World`: `osis::Osis` handles on `Runtime::Manual` and osis-host `Hub`s over in-memory queues under one `ManualClock` (the osis crate's tests use it): `add_host`, `cfg`/`account`, `run`, `advance`, `until(future, step, n)` (panics if it does not resolve), `set_online` (offline nodes still tick), `diagnostics`/`trace`, `traffic` (bytes per node) and `start_tally`/`tally` (upstream frames by message kind); a storm (`STORM` rounds in one settle) panics naming what it carries.

### Phase B: sync engines and hosts (core/test/sim/src/net)
- `NetSim::new(name, seed, NetConfig)`; `add_peer` (osis-host `Hub` over `HostStore` on a `MemoryBackend` and `MemoryRoot` with their own `Faults`), `replicate(a, b)`, `add_node(hosts, ClockSpec)` (`SyncEngine` over an `Engine`, its own skewed `ManualClock`). Actions are carried engine to engine: `Dial` makes a link (or a `Down`), `Send` queues bytes on the stream, `Close` drops it, `Wake` is fired when the node's clock reaches it. Connections are ordered byte streams: `LinkFaults { cut_pct, dup_pct, max_delay_ms }`, none looking at what is sent: each link direction releases at random moments of the network clock (keyed by link, direction and bucket; streams overtake each other, each keeps its order), a link is cut at keyed moments, what is on its way lost with it (both ends told), a release may arrive twice; compared runs (`with_arrivals`) deliver all that is due in a deliver step, any other run one message a step, so events and cuts fall inside a burst; only deliver steps move the network clock. Duplication repeats a message (a protocol violation the receiver must survive).
- Scheduler (`NetWeights`): write, deliver, tick (time, wakes, hub ticks, host redials), node crash (now or at a durable write) and restart, host crash and restart, partition / heal of a (node, host) or (host, host) pair. Also `sever`, `wipe_host` (data loss), `lose_disk`, `reconnect_all`. Setup is calm (no faults): flows helpers `person`, `pair`, `introduce`, `group` (+ `create_label`), `grant`, `remove`, `item`, `carry`, `quiet(max)`; `run(n)` turns faults on, `run_until_quiescent(max)` heals, restarts everything, settles a resync interval (61 s) at a time until idle and unchanged for two rounds, then checks.
- Quiescent invariants (`net/check.rs`): convergence by held deltas (two nodes holding the same deltas of an aspect read it the same); nothing pends; no certificate copy still waits (`SyncEngine::deferred`); holder liveness (every node of an account on an admin's roster, or of the owner, reads the group) and agreement (they read the same items); per host and label: no live entry tombstoned or back after a tombstone, every body a writer judges must-hold (`SyncEngine::verdicts`, the pass's own judgment) in a live entry (a known blob of that commit with a superset of the parts), no entry a node may delete as must-not-hold still live, deposited entries promoted by writers that applied them; purged bodies at no host and no honest node (`holds_blob`); state size (spec/requirements.md Scale): a node's stored bytes ≤ 2 × must + 2 KiB × entries + 16 KiB × labels + 64 KiB, where must and entries are the bytes and count of the live entries of its labels at the fullest honest host (`net/check.rs` `STATE_*`; measured at most 2.11x must and 0.47 of the bound); no ping-pong (three more resync rounds add or delete nothing). A run that never settles reports the churn; a settle of more than 5000 deliveries is a message storm. Phase A's per-event checks run on every node. `OSIS_SIM_TRACE=1` prints every trace line with the proto messages.
- Work sets against full passes (`core/test/sim/tests/it/work_sets.rs`, notes/sync.md Work sets): `net::with_full_passes(f)` makes every node added in `f` judge everything at each pass (`SyncConfig::full_passes`); a scenario run both ways may differ in messages only (work sets may send no more, checked only between runs alike to the bit), with no tolerance on: the counters of faults and writes, when each upload, deposit and registration first reached each host (`net::with_arrivals`: a missed trigger shows as a later arrival) and when a delete took a host's last copy of a commit, and `Report::parts` (what each node and host ends up with: commits, reads, `SyncEngine::state_digest` with entries, presence, carriers, blob records and held bytes). Arrivals and parts name a blob by commit and parts, never by ID, and go by what, not under which label: an ID, wrap, salt, stamp, the log order inside a batch and which copy of a commit stays are the luck of which seal drew first from the node's random stream (`compared_runs_ignore_the_random_stream`); for the same reason a deposit's relay wait hashes its commit, not its ID. Compared runs do not drift (`add_node_with`): a clock corrected by pings would move with when they went. A parted run is a real difference: read its lines. A passing run writes its counts past the test's capture. `honest_flows_wait_for_no_resync` is the same comparison with no faults. `full_passes` is the new pass with everything in scope: the harness guards that the work sets miss no trigger, not that the judging helpers are right (the sweep's invariants do that). Tier 1: scenario `keep_at_most` seed 1; tier 2: scenario `work_sets` seed 1 and every scenario at `OSIS_SIM_SEEDS` (20) from `OSIS_SIM_FROM` (0; `OSIS_SIM_ONLY` narrows): 1073 s for all at 100 seeds on 16 cores.
- Waivers (`NetSim::waivers`, constants in `net/mod.rs`) switch one invariant off for a scenario while its issue is open (none now); `excused` nodes (removed, lost) are left out of liveness, agreement and purge checks.
- Scenarios (`core/test/sim/tests/it/net.rs`): `two_nodes_host` (pairing, crashes mid-write), `three_accounts_two_hosts` (replication, grants by inbox, host crashes, partitions), `removed_writer` (removal, then a writer without the delete key purges the removed writer's body), `reader_relays` (writer severed from s1, a reader carries its stamped entries there), `stranded_reseal` (a node off past `ROLE_KEEP` across two rotations is refused, then re-paired), `host_loses_data` (writers re-register and re-upload), `offline_rotation` (a reader off across two removals walks the steps; removed holders hold no later epoch), replay exactness, `compaction` (two writers compact while writing; covered bodies deleted, then a purge), `disk_loss` (a node recovered from the card writes and converges), `grant_lost_on_crash`, `large_edits` (two nodes edit an 8 MiB blob in turn through a host; bytes sent per edit within 1.2x of the changed chunks plus the root node and 32 KiB).

### Phase C: scenario library, adversaries, version skew (core/test/sim/src/net/{library, adversary, skew, rogue}.rs)
- Library (setup by flows, a faulty `run` around the story's moment, the story's outcome, then the quiescent invariants; tests in `core/test/sim/tests/it/library.rs`): `revocation_bob`, `revocation_carol` (X and Y invalid at Alice and Carol; a re-grant restores both), `stolen_node` (`set_skew` winds the thief's clock back; a revoke backdated to the theft drops the forged edit, keeps the earlier one), `demotion_synced` / `demotion_unsynced` (the pre-sync keeps the late edit; `Unsynced { proceed }` loses it), `link_redeem`, `link_race` (single use; the loser, rotated out of K, is excused), `chat_join_leave`, `folders` (a folder actor owning a subfolder shared separately), `label_replacement`, `label_replacement_offline` (the old label keeps role-class commits and certificates only), `bundles` (a label exported and imported by hand to a node cut off from hosts), `disappearing` (expiring notes under crashes: gone from nodes and hosts), `keep_at_most` (replaced bodies gone after the bound), `concurrent_writers` (an admin, three writers and two readers granted at once: each body the admin wrote goes up once, by the admin; rarely a second upload by a lagging writer; `NetSim::count_uploads`). Story marks go in `s:story.0` (`library::tags`, `add_tag`), which random writes leave alone.
- Ephemeral (`net/ephemeral.rs`, scenarios `ephemeral`, `ephemeral_capped`): random ephemeral (map, string, an expiring flag, an expiring cursor keyed by node, `Put` like `osis::KeepAlive`) and signal writes on windows placed ephemerally in a group and on a durable item; faults add slow hosts (`NetWeights.slow`) and per-host caps (`add_host_with`: `ephemeral_capped` and odd `ephemeral` seeds give s0 a random cap: 0, below one snapshot, about the snapshots, twice them, plenty). `ephemeral::check` (in `quiescent`, first), the defined outcome past a cap made exact: a host is "lossy" when it lacks an entry an up node holds and cannot fit it (`ephemeral_fits`) while every plain entry it keeps is held by some up node; holders connected through non-lossy hosts read durable items' ephemeral state alike; a window synced by all of such a group or none; holders equal, expired flags and cursors absent; each up node's held commits within `COVER_FACTOR` times their cover plus one commit; host ephemeral charge within its cap and total. Signals: at most once per node over the run. After quiescence: `author_leaves` and `signal_reaches` (exactly once at every holder sharing a host with the sender). The durable checks skip `e:` aspects. Trace: `held_detail`.
- Adversaries: hand-driven `ClientSession`s straight into a `Hub` (`NetSim::rogue(s)`, `rogue_do(r, Command) -> events`, `rogue_raw`, `rogue_close`; `label_keys(n, s, g)`). `evil_host` (`NetHost.evil = Evil { drop_pct, hold_pct, replay_pct }`; `NetSim::dishonest` hosts are skipped by the label checks), `evil_host_drops`, `removed_replayer` (stale step refused, purged blob not back, post-removal write never read; colluding with the host, its secrets open no body written after its author held the removal), `malformed_blobs`, `deposit_flood` (bounded by the deposit budget), `equivocating_writer` (a writer's key signs a second commit reusing its counters on one aspect: every node holds both and reads neither; `NetSim::equivocation` exempts it from the honest-counter check), `tombstone_liar` (after a purge the host re-announces the purged entries, `NetSim::host_says`: no node takes the body back or re-uploads it). Expected outcomes are in each doc comment. A stolen pairing offer is out of scope (osis crate tests).
- Version skew: `future_format` (a commit rewritten to `FORMAT_VERSION + 1`: parked everywhere, `NetSim::parked_ok`, relayed, kept through an old writer's compaction), `newer_schema` (version 2 adds a struct field with a default and an aspect; the old node's write and compaction keep both).
- Blocked: `osis_sim::BLOCKED` maps scenarios to the issue that breaks them (empty); their tests are ignored with the issue's name and the sweeps skip them. A failing sweep seed of an unblocked scenario becomes an ignored test `<scenario>_seed_<n>` naming its issue. Known-failing repros of open issues (any crate) are named `repro_*` with `#[ignore = "issues/<name>.md"]`; every `--include-ignored` periodic command adds `--skip repro_`, and the fix renames the test (or un-ignores it).
- `sim-sweep` (dev profile, notes/build.md): `--seeds N --from S --only a,b --blocked --budget-secs T --jobs J --out DIR --list`; runs seed-major across scenarios on J threads until done or out of budget, prints failures per scenario and each failing seed's `osis_sim::replay` call, writes `DIR/failing-seeds.txt`, exits 1 on any failure. Nightly: `--seeds 10000 --budget-secs 6000`. The dev profile (opt-level 1) runs a 30-seed sweep in 1.3x the release time.
- Tier 1 runs seed 1 of the quickest (phase A: two_nodes, three_random, drift_parks; phase B: two_nodes_host and its seed 23, host_loses_data, grant_lost_on_crash, replay exactness; library: demotion_synced, stolen_node, keep_at_most, disappearing seed 104). Everything else is `#[ignore = "tier 2"]` (3-16 s each in debug) and swept nightly.

## Arrival-order differential (osis-node `tests/it/order.rs`)
Validity, un-merge and state must be functions of the held set (spec/requirements.md Decentralization).
- **Histories**: random on three `LocalPeer`s (0 owns G, 1 writes, 2 admin): content writes on every encoding including writes inside map entries, struct fields and texts in map entries, grants and re-grants, demotions (removal or to read) with frontiers before, inside and after the holder's writes, self-removals, roster and compaction snapshots, things created under things in later commits, `Nested` (one peer puts an entry, another, sent it alone, writes inside and maybe compacts), undo, expiry (`t:brief.0`, and ops 27-29: writes expiring after 1 s or 30 min; hours pass), bounded keeps (a kept register, keep-at-most-0 registers and map, a kept set, a kept nested map write; ops 30 and 31 under an absolute keep at most, a list insert and a register also under 0 after replaced, `retention_of`; every delivery ends with a `tick`, as node-sync's `expire_due` before every read), partial syncs and single sends. Steps are `Step::Aim`ed: at play time the author and thing are pointed at ones that can write it (`aim`), so 22% of commit steps make no commit; most writes are `Typed` (the schema's key, `fit`ted offsets), a quarter untyped for the `Slots` positions. `Forged` (`commit_hostile`): an invalid write, a commit on two things, a role commit on two things, a raised own grant, over things its author may have no chain to (`NewTop`). Each step's outcome is tallied and `run` asserts floors (`Seen::floors`; a floor must not fail on a lucky seed range); `OSIS_ORDER_STATS=1` prints them. Fresh nodes sync things by `seed % 3` (`with_syncs`, only under `run`): none; `Syncs::Early`; `Syncs::Late`.
- **Orders**: every commit made is a package; held sets are the whole set plus partial ones (alternately without one writer's commits after the setup, and a random two thirds). Each held set goes to fresh nodes (one seed, one clock) in several orders: as made (the reference), reversed, every order after the setup when the whole set is held and small, random shuffles; the first two again with late parts (`deliver_split`). Clock mode (`Cfg::clock` orders per held set, 2 in SMALL/LARGE, 24 in EXHAUST; `OSIS_ORDER_CLOCK=n` overrides, 0 off, 720 every order): the order's first 1..all commits arrive at an earlier clock (`clock_of`: just after or before the prefix's last `ts`, up to an hour after, or anywhere from an hour before the first commit, so drift parks), then the clock moves to the end and the rest arrive; `alike` ignores received times. Reversing what follows the setup with the setup first (exhaustive order #2) finds what random shuffles at scale do not. Focused histories (`focused_history`): two bounded aspects per history, their compactions, undo, demotions and grants, `Nested`, one clock pulled ahead, sends and syncs, cut to a prefix within the commit bound; `OSIS_ORDER_ALL_HELD=1` tries every subset of what follows the setup (2.3x).
- **Compared** by `alike`: reads, states, rosters, pending sets and timelines at the fresh nodes' time, and reads and states two hours later (`later`: every bound and expiry past), exact. Which bodies a node holds and how it dates deaths are per node and are not compared: **a seed where bodies or dates differ but reads agree and the per-node checks hold is not an issue.** Bodies dropped before a validity flip are given to both nodes again before comparing (`alike_exchanged`: `drop_flips`, `both_dropped_flips`, `purge_flips`; spec/crdt.md §4.6). One node's reopen compares exactly, facts included, and its reopened index against a rebuild's (`IndexDigest::first_diff`); a rebuild reads by `alike`. `plain_state` drops a deleted char from the compared states (spec/crdt.md §5.7 leaves it to the node).
- **Per-node checks** against a fresh node keeping every body (`oracle`, `Engine::keep_bodies`): (a) `body_past_bound` two hours later, no applied body under a keep at most held past its bound (absolute: valid or not; first death plus D plus grace; no death means no bound only for a body whose killers went with dropped bodies, never for a write invalid here or one an un-merge took), and `bound_kept_now` (a dead body under at most 0 is gone at once and none went before the keep-everything node's earliest superseder plus D); (b) `live_write_lost` later: reads as the keep-everything node on an aspect where nothing fell, flipped or was purged; given every body again reads as it and holds every write live there; (c) `value_past_end`, now and later, no value of a delta past its expiry or that a standing purge request drops, in the state, a held body or a waiting copy (`Engine::pending_value_past_end`). Mutants that fail them: never dropping at the bound, a body with a live write kept past it, a bound 12 h late, a lost keep-at-most timer, dropping before the bound, a keep-at-most drop taking a live value, ignoring a copy of a dropped body, a no-op expiry timer, a past-expiry part kept waiting, a purge holding nothing value-less. `live_write_outlives_its_body_at_the_bound` pins a rare shape (about 1 in 40 seeds). Not caught: a map entry key kept past expiry (no read check on keys; `waits::expired_entry_goes_with_nested_write_alike_spec_crdt_4_7` covers it), anything limited to an aspect flagged `PendingExpiry` until a compaction.
- `arrival_order_settled_*` (full sync, no commit held in part, every writer compacts every aspect): all orders read alike, the "until a writer next compacts" of the Deterministic exceptions (`alike_repaired` stands in for the repair snapshot). Not reached: every writer dropped what a reader kept, pinned by `flip_after_drop_only_a_reader_holds_it_lasts_to_the_next_write_spec_sync_7`; the repair path by `flip_after_drop_mended_by_a_writer_holding_it_spec_sync_7`. A part held pending at its expiry (flag `PendingExpiry`): that aspect's reads and states are not compared; with an early clock a commit such a part applied at one node only has its pending line left out (`parted`).
- **Runner** (`run`): one seed per task on a thread per core (`OSIS_ORDER_THREADS`); a seed's history and orders depend only on the seed, the length and the test, so `OSIS_ORDER_SEED=n` replays exactly; every failing seed is shrunk (dropping steps) and reported with its replay command, the shrunk `h = vec![..]` for `check(seed, &h, CFG)`, the two orders and the first differing line. `OSIS_ORDER_SEED`, `OSIS_ORDER_START`, `OSIS_ORDER_SEEDS`, `OSIS_ORDER_LEN` narrow or widen; `OSIS_ORDER_SHRINK=0` reports unshrunk; `OSIS_ORDER_DUMP=1` prints, on an order's failure, the packs, each order's reads as they change after every ingest, both nodes' flag sets and every judged part (`dump_orders`). `Engine::body_fragment` (no caller: a triage hook) gives a held body as a fragment where `body_page` gives only its read. One order costs about 10 ms of CPU, twice that with late parts; a failing large seed's shrink is sequential, about a minute.
- **Tests**: tier 1 `arrival_order_alike_small_*` (4 seeds of 8 steps, about 1 s), `arrival_order_exhaustive_small_*` (3 focused histories of up to 5 commits, every order: about 280 orders, 2.5 s wall), the found histories; tier 2 `arrival_order_alike_large_*` (60 seeds of 60 steps, 8 shuffles, 4 partial sets), `arrival_order_exhaustive_large_*` (40 seeds from 2023, histories cut to at most 6 commits, up to 720 orders each).

**held-model comparison** (`tests/it/held.rs`, `Cfg::model`): every node the order harness judges (now and later) is also converted into held-model's input and compared on in-scope aspects: applied commits (role commits too), validity, which edge writes apply, reads, no body the model says must be gone, no forbidden value, and every body the node dropped (given back from the keep-everything `Oracle::bodies`) one the model lets go. Input: every held commit and known creation record, undo, acting, invites, `until`, self-removals, finalizes and `required.0^` as the node's role input holds them (`ix.held`); author ids in tid order. Scope: register, map-of-registers and text aspects; left out (counted by reason): paged, ephemeral, waits for blobs or parked, suspect, other shapes, a `required.0^` prefix the model cannot say, and everything when the role input is outside the model. On: `arrival_order_purge_small` and `held_model_agrees_on_purge_rights` (tier 1), `arrival_order_purge_large`, `arrival_order_exhaustive_large`. `until` grants are not reached by these histories. Clocked deliveries are also compared after every ingest: a drop before its bound or a value kept past its expiry shows only there; a body past its keep-at-most bound is not required gone while the node has the aspect's deaths due (`ix.deaths_due`). Missing parts, and parts waiting alone in an applied commit, are fed as missing; `Dropped::gave` goes in as `dropped_valid`. Caught mutants: a purge request standing without the right, keep at most 0 dropping a live body with no grace, a purge right judged at the delta's `ts`, an already-expired arrival's value kept until the next tick. A keep-at-most-0 snapshot body dropped with no live write (`Dropped::live`) past a death is let go: liveness is judged at the drop (spec/commits.md §5), and a body held later may make a write it carries live; that acceptance trusts the node's `live` record, and early drops are judged by the order harness's bound check instead.

## Seed runners (`osis_base::testenv`)
`run_seeds(name, seeds, visible, replay, f)` (and `Seeds::run`, which announces an env range first) runs `f(seed)` for every seed on a thread per core (`OSIS_SEED_THREADS` overrides, one variable for every harness), a panic as its seed's failure (`caught`), results in seed order; it panics on an empty seed list. Each failing seed prints with its replay command as it fails, a progress line every 60 s, then a summary line, then a panic listing every failure in seed order. A seed whose thread has used `OSIS_SEED_TIMEOUT_SECS` (default 600) CPU-seconds, or no CPU at all for that many seconds (blocked), is marked hung and a new thread takes its place (CPU time from `/proc/<tid>/schedstat`). Not wall time: under heavy load a 5 CPU-s seed can take 700 s wall. Only the seed's own thread counts (seeds are single-threaded). A hung thread prints its backtrace at its next `testenv::checkpoint()` (called by `LocalPeer::sync` and the node test `Net::run`). A hung thread cannot be stopped or joined: when the rest is done with hung seeds still running, or as many seeds hang as there are threads, the runner prints every failure and hung seed and exits the process (101). `par_map`/`caught`/`threads` are the building blocks; the order harness uses them for its own shrinking runner.

Rule: a seed-ranged harness's tier-1 test runs fixed seeds and reads no environment; its `#[ignore]` `_large` twin reads the `OSIS_*_START`/`_SEEDS` range (`--ignored --exact <name>_large`), and every printed replay line names the twin. So `--ignored --exact` on a harness name either runs it or matches nothing (`0 passed`), never a silent default run.

## Forensic attack (`osis_store::attack`, `Engine::forensic`)
The definition of "forensically clean" (spec/requirements.md, Deletion). Evidence: the keys the current store tree yields, the label read secrets the node's code computes now (`Engine::forensic` adds them), and every row and blob the backend was ever given (`MemoryBackend::record_history`: `history`, `blob_history` with frees; `fork` copies a recording device). To a fixpoint it reads every row and blob raw (clear rows `r`, `p`, `j`; a blob stored in clear), opens rows with store keys (tree nodes yield more), takes every random-looking 32 bytes (at least 16 distinct byte values) of anything recovered as a candidate key on every blob (link keys, node-sync `y/b` keys, anything), and for entries keyed by a blob ID (store wraps, node-sync `y/e`; table pages decoded by the store's own `forensic_leaf`, overflow values joined) tries the value's leading wrap under every candidate as a read secret. Each find says where (`Source`: row, raw row, blob opened by key or by wrap, raw blob).
- Not covered: keys inside a blob stored in clear (raw blobs and raw rows are not harvested: ciphertext would flood the candidates; clear rows are); a wrap not at the start of a blob-keyed entry's value; secrets only derivable from dropped ones (the attack never runs KDF chains over old secrets); memory, swap, the root store's past records; non-memory backends (redb: `file_backend_holds_no_plaintext` greps the file). Markers find content; a dropped map key's keyed index hash is checked as an extra needle where it matters (`expired_entry_paged_*`).
- "Random-looking" misses a real random key with probability about 2^-55 (fewer than 16 distinct values in 32 random bytes); a false positive costs time only.
- Cost: about 40-60K candidates × the unopened blobs (and their wraps), and every store key × every row; a try first decrypts only the last plaintext byte (`aead::last_plain_byte`: padded plaintexts end in 0x80 or 0x00, so any other byte rules the key out; exact, the MAC pass still decides), so a wrong key costs one ChaCha block instead of a MAC pass over the blob. Under a second per attack; one thread per blob; crash loops fork one set-up device per crash point instead of setting up again; `shred` tests attack once per state (`common::recovered` checks several markers).
- Row lengths: `shredded_small_drop_keeps_unit_lengths_within_a_class_step` checks a shredded small drop leaves each rewritten unit's padded length equal or one Padmé class step away.
- Tests: store `tests/it/shred.rs` (units, wraps, blob keys and wraps, clear rows), node `shred.rs` (engine purge, expiry, keep-at-most, paged, chunked), `shred_sync.rs` (a synced node's purge online and offline, and a crash at every write of the purge and node-sync's forget), `flows.rs` (removal erasure, crash-safe), host `hygiene.rs`, `metadata.rs`, osis `hosting.rs` (host stores: rows only).

## Spec-to-test map
`python3 tools/spec_map.py` writes notes/testing-map.md: per numbered spec section, the tests and live vector cases citing it; the rule checklist; then gaps and uncited tests. Regenerate at integration (freshness is not part of the merge gate, since concurrent branches would conflict on it); `--check` tests freshness, `--uncited` (merge gate) fails on a test in core/ or cli/ (models exempt) that cites nothing, `--gaps` (merge gate) fails on a rule with neither a test nor an issue, or a MUST sentence no rule anchors.
- **Rules**: every MUST / MUST NOT sentence of spec/*.md plus the refusal rules of spec/permissions.md and spec/sync.md ("cannot", "refused"), each a `RULES` entry (file, §, anchor, a substring of the sentence) in the script. A test or an issue covers a rule by citing `spec/<file>.md §N (anchor)`; a new MUST sentence fails `--gaps` until it gets an entry. `VACUOUS` lists rules nothing can test yet (no batch verification; format version 1 only).
- **Not covered by the map**: SHOULDs (listed in the map, not gated), policy (budgets, rate limits, caps: osis-host tests cite them as `spec: none` or by section), declarative normative text with no MUST (covered only at section level), spec/requirements.md (no numbered sections or MUSTs; its items are covered by reviews), and the out-of-scope list of spec/requirements.md.
- **Metadata tests** (core/node/tests/it/metadata.rs, real engines over an osis-host hub): stored bytes hold no value, map key, tid, AK, node tid or signing key, or role secret (a label-ID control proves the search works); every stored blob's padded length is a Padmé length and 48 value lengths fall in at most 5 size classes (Padmé leaks O(log log n) bits by design); two labels share no blob ID, salt or wrap; claim sets of 3 and 4 readers are both 4, of 5 readers 8, shuffled; heartbeats write only `seen.0` on their node. Harness note: `Net::group` seeds every rotation with 1, so two groups of one admin get equal secrets and one label ID: use `group_seeded`.
- **Negative tests**: host refusals live in osis-host tests/it/limits.rs and hub.rs and osis-proto tests/it/loopback.rs (the host session); perm refusals in osis-perm tests/it/scenarios.rs; node ones in osis-node tests/it/flows.rs, peers_review.rs, negative.rs (keep at least); QUIC mutual auth and no 0-RTT in osis-transport `quic::tests` (tier 2, `--features quic -- --ignored`).

## Conventions for every crate
- **Spec citations**: put `// spec/<file>.md §N[.M]: <what>` right above each `#[test]` (inside `proptest!` too); a bare `§N` refers to the last file named in that comment. A test without its own citation inherits the file's `//!` header citations. Infrastructure tests with no spec section say `// spec: none (<why>)`.
- **Network in tests**: tier 1 uses `osis_transport::{Net, Sched}` (in-process, virtual steps, seeded faults and scheduling; no tokio); real-socket tests (QUIC on localhost) are tier 2.
- **Tiers**: tier-2 tests are `#[ignore]` (optionally also gated on `OSIS_SLOW=1`); a crate's tier 1 stays under about 10 s.
- **Seed loops**: a loop over consecutive seeds gives each its own run: `rng(seed | 1)` makes seeds 2k and 2k+1 the same history; use odd seeds via `step_by(2)` (the paged-apply harness seeds `seed << 1 | 1`). Harmless `| 1` left: crdt-model `tests/props.rs` (proptest draws random seeds), osis-node `it/walks.rs` (seeds multiplied by an odd constant first). A multi-seed tier-2 run goes through `testenv::run_seeds`, not a serial loop.
- **Vectors**: in the change that lands an encoding: write a generator `fn() -> VecFile` in `core/test/vectors/src/subjects/<subject>.rs` from fixed inputs (32 repeated bytes per seed), register it in `subjects::get`, add the crate as an osis-vectors dependency, flip the MANIFEST line to `provisional`, run `gen-vectors`, and add the file to `vector_files!` in core/test/vectors/tests/vectors.rs. Hand-written vectors get a checker instead. Rejections use rule IDs documented in core/test/vectors/README.md.
- **Fuzz**: a target per parser of peer or disk bytes (core/test/fuzz/README.md, "Adding a target"), seeds in `corpus.rs`; a crash becomes a regression test in the owning crate citing the spec section and `fuzz: <target>`.
- **Budgets and benches**: in osis-bench as above; a new external dependency for them needs its line in notes/architecture.md §5.

## Performance budgets
Coordinator-owned targets (Tess delegated; prefer simple and verifiable code that meets them over faster intricate code). Beyond them, one aspect at 10^5 and 10^6 commits: Scale checks, long aspect. At 10^4 commits per aspect and 10^4 things per group, release, a quiet box: a single local write, read or received commit under 50 ms; node open under 1 s; one sync pass after one new write under 100 ms; a late or out-of-order commit under 500 ms (a whole replay of one aspect is acceptable here). Rare events (revocation) under a few seconds. Work beyond these is not pursued without a realistic scenario missing them; issues that only chase speed below them are closed as within budget.

Host (osis-host on its own `Store`, redb and a file root, release, a laptop disk; `file::host_throughput_budget`, by hand): 1 KiB entries at least 1000/s uploaded and 1000/s deleted in 64-entry hub batches (a 1024-entry message in about a second); one single-entry upload batch plus one single-entry delete batch under 20 ms (the delete's shredding commit: two backend commits and the root's two fsynced slot writes, about 4 fsyncs). Measured at load 6, medians of 5: uploads 2160/s, deletes 2220/s, the single-entry pair 13.9 ms. Whole-space Reconcile reads decrypt pages: about 125 ms at 10^5 live entries, 1.06 s at the 2^20 per-label cap (notes/sync.md Hub stall bounds).
