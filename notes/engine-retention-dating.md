# Engine: when a body was replaced
> Summary: How the engine dates a body's replacement for keep-for-D, a function of the held set judged when asked (`retention/bounds.rs`; spec/commits.md §5 Bodies and deaths, spec/crdt.md §4.8): which writes died and when, why a covered drop moves no date, dating only where history is kept, the host delete margin, costs.

## The rule
- Tess: a body under keep-for-D is not dropped until D after the earliest valid replacing write the node holds. Judged when asked (node-sync's `part_keeps`, `drop_from` before a compaction drop, `Engine::replaced_at`), never stored: no death records, no replays, no crediting. Whether a write is dead, and when, is a function of the held bodies, never of how a part is paged.
- Rejected: death dates stored per body (dated by arrival, with crediting, recomputes after flips: the class of bugs this rule removes); dating at the superseder the state holds now (a register changing within D keeps its old bodies for good); deaths reported by the join (a stamp names many writes, so per-stamp reports dated snapshots early); dating by the superseder's header (a header names the aspect, not the writes).

## Which writes died (`deaths`)
- The part's winning writes (`Fragment::winners`, spec/crdt.md §4.8: by position, kind and stamp, never stamp alone, since one op stamps many writes) that do not win once the part joins, whole, a copy of the state at its positions (`Tx::state_part_at`, `Fragment::part_at`: O(part)). Winners, not absence: a position write beaten as `cur` stays as `prev`, a deleted text creation stays in its record, a write beaten in another record of its map dot stays in its own. `winners` is checked against a pairwise from-scratch definition (osis-crdt `tests_winners`). The part's own superseded writes are not its winners. Another held body holds a superseder of every dead write (none: `Error::Corrupt`). None dead: past a relative bound.

## When (`HeldWrites`; spec/crdt.md §4.8 When)
- A dead write dies at the earliest `ts` among held valid writes superseding it alone (osis-crdt `supersedes`, as held-model's `replaced_at`), read from any held valid body other than the part's own. Always the write's own stamp, never the holding body's: a valid snapshot's copy of a write whose delta is held invalid here (vouched) counts as the write itself (Fathom). Dating it at its snapshot instead let a later cover's drop redate it and bring a body out of history back (setting.rs `vouched_copy_dates_at_its_write_*`). A writer stamping early shortens what history keeps: storage reclaim only, never erasure (notes/deletion.md).
- A text element's creation dies only by a delete stamped after the latest held undelete of the element (`ByWrite::first_superseding`), so dropping the first delete of delete, undelete, delete moves nothing.
- Every body dates at the latest death of its writes, deltas too: a body drops only after every dead write it alone holds has left history. A snapshot first leaves out the dead writes a later held valid snapshot holds as winners (`HeldWrites::carried`: that one keeps their history; holding one as `prev` or deleted keeps none). Counting every carried write instead kept every snapshot of an aspect whose long-lived writes keep dying within D: held snapshots grew with the edit count, not the window (held-model `snapshot_replaced_at_last_death_no_later_snapshot_carries_*`). Holding more bodies dates no later; holding the same dates alike.
- The index: the aspect's held valid bodies are read once into `osis_crdt::ByWrite`, every write each holds, winning there or not (`Fragment::held_writes`: a deleted text creation still supersedes position writes below it), with the two earliest bodies holding each. `first_superseding` reads the positions on the dead write's path in stamp order, stopping past the best date found.
- Late arrival: a body arriving after its earliest superseder dropped is dated later, by what is held, and may show where a node holding both would not: an arrival, as a late setting is (Tess's decision 1; held-model's any-order property allows exactly that).
- Limit: a snapshot dropped while a later one carried its dead writes is not brought back if that later one turns invalid before their D passes: those writes' history goes with it.

## Invariant: a valid covered drop moves nothing
A valid covered drop never changes whether any held body is in history, nor its listing, nor the keep bound of a held body in history, nor the replaced-at of a part in history (a part out of history may redate, staying out); only arrivals (bodies, settings, validity changes) and the clock do. A drop is valid only past the body's keep bound (`drop_from <= now`); `Engine::drop_body` skips the bound and is outside it. Why a drop of body B (out: `out(r_B) <= now`) moves no date (`out` keeps replaced order), per date read:
- a superseder's `ts`: a live write of B stays in the cover with its stamp; a write W only a dead write d of B dated redates to at most death(d) <= r_B (supersession is transitive), so W was out and stays out;
- a snapshot's dead writes a later snapshot S carries: S dropping adds back deaths <= r_S; a body in history has r > r_S;
- a text creation's latest undelete: an undelete's body drops only once the undelete is dead, by a delete no earlier than the creation's date, so the creation was out and only dates earlier;
- the state (`deaths`, `delta_live`): a cover carries every live write of B;
- headers, which outlive every drop (a fire's `ts`, the purge floor, a purged part's request `ts`, `kept_whenever_replaced`, undo's bound), the settings timeline (`retention^.0` is never covered-dropped) and the clock (`at_clock`).

Each argument is per aspect. Forgetting a header is outside it: undo then counts the forgotten `ts` (notes/engine-retention-history.md Undo).

Tests: setting.rs `covered_drops_move_nothing_*` (random histories, covered drops in random order), `an_unkept_part_dates_no_other_part_*`, `vouched_copy_dates_at_its_write_*`, `a_covered_drop_redates_nothing_*`, `a_body_dies_at_its_latest_death_*`, `text_creation_dies_at_delete_after_undelete_*`.

## Dated only where history is kept (spec/commits.md §5 Keep bounds)
- A covered drop needs the body out of history on the node's clock, `out(r) <= now`, and `out(r) >= r`, so a replacement dated after the clock waits for the clock (`bounds::at_clock` adds `r` to the drop bound: `part_keep`, `part_keeps`, so `drop_from`, `settle_none`, memory drops).
- Per part: a part is dated only where some held setting keeps history for its own aspect (`bounded`, in `part_replaced_at`), by its own writes, kept by that aspect's timeline; elsewhere it adds no bound, ahead of the clock or not (`replaced_ahead_of_the_clock_waits_only_where_kept_*`). Rejected: dating per thing (an aspect waited on unrelated aspects) or a commit's parts at the latest death of all of them (a covered drop on an unkept aspect redated another part and brought a body out of history back: `an_unkept_part_dates_no_other_part_*`).
- Listing reads `body_history_keep` (the part's own history and the purge floor alone: purge on replace and no history list nothing, held-model AMBIGUITIES 68).
- Limit: a wall clock set back is not noticed, so node-sync's cached keeps judged before it stand.

## Host delete margin (Fathom)
`MAX_DRIFT` applies to host deletes only. A local drop changes only this node's reads, which follow its clock, so it happens at the keep bound; a right holder deletes at hosts `MAX_DRIFT` later, once every clock agrees. `drop_part_for` records it as `Dropped::hosts_from` (the keep bound judged while the body is held, plus `MAX_DRIFT`; 0 when it had none); node-sync's `judge` waits for it. No timer or row of its own (`deletion::host_delete_waits_drift_past_history_*`).

## Re-judging and costs
- node-sync judges a body again when it may have been replaced: an apply's join marks the commits owning the writes it superseded (`retention::Killed`) and the aspect's snapshots (`mark_killed`; a reset joined by pages marks every bounded body); a rebuild (flip, un-merge, drop, expiry) marks every held bounded body of the aspect (`mark_bounded`). A kill marks bodies only on an aspect a setting keeps (`bounded`).
- Memos: the index per aspect (`Core::held_writes`, notes/engine.md Derived values) and each part's date (`Core::replaced_dates`), recomputed when the aspect's held bodies, their validity or (for a date) its state moved. A body joining only amends the index (`amend_held_writes`: a write decodes its own body, not the aspect's) unless validity moved (issues/derived-state-recomputed.md). Counted: retention `replaced_bound_judged_once_per_change_of_its_aspect_spec_commits_5`, `a_join_decodes_its_own_body_not_the_aspects`.
- A judgment walks the part for its winners a fixed number of times (`retention::tests::reset_judged_walks_parts_once_*`, `Core::death_walks`); judging every body of an aspect reads about one body per judgment (`scale::judging_every_body_reads_each_once_spec_commits_5`). A judgment right after a change still reads the aspect's held bodies: O(the aspect's history) per change that kills a write. The part is decoded whole, O(part) memory (notes/engine-paged.md Streamed readers, Left whole).
- A kept, frequently edited aspect holds snapshots within its D window (3 at 8 and at 80 edits; `scale::kept_snapshots_stay_within_their_window_spec_commits_5`). A late delta older than n and 10n held commits costs flat (`scale::late_delta_deaths_are_flat_spec_crdt_4_8`).
- Tests: retention.rs `snapshot_*`, `deaths_*` (`deaths_never_early_random_*` against a node keeping every body, `OSIS_STRAYS_START`/`_SEEDS`), `skewed_clocks_dated_at_earliest_superseder_*`, `text_delete_replaces_at_earliest_delete_held_*`. The independent truth is held-model's `replaced_at`: order.rs `kept_snapshot_replaced_at_latest_death_any_order_*`, `replaced_alike_in_any_order_*`, `text_redelete_*`, `snapshot_after_late_dated_death_not_credited_*`; pass_marks `body_judged_once_per_body_*`.
