# Rules a node applies to what it cannot read
> Summary: Audit of every judgement rule against "a node that cannot read L judges nothing of it" (keeps ciphertext, relays, follows only signatures, headers and its own grants): where each rule runs on unread data (main, c55c33a8b; net-6b noted), what it does there, what it should do, the bugs (issues/lifetimes-unread-retainer.md, issues/sync-demotion-drops-copy.md, issues/stranded-holder-unread.md), and the proposal: a keeper holds no engine state of a thing it keeps, only a sync record.

## Where a node holds what it cannot read
- **A, a sync ID kept unread**: rows in the hub store (`osis_host`), served and pulled by node sync's unread path (`core/node/src/sync/unread.rs`). Node sync's read set is `compute_synced` (`sync/mod.rs:1009`: a read secret of the group held); everything else in the hub store is unread (`HostStore::reads`, `store/reader.rs:56`). Already a separate path: stamps, delete keys, deposit key and budget, expiry, tombstones, caps. Nothing of the engine runs on it except the sync secret behind the sync-key proof (`shared_proof_keys`, `shared.rs:930`).
- **K, a keeper's engine state of a thing T it holds sync on**: the sync box, and the inbox chain's role-class projections of T (`roles.0`, `keys.0`, `label.0`, `secrets.0`, `created.0`) that `send_inbox` carries to any holder below read (`sync/local.rs:410-421`) and `accept_inbox` and node sync ingest unfiltered (`flows/edges.rs:664`). Every engine rule runs on it as on a reader's data. **Every bug of the class lives here.**
- **X, certificate parts that authorize what the node reads**: author chains, cards, signing chains of other accounts (spec/permissions.md §4 last bullet). Full judgement is right: they decide the validity of data the node reads.
- **C, content parts of unread things inside commits on read labels**: left out by `copy_stale` (`labeling/blobs.rs:451`), filed by `await_reading` (`:460`).
- **F, forgotten things** after lost access: `forget` keeps `created.0`, `retention^.0`, `roles.0`, `keys.0`, `label.0` (`retention/forget.rs:33`).

The engine's only reading gates are `rotation/lost.rs` (`readable`, `may_hold`, `may_take`, `may_learn`, 14 call sites) and `forget::read_path`. No validity, purge, retention, destruction or lifetime rule asks whether the node reads the thing; most "holds secrets of T" tests read `ix.secrets`, where a keeper's sync secret counts.

## Proposal: a keeper judges nothing of T
The rules above are right for readers; the bugs come from running them on K, a partial copy of T's role history. Each fix so far added an exception inside a full rule (`boxed_by`'s sync invariant, `holds_sync`'s second walk, §5.5 Scope, the role waits' full-source skip). Instead a keeper should hold no engine state of T: no role-class part, no `keys.0`, no timeline. It holds a **sync record** per T it keeps: T's sync IDs (from the payload), its sync secrets by epoch (from boxes to its own key), and nothing else. Its rules:
- Take a sync box from an inbox blob it opens with its own key; the secret is its own proof (its sync key matches a step of the label, which the label's signed path shows). No author chain, timeline or epoch rule.
- Keep L while its account's S lists T (net-6b) and its record holds a sync secret; an open ends when the head's sync key is one it does not hold (spec/sync.md §5, as built); a removal notice is a hint to stop, as is the head moving on with no new box.
- Everything else of L is A: the hub store's rules.

What it removes: `Held::unjudged`, `holds_sync`, `walk_apart` and §5.5 Scope (net-6b, about 60 lines); the sync branches of `may_hold` and `drop_actors`; `boxed_by`'s sync invariant; `send_inbox`'s chain and `keys.0` for holders below read and `accept_inbox`'s unfiltered chain for a sync grant; the sync side of `inbox_actors`, `own_holder`, `add_history`, `hold_secrets`; net-6b's `note_inbox_things` role test; the spec §3 sentence on keeping sync by §5.5. On the order of 200-300 lines of conditions and five spec sentences, and the class: none of the 30-odd rules above can see a keeper's T, so no new rule needs a cannot-read case. It also ends a requirements tension: the chain shows a keeper its granter's edges on T (spec/requirements.md, Data a node cannot read: no role grants).

Cost: a keeper no longer checks that its granter may grant. Anyone holding T's sync secret can already open L everywhere, and the account's S and `host^.0` limits bound what it keeps, so nothing is lost that the sync secret did not already give. Still needed separately: the lifetimes reading gate (F and X) and the read→sync move of L into A, which are reader-side.
