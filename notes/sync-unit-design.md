# Sync unit design: one independent thing and its dependents
> Summary: Design study for the decided sync unit (notes/sync-unit-scope.md): what the built model's multi-unit carriage consists of and what becomes of each piece; the two kinds of derived blob a unit holds for its sync holders (a handle per thing its strongs target outside it, a key per unit its thing was granted), both functions of what every reader of the unit holds; the home a strong to a dependent thing carries; entry by a secret of a thing the unit's step lists; the one walk over those blobs for readers and for nodes keeping a closure unread; promotion, attach and detach, removal, status over a closure, counted costs of many sync IDs, the wins with their numbers, and the cases that cross the account design. The staged build: notes/redesign-plan.md. Nothing here is built; the walk, the blobs at stores over time, forks, a hostile writer, homes and work at a change are modelled, with four findings, and promotion is to model (notes/closure-model.md, The unit model).

**Principle: a sync ID carries its independent thing and the dependents that thing reaches, nothing else; everything a holder of a unit needs of it is a function of what every reader of that unit holds.** A unit says two things to its sync holders: which things its strongs point at (by a handle any writer computes from the reference alone), and which units its own thing may enter (from the grants it took, which travel under it). A unit is in a node's closure when an entered unit points at it and an entered unit may enter it; the node enters with a secret of a thing that unit's step lists, and holds a thing's secrets through units it has entered. So no blob waits for a writer that happens to know more than its unit holds, none is judged by fewer than all the unit's writers, nothing is written into a unit because of what refers to it, and nothing is written into a referrer because its target's keys, roles or unit changed. Readers and nodes keeping a closure unread run one walk over the same blobs.

Marks: **[checked: source]** read in the spec or code named; **[measured]** counted in a debug test (Costs, below); **[computed]** from byte layouts; **[argued]** reasoned, to be modelled before it is built; **[modelled]** holds in held-model `unit`; **[modelled: fails, ...]** the model shows it false (notes/closure-model.md, Findings). The design introduces no picked number. Open questions for Tess: issues/unit-dependent-release.md (§6), issues/unit-key-paths.md (§4), issues/unit-reference-home.md (§2), issues/unit-keeper-learns.md (§3).

## 2. What a unit holds for its sync holders
Two classes of derived blob, both in the unit's required set as certificates are, neither an aspect nor a field of a commit, both sealed for holders of a sync secret of the unit's thing and for nobody else. **What a writer must hold to log or judge one is the unit itself**: every writer of the unit logs every blob it lacks and deletes every blob it should not have, and all of them judge alike [modelled: every reader of a unit, whatever else it reads, computes both sets alike; notes/closure-model.md, The unit model]. Everything a blob is computed from sits in the same sync ID as the blob, so a writer judges a store's blobs at the end of a complete pass of that store (as must-not-hold is judged: spec/sync-judgement.md §2), holding by then every commit the store has that justifies one [modelled: after every single visit by a writer, whatever else it holds, the store's blobs are exactly what its own entries give; two writers with different holdings settle in every schedule; judged before the pass, a writer deletes what the store justifies, and judging by less than the store holds undoes another writer for good]. Why not one blob naming the target's unit: that name is no function of the referring unit (a strong is a tid; a dependent target's unit is its independent's, which nothing about its tid shows), so only a writer that happened to sync the target could log it, the set depended on who had once known, and a forgery could outlive every writer able to judge it (notes/closure-model.md, Rejected readings).

**The handle of a thing**: `kdf("handle", tid)`, one value per thing, the same in every unit, computable by anyone who holds the tid and by nobody else.

**Handle blobs: what the unit's strongs point at.**
- **The set**: S(G) holds one handle blob for each thing outside G's unit that a live strong of G's unit targets, and one for each home such a strong carries (below). No role is read, of G or of anyone, and no other unit: every reader of G reads every strong of G's unit and the records and promotions that say which things are G's own (§5, §6).
- **What it holds**: the handle. Nothing of what the thing is, where in G the reference sits, whether anyone can enter it, or whether it is a target or a home.
- **Logged** by the writer of the strong in that transaction and by any writer of S(G) that finds one missing; **deleted** by any writer when its handle is none of the unit's (as record and list blobs go: spec/sync-ids.md §6 Delete, Certificate blobs; no tombstone at own stores). Of two blobs with one handle the lower blob ID stays. There is no other rule [modelled].
- **Under a dispute** over what is in G's unit (a promotion one side counts and the other does not, §5): a handle is the unit's when any side's view makes it one. A handle too many names a thing no key reaches or a unit the node may enter anyway; sides never delete each other's blobs [modelled: forked histories settle and no side's keeper ends without a unit its side's blobs give; each writer judging by its own side never settles].

**A strong's home.** A strong to a dependent thing X says nothing of where X is, and "a strong to a dependent thing brings its independent's unit" (Tess) needs it said in G's own data. So the delta that writes a strong to a thing its writer holds as a dependent of another unit carries, beside the value, the tid of that unit's thing: the strong's **home** (delta metadata, as purgeable-by is: spec/commits.md §5; a snapshot carries on the homes of the strongs it keeps [modelled: a cover changes no handle]). A home lives as long as its delta, not its strong [modelled: fails, a concurrent add of the same strong from a bare tid outlives the homed delta's removal, and the strong stands with no home]. The value is unchanged: a reference is still a tid, equal to any other reference of its kind to that thing, and no API shows a home.
- **Filled** by the writing node from what it holds: X held as a dependent of H gives H; else a home some held delta already gives for X (the reference was copied from a thing the node reads); else none [modelled: fails to be a function of the strong's value, a node that covered a removed homed delta re-adds with no home where one that did not cover re-adds with it]. A strong to an independent thing needs none.
- **What it means**: the strong points at X and at H's unit. S(G) lists both handles; a node enters H's unit where it may, and holds X where that unit carries it (§6). It is the writer's word, as the strong is: a writer naming a false home has added a strong to that thing, which it may do anyway.
- **It does not go stale in a way that matters**: after X is promoted, X's own handle (listed all along) finds X's unit by a key of G or of H (H is X's mint admin; §5), and the home still brings H's unit, as it did before; after a thing between X and H is promoted, H's unit lists that thing and the walk goes on from there, where H still references it.
- **A strong with no home to a dependent thing** (written from a bare tid by a node that never held the thing, and copied from nowhere that knew) brings nothing by itself: the thing is held where its unit is in the closure by another route. spec/requirements.md Data model's "takes with it the part of its closure it can itself sync" narrows by this corner: issues/unit-reference-home.md [modelled: without homes the walk enters less].
- **Bytes** [computed]: 15 B and a map entry per (target, home) pair per delta, for strongs to dependents of other units only.
- Rejected: the home inside the reference value (two references to one thing then differ as values, set elements and map keys: an app would observe how a thing was keyed when referenced); the target's whole record chain in place of the home (verifiable, 47 B a level, and still says nothing of which link is independent); the unit announcing a handle for each dependent it carries (a blob per dependent thing in every unit, millions in one; a node keeping the unit unread learns how many things it holds; and the unit must first be found).

**Key blobs: which units the unit's thing may enter.**
- **The set**: S(K) holds one key blob for each independent thing T on which a thing of K's unit holds a direct grant at sync or above with key access (spec/crypto.md §4 Key access), T's mint-admin edge included. A dependent holds a grant with key access only once its keyset part turned invalid (spec/permissions.md §3); a holder of K's sync secret then holds no sync-role key of it [modelled: fails, a node keeping K unread knows no tid of K's dependents, so it enters T by such a holder only through the key's first credential and loses it at T's next rotation, where a reader of K keeps it]. It is a function of S(K): a role commit on T naming K, T's boxes to K and T's `sync.0` are in S(K)'s required set (checked: spec/sync-judgement.md §2 A grant naming a holder travels under the holder's sync ID; spec/sync-ids.md §2 for a mint admin's record edge), so every reader of K holds them.
- **What it holds**: for lookup, `kdf("key tag", handle of T)`; sealed under `kdf("key", sync secret of K, handle of T)`, T's sync IDs (each owned one after a forked promotion, spec/sync-ids.md §2 [checked], as K's readers verify them from the carried `sync.0`) and, when its logger holds T's newest sync secret, that secret with its epoch as a first credential. A sync holder of K opens it only when it holds T's handle, which it gets from a handle blob of a unit it entered; without it the blob shows that K was granted something, and whether another unit it keeps was granted the same thing (issues/unit-keeper-learns.md).
- **Logged** by the writer that takes the grant (it stamps the grant's entries under S(K)) in that transaction and by any writer of S(K) that finds one missing; **deleted** by any writer when no grant stands; of two alike the lower blob ID stays; one whose content differs from what S(K) gives is replaced by any writer.
- **Under a dispute** over the grant: the blob stays while any side's view of T's role record, as S(K) carries it, gives K the grant [modelled]. A key too many opens nothing: T's step decides who enters [modelled: fails, a key logged by the granting side carries T's sync secret as its first credential, and a node keeping K unread on the other side enters T by it until T rotates; that secret is the one the grant's box already gave K's sync holders].
- **A grant, a lowering or a removal on T** writes one key blob in the granted holder's unit, beside the role commit that travels there already, and nothing in any unit that references T. **A rotation** of T writes nothing outside S(T): a key blob names a unit, not a secret (a first credential that went stale is ignored) [modelled: work at a change, n and 10n].
- Rejected: the key blob readable by every sync holder of K (simpler by one kdf; such a node then learns the opaque sync ID of everything K was granted, referenced or not: the issue above); a peer answering "which unit has this handle" (the answer is nobody's signed word, and a hostile peer steers a node into any unit it may enter).

**Sealing and rotation.** Handle blobs are sealed under `kdf("handles", sync secret of G)` at G's sync epoch current at the logger; a step registering a new sync epoch carries the superseded sync secret sealed under the new one (48 bytes [computed]), so a holder of the head's sync secret opens blobs of earlier epochs, and a removed holder opens none logged after its removal. Rejected: logging the blobs again at each rotation (work in the number of references at every removal from G; spec/requirements.md Scale has millions of things referenced from one thing). Both classes carry a prefix class of their own, are role entries (spec/sync-ids.md §6 Role entries: taken outside caps' refusal, applied at a source before the deletes they cause, spec/sync-judgement.md §3 Role entries first) and have order 0.

**Bytes** [computed from notes/thing-cost.md, Per entry, per sync ID a blob is under; spec/wire.md §5]: a handle blob of about 110 B (prefix about 62, handle 32, AEAD tag 16) and its entry (blob ID 32, filler wrap 57, stamp 64), about 280 B on the wire and 400 B stored per node, per thing referenced across a unit boundary and per distinct home; a key blob about 60 B more, per grant held. Many strongs into the dependents of one other unit cost a blob each and one for the home; the cheap route is a strong to an independent thing.

**How a unit is entered: by a secret of a thing its own step lists** [modelled: no secret from a unit not entered; a key per chain with secrets at any depth enters through a holder outside the closure].
- Today [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Boxes, Holder actors; spec/sync-ids.md §2 What a sync holder learns, §3 Claim set; spec/sync-judgement.md §2]: a grant to G on H writes one box per granted role, each sealed to G's KEM key of that role. So H's sync secret is boxed to G's sync keyset and any holder of G's sync secret can open that box. It never gets the bytes: the box sits in H's `secrets.0` (a role part under H's roles secret), and its copy under S(G) is wrapped under G's read secret. A step's claim set lists the readers of its read epoch only. A node keeping G unread therefore keeps H only by a ticket of its own, and gets each new sync secret of H by a fresh ticket (issues/one-open-proof.md).
- **Design**: H's step lists every holder thing with a direct grant on H at sync or above with key access (H's own roster, no chain), not only readers: a claim by the holder's sync-role X25519 key (today the read-role key, spec/sync-ids.md §3), and an **entry box** per such holder giving the sync secret the step registers (today entry boxes exist for link keys only, spec/wire.md §7). A node holding the sync secret of any listed thing opens S(H) at any store by that thing's claim (spec/sync-opens.md §4, unchanged), receives the head step, opens the entry box, and holds H's sync secret: it keeps S(H), opens its handle and key blobs, and goes on.
- **Consequences**: when H rotates, its next step carries the new secret to its holders' holders, so nothing is written in any referrer and a node keeping the closure unread does not stop at H (spec/sync-judgement.md §6 Stops) while a thing whose secret it holds is listed. When a listed holder rotates, H's required rotation (spec/permissions.md §3) seals to its new keyset. A grant or re-seal boxing a holder thing at sync is due a step listing it, as a rotation is (an admin may already issue a step for that: spec/sync-ids.md §6 Steps); until an admin issues it, the key blob's first credential carries a node keeping the holder unread.
- **Bootstrap for readers** stays as built: H's boxes to G and H's `sync.0` are in S(G)'s required set, the one place a unit carries role parts of another thing beside evidence, and what G's key blobs are computed from. It names a direct grant only: the chain form of spec/permissions.md §4 Role changes travel (the role records of a chain's middle things under every unit above) goes with the readable-closure unit (§1), and a chain is used link by link (§3). The read secret is in no step (Tess: not in this change; the step's X25519 exchange is not quantum-resistant, which a read secret must be, spec/requirements.md Confidentiality between readers; the form sealed to a KEM key is about 1.2 KB per holder per step).

## 3. The walk
One rule for a node that reads a closure, keeps it unread, or any mix, over the blobs of §2 and nothing else [modelled: the walk over blobs each computed by a reader of its unit alone enters what a reader served by all-seeing writers enters; notes/closure-model.md, The unit model].
- **State**: the units the node has entered and the secrets it holds. Its roots are what its own strongs and tickets enter, as now (spec/sync-set.md §2, §4).
- **Rule**: an independent thing's unit is entered while (1) an entered unit holds a handle blob with its handle, (2) an entered unit holds a key blob for it, and (3) its head step lists a thing whose secret the node holds (the unit of (2), where steps are current); or by a ticket's secret. **Read** when the node holds a read secret of the unit's thing (from that thing's boxes to a holder whose unit the node reads, by Bootstrap, or from a ticket), else **kept unread** by the sync secret the step gives. The walk is the least fixpoint, so the order in which units, blobs and steps arrive does not matter. A unit the node cannot enter is not in its closure; a read of it fails and nothing else is affected.
- **A reader walks by the blobs too.** A writer logs the blobs its own commit makes due before its walk reads them, so its own writes take effect at once; a reader that cannot write waits for a writer of that unit, any one of them (spec/requirements.md Convergence: at the latest once a writer of that data has synced). So a reader and a node keeping the same roots unread enter the same units always, not only once every writer has caught up [modelled: after every step of every schedule and random history; fails where a step lists a dependent holder, §2 Key blobs].
- **The pointing unit and the unit with the key need not be the same**: G -> H -> K with H holding no role on K. S(H) lists K's handle; a node syncing H alone holds no key for K and does not enter it (spec/requirements.md Data model: a reference from something that holds no role on its target does not keep the target). A node syncing G, which holds a role on K, finds K's key in S(G) and enters K by G's secret, read or unread (same item: a thing takes with it the part of its closure it can itself sync). Nobody who writes H needs to know anything of K but its tid.
- **A chain of grants through units of the closure** needs nothing more: G holds a role on W, W on H, and the closure reaches W. The walk enters W by G's key and H by W's; a reader gets H's boxes to W from S(W) (Bootstrap), which it reads. A chain through a holder the closure does not reach: §4.
- **Dropping**: a unit the rule no longer gives, and no own strong or ticket enters, leaves by spec/sync-set.md §3 (the pass in flight ended; no grace period). Reachability from the roots decides, not a count of referrers, so a cycle nothing outside references is released whole (spec/requirements.md Data model), by the removal search the tree already runs (checked: units.rs module comment, notes/closure-model.md P3, P5 of the readable-closure model).
- **A hostile writer of a unit** can forge, or delete, its handle and key blobs at a store. A forged handle makes a node enter only a unit some entered unit holds a key for; a forged key, only a unit whose step lists a thing the node holds a secret of: nothing it was not already given, at the cost of the room it takes. A deleted blob makes a store drop what only it reached. Either is set right by the next honest writer of that unit to visit, whichever it is, from the unit alone (spec/sync-judgement.md §4 Logged again once) [modelled: forged handles, keys naming any unit and deletions, each repaired by one honest visit; no keeper enters a unit no step lists it on]: a bounded writer, bounded churn; issues/cert-blob-hostile-delete.md is the same exposure for record blobs. A unit no honest writer visits is not repaired, as nothing of it is.
- **Caps** (`caps^.0`, spec/sync-ids.md §5): the blobs are entries and count; a per-thing cap on a root the node entered counts the entries of every unit that root reaches (a unit two roots reach counts under each), so a hostile writer spends the root's room and nothing else. Past a cap the unit is full (spec/sync-streams.md §4 Full), a defined outcome.
- **Work**: per handle held, one lookup of its key tag in the node's index of key blobs (one index over the units it entered); per unit entered, one open and one head step. Nothing in the number of grants a unit holds that no handle meets, or in the units of the closure per reference.
- **What a node keeping units unread learns** that it does not today: at rest, for each unit it keeps, a handle per thing its strongs point at outside it (their number; the same handle wherever the thing is referenced, so which kept units point at a common thing, kept or not; a party that knows a thing's tid can tell whether a kept unit points at it); how many grants at sync or above its thing took, and whether two kept units took one on the same thing; the sync ID of a unit only once both a handle and a key for it are in units it entered, which is when it may enter it; hence, among the units it keeps, how many there are in a closure, which refers to which and which holds the key to which. Over time: which unit each entry is in, so activity per independent thing; when a reference or a grant comes and goes; at a promotion, which earlier entries of the former unit were the promoted thing's (§5). It learns no tid, record, roster, role of a grant, content or author, no sync ID of a unit it may not enter, and nothing of the dependents inside a unit beyond the handles of those referenced from a unit it keeps.
- spec/requirements.md (Data a node cannot read) says "the opaque sync units it keeps, which of them refer to which and which each item is in" and "never ... its role grants". Handles of things it does not keep and the count of grants go beyond those words: issues/unit-keeper-learns.md.

## 4. A role through a holder outside the closure
- **The rule**: a role through a holder counts for a closure while the closure holds the holder. G -> X, X in H's unit, G holding a role on H only through W (G on W, W on H), no strong of the closure reaching W: H's key is in S(W), which the node has not entered, so H is not entered, for readers and nodes keeping G unread alike [modelled: `join_on_the_finding_worlds`]; the reference is carried and its target is not (`NotCarried`) until something in the closure references W or G is granted a role on H itself. Sharing through a thing the grantees also reference (a realm and its documents, a group its members follow) is unaffected. It follows from the principle with nothing added: a key is in the unit of the thing that holds the grant, and a node holds a unit's blobs by entering it.
- **Not built: key paths** (a reference naming the holder units on its way, entered for keys only). Modelled and found short of what it was for: it misses a chain the root holds and the referrer does not, and a read chain beside a direct sync grant; under forked sides over a path grant two writers log and delete the path without end (notes/closure-model.md, Finding D, Finding E); and it needs blob content only some writers can judge, which is what §2 removes. issues/unit-key-paths.md asks Tess to close the question on the rule above.
- Rejected: carrying a chain's role records and boxes under every unit above it (the built Role changes travel: a grant below writes into every unit above, and gives a node keeping those units unread nothing); a node entering, for keys, every unit its entered units hold a key for (work and storage in the grants a thing took, not in what it references; a holder that declined to reference a thing would still fetch its unit's role data); a step listing holders of holders (a unit would depend on who holds roles on its holders, against spec/requirements.md Permissions).

## 5. Promotion
A dependent thing X of H becomes independent by one role commit P (`keys.0`, `secrets.0`, `sync.0` with S(X)'s genesis step; checked: spec/permissions.md §3, spec/sync-ids.md §2). X and the dependents whose independent it becomes move from H's unit to X's.
- **P stays in the former unit**: S(H)'s required set keeps X's `created.0`, `sync.0` and P's boxes to the mint admin while the record edges stand (checked: spec/sync-ids.md §2, the nearest thing with a sync ID up the record chain carries them). From those, by §2's two rules and nothing new, S(H) holds a key blob for X (H's thing holds admin on X by its record edge) and, where H's unit still references X, X's handle. So a reader of H that gets everything late still finds, in S(H) alone, that X moved and where. This is the rule that must not be lost: if P left S(H) with X's bodies, a late reader would see X frozen.
- **X's live bodies move by entries, not by bytes**: each is logged under S(X) as the held blob under a new wrap (checked: spec/sync-judgement.md §3 Own store, a held blob verbatim under a wrap for S), by the promoter in P's transaction and by any writer of S(X) holding a body S(X) lacks. P's history boxes carry the derived secrets (checked: spec/crypto.md §3 Making a thing independent), so a reader of X alone opens the sections. One entry per live blob of X's subtree: the one cost in the size of the thing, paid once per thing, as the built model pays it when S(X) is created.
- **They leave S(H) last**: X's bodies are outside S(H)'s required set once P is held, judged at the end of a complete pass begun after P arrived (checked: spec/sync-judgement.md §2 must-not-hold). New rule [argued]: a writer deletes such a body at a source keeping S(H) unread only once that source's stream of S(X) shows a carrier of each of its live deltas, a source full for S(X) counting as holding it (the Carriers rule of spec/sync-compaction.md §1, across the two sync IDs). So a node keeping the closure unread always holds X's content under one of the two.
- **A writer that has not seen P** writes X under S(H), sealed under a derived secret. Its stamp is good there, so stores take it; every reader of H holds X's unit too (H holds admin on X by its record edge), judges the body must-hold under S(X), and logs it there (verbatim where X's readers can open it; a fresh encryption under X's own epoch where it was sealed under a derived epoch newer than P's, spec/crypto.md §5 Passing on), then deletes it from S(H) by the rule above. The author does the same once it holds P. A reader of X alone gets it when the first node holding both units has synced, which the convergence requirement allows ("at the latest once a writer of that data has synced").
- **No blob of a unit that references X changes**: its handle blob for X was there while X was dependent, meeting no key; after P it meets X's key in S(H) (entered by the strong's home, §2) or in the referrer's own unit where it was granted a role on X. A promotion by someone who cannot write the referrer therefore keeps the path with no write there, whether or not H still references X [argued].
- **A node that gets P late**: until then it counts X dependent, logs X's bodies again under S(H) where others deleted them (bounded: once per change of ground, spec/sync-judgement.md §4), and misses writes made under S(X) only. P and the two blobs are role entries in S(H) with order 0 (fetched first, spec/sync-streams.md §5), so the first pass of S(H) ends the state. A node keeping H unread that gets them late holds X's older content under S(H) until then and everything after.
- **What a node keeping both unread sees**: the same blob IDs appear under S(X) and then leave S(H), so it learns which earlier entries were X's. Logging fresh encryptions instead hides that at the cost of X's bytes sent again (notes/redesign-plan.md, Q5).
- **Open, to model before building**: a promotion judged invalid (the thing counts dependent again, spec/crypto.md §3) moves the bodies back, and under a dispute each side logs under the unit its view gives, the handle and key blobs standing while either side's view asks for them (§2, Under a dispute); forked promotions (two sync IDs, both in the key blob, read from the carried `sync.0`); a mixed commit on X and a thing staying in H's unit (one blob under each, the other's section sealed: spec/sync-judgement.md §1 Sealed sections).

## 6. Inside a unit, attaching, detaching, release
- **A dependent thing is in its unit while its unit reaches it** [modelled (P1, release); waits on issues/unit-dependent-release.md]: S(H) carries H and each dependent of H reached from H by strongs through things of H's unit, and nothing for having been minted there (spec/requirements.md Offline and network; spec/sync-set.md §2 as it stands). Every reader of H reads those strongs, so every reader computes the same unit, and a node entering a unit holds it whole. A dependent the unit stops reaching leaves S(H) as built: holders drop it, delete-key holders delete its entries at stores keeping S(H) unread, a holder whose view still reaches it logs it again (spec/sync-set.md §3 A thing leaving a sync unit, on one sync ID); the handles only its strongs gave leave S(H) (§2). Work in proportion to what is released. The other rule, a unit carrying every dependent ever minted in it, makes the unit a function of records alone and never releases a thing (notes/closure-model.md, control `Unit::All`).
- **What that costs**: a strong from another unit does not keep a dependent thing its own unit has dropped. G → X with X a dependent of H that H's unit no longer reaches brings H's unit by its home and holds no X (`NotCarried`), whenever the strong was written; were X independent, the same strong would keep it. A node cannot leave a thing it makes in that state: the making rule (spec/sync-set.md §2 Making a thing) asks that a new dependent thing be reached inside its own unit or the local part.
- **Attach** (a strong to a thing outside the unit added in G's unit): the commit, and a handle blob for the target and for its home where no live strong gave them already [modelled: 1 blob, 2 with a home, n + 1 for n strongs into one unit's dependents]. Nothing of the target's unit is touched or sent to anyone who holds it. **Detach**: the commit; the handles no other live strong gives leave; the target's unit is released at each node that nothing else reaches it from (spec/sync-set.md §3, with "for each sync ID that carried R" now one sync ID).
- **A thing in several closures**: one unit, one copy per node, pointed at by each referrer; kept while the walk gives it (§3).
- **A referrer that cannot enter its target**: the handle blob is there all the same, and gives a node nothing it can use alone: it enters the target only where a unit it entered holds the key, whatever reference led it there (§3). A later grant to the referrer writes its key blob with the grant, and nothing about the reference.
- **Goes**: the pull anew of an enclosing sync ID at a new strong (spec/sync-judgement.md §3 Dropped bodies: the minting commit travels in the target's own unit); spec/sync-set.md §5's "a sync unit holding it syncs here" reduces to the thing's unit; spec/sync-set.md §3 A thing leaving a sync unit shrinks to the first item above.
- **A strong from outside to a dependent thing brings its independent's unit** (decided, Tess) [modelled: `join_on_the_finding_worlds`, with and without the unit reaching the dependent]. X is a dependent of H; G, another unit, references X strongly. The strong carries its home H (§2): S(G) lists X's handle and H's, and a node for which an entered unit holds H's key enters H's unit and holds it whole, X where that unit carries it. That is a function of the strong: it does not turn on whether H reached X when the strong was written or on who has synced since. Nothing is promoted on reference. Whoever reads X reads H's unit already (X's secrets derive from H's), so nothing new is shown; the cheap route is making X independent, which sharing it does. The one strong that brings nothing is one with no home (§2; issues/unit-reference-home.md).

## 7. Removing a holder from a root
- **Rotations**: unchanged. A departure from A requires rotating, on every independent thing A's roles open, the roles the leaver could open through A (checked: spec/permissions.md §3 Required rotation): one rotation commit and one step per unit below, by whoever may rotate it, with one Register per store. The built model pays the same, since every independent thing already has its own sync ID with its own steps; what it adds is nothing here, and what it loses is nothing: the removed party could already fetch each inner thing under that thing's sync ID.
- **When the removed party stops fetching a unit from a node keeping it unread**: when that unit's next step is registered there (spec/sync-judgement.md §6 Stops; spec/sync-opens.md §6), which needs an admin of that unit to issue it (spec/sync-ids.md §6 Steps). Per unit, not per root: a unit the remover does not administer keeps serving the removed party its ciphertext until one of its admins acts. Reading new content ends by the key rule, unchanged (spec/requirements.md Permissions).
- **What a node keeping the closure unread does**: follows each unit's Register; each new step's entry box for a listed thing whose secret it holds gives it the new sync secret (§2), in whatever order the units' steps arrive, since a unit kept at a head whose box it cannot open yet shows its older credentials and is moved on (spec/sync-ids.md §6 Walk, Behind).
- **Handle and key blobs**: none is rewritten, in the root's unit or below, whatever the number of members; the removed holder's own unit loses the one key blob for the root. New ones are sealed under the new sync epoch.
- **With the account redesign**: a removal of a signer is one entry under each unit where the signer could write or invite, in that unit's required set for the unit's life whether or not the unit holds a commit of the signer. The copy comes with the edge writes joining the holder the signer was removed from to the unit's thing, so the unit's readers judge it required from what the unit holds. Who logs it and how the units are found: §12, cases 2 and 6.

## 9. First sync and "caught up" over a closure
- Status stays per sync ID (spec/sync-status.md). **A closure's status** is derived: the units the walk reaches from a thing, each with its status, and a count of members listed but not yet open anywhere.
- **Caught up over a closure**: every unit the walk reaches is heard out (spec/sync-status.md §1) and the walk over the blobs held gives no unit not yet entered. It is a fixpoint: a pass of one unit can add members.
- **First sync of a closure**: handle and key blobs have order 0 (fetched first, spec/sync-streams.md §5), so the walk outruns the content. Units found in one round open in one Offer and join in one Pull (checked: spec/sync-streams.md §4 Joining), so round trips grow with the closure's depth, not its number of units [argued]; the measured 4 deliveries per unit says today's opens do not batch that well.
- **History complete for a thing**: a complete pass of its one unit. `wait_synced` on a thing waits on its unit; a wait over a closure waits on the fixpoint above and, like any wait, runs to its timeout while a unit some other node may hold is unconnected.

## 12. Cases crossing the account design
Each [argued] from this note, notes/write-path-design.md and notes/account-membership-design.md; S1 is a node's signer, A its account, both ordinary (A an independent thing).

1. **A commit with a stated path arrives in a unit.** S1 writes T, in unit U, with path [A, Org]. U's required set holds the commit's blob and, as evidence copies, what checks the path: the records of S1, A, Org and T's record chain; the edge writes on (S1, A) and (A, Org) with every held later change to them; (Org, T) is U's own role data; the vouches naming a held lowering; the same for those writes' own paths. The writer's node logs with the commit whatever of that U lacks (it holds all of it: it is a member of A and writes through Org); any node holding U and a later change to one of those edges logs that. A node keeping U unread stores the blob and the copies as entries it cannot tell apart, and learns nothing of the path. A commit that also writes a thing of another unit is one blob with a wrap under each, each unit holding the evidence of its own thing's path.
2. **A removal logged where the removed signer could write.** The remover reads A's unit: each grant A took at write or with an invite names its target's sync ID (Bootstrap, §2), so each is a unit to log under; a target that is a holder is followed through its own unit, and a promoted dependent through its record edge, which the former unit keeps (§5). Under each unit: one entry, the copy of R with that unit's leaves and the edge writes joining A to the unit's thing, stamped by the remover where its path gives write, deposited otherwise. A unit the remover does not hold it holds for the errand (case 3). What it cannot reach it keeps as a debt (R, sync ID); and any node holding R, a unit carrying it and a unit that one reaches logs it on, so a holder's unit the remover never held is walked by that holder's own members. Cost: an entry and a stamp per unit, a pass of each holder's unit; nothing per dependent thing (one unit), nothing in units the signer could only read.
3. **Hearing a unit out before removing, and what a unit lets a node fetch.** Nothing is opened at a peer for a sync ID the node does not sync (checked: spec/sync-opens.md §4, the shared set), and a commit's header is not fetched apart from its parts (checked: spec/sync-ids.md §1, one blob). So no header-only pull of a unit outside the closure exists, and none is added: the node **holds the unit for the errand**. One of its own things (its node's local part) gets a strong to the unit's thing; the walk enters the unit read by the secret held through A (§3); a complete pass brings its commit blobs; the strong goes and the unit is released (spec/sync-set.md §3). The same hold serves logging R under a unit outside the closure. A unit the account holds only sync on cannot be heard out, and need not be: the removed signer could not write it.
4. **A public profile.** The profile P is an independent thing public reads: one unit. Its steps list public by static entry, the same bytes on every public unit, with an entry box in symmetric form carrying the read and sync secrets (notes/write-path-design.md §3, special case 4), so anyone holding S(P) opens and reads it, a node keeping a referrer unread included; keeping P unread is then a choice, not an inability. Each unit G whose things reference P holds P's handle, as for any target (§2). P's key has no holder's unit to sit in (public has none), so the delta writing a strong to a public thing carries P's sync ID beside the value, as it carries a home, and S(G) holds a key blob for P computed from its own deltas [argued; A5's study settles it]. P is stored once per node and kept while any kept unit lists it. Against the five special cases: public signs nothing, so no blob, step or deposit of P is public's own; public holds at most read, and a sync-only grant to it is allowed and pointless; public has no unit, so nothing is logged "under the holder" for it and Bootstrap has no public case; its keys are constants, so P's steps never go stale for it; a departure at or below read is due neither a rotation nor a step while public reads. What it shows: P's evidence names, to everyone, the signer that wrote each change of P and that signer's grant on A; and a node keeping G1 and G2 unread, opening their handle blobs, sees both point at one thing (issues/sync-units-linked-at-rest.md). A hostile owner filling P spends the cap of each root that reaches it (§3, Caps), as a bounded writer of those units.
5. **An externally grantable thing.** A's admins enable `ticket@~0` on A's inbox I and place A's card on a thing outsiders read: A's tid and KEM keys, I's tid and record, its read KEM key, its sync ID and deposit seed (notes/write-path-design.md §3-§4). I is A itself, or a thing A administers with a unit of its own, listed by A's unit like any member. An outsider seals its fire (a commit by its own signer with its path, its evidence inside the sealed blob) to I's read KEM key and hands it, signed with the deposit key, to any peer that may hold S(I) (checked: spec/sync-inbox.md §2-§3). A node keeping S(I) unread checks the signature and the budget and stores it unstamped; it learns nothing of the sender. A writer of I promotes it once the fire is judged valid (the setting names the signal) and its evidence is under S(I). The outsider needs a peer holding S(I): one it meets, or one it dials from a dial entry it reads, which a public profile alone does not carry.
6. **Promotion while a removal of one of its writers is on its way.** X is a dependent of H; S1's account writes H, so X by descent; R removes S1. R's list has a leaf per thing, so X's leaf is the same before and after X is promoted. S(X) is a unit the removed signer could write, reached from H by X's record edge: the promoter logs under S(X), with X's bodies, each removal S(H) carries; a remover that walked H's unit before the promotion arrived missed S(X), and the standing duty covers it (any node holding R, S(H) and S(X) logs it). Until then a reader of X alone counts a backdated write of S1 that reaches it: availability, as for any unit owed. A write of S1 stamped before R and logged under S(H) by the late-writer rule (§5) is invalid at every reader of H, who hold R, so none logs it on under S(X). A promotion made by S1 itself and unlisted is invalid, which is the promotion judged invalid of §5 (to model there).
7. **The account as a unit.** A's unit is A and its dependents: home and every private thing minted with A, in any number, one sync ID. Its members are A's roster; S(A)'s steps list each signer by a claim under its own key, with an entry box carrying the read and sync secrets, since a signer has no unit in which its grant could reach it. **Pairing**: the member grants the new signer a role on A and, as an admin, issues a step listing it; the new node gets A's tid and sync ID over the pairing connection, opens S(A) by its claim, reads the entry box and then A's role data. **Removing a node** is R, A's rotation and a step of S(A) in the remover's hands, so the removed node stops fetching A's unit at each store's next Register; units below wait for their own admins (§7). **What A's nodes hold**: A's unit, and what its strongs reach; a thing only some nodes should hold is referenced weakly in A's unit and strongly from that node's own things. **A node keeping A's closure unread** holds S(A)'s entries (commits of A and its dependents, role commits, evidence copies, deposits while A is its own inbox) and walks by its handle and key blobs to each unit A's unit references and may enter: accepted things, promoted home things, the inbox, the profile. It learns the number of units, which refers to which, their activity, and that S(A)'s claim set changed; no signer, tid or roster.

## Assumed of the account design
- An account is an independent thing with a sync ID; its signers are key things, each listed in its steps by a claim under its own key with an entry box (§12, case 7), so the chain from a node's signer to any unit's sync secret is the one mechanism of §2. Static entries stay for link keys and the public actor.
- Evidence is derived per unit from the commits it holds (notes/write-path-design.md §2), so a body moved at a promotion brings its evidence under S(X) by the same rule, logged by whoever logs the body.
- A removal of a signer is logged under each unit where the signer could write, invite or make an enabled reader-level act, by a writer of the unit or as a deposit, found and carried on as §12 cases 2 and 6 say (notes/account-membership-design.md §4).
- A public thing is one the public key actor reads; its handle is listed like any target's (§2), its key comes from the strong's own delta (§12, case 4), and anyone opens S(H) by the public actor's static entry. Which acts the public actor is barred from is that design's.
- A grant a holder takes is in the holder's unit with the target's boxes and `sync.0`; its members need no ticket. Tickets stay for what a node enters by a secret handed to it.

## Rejected
- **The handles as an aspect, or a part of the commit writing the reference**: a node keeping the unit unread would merge a CRDT and judge covers and validity to know the current set; a derived blob it either holds or does not is all it can check.
- **The handles in the clear, or as store operations**: fixed otherwise by Tess (notes/sync-unit-scope.md).
- **A count of referrers at the node**: a cycle never reaches zero (notes/sync-unit-scope.md).
- **One blob per target naming the target's unit, logged by a writer that knows it** (optionally with the unit's secret, or a key path): the name is no function of the referring unit, so the set turns on who once knew, a node keeping a closure unread misses what only a reader of the root can name, and a forgery outlives every writer able to judge it (notes/closure-model.md, Rejected readings).
- **A handle only where the referrer holds a role on the target**: by a chain of roles it reads role records the unit does not carry, so readers split (notes/closure-model.md, control `Member::Role`); by a direct grant every reader agrees, and a node keeping a root unread still misses whatever the root can sync through a unit that cannot (the same control).
- **The root listing every unit of its closure**: only readers of the root can keep it right, so a reference written by someone who reads an inner unit alone waits for one (notes/sync-unit-scope.md). The root's key blobs are not that list: they say what the root may enter, written with each grant by whoever takes it, and which of those units are in the closure comes from each unit's own handles.
- **The clause weakened instead** (a node keeping a closure unread holds a reference's target only where the referring unit can itself enter it, or only once a node reading both has synced): the first cuts a root's node off from what the root can sync through a unit that cannot, the common shape of a shared folder pointing at a private thing of the sharer's; the second leaves the kept set turning on who has synced. Neither is needed.
- **A reader holding of a unit only what its strongs reach there**: a unit is then a different set at each node, and what a store keeping it unread must hold is no function of the unit.
