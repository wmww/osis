# Sync convergence: compaction, purge, deletion at labels
> Summary: Why spec/sync.md §6-§7 (and the deletion parts of §5, §9) are shaped as they are: one convergence rule per body, "covered means redundant", tombstones, no timed deletes, purge reaching snapshots, purge commits kept `ROLE_KEEP`, positive uncovered, snapshot-first drops, carriers and their tiebreaks, a purged body waiting for its rewrite (and the full-label Replace), header-only lasting blobs, why no recycle bin; the TLC findings behind each; accepted limits; how node sync judges and deletes (live writes, chunks, what is never judged, compaction agreement, carriers). Neighbours: notes/sync.md (index), notes/whole-commits.md (one blob per commit), notes/crdt.md (merge and compaction in the engine), notes/convergence-study.md (validity flips against dropped data).

## The rule
- **Convergence is the one rule**: per body must-hold, may-hold or must-not-hold under a label. Retag on join, cleanup on leave, compaction and repair after a bad deletion are all this rule. The may-hold class keeps a compactor's drop and a lagging holder's re-upload from ping-ponging. A wrongful deletion is healed at the next holder sync by a fresh encryption.
- **Judgments compare the parts the label's actor may read** (`readable`), so nodes holding more secrets judge alike. `body_deleted` matches a deleted blob's part set exactly (a rewrite's superset deletion is not the body's).
- **A kept part of a blob that also holds a dropped one** is carried by the rewrite this node places (`body_sends` runs before `deletions` in a pass; `carried` asks a snapshot only for dropped parts' live writes): with mixed classes in one blob, a "no carrier: snapshot first" rule would snapshot every kept aspect of every drop.
- **Covered means redundant under join**, not "inside the snapshot's frontiers": a snapshot that omits content covers nothing it omits, so a bad snapshot is harmless and anyone can re-snapshot. It also absorbs the versioning rule that a node never compacts what it could not join. A held body or snapshot is judged by the join alone; tables speak only for a body not held. Comparing frontiers makes cover stop at a gap: a fresh node's snapshot lists a session at 0 (it held later writes only, the older snapshot had pruned the session), so holders of a dead body find it uncovered and upload it, and a right holder that dropped it deletes it each pass, forever (`snapshot_rights`, `sessions::frontier_gap_covers_what_joins_unchanged_spec_commits_5`). Inheriting frontiers does not help: the pruned session has no entry to inherit. Limit: a dead body is covered by a snapshot whose writer never saw it, so a revoke that revives it is met by `takes_late` as with a pruned session.
- **Must-hold needs a positive "uncovered"**: a node that cannot join a body (parked) or has not fetched a snapshot's pages treats it as may-hold, never must-hold, or an old-version writer and a compactor would re-upload and delete forever. A strict-subset projection of a commit under a label (a writer whose timeline lagged uploaded less) is must-not-hold.
- **A must-not-hold blob holds nothing** (TLC S17): it neither holds a body "the label lacks" nor makes another blob a strict subset, so a purger's rewrite goes up at once beside the purged blob still waiting for its carrier (the rewrite itself, S18), and stays (read literally, the rewrite would wait on that deletion, a snapshot). A writer that has not applied the purge yet deletes the rewrite as a strict subset of its own body until it does (S4). In node sync, must-not-hold here is "holds a dropped delta (purge, expiry, compaction) or one the label's actor may not read" (`holds_nothing`; unsettled views count it too, which only keeps or uploads more).
- **Must-not-hold wins every overlap with must-hold** (purged while uncovered): the must-not reasons are the ones that remove content. A body holding no delta (every delta purged) is covered by definition; its header lives on in the nodes that applied it.
- **Keep bounds never change what is read**; only expiry kills values. The TLA+ property "a holder can always serve current state" is too strong for readers (they cannot write snapshots): what holds is that live state is never lost from a node's state or from the label.

## Live writes and carriers
- **Live writes leave a label only when another entry carries them** (TLC S2, S5, S10): a replaced purge-on-replace body, a strict subset, or one of two duplicates is deleted at a label only while a covering snapshot, a superset blob of the commit or an identical body is under it, and a right holder that may write the aspect uploads the snapshot itself when none is there (else nobody is obliged to and the host keeps the body forever). Locally a node drops the body at the bound and keeps the live writes in its state (spec/crdt.md §4.6); a snapshot it writes from that state covers every body it applied.
- **Local drops of live bodies snapshot first** (TLC S11): a body purged on replace written offline and dropped at its first death before any upload would leave its live write in one node's state with no body or snapshot anywhere, if snapshot-first fired only for deletions at a label. So a writer writes a covering snapshot (a local commit, uploaded later as must-hold) before dropping any body with live writes it holds no snapshot for; readers drop and keep the state.
  - A part of a waiting commit keeps its body until the commit applies.
  - A replaced purge-on-replace body goes under the cover every writer holding it writes in the replacing transaction (notes/engine-retention.md, Purge on replace): every replacing edit costs a snapshot of the aspect, the cost that option pays.
- **Carrier tiebreaks** (TLC S12): the host cannot read the bodies, so two right holders with stale views could each delete one of two identical bodies, or one of two covered-equal snapshots, each counting the other as the carrier. So an identical body carries only the one with the higher blob ID, of covered-equal snapshots the higher commit ID goes, and a must-not-hold blob (a strict subset among them) never carries.
- **A purged body waits for its rewrite** (TLC S18): judging a dropped body's liveness by its dropped deltas only would delete a blob holding a purged delta and a live one in the pass that places the rewrite, and a refused upload (a rotation racing the purge, code 9) would leave the label without the live write until the purger retried; waiting for a covering snapshot instead costs a snapshot per purge (the rewrite, a subset, carries nothing). So every delta's live writes count; a kept one is carried by any other blob of the commit that is not must-not-hold (the rewrite, once present at that host) or by a covering snapshot; and a right holder holding such a blob asks for no snapshot (`carried`, `snapshot_first`). Cost: the purged value's commit blob stays at the host one more round trip (its chunks go at once). Rejected: any held blob of the commit as a carrier (keeps the race); waiting for a snapshot.
  - **Full label**: a code 12 on the rewrite would keep the purged body as long as the label stays full. So a rewrite the host refused for the cap (`SyncEngine::capped`, in memory) counts as the carrier when it is a strict subset of the body (`rewrite_capped`), and is uploaded again naming the body as replaced, with its delete item (`Fate::Delete`'s `via`, `replace_sends`; spec/sync.md §5 Replace): the host admits it in the body's room and removes the body in one step, so the label never lacks the kept live writes (`sync_review::purged_body_leaves_full_label_spec_sync_7` checks every hop). Deleting the body first and re-sending on the delete's OK is wrong: another upload can take the freed slot in between. This is node sync's only use of Replace.
  - While a replacing upload is in flight a plain upload of the same blob waits (`Requests::replacing`): two statuses of one blob could not be told apart. Open the other way round: the pass re-sends a capped rewrite plain before `deletions` sends it replacing, so both are often in flight; the first status takes the replacement list, so a plain OK followed by a refused replacing upload (code 9 after a rotation, delete rate) marks the body absent while the host keeps it (until a reconcile). Waiting for the plain one stalls the purge for good (the pass sends it every time); a fix would skip the plain retry of a rewrite a replacing upload will carry.
  - The purge request is a new entry and waits for space like any write. An `Outside` superset (holds a delta the label's actor may not read) waits likewise for its readable projection; deltas the actor may not read are skipped (their carriers are under their readers' labels; counting them would keep such a blob, purged or not, forever).

## Purge
- **Purge reaches snapshots**: a compactor's snapshot carries everyone's live values, so purging a write also makes it value-less in snapshot bodies, which are held stripped (leftovers) and deleted at hosts; a writer then holding no covering snapshot under a label re-snapshots there (a deterministic trigger; duplicates are covered-equal and one is dropped). Snapshots are `writer`, so any writer can remove a stale one at hosts; they are redundant by construction, so that power costs only a re-upload.
- **A purge commit is kept `ROLE_KEEP` after its `ts`** (TLC S1): the purge SHOULD name dead writes and reset the rest, so the next snapshot covers the purge commit at once and a compactor could drop it; a node that held the purged body and had not yet seen the purge would then keep the value forever (the body is covered, hence may-hold, and nothing tells it). The same staleness class as revokes (90 days of role history); a node offline longer is an offline copy (spec/requirements.md, Threat model). Rejected: never covering purge commits (a blob per purge forever); carrying purge items in snapshots (the compactor would vouch for the purger's right, which snapshots never do). A purge commit with `expires` reaches only deltas expiring no later than it, for the same reason: the host drops the request unattended at its expiry, when everything it reached has expired too. The API puts a purge and its resets in a commit of their own, so an app's disappearing writes never inherit the 90 days.
- **A node offline past `ROLE_KEEP` misses purges** (TLC S15): the purge commit is compacted, the returning node's body is covered (may-hold) and it keeps the value bytes until it compacts; the value itself is dead by the snapshot's reset when the purge named dead writes (the SHOULD), so it re-spreads nothing. An offline copy; accepted, no mechanism. Confirmed in the model: the purge commit's `ROLE_KEEP` overrides its keep-at-most too; any node dropping a purged body keeps the rewrite (a reader's stays local, it cannot stamp); a remover uploads its removal rotation before anything else.
- **Header-only blobs exist only as lasting headers** (Tess: tombstones when history is on; the TLA model's S14 still says may-hold for every retention). A purge that empties a body of any other retention leaves nothing under the label; headers persist only at the nodes that applied the commit (dedup by commit ID needs them, and a commit-form dot resolves its author through them). A purged permanent delta's commit, and its purge commit, stay must-hold as a header-only blob where no other body of the commit is wanted (`retention::lasting`, `commit_bodies_under`).
  - Why the request too: atomic apply counts a missing delta as held only by a held request; a new node with the blob alone would wait for good, and treating a header-only blob as proof of a purge would let any writer hide another's message from new nodes by uploading one. A writer keeps a witness per purge commit, so the cost stays a header per write, also for an author naming one message many times.
  - What a host learns: one more small stamped entry with no commitments (as a `nobody` body has) close to a deletion it already sees; the header stays encrypted. Readers see what the header always showed (author, actor, `ts`, retention, AK, delta hash, chunk blob IDs); the delta nonce is gone, so no guess of the value can be confirmed.
  - A purge commit's own delta purged in turn: the first request applies as its lasting header at a new node, tombstone shown (`a_purge_purged_in_turn`). A header-only blob applies as its header alone, a lasting one's other parts joining as they come (`a_lasting_header_applies_and_the_rest_joins`).
  - Cost: a lasting check is one view scan per commit with no body under the label, per pass, and for a purge commit one local read per named thing (`purge_one` notes a request that dropped a permanent delta, `z/`). Walking the aspect instead costs every full pass R x M for R aspect-wide requests (M the aspect's commits; measured with 10 requests at 2*10^4 messages: a full label judgment 400 ms instead of 35 ms).
  - A node that does not judge a header-only blob of such a commit lasting leaves it (may-hold; `delete.rs::judge`): it may not hold yet what made another node judge it so, and a delete there meets the holder's re-upload every pass, entries piling up (`a_header_only_some_judge_lasting_is_left_alone`). A purge that falls leaves its header-only blobs so; the price is a forged or stale header staying, at most what any stamped garbage costs.

## Tombstones; no timed deletes
- **Tombstones** refuse the identical blob for `TOMBSTONE_LIFETIME` (30 days), so a stale relay cannot resurrect a purged body. Holders never need them: they sync before they upload, and a node holding the purge strips the value from any copy that arrives later. The lifetime is a storage-versus-churn dial: infinite is O(deletions) host storage; zero lets any relay or bundle from before the deletion put the ciphertext back until a holder deletes it again. A peer staler than the log horizon returning later than the lifetime can resurrect, and holders heal it. Blob IDs are hashes of ciphertext under random keys, so a tombstone is no fingerprint.
- **No timed deletes**: purge on replace is deleted at hosts in the replacing writer's sync, under its cover; expiry is the only timer a host runs on its own, and nothing on the wire asks a host to delete later.

## Why no recycle bin
A bin at the host (writers retire entries with a soft key, restore within a grace period) is not a permission boundary: a writer can already destroy irrecoverably everywhere by compacting with an empty snapshot, while holders keep no bin. Nothing ever lists the bin or restores from it, so it is a delayed delete; and a retire carries no reason, so the client rule "re-upload what still changes state" undoes moderation retires. Retention per delta gives the three roles their deletion powers directly, "covered means redundant" gives the recovery a bin was for, and compaction needs no delay. Moderation is not deletion: a chat admin removes the participant, or the app hides the message with a chat-written marker (notes/chat.md).

## Rejected
- Soft retire, a recycle bin, restore, and a generation counter forcing concurrent uploaders to re-tag (above).
- Compaction across clock skew by other rules: only the placer deleting its own entries on time grounds (entries of a lost or removed placer then stay for good); judging others' entries on the overlap of what both nodes drop (the only overlap a node can compute is "past by the largest skew", which is the `MAX_DRIFT` wait chosen, spec/sync.md §7).
- Leases (entries lapse unless refreshed): conflict with immutable expiry and with a host being the only backup.

## Reasons behind spec rules
The rules are in spec/sync.md at the section named.
- §6 Convergence: a deleted body is not re-sent because a right holder's keep bounds, judged from the bodies it holds (spec/commits.md §5), had passed.
- §7 Compact: the `MAX_DRIFT` margin past the keep-at-least bound means no node whose clock lags by up to `MAX_DRIFT` still judges a dropped body must-hold. Sweep: what a removal or expiry hid dies (spec/crdt.md §4.8), its bodies follow their keep bounds and no snapshot carries it.
- §9 Sending: waiting for an unanswered upload and coalescing means an aspect goes up as about one entry per round trip whatever the write rate.
- §9 Compaction: a node keeps bodies it authored until one of its own snapshots covers them because a demotion's frontier lists no ephemeral commit, so a compactor's demotion invalidates every snapshot it wrote, and what those covered is then gone at every holder but the authors.
- §9 A snapshot turning invalid: what it covered comes back to hosts and readers from each writer that held it when it fell and still holds the covered writes (its own it kept). A write no such writer holds (its author restarted, or never held that snapshot valid: it learned of the demotion first) stays gone at the hosts and readers that dropped it until its author writes the aspect again.
- §9 New sessions: a body invalid for good (an explicit demotion with no re-grant after it, spec/permissions.md §5.2, then a new grant) has no later body of its node behind it. What the node wrote under the old session after that grant's key and before it learned of the demotion stays held uncovered, within the caps, until the node restarts.
- §9 Relay-only: within `MAX_DRIFT` of its `ts` a signal may arrive late, and a restarted node may deliver one again that a replayer uploads to a host that forgot it.

## Limits (accepted)
- An ex-writer or ex-admin keeps old-epoch delete keys and, as a reader, the sync key, so it can delete its own old `author` bodies or old `admin` bodies at hosts; healed by any holder's next sync. Level 3 needs the write key, so `writer` bodies are safe.
- A malicious current writer can wipe `writer`-purgeable bodies at a host; write access already lets it reset them everywhere.
- Host storage under `author` and `author_or_admin` grows with superseded bodies until a snapshot with the right over them is written and their right holders sync: under `author_or_admin` an admin's or their author's, under `author` their author's only. So departed authors' `author` bodies stay for good, their `author_or_admin` ones until an admin compacts (a removal is the natural moment). Measured in notes/growth.md.
- Bounds read on a node's own clock straddle: two nodes either side of a keep-at-least bound or a grant's `until` (`stale_card`) may delete and re-place one body for as long as their corrected clocks differ (seconds).
- Relative keep bounds measure from the killing commit's `ts`, so a backdated kill shortens "at least D" by up to the drift cap.

## In node sync
The engine side (deaths, timers, `drop_part`, snapshots) is in notes/engine-retention.md and notes/crdt.md; the classification is `labeling/converge.rs` (`classify(Facts) -> Hold`, `may_delete(why, live, carried)`), the deletions `sync/pass/delete.rs`.
- **Live writes** (`Engine::delta_live`): a dot the delta owns survives in the state; unknown counts as live; writes of invalid or undone deltas are stripped from the state, so they count dead until a validity flip or redo puts them back; cached per delta in `Core::live_bodies`, forgotten when that aspect's state changes. `Engine::body_live` (any delta of the body's class) is for keep bounds only. A deletion waits for a carrier only while the body has a live write (spec/sync.md §7), and asks a carrier per delta holding one of its own.
- **What a node deletes it would not upload**: carrying a delta (deleter, `carried`) and needing a blob to carry it (holder, `must_carry`, `delete_stands`) are one judgment per delta: kept or a live write of its own, and no snapshot at the host covering it. Two ways they split and looped (sims `demotion_unsynced`, `coverer_revoked`): a held delta counted carried by a carrier recorded when it dropped earlier, after that cover fell (a held delta is carried only by the join, `snapshot_carries`; recorded carriers stand in only for dropped deltas, `fallen_carrier_carries_nothing_spec_sync_7`); and a dead delta counted as needing a blob because a class-mate delta was live and covered (liveness per delta, not per body).
- **Deletions** in a pass: must-not-hold entries present at the host, by the label delete key, else a delete-key signature with an established label key; carriers first (a later snapshot entry of the aspect at that host; else `snapshot_first` when the node may write). Expired entries are forgotten, never deleted. Deletes lacking a key (`Note::keyless`) wait until any role-class write, flip or late part. Deletes take their delete keys from the header (`delete_secrets_of`, `chunk_delete_secrets`: the header's keys matched over every held epoch along the record chains).
- Dropped bodies (purge, expiry, compaction) lose key, ciphertext and wraps at once (`forget_dropped`); `compact` forgets the bodies it drops (spec/commits.md §5). Carriers: the snapshot recorded when a body was dropped (`y/s`), else one that covers it; `carrier_of` follows dropped carriers to one still held, and a present snapshot covered-equal to the carrier stands in for it when its frontier also reaches the dropped body's counters (`covered` of a dropped body checks only that frontier: its content is gone). Carriers are kept per aspect (`y/s` holds a list), so `snapshot_first` does not write a new snapshot every pass for a body of several aspects.
- **Compaction agreement** (simulator-established; breaking it brought back churn): every node drops the bodies covered by each content snapshot under the label, its own snapshots included, per (snapshot, body) until dropped or settled kept (`drop_covered`, `covered_judged`: established uncovered, or the lower of covered-equal snapshots; trimmed each pass to pairs whose bodies are both still held; a body before keep-at-least or not established is judged again, `covered()` only after the cheap checks). So the drop set follows the held snapshots and bodies, not who wrote them or when they arrived. `drop_covered` drops only the higher of covered-equal snapshots (the must-hold tiebreak keeps the lower) and waits until a keep-at-least bound is `MAX_DRIFT` past (`compaction_across_skew_no_churn_spec_sync_7`).
- A must-hold body counts as held by any present blob of its commit holding its uncovered parts (`carried_by`; `Verdicts::must_hold` reports those parts). Atomic apply counts a missing delta whose counters an applied snapshot's frontier already gave (receipt `holds`) as dropped; `Engine::covered` requires a session-form body's counters within the snapshot's frontier, and of one snapshot by another the coverer's frontiers to reach the other's. A projection is not a strict subset of a superset whose extra deltas a snapshot under the label covers.
- Must-hold judges with held snapshots only (`unsettled_sets`: pending snapshots, entries being fetched); deletions keep the full unsettled rule.
- **Chunks of dropped deltas** (`dropped_chunks`): a chunk, tree-node or page blob whose creating deltas (the class whose delete-key indices are the chunk's own; none such: the whole commit) are all dropped, and that no live commit lists nor live tree links (a lazy node fetches the missing tree nodes first), is deleted where present by the label delete key or a delete key of its delta (a chunk never fetched is fetched for its salt); its bytes go once no entry names it. Residue: a chunk of a dropped delta that shares its class with live deltas of the same commit stays until they drop.
- **Never judged** (left alone): unopened entries (chunks of live deltas, newer epochs), commits not applied, role-class commits and node-card or heartbeat commits outside the closure (others' certificates: notes/node-sync.md), projections that are a subset of a desired one (the superset goes up first), deltas this node does not hold.
- The pass judges chunk entries (purged chunks go) and resolves linked header lists before projecting bodies and checking the prefix. Covered drops mark changes (`Core::changed`), so node sync forgets the blobs and re-judges.
