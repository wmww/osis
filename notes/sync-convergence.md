# Sync convergence: compaction, purge and deletion
> Summary: Why spec/sync-judgement.md §2-§4 and spec/sync-compaction.md §1 are shaped as they are: one judgement per body, "covered means redundant", positive uncovered, must-not-hold holds nothing, carriers and their tiebreaks, a purged body waiting for its rewrite, purge reaching snapshots, purge commits kept `ROLE_KEEP`, header-only lasting blobs, tombstones, no timed deletes, why no recycle bin; the TLC findings behind each; accepted limits; what is never judged. Neighbours: notes/sync.md (index), notes/whole-commits.md (one blob per commit), notes/crdt.md (merge and compaction in the engine), notes/convergence-study.md (validity flips against dropped data), notes/ephemeral.md (the memory class, spec/sync-compaction.md §2).

## The rule
- **Convergence is the one rule**: per body must-hold, may-hold or must-not-hold under a sync ID; join, leave, compaction and repair after a bad deletion are all this rule. May-hold keeps a compactor's drop and a lagging holder's logging again from ping-ponging. A wrongful deletion is healed by a fresh encryption (spec/sync-judgement.md §4).
- **Bodies are what S's actor reads; copies are whole blobs** (spec/sync-judgement.md §1). Everything judged of a body (deletes, strict subsets, carriers) compares the parts S's actor may read, so nodes holding more secrets judge alike: a deleted blob holding only more the actor cannot read was this body's (else its holder logs it again for the dropper to delete, forever), one holding more it reads was another body's (a rewrite replaced it). A dropped delta makes its whole blob must-not-hold, readable by the actor or not, so duplicates compare the whole delta set: a blob holding a delta one node dropped is no copy of that node's rewrite (else a holder deletes the rewrite as the higher duplicate and the dropper logs it again).
- **Covered means redundant under join**, not "inside the snapshot's frontiers": a snapshot that omits content covers nothing it omits, so a bad snapshot is harmless and anyone can snapshot again; it also absorbs the versioning rule that a node never compacts what it could not join. A held body or snapshot is judged by the join alone. Comparing frontiers makes cover stop at a gap: a fresh node's snapshot lists a session at 0 (the older snapshot had pruned it), so holders of a dead body find it uncovered and log it, and a right holder that dropped it deletes it each time, forever (`sessions::frontier_gap_covers_what_joins_unchanged_spec_commits_5`); inheriting frontiers does not help, the pruned session has no entry to inherit. Limit: a dead body is covered by a snapshot whose writer never saw it, so a revoke that revives it is met by `takes_late` as with a pruned session.
- **Must-hold needs a positive "uncovered"** (spec/sync-judgement.md §2, established uncovered): a node that cannot join a body (parked), has not fetched a snapshot's pages, or knows a snapshot under S stamped after the body that it no longer holds (forgotten, or dropped under a later cover not under S) treats it as may-hold, never must-hold, or an old-version writer and a compactor, or a holder and a co-writer that dropped the body under that snapshot, would log and delete forever (an author's own body kept under others' covers). A snapshot not held stamped no later than the body never saw it, so it counts as not covering: else an old forgotten snapshot left at a full source would hold back every later own write. A snapshot this node judged invalid does not count.
- **A must-not-hold blob holds nothing** (S17): one holding a dropped delta (purge, expiry, compaction). It neither holds a body S lacks nor makes another blob a strict subset, so a purger's rewrite is logged at once beside the purged blob still waiting for its carrier (the rewrite itself, S18). A strict-subset projection of a commit (a writer whose timeline lagged logged less) is must-not-hold; a writer that has not applied a purge yet deletes the purger's rewrite as a strict subset of its own body until it does (S4).
- **Must-not-hold wins every overlap with must-hold** (S3; purged while uncovered): the must-not reasons are the ones that remove content. A body holding no delta (every delta purged) is covered by definition; its header lives on in the nodes that applied it.
- **Keep bounds never change what is read**; only expiry kills values. What holds is that live state is never lost from a node's state or from S ("a holder can always serve current state" is too strong: readers cannot write snapshots).

## Carriers
- **What a blob alone may hold leaves S only when another entry carries it** (S2, S5, S10; spec/sync-compaction.md §1 Carriers): per delta, a live write, a kept delta, or one dropped as covered and not purged. Kept: the holder logs such a blob again, so a deleter not waiting meets an author logging it again each time. Dropped as covered, dead or live: a holder lacking the cover judges the delta live and uncovered, so the cover is logged first; liveness alone is per node and cannot decide a delete. A purge never waits on a cover: the purge is why it goes.
- So a replaced purge-on-replace body, a strict subset or one of two duplicates is deleted under S only while a covering snapshot, a superset blob of the commit or an identical body is there, and a right holder that may write the aspect logs the snapshot itself when none is there (No carrier; else nobody is obliged to and the source keeps the body forever). Locally a node drops the body at the bound and keeps the live writes in its state (spec/crdt.md §4.6); a snapshot it writes from that state covers every body it applied.
- **Live writes** (`Engine::delta_live`): a dot the delta owns survives in the state; unknown counts as live; writes of invalid or undone deltas are stripped from the state, so they count dead until a validity flip or redo puts them back.
- **What a node deletes it would not log**: carrying a delta (deleter) and needing a blob to carry it (holder) are one judgement per delta: kept or a live write of its own, and no snapshot under S covering it. Two ways they split and looped: a held delta counted carried by a carrier recorded when it was dropped earlier, after that cover fell (a held delta is carried only by the join; recorded carriers stand in only for dropped deltas); and a dead delta counted as needing a blob because a class-mate delta was live and covered (hence liveness per delta, not per body). A strict subset is judged only once established, as must-hold's uncovered is: a node fetching the cover a co-writer dropped the extra delta under would otherwise delete the co-writer's rewrite each time it is logged.
- **A kept part of a blob that also holds a dropped one** is carried by the rewrite this node logs: with mixed classes in one blob, "no carrier: snapshot first" would snapshot every kept aspect of every drop.
- **Local drops of live bodies snapshot first** (S11): a writer writes a covering snapshot (logged later as must-hold) before dropping any body with live writes it holds no snapshot for, else a purge-on-replace body written offline and dropped before any reader pulled it leaves its live write in one node's state alone; readers drop and keep the state. A part of a waiting commit keeps its body until the commit applies. A replaced purge-on-replace body goes under the cover every writer holding it writes in the replacing transaction (notes/engine-retention-covers.md, Purge on replace): every replacing edit costs a snapshot of the aspect, the cost that option pays.
- **Carrier tiebreaks** (S12): a node keeping S unread cannot read the bodies, so two right holders with stale views could each delete one of two identical bodies, or one of two covered-equal snapshots, each counting the other as the carrier. So an identical body carries only the one with the higher blob ID, of covered-equal snapshots the higher commit ID goes, and a must-not-hold blob (a strict subset among them) never carries.
- **A purged body waits for its rewrite** (S18): every delta's live writes count, and a kept one is carried by any other blob of the commit that is not must-not-hold (the rewrite, once present at that source) or by a covering snapshot; a right holder holding such a blob writes no snapshot. Judging only the dropped deltas would delete a blob holding a purged delta and a live one before the rewrite arrives, and a refused rewrite (a rotation racing the purge, code 9) would leave S without the live write until the purger logged again. Cost: the purged value's commit blob stays at the source one more round trip (its chunks go at once). Rejected: any held blob of the commit as a carrier (keeps the race); waiting for a covering snapshot (a snapshot per purge).
  - At a source full for S (spec/sync-streams.md §4 Full) the rewrite cannot arrive, but the purge is not held back: a carrier the source lacks counts as held there (spec/sync-judgement.md §3 Deletes), so the body's delete goes, its room frees, and the source pulls the rest once it has room.
  - The purge request is a new entry and waits for room like any write. Deltas S's actor may not read are skipped (their carriers are under their readers' sync IDs; counting them would keep such a blob, purged or not, forever).

## Purge
- **Purge reaches snapshots**: purging a write also makes it value-less in snapshot bodies, held stripped (leftovers) and deleted at sources keeping S unread; a writer then holding no covering snapshot under S snapshots again there (duplicates are covered-equal and one is dropped). Snapshots are `writer` and redundant by construction, so any writer removing a stale one costs only a logging again.
- **A purge commit is kept `ROLE_KEEP` after its `ts`** (S1): the purge SHOULD name dead writes and reset the rest, so the next snapshot covers the purge commit at once and a compactor could drop it; a node that held the purged body and had not yet seen the purge would then keep the value forever (the body is covered, hence may-hold, and nothing tells it). The staleness class of revokes (90 days of role history); a node offline longer is an offline copy (spec/requirements.md, Threat model). Rejected: never covering purge commits (a blob per purge forever); purge items in snapshots (the compactor would vouch for the purger's right). A purge commit with `expires` reaches only deltas expiring no later, so a node keeping S unread drops it unattended when everything it reached has expired too; the API puts a purge and its resets in a commit of their own, so disappearing writes never inherit the 90 days.
- **A node offline past `ROLE_KEEP` misses purges** (S15): the purge commit is compacted, its body is covered (may-hold) and it keeps the value bytes until it compacts; the value is dead by the snapshot's reset when the purge named dead writes (the SHOULD), so it spreads nothing again. An offline copy; accepted. The purge commit's `ROLE_KEEP` overrides its keep-at-most too; any node dropping a purged body keeps the rewrite (a reader's is deposited, it cannot stamp); a remover logs its removal rotation before anything else.
- **Header-only blobs exist only as lasting headers** (Tess: tombstones when history is on; the TLA model's S14 still says may-hold for every retention). A purge that empties a body of any other retention leaves nothing under S; headers persist only at the nodes that applied the commit (dedup by commit ID needs them, and a commit-form dot resolves its author through them). A purged permanent delta's commit, and its purge commit, stay must-hold as a header-only blob where no other body of the commit is wanted (spec/sync-judgement.md §2 Header-only blob).
  - Why the request too: atomic apply counts a missing delta as held only by a held request; a new node with the blob alone would wait for good, and treating a header-only blob as proof of a purge would let any writer hide another's message from new nodes by logging one. A writer keeps a witness per purge commit, so the cost stays a header per write, also for an author naming one message many times.
  - What a node keeping S unread learns: one more small stamped entry with no commitments close to a deletion it already sees (notes/unreadable-data.md). Readers see what the header always showed; the delta nonce is gone, so no guess of the value can be confirmed.
  - A header-only blob applies as its header alone, a lasting one's other parts joining as they come; a purge commit's own delta purged in turn applies as its lasting header.
  - A node that does not judge a header-only blob of such a commit lasting leaves it (may-hold): it may not hold yet what made another node judge it so, and a delete there meets the holder's logging again each time. A purge that falls leaves its header-only blobs so; the price is a forged or stale header staying, at most what any stamped garbage costs.

## Tombstones; no timed deletes
- **Tombstones** refuse the identical blob for `TOMBSTONE_LIFETIME` (recommended 30 days), so a stale relay or bundle cannot bring back a purged body; holders never need them (they pull before they log, and a node holding the purge strips any later copy). A storage-versus-churn dial: infinite is O(deletions) storage; zero lets any relay or bundle from before the deletion put the ciphertext back until a holder deletes it again. A peer staler than the log horizon returning later than the lifetime can bring it back, and holders heal it. Blob IDs hash ciphertext under random keys, so a tombstone is no fingerprint.
- **No timed deletes**: purge on replace is deleted in the replacing writer's own judgement, under its cover; expiry is the only timer a node keeping S unread runs on its own, and nothing on the wire asks a node to delete later.

## Why no recycle bin
A bin at nodes keeping S unread (writers retire entries with a soft key, restore within a grace period) is no permission boundary: a writer can already destroy everywhere by compacting with an empty snapshot. Nothing lists or restores from it, so it is a delayed delete, and a retire carries no reason, so "log again what still changes state" undoes moderation retires. Retention per delta gives the roles their deletion powers, "covered means redundant" the recovery a bin was for. Moderation is not deletion: a chat admin removes the participant, or the app hides the message with a marker (notes/chat.md).

## Rejected
- Soft retire, a recycle bin, restore, and a generation counter forcing concurrent loggers to re-tag (above).
- Compaction across clock skew by other rules: only the logging node deleting its own entries on time grounds (entries of a lost or removed node then stay for good); judging others' entries on the overlap of what both nodes drop (the only overlap a node can compute is "past by the largest skew", which is the `MAX_DRIFT` wait chosen, spec/sync-compaction.md §1).
- Leases (entries lapse unless refreshed): conflict with immutable expiry and with a node keeping S unread being the only backup.

## Reasons behind spec rules
- spec/sync-judgement.md §3 Neither: a deleted body is not logged again because a right holder's keep bounds, judged from the bodies it holds (spec/commits.md §5), had passed.
- spec/sync-compaction.md §1: the `MAX_DRIFT` wait past the keep-at-least bound before deleting at peers means no node whose clock lags by up to `MAX_DRIFT` still judges a dropped body must-hold. Sweep: what a removal or expiry hid dies (spec/crdt.md §4.8), its bodies follow their keep bounds and no snapshot carries it.

## Limits (accepted)
- An ex-writer or ex-admin keeps old-step delete keys and, as a reader, the sync key, so it can delete its own old `author` bodies or old `admin` bodies at nodes keeping S unread; healed by any holder's next pass (spec/sync-judgement.md §4). Level 3 needs the write key, so `writer` bodies are safe.
- A malicious current writer can wipe `writer`-purgeable bodies at nodes keeping S unread; write access already lets it reset them everywhere.
- Storage at nodes keeping S unread under `author` and `author_or_admin` grows with superseded bodies until a snapshot with the right over them is written and their right holders sync: under `author_or_admin` an admin's or their author's, under `author` their author's only. So departed authors' `author` bodies stay for good, their `author_or_admin` ones until an admin compacts (a removal is the natural moment). Measured in notes/growth.md.
- Bounds read on a node's own clock straddle: two nodes either side of a keep-at-least bound or a grant's `until` (a node card ending) may delete and log one body again for as long as their clocks differ; spec/sync-judgement.md §4 bounds it to one logging per change of ground.
- Relative keep bounds measure from the killing commit's `ts`, so a backdated kill shortens "at least D" by up to the drift cap.

## Left alone
- **Never judged** (left alone): entries a node cannot open (chunks of live deltas, newer epochs), commits not applied (spec/sync-judgement.md §1 Unjudged blobs), deltas this node does not hold, and anything as outside the closure while a group of it holds part of its `group.0` as a header only (`Engine::closure_whole`: a fallen snapshot's covered list until a holder logs it again; deleting meanwhile what that holder, reading the thing in the closure, logs again is a storm). Limit: where no node holds that body any more (spec/permissions.md §5.2 Limit) the part stays a header for good and these nodes delete no outside-closure entry of S again.
