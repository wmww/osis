# Sync convergence: compaction, purge, deletion at labels
> Summary: Why spec/sync-judgement.md §2 (and the deletion parts of §5) are shaped as they are: one convergence rule per body, "covered means redundant", tombstones, no timed deletes, purge reaching snapshots, purge commits kept `ROLE_KEEP`, positive uncovered, snapshot-first drops, carriers and their tiebreaks, a purged body waiting for its rewrite, header-only lasting blobs, why no recycle bin; the TLC findings behind each; accepted limits; how sync judges and deletes (live writes, deletes waiting for the source, chunks, what is never judged, compaction agreement, carriers). Neighbours: notes/sync.md (index), notes/whole-commits.md (one blob per commit), notes/crdt.md (merge and compaction in the engine), notes/convergence-study.md (validity flips against dropped data), notes/ephemeral.md (the memory class, spec/sync-compaction.md §2).

TLC findings S1-S18 are core/test/tla/sync/AMBIGUITIES.md's.

## The rule
- **Convergence is the one rule**: per body must-hold, may-hold or must-not-hold under a label (`labeling/converge.rs` `classify`); join, leave, compaction and repair after a bad deletion are all this rule. May-hold keeps a compactor's drop and a lagging holder's re-upload from ping-ponging. A wrongful deletion is healed at the next holder sync by a fresh encryption.
- **Bodies are what the label's actor reads; copies are whole blobs** (spec/sync-judgement.md §1). Everything judged of a body (`entry_for`, `delete_stands`, strict subsets, carriers) compares `readable` parts, so nodes holding more secrets judge alike: a deleted blob holding only more the actor cannot read was this body's (else its holder places it again for the dropper to delete, forever), one holding more it reads was another body's (a rewrite replaced it). A dropped delta makes its whole blob must-not-hold (`holds_nothing`, opened parts), unreadable or not, so duplicates compare the whole delta set (`ais`): a blob holding a delta one node dropped is no copy of that node's rewrite (else a holder deletes the rewrite as the higher duplicate and the dropper places it again).
- **Covered means redundant under join**, not "inside the snapshot's frontiers": a snapshot that omits content covers nothing it omits, so a bad snapshot is harmless and anyone can re-snapshot; it also absorbs the versioning rule that a node never compacts what it could not join. A held body or snapshot is judged by the join alone; tables speak only for a body not held. Comparing frontiers makes cover stop at a gap: a fresh node's snapshot lists a session at 0 (the older snapshot had pruned it), so holders of a dead body find it uncovered and upload it, and a right holder that dropped it deletes it each pass, forever (sim `snapshot_rights`, `sessions::frontier_gap_covers_what_joins_unchanged_spec_commits_5`); inheriting frontiers does not help, the pruned session has no entry to inherit. Limit: a dead body is covered by a snapshot whose writer never saw it, so a revoke that revives it is met by `takes_late` as with a pruned session.
- **Must-hold needs a positive "uncovered"**: a node that cannot join a body (parked), has not fetched a snapshot's pages, or no longer holds a snapshot under the label stamped after the body (forgotten, or dropped under a later cover that is not at the label) treats it as may-hold, never must-hold, or an old-version writer and a compactor, or a holder and a co-writer that dropped the body under that snapshot, would re-upload and delete forever (an author's own body kept under others' covers, `snapshot_not_held_leaves_what_it_may_cover_unplaced_spec_sync_6`). A forgotten memory snapshot stays one of the label's while an entry of it is there (`aggregate` reads its forgotten header), so a full pass judges as the incremental ones do. A snapshot not held stamped no later than the body never saw it, so it counts as not covering (`stamp_of`): else an old forgotten snapshot left at a full host would hold back every later own write. Must-hold judges with held snapshots only (`unsettled_sets`: pending snapshots, entries being fetched); deletions keep the full unsettled rule.
- **A must-not-hold blob holds nothing** (S17): one holding a dropped delta (purge, expiry, compaction) or one the label's actor may not read (`holds_nothing`; unsettled views count it too, which only keeps or uploads more). It neither holds a body "the label lacks" nor makes another blob a strict subset, so a purger's rewrite goes up at once beside the purged blob still waiting for its carrier (the rewrite itself, S18). A strict-subset projection of a commit (a writer whose timeline lagged uploaded less) is must-not-hold; a writer that has not applied a purge yet deletes the purger's rewrite as a strict subset of its own body until it does (S4).
- **Must-not-hold wins every overlap with must-hold** (purged while uncovered): the must-not reasons are the ones that remove content. A body holding no delta (every delta purged) is covered by definition; its header lives on in the nodes that applied it.
- **Keep bounds never change what is read**; only expiry kills values. What holds is that live state is never lost from a node's state or from the label ("a holder can always serve current state" is too strong: readers cannot write snapshots).

## Carriers
- **What a blob alone may hold leaves a label only when another entry carries it** (S2, S5, S10; spec/sync-compaction.md §1 carriers): per delta, a live write, a kept delta, or one dropped as covered and not purged (`part_to_carry`; `may_delete(to_carry, carried)`). Kept: the holder places such a blob again (`delete_stands`), so a deleter not waiting meets an author re-placing it every pass (`kept_delta_waits_for_its_carrier_spec_sync_7`). Dropped as covered, dead or live: a holder lacking the cover judges the delta live and uncovered, so the cover goes up first (`work_sets` seed 963785799951); liveness alone is per node and cannot decide a delete. A purge never waits on a cover: the purge is why it goes.
- So a replaced purge-on-replace body, a strict subset or one of two duplicates is deleted at a label only while a covering snapshot, a superset blob of the commit or an identical body is under it, and a right holder that may write the aspect uploads the snapshot itself when none is there (`snapshot_first`; else nobody is obliged to and the host keeps the body forever). Locally a node drops the body at the bound and keeps the live writes in its state (spec/crdt.md §4.6); a snapshot it writes from that state covers every body it applied.
- **Live writes** (`Engine::delta_live`): a dot the delta owns survives in the state; unknown counts as live; writes of invalid or undone deltas are stripped from the state, so they count dead until a validity flip or redo puts them back; a memo per delta (`Core::live_bodies`).
- **What a node deletes it would not upload**: carrying a delta (deleter, `carried`) and needing a blob to carry it (holder, `must_carry`, `delete_stands`) are one judgment per delta: kept or a live write of its own, and no snapshot at the host covering it. Two ways they split and looped (sims `demotion_unsynced`, `coverer_revoked`): a held delta counted carried by a carrier recorded when it dropped earlier, after that cover fell (a held delta is carried only by the join, `snapshot_carries`; recorded carriers stand in only for dropped deltas, `fallen_carrier_carries_nothing_spec_sync_7`); and a dead delta counted as needing a blob because a class-mate delta was live and covered (hence liveness per delta, not per body). A strict subset is judged only in a settled view, as must-hold's uncovered is: a node fetching the cover a co-writer dropped the extra delta under would otherwise delete the co-writer's rewrite each time it is placed (`work_sets` seed 963785799948).
- **A kept part of a blob that also holds a dropped one** is carried by the rewrite this node places (`body_sends` runs before `deletions` in a pass; `carried` asks a snapshot only for dropped parts' live writes): with mixed classes in one blob, "no carrier: snapshot first" would snapshot every kept aspect of every drop.
- **Local drops of live bodies snapshot first** (S11): a writer writes a covering snapshot (uploaded later as must-hold) before dropping any body with live writes it holds no snapshot for, else a purge-on-replace body written offline and dropped before any upload leaves its live write in one node's state alone; readers drop and keep the state. A part of a waiting commit keeps its body until the commit applies. A replaced purge-on-replace body goes under the cover every writer holding it writes in the replacing transaction (notes/engine-retention-covers.md, Purge on replace): every replacing edit costs a snapshot of the aspect, the cost that option pays.
- **Carrier tiebreaks** (S12): the host cannot read the bodies, so two right holders with stale views could each delete one of two identical bodies, or one of two covered-equal snapshots, each counting the other as the carrier. So an identical body carries only the one with the higher blob ID, of covered-equal snapshots the higher commit ID goes, and a must-not-hold blob (a strict subset among them) never carries.
- **A purged body waits for its rewrite** (S18): every delta's live writes count, and a kept one is carried by any other blob of the commit that is not must-not-hold (the rewrite, once present at that host) or by a covering snapshot; a right holder holding such a blob asks for no snapshot (`carried`, `snapshot_first`). Judging only the dropped deltas would delete a blob holding a purged delta and a live one in the pass that places the rewrite, and a refused upload (a rotation racing the purge, code 9) would leave the label without the live write until the purger retried. Cost: the purged value's commit blob stays at the host one more round trip (its chunks go at once). Rejected: any held blob of the commit as a carrier (keeps the race); waiting for a covering snapshot (a snapshot per purge).
  - At a source full for L (spec/sync-streams.md §4 full) the rewrite cannot arrive, but the purge is not held back: deletes there wait for no entry it would pull (`At::full`), so the body's delete goes, its room frees, and the source pulls L again once it has room.
  - The purge request is a new entry and waits for space like any write. An `Outside` superset (holding a delta the label's actor may not read) waits likewise for its readable projection; deltas the actor may not read are skipped (their carriers are under their readers' labels; counting them would keep such a blob, purged or not, forever).

## Purge
- **Purge reaches snapshots**: purging a write also makes it value-less in snapshot bodies, held stripped (leftovers) and deleted at hosts; a writer then holding no covering snapshot under a label re-snapshots there (duplicates are covered-equal and one is dropped). Snapshots are `writer` and redundant by construction, so any writer removing a stale one costs only a re-upload.
- **A purge commit is kept `ROLE_KEEP` after its `ts`** (S1): the purge SHOULD name dead writes and reset the rest, so the next snapshot covers the purge commit at once and a compactor could drop it; a node that held the purged body and had not yet seen the purge would then keep the value forever (the body is covered, hence may-hold, and nothing tells it). The staleness class of revokes (90 days of role history); a node offline longer is an offline copy (spec/requirements.md, Threat model). Rejected: never covering purge commits (a blob per purge forever); purge items in snapshots (the compactor would vouch for the purger's right). A purge commit with `expires` reaches only deltas expiring no later, so the host drops it unattended when everything it reached has expired too; the API puts a purge and its resets in a commit of their own, so disappearing writes never inherit the 90 days.
- **A node offline past `ROLE_KEEP` misses purges** (S15): the purge commit is compacted, its body is covered (may-hold) and it keeps the value bytes until it compacts; the value is dead by the snapshot's reset when the purge named dead writes (the SHOULD), so it re-spreads nothing. An offline copy; accepted. Confirmed in the model: the purge commit's `ROLE_KEEP` overrides its keep-at-most too; any node dropping a purged body keeps the rewrite (a reader's stays local, it cannot stamp); a remover uploads its removal rotation before anything else.
- **Header-only blobs exist only as lasting headers** (Tess: tombstones when history is on; the TLA model's S14 still says may-hold for every retention). A purge that empties a body of any other retention leaves nothing under the label; headers persist only at the nodes that applied the commit (dedup by commit ID needs them, and a commit-form dot resolves its author through them). A purged permanent delta's commit, and its purge commit, stay must-hold as a header-only blob where no other body of the commit is wanted (`retention::lasting`, `commit_bodies_under`).
  - Why the request too: atomic apply counts a missing delta as held only by a held request; a new node with the blob alone would wait for good, and treating a header-only blob as proof of a purge would let any writer hide another's message from new nodes by uploading one. A writer keeps a witness per purge commit, so the cost stays a header per write, also for an author naming one message many times.
  - What a host learns: one more small stamped entry with no commitments close to a deletion it already sees (notes/unreadable-data.md). Readers see what the header always showed; the delta nonce is gone, so no guess of the value can be confirmed.
  - A header-only blob applies as its header alone, a lasting one's other parts joining as they come (`a_lasting_header_applies_and_the_rest_joins`); a purge commit's own delta purged in turn applies as its lasting header (`a_purge_purged_in_turn`).
  - Cost: one view scan per commit with no body under the label, per pass, and for a purge commit one local read per named thing (`purge_one` notes a request that dropped a permanent delta, `z/`). Walking the aspect instead costs R x M per full pass for R aspect-wide requests over M commits (10 requests at 2*10^4 messages: 400 ms instead of 35 ms).
  - A node that does not judge a header-only blob of such a commit lasting leaves it (may-hold; `delete.rs::judge`): it may not hold yet what made another node judge it so, and a delete there meets the holder's re-upload every pass (`a_header_only_some_judge_lasting_is_left_alone`). A purge that falls leaves its header-only blobs so; the price is a forged or stale header staying, at most what any stamped garbage costs.

## Tombstones; no timed deletes
- **Tombstones** refuse the identical blob for `TOMBSTONE_LIFETIME` (30 days), so a stale relay cannot resurrect a purged body; holders never need them (they sync before they upload, and a node holding the purge strips any later copy). A storage-versus-churn dial: infinite is O(deletions) host storage; zero lets any relay or bundle from before the deletion put the ciphertext back until a holder deletes it again. A peer staler than the log horizon returning later than the lifetime can resurrect, and holders heal it. Blob IDs hash ciphertext under random keys, so a tombstone is no fingerprint.
- **No timed deletes**: purge on replace is deleted at hosts in the replacing writer's sync, under its cover; expiry is the only timer a host runs on its own, and nothing on the wire asks a host to delete later.

## Why no recycle bin
A bin at the host (writers retire entries with a soft key, restore within a grace period) is no permission boundary: a writer can already destroy everywhere by compacting with an empty snapshot. Nothing lists or restores from it, so it is a delayed delete, and a retire carries no reason, so "re-upload what still changes state" undoes moderation retires. Retention per delta gives the roles their deletion powers, "covered means redundant" the recovery a bin was for. Moderation is not deletion: a chat admin removes the participant, or the app hides the message with a marker (notes/chat.md).

## Rejected
- Soft retire, a recycle bin, restore, and a generation counter forcing concurrent uploaders to re-tag (above).
- Compaction across clock skew by other rules: only the placer deleting its own entries on time grounds (entries of a lost or removed placer then stay for good); judging others' entries on the overlap of what both nodes drop (the only overlap a node can compute is "past by the largest skew", which is the `MAX_DRIFT` wait chosen, spec/sync-compaction.md §1).
- Leases (entries lapse unless refreshed): conflict with immutable expiry and with a host being the only backup.

## Reasons behind spec rules
The rules are in spec/sync.md at the section named; §9's reasons are in notes/ephemeral.md.
- §6 Convergence: a deleted body is not re-sent because a right holder's keep bounds, judged from the bodies it holds (spec/commits.md §5), had passed.
- §7 Compact: the `MAX_DRIFT` margin past the keep-at-least bound means no node whose clock lags by up to `MAX_DRIFT` still judges a dropped body must-hold. Sweep: what a removal or expiry hid dies (spec/crdt.md §4.8), its bodies follow their keep bounds and no snapshot carries it.

## Limits (accepted)
- An ex-writer or ex-admin keeps old-epoch delete keys and, as a reader, the sync key, so it can delete its own old `author` bodies or old `admin` bodies at hosts; healed by any holder's next sync. Level 3 needs the write key, so `writer` bodies are safe.
- A malicious current writer can wipe `writer`-purgeable bodies at a host; write access already lets it reset them everywhere.
- Host storage under `author` and `author_or_admin` grows with superseded bodies until a snapshot with the right over them is written and their right holders sync: under `author_or_admin` an admin's or their author's, under `author` their author's only. So departed authors' `author` bodies stay for good, their `author_or_admin` ones until an admin compacts (a removal is the natural moment). Measured in notes/growth.md.
- Bounds read on a node's own clock straddle: two nodes either side of a keep-at-least bound or a grant's `until` (`stale_card`) may delete and re-place one body for as long as their clocks differ.
- Relative keep bounds measure from the killing commit's `ts`, so a backdated kill shortens "at least D" by up to the drift cap.

## Left alone
- **Never judged** (left alone): unopened entries (chunks of live deltas, newer epochs), commits not applied, role-class commits and node-card commits outside the closure (others' certificates), projections that are a subset of a desired one (the superset goes up first), deltas this node does not hold, and anything as outside the closure while a group of it holds part of its `group.0` as a header only (`Engine::closure_whole`: a fallen snapshot's covered list until a holder logs it again; deleting meanwhile what that holder, reading the thing in the closure, logs again is a storm). Limit: where no node holds that body any more (spec/permissions.md §5.2 Limit) the part stays a header for good and these nodes delete no outside-closure entry of the label again.
