# osis-perm: the walk along stated paths
> Summary: Design and build state of osis-perm's walk along stated paths (A1 step 3, `osis_perm::paths`, unwired): its one rule, the two walks and content by point query, input and output, the event log by reach per component that makes from-scratch evaluation, incremental update and forgetting one definition, the walk by reach (a part walked again only where what its step reads changed; what stays component-wide and why), components and inert parts, trust per component, cost shapes and their count tests, what the differential against roles-model `paths` compares and at what size it passes, controls and mutations, the draft-spec corrections it forced, what the wiring owes.

**Principle: a part's right rests on the edges of the one path it states, read in key order, and a lowering is judged once, at the first pass that reaches it holding its right.** Every rule of notes/a1-spec/permissions.md §5.1 follows from it: what a part needs of each edge (admin, an invite grant's invite, a finalize's write), what a lowering puts in question (the parts resting on its edge whose need it no longer meets), when it is void (its drops would take what its own path rests on, or its remaining admin), the last-admin rule (one named edge, read in the pass), seeds (a trusted write's drops read before the walk). Nothing searches for another path.

**Content (step 3b) follows from it: a delta's validity is a point query along its one path, derived at read from what the walk stores per edge; nothing is stored per delta but its facts, so a lowering costs nothing per delta it cuts.**

## Step 3b design: two walks, content by point query
- **Two walks** (draft §5.1 Vouches): `first` (vouch entries unread) and `timeline` (each counting entry read as naming its commit's parts on its vouch's thing in its removal's list), two `walk::Walk`s fed the same commits. Counting entries are counted per vouch, for the vouches of the components the first walk ran in and those listing a role commit that arrived or went (Scale, below). A changed entry changes only its removal R's list, which only R's own verdict and seeds read, so the timeline treats R as added again at its key (its component updated from there, R's seeds read again where R is trusted): one definition for incremental and from scratch. A trusted write the timeline does not apply is void (`Paths::settle`, per component). Every reading (statuses, histories, finals, void trusted, disputes, content) is the timeline's. The first walk runs only in components holding a vouch (Two walks, below), so elsewhere the role walk runs once.
- **Stored per edge (timeline)**: its applied writes by key (value, value before, explicit, the edge as restored before in role and invite: `Paths::writes`; and the edge as restored after), and per level (write, admin) the set of its **cuts** (applied explicit writes whose value gives below r at their `ts` and that take the edge as restored from r or above), kept with the entries. Per edge, the **first chained** key giving write and giving admin (draft §4), kept by the timeline's chained pass (Reach, The chained pass).
- **Stored per delta**: its facts alone (`paths::Fact`: commit, position = the start of its `ts`, thing, aspect, hops as (holder, target) pairs, role needed, class, purge author, retention), in the store behind the seam (Scale, The delta-facts seam). No status; an addendum's validity is the store's too.
- **Indexes by what is named**: in memory, `named[c]`: the edge writes whose frontier holds c (role state); in the store, the addenda whose list holds c, the snapshots carrying c (a list naming a snapshot names the commits it carries, for that snapshot's thing and aspect; read as empty for a held role commit, which no snapshot carries), requests by author per (thing, aspect, author), addenda by (edge, position). Kept by `put` and `remove`.
- **Counting entries** (draft §5.1 Vouches): an entry of applied vouch V on N keyed by R and listing c counts where an edge write of R on h applies as a lowering in the first walk (whatever it takes from the value: R falls against the edge as restored, so it may drop with nothing taken, README Open 18), V is ordered after it, and c, held, has a role-class part on N, rests on h, is not V's signer's and is ordered before R: by its edge-class parts' key, else at the start of its `ts` (§5.2 Position).
- **Judging one delta** (`Paths::content`): per hop a point query (value before its position, the last cut at or after it: O(log) each); then, only if it failed, its namers (`named` of its commit and of the snapshots carrying it), then the addenda naming it, then the re-grant (finals and first chained per hop). So a delta appended at the end costs O(hops · log), whatever the history, removals or addenda held; a cut delta costs that plus what names it.
- **Addenda** are the one stored validity (draft §5.2: a least fixpoint over every signer), judged again only where an update reaches them (Scale, Counting entries and addenda): never on a plain delta's arrival, never for an update elsewhere. Plain content reads the stored addenda set, so its query is not recursive.
- **`Changed`** names what the engine must re-judge, never per delta for a cut: `parts` (edge-class status changes, every new part, addenda whose validity changed), `edges` (per edge the earliest key its history changed: deltas positioned after it on a path through it), `cuts` (per edge the latest key of a cut added or removed: deltas positioned at or before it), `regrant` (edges whose final role or first chained point changed: every delta through them), `named` (commits whose namers changed: carried by a snapshot taken or forgotten, listed by an addendum or a frontier forgotten or by a commit updated in place, what a snapshot so listed carries, a counting entry's). A delta's status can change only through one of these or through a delta naming it whose own verdict moved: the engine judges again what `Changed` reaches, then, for each judged delta whose verdict moved, the deltas it names (an addendum's list, a snapshot's carries, a request's (thing, aspect, author)), until none moves; a forgotten delta counts as moved, so what it named (a purge request's thing, aspect and author, an addendum's list) is judged again (`incremental::rejudged`, checked at every update of the incremental differential; over 200 content worlds' arrivals it judges 360 deltas again for 186 moved, of 4,500 held). A removal flipping 100,000 deltas reports one `cuts` entry. The engine keeps deltas indexed by (edge, position) to turn a range into the deltas it re-merges: that work is the re-merge's own, and a removal's frontier, which names what stays, is the same size.

## Input
Plain structs (`paths::Commit`, `Part`, `PartKind`, `EdgeWrite`, `Delta`, `DeltaClass`), independent of the header's types, so the header's renames do not reach them; step 2's adapter fills them from held commits.
- `Commit`: commit ID, `ts`, signer, its parts in header order (a part's index is its `WriteId` index). `Part`: the thing written (`on`), the stated path [A1..Ak], and an edge write (holder, value as `types::Edge` or a removal, frontier or none, remaining-admin holder, adminless mark), a vouch (removal, list) or a `Delta`.
- `Delta`: aspect (`Ak`), the role it needs (write; admin for an admin-only aspect, `sync.0`, `keys.0`, `secrets.0`, a snapshot carrying one), purge-author index into [signer, A1..Ak] (past the path: bad input, invalid), retention, class: `Plain` (a content commit's delta), `RoleClass` (judged like content, counts for vouches), `Snapshot { carries }`, `Addendum { removal, list }`, `PurgeByAuthor { author }` (a request by author on this part's thing and aspect, judged as a delta needing write along its path: how the header's `[tid, ak, author]` item meets a path is step 4's adapter question, notes/a1-spec/README.md step 1 *For step 2* (c)).
- `remains` and the mark are per part, not per commit: the header carries one of each per commit (the adapter copies it to each part).
- Records: thing to mint admin, given at construction (descent reads them); a changed record set means a new `Paths`.
- Trust set: `WriteId`s of edge writes.
- A finalize is not flagged: it is a removal of another holder's grant with no frontier that is not a self-removal (spec/permissions.md §2 Finalize), read off the part.
- Order within a commit (§5.1 step 1): by thing written, then the part setting an edge of the commit's own path after every other part on that thing, vouches included (a self-removal of the path's last thing, or the final record edge of a descent), then `roles.0` before `vouches.0`, then holder tid. Assumed: the header orders its aspects by thing then AK; step 2 checks this against the header.
- Bad input the walk sees: members naming a thing twice (content too), an edge-class part on its own signer (no edge write targets a key actor: a key actor's self-removal on itself would otherwise apply), two edge writes on one holder in a commit. A part on another key actor needs the adapter, which knows which tids are key actors: the walk applies it nowhere only because no grant on a key actor exists.
- A key actor's delta on itself along the empty path has the members [S] and no hop: valid (draft §1 Path). Along any other path it names S twice.

## Output
- Per edge-class part: `Status` (applied, applied as an invite grant, void, dropped, unapplied).
- Per delta: `Content` (valid by: held, listed, vouched, re-granted; copied; invalid), a point query (`content`, `content_counted`); `rests` (its hops and position, what the engine indexes) and `naming` (the addenda, snapshots and requests whose verdict it follows).
- `copies`: commits a valid snapshot carries that no valid held delta is (held or not). `purged`: held plain deltas a valid request by author holds the right over.
- Per edge (the timeline): history (key, value), `writes` (with the value and edge as restored before each, explicit or not; `Applied::cuts(r)`), the value and role just before any key, finals and invites, `chained` (first chained key giving write, admin).
- `counting`: the counting entries, (removal, commit, the vouch's thing): an entry keeps the commit's parts on that thing alone (`Walk::listed`, `Facts::listed`). Trusted writes the timeline does not apply (void, seeds lost).
- The dispute set (`Candidates::Explicit`, `&mut self`): read over what applies, which deltas are valid and the final roles; per candidate the trust switched on and back in place, compared where the switch's `Changed` reaches (`disputes_counted` counts it).
- `Changed` from each update (design above).

## One definition: the walk as an event log
The walk is a run of passes over the held parts in key order; D and the verdicts carry from pass to pass. Each component (Scale, below) keeps:
- the pass state: per edge its applied writes by key, each with the edge's state just after it (value, edge as restored in role and invite), so the state before any key is read off the entry before it; per part, applied and applied as invite;
- an **event log**: each verdict given (the lowering's key, its drops, the inert parts its audit would drop), keyed by its reach (the greatest key the walk had reached when giving it) and the order given.
A pass that ends at a lowering whose drops join D does not restart from the start: the next pass starts at the commit of the earliest drop, since every part before that point steps unchanged (D and the verdicts changed only after it).

Changing a component's input at key p (parts added or forgotten, a seed counted up or down, a removal's list read further): the events reaching p or past it are forgotten (verdicts and drops cleared), and the walk continues from the earliest of p, those events' keys and their drops' keys. Why that equals the from-scratch walk: every event reaching below p was given by a pass that had walked only parts before p, so the from-scratch walk over the new set gives the same events in the same order, and its pass state there is the state the kept events give. From scratch is the same call with every part new. Which parts after that point are walked again is Reach's (below). No cache, no fallback, one code path.

Trust (draft §5.1 Trust set) is the same machinery: seeds are counts per part, changed at the seed's key; an update of a component under a trust set activates its void trusted writes again and runs the loop (walk, void what the timeline does not apply, walk again), each round an update of that component.

## Reach: walking again only what reads a change
**Principle: a part is walked again iff something its step reads changed.** A step is a function of what it reads, so a part whose reads are unchanged gives the entry it has; the walk by reach skips exactly those, and reads as the whole walk (every part after the earliest change walked again: `Paths::whole`, a test mode) in every reading.

**What a step reads** (walk.rs `step`), each local to an edge set or to the part itself:
- of edges, only their state just before its key: its path's edges (what it needs; the last-admin rule's path last edge is one of them), its own edge (the value it lowers or ends, the edge as restored, whether a grant is current) and its named remaining admin (the last-admin rule). These are `Item::reads`, indexed per edge by key (`readers`). A state is ts-free (a value with its `until`, the edge as restored with `until` disregarded): each reader reads it at its own `ts`, so an `until` passing needs no entry;
- of itself: its verdict, whether it is dropped (D) or seeded (trust), and its input (part, list);
- only while a lowering is being judged (its verdict is None and it falls): `keeps` (setters on its path's edges before its key, recursively), the audit (the parts resting on its own edge before its key: applied, dropped, void), its commit's earlier drops. None of these is read again once the verdict is given (judged once), and the judgment happens only where the step's own reads above make it fall with no verdict, so they need no index: whenever a judgment runs, the state it reads is the whole walk's at that moment (below).

Nothing else is read: no pass counter, no global position. `counting`/`vouched` reach a step only through a removal's list (its input: the removal is walked again, its verdict forgotten); a trust switch only through seeds (`seeded`, per part).

**The worklist.** An update's walk keeps a set of (key, part) to walk again, taken in key order: the parts whose input changed, the lowerings and drops of the events it forgot. Walking a part again computes its entry from the state before its key and replaces the one it had (a dropped, seeded or forgotten part has none). Where the edge's state just after the key changes (value, edge as restored, invite), the edge's readers after the key up to and including its next entry join the set: past that entry they read the state the entry sets, which changes only if that entry's own part's does (its writer reads its own edge, so it is in the range). A write that changes nothing after it (a grant re-stating the value, a write applying nowhere) stops at once.

**Why it is exact.** By induction in the order the whole walk steps parts (pass, then key): when the walk by reach takes key k, every part before k whose entry differs from the whole walk's at that moment has been walked again, since its reads changed and a change only ever puts later readers in the set. So the state before k equals the whole walk's, and a part not in the set has the reads it had when last walked, hence the same entry. D and the verdicts are the same carried values in both. A judgment happens only for a part whose verdict is None and whose reads make it fall: such a part is in the set (a verdict forgotten puts its lowering in the set; one never given means its reads changed since it last stepped without falling), so both walks judge the same lowerings at the same moments with the same state. Restarts: a pass ends once the commit of a lowering with drops took effect (the parts of that commit in the set are taken first); the dropped parts join the set, so the next pass starts at the earliest drop, as the whole walk's does; parts left in the set are all after the ending commit. The state is read by key, so a part walked again replaces its own entry in place.

**The check**: `reach::reach_reads_as_the_whole_walk` and `paths_reach_agree_large` (100,000 at full size, a tenth at the gate's) compare the walk by reach with `Paths::whole` update by update (every `Changed`, and never more steps) over tiny, content, story worlds and part soups (trusted soups among them): from scratch, one commit at a time in a shuffled order, half forgotten one at a time, an edge write trusted and put aside, the dispute set at the end. `Paths::whole` shares the step, the entries, the event log and the restarts, so a plant there fails it on neither side: the independent checks are the model differentials and incremental against a fresh `Paths::evaluate`, and, by hand, `dump::paths_dump_readings` (`OSIS_DUMP=file`, `OSIS_DUMP_N` worlds per kind: every reading and every update's `Changed` per world, hashed one line each) to diff two builds of the walk. Plants, each failing a default test: no readers walked again; the edge's next writer not walked again; the chained pass's crossing range ending at the earlier key (each `reach_reads_as_the_whole_walk` and the older differentials); a forgotten event's drops not walked again (the hand worlds of restarts); the remaining-admin edge not indexed (`incremental::the_named_remaining_admin_arriving_late_walks_its_reader_again`); a state change only in `until` not walking readers (`reach_reads_as_the_whole_walk`, `incremental_equals_from_scratch`); a commit's later parts taken after the restart its earlier drops start, a plant `Paths::whole` shares (`hand::a_commits_later_part_is_taken_before_the_restart_its_earlier_drops_start`, the model); a forgotten event with drops kept (`incremental::a_verdict_kept_past_a_later_restart_would_misjudge` and many hand worlds).

**What stays component-wide.**
- **Forgetting events by reach.** An update at p forgets every event reaching p in its component: the lowerings judged after the walk reached p, on any thing of the component, are judged again, and their drops undone and redone (each with its restart). Keeping an event whose own reads did not change is not exact, because a judgment sees D as the pass that first reaches it finds it, and D gains a lowering's drops only when a pass reaches that lowering: which of two lowerings a pass reaches first is the component's whole restart sequence. World: a grant j at 40 sets g and rests on h; Q at 60 is a lowering on q whose path runs through g; E at 70 is a lowering on h listing nothing. In the walk, Q applies by j and falls: judged applied; E drops j and the pass restarts: Q applies by its verdict. Now a part at 50 changes what Q reads but nothing E reads. Kept, E's drop of j is in effect when Q is judged again: Q does not apply through g, gets no verdict, and stays unapplied; the whole walk judges Q before E reaches j: applied. A finer exact rule needs each kept verdict's moment in the new walk relative to the lowerings judged again; one shape is argued but not built: a verdict with no drops (no restart, D unchanged) is kept while nothing its step or judgment read changed before the walk first passes its key, and forgotten (no rollback needed: it changes only its own entry) when something does. The world is `incremental::a_verdict_kept_past_a_later_restart_would_misjudge` (every arrival order, every subset forgotten, against the model). The walk is still one function of the held set and trust set (passes and restarts run in key order, deterministically); what depends on the restart sequence is only which verdicts an update may keep. Cost: a backdated part costs the lowerings judged after its key anywhere in its component, each one step plus its void check and audit (`counts::an_organisation_judges_again_the_lowerings_after_the_key`: 1 + p + k over k teams each with one later removal; with r removals per team 1 + r + k·r steps and 2(k·r − r) + 1 setters: 2,021 steps and 3,961 setters at k = 100, r = 20, about 20,000 steps at k = 1,000). Any member's backdated role write (a device back online) forces it, but only in proportion to the component's lowerings after its key, never its parts or content; acceptable for the wiring. The finer rule needs a kept verdict's judgment reads (its `keeps` chain and audit at the moment a pass first reaches it) tracked as reads, which the walk does not index: not built.
- **Void trusted writes**: activated again on each update of their component (from scratch under trust), their seeds counted up and down: the events after the earliest seed forgotten and judged again.
- **The first walk** of a component that held no vouch when its input changed (`stale`): walked whole from its start once it holds one.
- **Counting entries** (vouch counting's seam, under redesign): recounted per vouch of each component the first walk ran in.

**The chained pass by reach** (draft §4). A chained-capable write reads, per edge of its path, only whether a chained write of an earlier commit sets it at a threshold (admin; an invite grant's role on each edge but the last, an invite of it on the last). Per edge the chained writes are kept by exact level (`chain_by`: one role set and one invite set each), a threshold's earliest key the least over its level and those above. Judging a write again moves it in or out of its sets; where a threshold's earliest key moves from a to b, the writes whose commit start lies after the earlier and at or before the later are judged again (`chain_readers`: the chained-capable writes by the edges of their path). Taken in key order, a write reads only earlier commits: no restarts (chained-ness reads no D). `first` is the Write and Admin thresholds' earliest keys.

**Memory.** Per edge-class part: `readers` holds one (key, slot) entry per edge it reads (its path's edges, own edge, named remaining admin: hops + 2 at most), `chain_readers` one per path edge for a chained-capable write, `chain_by` one key in a role set and one in an invite set per chained write. A key is 64 bytes, an entry 72, about 110 in a B-tree at its usual fill: about 0.9 KB per role part along a two-edge path ([O] to a team), 7 KB along 64 edges; content never enters them. An applied part's history entry is its key and an `Entry` (an `Applied` of two edge values, about 160 bytes): about 330 bytes in the B-tree, a fixed size whatever the history (an edge of 10,000 writes keeps about 3.3 MB); no entry copies path state. With the item and the component's order, chain and id maps, a role part costs about 1.8 KB per walk (both walks where its component holds a vouch): about 180 MB per 100,000 role parts. Each index carries the 64-byte key; a dense rank per key would cut the reach indexes to about a fifth.

## Cost shapes
- A part appended at the end of key order: its own steps only: a point query per hop (an edge value read), plus, for a lowering that falls, the parts resting on its edge (its candidates: the index `resting` per edge, inert ones in `inert_rest`) and its void check. Under a trust set the same (seeds counted for the new part alone).
- A void check (`keeps`) reads back along the setters of its path, each grant's own path in turn, memoized per check (each grant's `keeps`, and per (edge, position) the setter deciding a scan below it), so one check reads each edge position at most once: linear in the chain it reads. k void lowerings at the end of a chain of k grants cost k² setters read (finding 17; accepted, Open 2), each forced by one party's own writes. Iterative, never recursive (an adversary's chain is unbounded).
- A backdated part, a forgotten edge-class part, or a lowering applying with drops: the parts reading what it changed (Reach), and the lowerings of its own component judged after its key (Reach, What stays component-wide); other components do nothing. A change that every reader of an edge must see (the hub edge of an organisation lowered) walks each of them again: inherent. A chain where each part walked again changes the next part's edge walks the chain once: bounded by the parts reading what changed, each once per change (the set runs forward in key order, so a cycle of holders ends).
- A merge: the smaller component's entries moved (parts, events, chained-capable writes, vouches), then the joining part walked (Reach). Who can join components: only a part whose every hop a chain of held writes can set (Inert parts); a party joins k components with one such write, at the cost of the entries moved, about one component's each (`counts::a_path_joins_components_only_through_held_edges`), and the joined component stays joined (Scale, Forget).
- From scratch: one step per part, plus per restart its drops and what reads them. k lowerings at the end each dropping one early write restart k times: n + 4k steps over n + 3k parts (`counts::k_lowerings_with_drops_restart_k_times`; the whole walk, from each drop through the n parts after it: about k·n). One void check through an edge of m dropped grants reads about 2m setters (`counts::a_void_check_reads_each_setter_once`), so k such lowerings cost k·m from one party's own 2m + k writes.
- Trust: a void trusted write costs, on each update of its component, its seeds' readers twice and the lowerings judged after its earliest seed (it is activated again, then voided again): a trusted write the node's own walk does not apply, rare. A trust switch: an update of the components of the writes switched.
- Memory: one history entry per applied part, one event per lowering judged, each part in at most one event's drops (D only grows within a walk), so a component of n parts keeps logs of size O(n); the reach indexes (Reach, Memory) are O(parts × path length). An update's journal keeps each changed history entry as it was. A forgotten part leaves a nil slot and its interned edges stay (a few words each, until the walk is rebuilt from what is held).
- Disputes (Open 3 (a)): no copy; per candidate the trust switched on and back in place, each an update of the candidate's component (the reading after equals the reading before, the store's addenda verdicts included); compared: the parts `Changed` reports (applied or not), the edges whose final role it reports, and the deltas it reaches through the store's (edge, position) index (`Store::through`) or names (an addendum it reports names its list's deltas: validity reads no other namer). A candidate on A beside n parts and deltas elsewhere: 7 steps and no verdict read at n = 100 and 1000 (`counts::a_dispute_probe_reads_its_component_alone`; mutation, every delta compared: 800 and 8,000).
- Content: see Step 3b design. A delta's query: per hop a binary search in the history and the last cut, then (failing) its namers, the addenda naming it, the re-grant. Addenda: judged again only in F (Scale), so k addenda arriving one at a time cost O(k) and an update elsewhere costs none. Counting entries: per vouch in the components the first walk ran in.
- Two walks: every role-walk cost above twice in a component holding a vouch, once elsewhere; a component gaining its first vouch (or merging with one the first walk never walked) is walked in the first walk from its start.
- A delta under k cuts on its edge: one binary search and the edge's last cut per hop, the same at k = 10 and 100 (`counts::a_delta_under_k_cuts_is_a_point_query`).
- Memory: the walk keeps edge-class state alone (parts, edges, logs, histories, role commits' facts, frontier names): the same at 1,000 and 10,000 content deltas (`counts::delta_facts_live_in_the_store`: 136 entries). Delta facts are the store's: `MemStore` keeps a `Fact` (about 0.5 KB with its index entries) per held delta, which the engine's store index replaces. Judging an appended delta: 2 store lookups (held? and its facts), the same at both sizes.
- **Attacker-drivable** (any signer whose commits a node holds, valid or not):
  - A cut delta's query reads every frontier and addendum naming its commit, junk included: k of them cost k per judgment (`shapes::thousands_of_addenda_naming_one_commit_cost_one_read_each`). The engine judges it again only when `Changed` reaches it; a bound needs the named lists indexed by validity.
  - An addendum listing a snapshot reads, in the fixpoint, every commit the snapshot carries: a writer's snapshot carrying m commits costs m per addendum naming it, each time it is in F.
  - Joining components (above): a party with held chains into k components merges them for good, with one write.
- **Where components buy nothing**: an organisation O whose admins act on its k teams through O is one component by design (each such write rests on an edge on O), and so are teams once joined by a hub's writes, after those are forgotten. Reach makes the steps independent of k (`counts::an_organisation_costs_what_reads_the_change`); what the component still costs is the lowerings judged after a backdated key on any team (Reach, What stays component-wide).

## Built
- `core/perm/src/paths/` (`mod.rs` the input and output types and `Paths`: the two walks, trust per component, counting per vouch, forget and update, disputes; `walk.rs` one walk: components, inert parts, the event log by reach, seeds, the chained pass; `path.rs` members and hops; `content.rs` deltas, namers, addenda by reach): every rule of draft §1 Path (hops, descent, the last edge, [S]), §2, §1 The last admin, §5.1 steps 1 to 5, Trust set (void read in the timeline, both walks redone), disputes (content validity included), Vouches (first walk, counting with the draft's four conditions, the second walk as the timeline), §5.2 Content, cut, the frontier listing (a counting entry too), Addenda (least fixpoint, never for a role-class delta, a snapshot, a commit carrying a purge request, or a role commit's addendum), Re-grant from each edge's first chained write strictly after its key, Compaction snapshots judged along their path (copies of a valid one shown; a list naming a snapshot names what it carries for its thing and aspect), admin-only and role-class deltas by their role, §6 the `author` class over held plain deltas. `add`, `forget`, `update` and from scratch by one path, per component. No unsafe, no new dependency, no indexing that can panic.

## Owed (not built)
- Step 4 (purge author, delete keys): the `admin` and `writer` purge classes (Destruction among them: a `created.0` request, its right judged without the destroyed thing's later edge writes); vouches for purge requests (the draft's OPEN); requests reaching snapshots, addenda and copies of commits not held; bodies (an addendum's list counting while its body is held; a dropped body still the signer's write).
- Step 5 (lists and certificates): own covers (the own-cover validity clause, and whether one outlives its author's demotion: OPEN); writing vouches and addenda; the list a remover writes.

- Not in the walk, for the adapter or the engine: external fires (§5.2: valid by the setting where the path does not give the role), `created.0` always valid, vouch and addendum entries a delta creates (`put_new`; the adapter passes only those), ephemeral parts as their durable kind.
- The `Store` contract the engine's index keeps: lookups in any order (`Reversed` in `single.rs` reads alike); reads see the walk's own writes within an update (the addenda fixpoint writes verdicts with `set_valid_addendum` and reads them back in the same `add`, `forget`, `update`, `set_trust` or `disputes`); `put`, `remove` and the verdict writes belong to the transaction holding the commits they follow. `Paths` reads a commit as held when the store has its deltas (`add` skips it), so a `Paths` built at load over a store that kept its facts would skip those commits, a role commit's edge-class parts among them: the walk is rebuilt over an empty store (or one the rebuild fills), and the stored addenda verdicts, derived state, are written anew by that rebuild's `add` (every addendum judged), so a crash mid-update leaves nothing stale. `disputes` writes verdicts and switches them back.
- The walk's API for the wiring is unchanged by Reach: `Paths::whole` is a test mode beside `Paths::single`; `Counts::steps` counts parts walked (again), forgotten and dropped ones included (their entries leave).
- The wiring: the adapter from held commits (`Commit`, `Part`); calling `add`, `forget` (covered, expired, purged, released, access lost) and `update` (a frontier leaf arriving) as the engine holds and lets go; the trust set restricted to what the draft allows; parts on key actors other than the signer as bad input (the adapter knows key actors); key actors' own edges; the engine's `Store` over its store index (by `WriteId`, (commit, thing), what lists or carries a commit, requests by author, addenda by (edge, position), addenda verdicts) and its index of deltas by (edge, position) to turn `Changed` into deltas to judge again, with the same rule for a forgotten delta's namees; external fires, `created.0`, `put_new`.

## Scale: components, forget, the seam
**Principle: an update does work in proportion to what it changed. A verdict reads only its component, so an update walks only the components it reaches, and in them only the parts whose reads changed (Reach); forgetting a commit is adding in reverse; content is read by point query through a seam the engine backs with its store.** One code path: the global walk is the one-component case, from scratch is an update from the start.

**Components, by what a verdict reads** (the reads are notes/a2-spec/README.md (e)). A part **touches** its own edge (an edge write's), its path's hop edges and its named remaining-admin edge. Its step reads those edges' pass state; its audit and seeds read the parts resting on its own edge, which touch it; `keeps` reads the setters of its hops (a setter touches the edge it sets), then each setter's hops; a commit's earlier drops rest on an earlier lowering's edge and `keeps` reads them only as setters it chases; the chained pass reads a write's hops and sets its own edge. A counting entry makes removal R's list (the timeline) read vouch V's verdict in the first walk, so V touches the edges of R's edge-class parts. Content reads edges by point query and touches nothing. A **component** is a class of the smallest equivalence on edges under which every non-inert part's touched edges are one class; its parts are those touching its edges. Nothing smaller is closed: a lowering on e reads (audits) every part resting on e, and that part's applying writes its own edge, so the relation runs both ways through every part.
- **A component is a set of things**: every live edge on a thing T is in the component of T's record edge (by induction on liveness: a live edge is set by a non-inert write, whose path's last hop into T is live), and a thing with no record has no live edge. So a write's remaining-admin edge, if ever set, is in its component already (its join is implied; a plant dropping it fails nothing, as it should); two things join where a part on one states a path through an edge on the other, or a vouch on one names a removal on the other (`single::a_vouch_joins_its_removals_component`: without that join the removal's component holds no vouch, its first walk never runs, and the entry never counts).
- **The check**: `Paths::single` is the walk with partitioning off (every part walked, inert or not, in one component, and walked whole: Reach), a test mode; `single::components_read_as_one_walk` and `paths_components_agree_large` (100,000 at full size, about 140 CPU-s, a tenth at the gate's, over tiny, content, story worlds and part soups, trusted soups among them) compare every reading, counting entries, void trusted writes and disputes with it, from scratch, after a shuffled arrival and after forgetting half.
- A commit writing several components joins nothing: its parts in different components read no common edge; the pass ending after a commit with drops replays the other components' parts unchanged.
- **Inert parts**: an edge is **live** if it is a record edge or the own edge of a held non-inert edge write; a part with a hop that is not live (or members naming a thing twice) can never apply, in any walk, and is **inert**: it touches only its own edge (its seeds, when trusted). Its status is Dropped iff some lowering's audit or some trusted write's seeds would drop it, kept as its set of droppers (no component reads another's: the set is a union). Liveness is the least fixpoint, grown on each add (an edge turning live wakes the inert parts waiting on it; each part wakes once); a forget leaves it as it was (an over-approximation: a non-inert part is walked like any other, so this is sound). So a path through things joined by no held edge joins nothing; a party joins components only through edges a chain of held writes reaches, its own path's first edge included.
- Per component: its parts in key order, its event log, its active trusted writes, its vouches, its chained-capable writes. One union-find over edges (by edge count); a component's maps live with its root.

**The event log by reach.** Each event carries its reach: the greatest key the walk had reached when giving it (its key, or the component's reach before it). An update from key p forgets the events with reach ≥ p and continues from the earliest of p and those events' keys and drops (their lowerings and drops walked again, Reach). In one component this is the cut at the first event at a key ≥ p (reach is that log's running maximum). A merge keeps both logs, each event its reach, and later events reach past both: the union's walk from scratch keeps, of each side, exactly that side's events before its first key ≥ p (the other side's restarts replay this side unchanged), so forgetting what reaches p on each side is exact and forgetting the later events is sound (forgetting any set closed upward in reach, walked again from its earliest key and drop, replays to the same events).

**Merge** (a new non-inert part touching two components): the smaller component's maps move into the larger, O(s log n) for s the smaller's parts, events and chained-capable writes; then the update from the joining part's key in the merged component, as a backdated part there. Components never split while commits are only added.

**Trust per component**: a trusted write's seeds are counts per part (a part may be several writes' seed); seeds change with a new part resting on an active trusted write's edge, a trusted write activated or voided, its list changing (a counting entry) or a part forgotten, each at the seed's key, the component then updated from the earliest. An update of a component reads as from scratch: its voided trusted writes are activated again, then the loop (walk, void what the timeline does not apply, all at once, walk again) runs, each round an incremental update of that component. Disputes: per candidate the trust switched on and back in one copy, each an update of its component; the dispute is read where the first `Changed` reaches (applied parts, final roles, the deltas it reaches or an addendum it reports names).

**Counting entries and addenda, by reach.** Entries are recounted per vouch: the vouches of components the first walk ran in, and those listing a role commit that arrived or was forgotten; an entry is counted per vouch keeping it. Addenda: an addendum's validity reads its own path's edges (held, listed, re-granted, its vouching path) and its namers' (a namer vouches by its own path and its removal's cut on the named delta's path, read on the named delta's path). An update re-judges the addenda whose hops `Changed` names (edges, cuts, re-grant), whose commit it names or that arrived, and every addendum their lists name, in turn (the forward closure F): F leaves the valid set, then F's members valid by themselves or by a valid namer outside F join it, and the worklist spreads inside F. Exact: outside F no read changed.

**Forget and update.** `forget(commits)`: each edge-class part leaves at its key, its component updated from there (as a backdated add); a delta leaves the indexes alone (no walk: its verdict was never stored), an addendum seeds F with what its list named, a snapshot names what it carried in `Changed.named`; a vouch's entries go; a forgotten role commit's entries go. A component that falls apart stays merged: components only bound work, splitting needs its connectivity again (its size), and liveness stays an over-approximation anyway; what it costs is later updates walking parts that are no longer joined. `update(commit)` (a chunked frontier whose leaf arrives late, a part's input changing in place): forget and add in one update, one `Changed`. Meanwhile the walk reads the frontier the node holds (the leaves it holds: the differential's views cut frontiers so), as the draft reads a list: it names what it names. Property: any order of adds, forgets and updates reads as from scratch over what is held, `Changed` exact.

**The void check, linear in its chain.** Within one check, the scan of an edge's history below a position decides the same setter whatever chased it there (the check's drops and memo are fixed), so the check caches, per (edge, position), the deciding setter, filling every position a scan passed: each edge position is read once per check. k checks cost k times their chains, each bounded by the writes on the chain: a party forces m per check only with m writes of its own on edges of the chain.

**Two walks.** The first walk (vouch entries unread) is needed only to count entries, and an entry needs a vouch: it runs only in components holding a vouch (V joins R, so an entry's removal is in one). Elsewhere the timeline is the walk and no entry counts there. A component whose input changed while it held no vouch is marked stale in the first walk and walked there from its start once it holds one (a vouch arriving, a merge). Where no entry's removal is, the first walk reads as the timeline (`Paths::first_walk_differs`, checked in every differential world). Behind the seam it has (`Paths::recount`, the timeline's `vouched`): removing vouch counting deletes the first walk (the `timeline` flag's other branch, `Comp::stale`, `vouch_comps`), `recount`, `set_entries`, `drop_entries`, `counting`/`keeping`/`by_vouch`, `first_walk_differs`, the timeline's `vouched` and `set_vouched`, `VouchItem`, the vouch link in `activate` and the `by_removal`/`listing` indexes, and content's `counted`/`set_counted` and its use in `listed`; the vouch part itself stays an edge-class part.

**The delta-facts seam.** Content reads delta facts through a trait (`paths::Store`, `Paths<S: Store = MemStore>`, `Paths::with_store`): `put`/`remove` a `Fact`, `get` by `WriteId`, the deltas of a commit (on a thing), the addenda listing a commit, the snapshots carrying one, requests by (thing, aspect, author), addenda through an edge within a position range, the stored addenda verdicts, and `all` (full readings: tests, disputes). A commit's purge flag is read off its deltas; whether it is a role commit is memory's (role commits' facts stay in memory, as do the frontiers naming commits). `MemStore` is the in-memory backing; the engine backs it with its store index, the same index by (edge, position) that turns `Changed` into deltas to judge again. In memory stays only the edge-class state (items, logs, histories: growing with role changes); the walk holds only commits with an edge-class part.

**Count tests (designed shapes).** An appended part in a world of c components: steps in its component alone. A backdated edge part: its component from its key. An update: addenda judged only in F. A trusted component: an appended part costs its own step. A merge: the smaller's entries moved plus the walk from the joining key. A forget: a delta, no walk step; an edge part, its component from its key. One void check: linear. From scratch: linear in parts. Memory: in-memory entries the same at n and 10n content deltas; store lookups per judged delta constant (its path read from the walk).

**Built**: `Paths::single` and the comparison against it (above); hand worlds (`single.rs`): liveness in all 720 arrival orders (a chain, a mutually resting pair made live by a backdated grant), every subset of a world of every forget case forgotten and added again under three trust sets, a frontier leaf listing a drop arriving and going, a store returning every lookup reversed (`any_lookup_order_reads_alike`); the void check's cache; components (union-find by edge count, inert parts and liveness, the event log by reach, merges moving the smaller side), trust per component (seeds as counts), the walk and the chained pass by reach (Reach: `readers`, `chain_readers`, `chain_by`, the worklist, `Paths::whole` and its comparison), counting per vouch, addenda by reach, `forget` and `update`; their count tests (Counts) and the default and `_large` properties `incremental::forgetting_and_updating_equal_from_scratch` / `paths_forget_agrees_large` (adds and forgets one to three at a time in random order, a forget of what is not held, forget then re-add, frontier leaves arriving by `update`, each `Changed` checked, the end against from scratch, disputes in one world in eight), and `shapes.rs`'s `robust` forgetting a shuffled half and re-adding it; the first walk only where vouches are (`counts::the_first_walk_runs_only_where_a_vouch_is`); the delta-facts seam (`counts::delta_facts_live_in_the_store`); disputes per component read off `Changed` (`counts::a_dispute_probe_reads_its_component_alone`). **Left**: a component that falls apart after a forget stays joined (Forget and update; by reach it costs only the event log's forgetting); keeping verdicts given after an update's key that nothing it changed reaches (Reach, What stays component-wide: the shape argued for verdicts with no drops).
