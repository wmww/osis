# Keys: holder actors at depth 0
> Summary: Why a thing never looks inside its holder actors for keys (Tess) and why each rule of spec/crypto.md §4 Holder actors exists: the trade, Seal and joint seals, boxes judged by state, stale boxes, blocked through holder actors, judging by order where the secret is lacking, foreign keysets, left out and its record with causes, box records, carried commits and carried demotions, destroyed holder actors, the accepted leaks, and what the rotation property's checker excuses. Key cycles: notes/key-access.md. Models: core/test/tla/rotation/Depth0.tla, LeftOut.tla (README).

Principle (Tess's decision): **for keys, a thing never looks inside its holder actors.** No names of departures below the holder actor, no keyset names, no departure walk. A departure inside a holder actor M reaches A only as M's new keyset, and what ends a right through M travels with it (Carried demotions): rights through M are judged at A from evidence inside M anyway (a write's evidence holds the edge writes of its stated path in M and below), so the end of such a right rides on the keyset A needs anyway. This is the keys principle (rotating needs no secret; a node judges what it can open, never past it) carried to holder actors: the blocked walk and the left-out record follow from what a node holds, not exceptions to depth 0. Reading inside members would drop the mark but make every admin of A hold every member's keys. The rules are pure functions in `osis_keys::members`, the engine gathering inputs (`rotation::members`); the rules themselves are the spec's, this note keeps why.

## The trade
Looking inside holder actors locked M out of A while a departure inside M was known and M's keyset was an ignorant or forged fork. Depth 0 seals to that keyset instead, so the departed party reads A's new content until M's next knowing rotation (a confidentiality residual for a liveness one, in the same rare shapes: a concurrent rotation of M by an admin that has not seen the removal, or a withholding peer plus a removed admin of M forging M's `keys.0`). Both heal at M's next knowing rotation, and the same residual already exists one level up (spec/requirements.md Permissions). A depth limit instead of depth 0 deletes almost nothing: the cost was in the first level (names below the holder actor, keyset names, bound seals, grant names, the box judgement). `ensure_rotated` cascades over the holder actors this node may rotate (post-order, each once), so where it may rotate M its one rotation of A seals M's new keyset. Tests: `rotation::tests_members`, osis-engine `flows::cascade_two_hops_spec_perm_3`, `inside_member_departure_leaves_holder_out_spec_perm_3`.

## Seal
- **Seal** (rule 1, `seal_keyset`): anchoring at the keysets A's current boxes to M name, through held valid keysets only (a forged keyset lists everything), keeps A's next rotation off an ignorant fork of M, whose history box would hand A's content since the removal to the departed node.
- **Joint seals** (`SecretBox::Joint`): while the anchors fork, one box opening only for a holder of every fork's keyset. A party removed from M on one fork opens nothing; a member granted on one fork opens it once M is re-sealed at the others or next rotates. Nothing about A waits on M (Joint.tla: `J_gone` passes with every admin of M gone, `J_wait_gone` is the waiting rule's stall, `J_newest` the leak of sealing at one fork). So a fork made before a known removal is sealed jointly with the fork that covers it, with no rotation of M (`forked_anchors_seal_jointly_spec_crypto_4`).
  - Rejected: waiting for M's merge (notes/keys.md, Forks stand); leaving M out instead (drops the anchors: the next re-seal sealed M at its newest keyset, perhaps an ignorant fork); a deterministic merge any member could compute (it still needs a node that may rotate M online, an account's epochs are named by their rotation's commit ID so two merges never coincide, and two merges made knowing different departures must differ anyway).
- A joint box is judged as a whole (`rotation::blocking`, `joint_blocked`): stale or tainted when any keyset is (those bind M's rotators, whoever opens the box), blocked by departures only when every keyset misses one. It opens for no one a keyset it names keeps out, alone or pooling secrets, so it is as safe as a box at its least exposed keyset (JointRule.tla: `every` passes `NoLeak`, `NoPooledLeak` and `Seals`).
  - Where every fork misses a departure (two concurrent removals, one per fork), M is left out until a rotator of M re-keys it knowing both, never if all are gone: required by spec/requirements.md "rights held by different parties never combine" (each removed party opens the other's fork). Rejected: blocking only for a departure every keyset misses, per edge or per departed party (a party need not be one node: q in N and P, N removed from M on one fork, q from P on the other, q opens both, and nothing a node holds shows q in N; removed parties pool secrets: JointRule.tla `edge`, `party` fail both); blocking when any keyset is blocked (`Seals` fails).
- With a tainted anchor's only candidate, M is left out and re-sealed once one exists (waiting on taint held A's removals while a grant stayed hidden).
- An account is sealed jointly at every head past the anchors: a reader cannot tell a head boxed to a node removed from M (a concurrent rotation by a node that did not hold the removal) from the others. A mint admin's record edge is never the keyless grant (its key yields A's from A's creation). A rotation of M made by this section takes the rotations due above it in the same step: M's rotation makes boxes to M stale, and answered at once, no other rotator meets them and forks.

## Boxes judged
Every rule reads the `secrets.0` state's boxes; an invalid delta's boxes count for nothing. Such a box still opens, but carries only a secret its author held (a secret once shared cannot be un-shared), and an author lowered below that role has a departure of its own that rotates the role. Counting them let a reader force one rotation per junk commit, protecting nothing (`reader_junk_boxes_force_no_rotation_spec_crypto_4`). An admin re-granting a party it demoted while itself removed hands it the epoch its demotion made, and its own removal's rotation bounds that (`invalid_regrant_hands_on_only_what_its_author_held_spec_crypto_4`); the demoted-admin case is an accepted leak (notes/keys-coverage.md).

## Stale
- **Stale** (rule 2): not "any newer keyset" (that moves A onto an ignorant fork), and an unknown keyset is not stale (a co-writer may have sealed to one not received yet). Staleness carries a removal up: M's knowing rotation lists the keyset its departed party opens, every box to M at it goes stale, and so on up.
- Every rotation of M the cascade makes is followed in the same step by the rotations then due above that the node may rotate (`rotation::rotate_above`): left to each pass, every rotator of A met A's box to M stale at once and each made a fork (`holder_rotation_rotates_above_at_once_spec_crypto_4`). A removal's or finalize's own rotation is not followed upward (callers read the role commit and its rotation as one step): sync's role duty rotates each thing above at its next step, so concurrent judges fork it once, bounded by the thing's rotators.
- **A stale box blocks at any epoch the walk follows** (`Walk::Encrypt`, sealing included), judged from state without the epoch's secret. Without it in Seal, a co-writer of A that could not judge the departure below re-sealed M where a knowing node left it out, and the two rotated A for good (`co_writer_seals_no_keyset_with_a_stale_box_spec_crypto_4`). Without it for writing, a writer of G holding M's knowing keyset but none of A's secrets wrote under G's epoch boxing A at an epoch naming M's superseded keyset, and the party removed from M read it through A (`writer_leaves_out_at_a_stale_box_below_spec_crypto_4`, `tests_deep::deep_departure_holds_plain_spec_crypto_4`, Depth0 `D0_rs_noenc` fails `NoLeakDeep`). One rule for both: any rotator of G holding the stale box and M's keyset rotates G and Seal leaves A out; a reader of G waits for one, placing meanwhile.
- A left-out holder actor is re-sealed once its rotator rotates it; with all its rotators gone it stays out of G for good, as Seal already leaves it on G's next rotation (spec/requirements.md Permissions: a holder that missed key changes may be unable to read until a current holder acts for it). The leave-out's liveness is a requirement on sync (Left out, then followed by report).
- A false box record (a hostile writer of A naming M's superseded keyset) makes G's writers rotate G once per record, as it forces one rotation of A: no stall, ended by the writer's removal from A. Churn below costs G at most one rotation per rotation below it, and none per write (`tests_deep::churn_below_rotates_g_once_per_rotation_below_spec_crypto_4`).
- Keyless boxes bind rotators only, and stale ones bind rotators only for logging (`Walk::Log`): a reader held back by one logged no certificate copies, so M's new keyset never reached A's rotators.

## Blocked through holder actors
- **Blocked through holder actors** (`rotation::blocked_epoch`, Depth0 `D0_above`, `D0_readers_out`): needed because a removal can go alone: A stays uncovered while its rotation waits, and a rotation of G above A sealing A at that keyset let the removed node read G's new content. Out, not Wait: waiting froze G's rotations with A's and still left G's existing epoch to write under.
- The rule is what a departed party opens, judged from what the node holds, never from its rights: the walk follows every box at an epoch whose secret the node holds, whether or not it may rotate that thing. Stopping at things the node may not rotate let G's admin, a reader of A and M only, write G content a party removed from M opened through A (`D0_readers`, `box_walk_passes_actors_the_node_may_not_rotate_spec_crypto_4`).
- Keyless binds rotators only: it says a rotation is due, not that a departed party opens the epoch. Logging (`Walk::Log`, the read gate for logging certificates, revokes and own role commits) follows boxes only through things the node may rotate, so a reader still logs what tells the rotators (under the full walk M's members, blocked on A, never logged M's new keyset where A's rotator reads it). Read blocked for encrypting but not for logging gives no write or delete keys and no steps, so nothing fresh but those is logged. Logging reads it where it wraps: a role commit wrapped under a sync ID (a certificate or this node's own) by `Walk::Log`, anything else by the encrypting walk (`Engine::wrap_blocked`).

## Judged by order where the secret is lacking
"Never looks inside" means "never past what the node itself holds", but an epoch of M whose secret the node lacks is still judged: blocked when its earliest held valid keyset body is ordered before the newest edge write an epoch covering a known departure must name (`Departure::newest_needed`): its rotator held nothing ordered after the departure, so it cannot cover it. Without it, a node knowing a departure from M but holding none of M's secrets sealed M at M's pre-departure keyset and the departed party opened A's new epochs until M rotated, for good where M's only admin is hostile. Counting such an epoch as uncovered outright would bar a reader of M from ever sealing M's write role once a writer left M. Model: Depth0 `D0_order` (`NoLeakOrder`; fails with `Order = FALSE`), `D0_order_live`. Test: `departure_from_an_unread_holder_is_judged_by_order_spec_crypto_4`.
- **A remover sealing to an ignorant fork** (accepted, Tess): an admin of M through a keyless grant removes P from M, then seals A to a keyset of M made by another admin unaware of the removal but ordered after it; P reads A's new content, even after being removed from A directly, until M's knowing keyset reaches it (spec/requirements.md Permissions; `rotation_holds_large_spec_crypto_4` seed 618244500061). A node cannot tell: rotation commits carry no frontier and a rotation's names are inside its history box. Unstudied option: compute the names digest of the lowerings on M ordered before the keyset with and without the departure; a match proves knowing or ignorance.

## Foreign keysets
A keyset whose own box (recorded by its rotation commit) names a keyset held invalid or foreign is foreign (spec/crypto.md §4 Foreign): it rests on what the node's side does not apply (one side per party, notes/disputes.md Rotations under a fork). It supersedes nothing and makes nothing due, so a permanent fork on a holder actor costs each side a bounded number of rotations instead of the sides rotating in turn forever (`ForkRot.tla`); it stays current and its history is kept (a rotation's history box carries it), and it is never the newest (blocked by its own box: nobody there writes under it). A re-seal's box naming a keyset held invalid makes its epoch stale, not foreign: the epoch was not built on it. Engine: `rotation::members::Foreign` (the view marks it, `RoleEpochs::set_foreign`; the index keeps each epoch's own records, `SealedAt::own`, and the held invalid keysets, `Indexes::keysets_invalid`); tests `rotation::tests_foreign`.
- **Depth**: an epoch boxing a foreign keyset is foreign in turn. Above M, a foreign keyset of M is half valid: its list keeps what it lists stale, but it anchors nothing and Seal never picks it (a stale keyset Seal then gives is blocked); and a rotation lists no foreign epoch in its keyset, carrying it in its history box only. Model `ForkDepth.tla` (T above U above B; README there) checks both cases at depth: the fork (B forked, U's and T's rights direct) and the honest race (U's knowing rotation km boxes B at a keyset a concurrent demotion invalidates, so km is foreign where the demotion is).
- Each half is needed. A foreign keyset held invalid above (its list passing nothing on): in the race the writer of T, not reading U, seals U at the keyset km superseded and the party km removed from U reads T (`FD_race_drop`; `a_box_naming_an_invalid_keyset_is_recorded_with_its_rotation_spec_crypto_4`). Counted as any valid keyset above (an anchor, a Seal candidate): a side's T anchors at the other side's keyset of U, Seal gives it, it is blocked, so U is left out of T, re-keyed, and the re-key's list makes the other side's keyset stale above in turn, for good, or a side leaves U out of T for good (`FD_fork_builder`, `fork_two_below_one_side_first_stops_rotating_spec_crypto_4`). Listed by rotations: the other side's rotation of U lists a side's own keyset, stale to T there (`FD_fork_list`).

## Left out
- **Left out, then followed by report** (spec/crypto.md §4 Reported): left out lasts until M's next keyset reaches the node; sync rotates what is due before it encrypts (notes/engine-lost-access.md Owed role commits), so no eager trigger is needed. M's readers log M's keysets under A's sync IDs only while they hold A's newest epoch, which a leave-out denies them, and a node gone from M no longer reads M's sync IDs: so M's members' stranded reports on A carry M's current `keys.0` commits (notes/sync-access.md, Re-seal and stranded reports; `Engine::holder_keys`, osis-sync `inbox/stranded.rs`). Without them A's admin, gone from M after leaving M out, never learned M's next keyset (`D0_gone_live2` fails with `Notice = FALSE`, as does `D0_order_live`).
- The node's own departures block nothing (`rotation::others`): the departed party is the node, which holds the epoch, and counting them left M out of everything a node removed from M administers (`D0_gone_live_secret`, `own_removal_from_holder_actor_keeps_sealing_it_spec_crypto_4`). Rejected: judging M only while a member (`D0_gone_role`: a node that left M out as a member re-seals it at that keyset once gone).
- **Left out is recorded with its causes** (spec/crypto.md §4 Left out; the record, `Keyset::left_out`). Principle: a record is evidence, not a verdict, and every block has a cause. It says "keyset k of M was blocked here by these commits", and every node re-derives its effect from those commits as it re-derives every other verdict: a cause stops standing only where a node can show what it showed is gone. Model: `LeftOut.tla` (README).
  - Why a record at all: one input of the blocked judgment needs secrets. A node reading M judges M's keyset by its names (inside its history box); a co-writer of A holding none of M's secrets judges it by order, which passes an ignorant keyset made after the departure. Without the record the knowing node left M out, the co-writer re-sealed M at that keyset, and the knowing node rotated A again, once per re-seal for good where no rotator of M re-keys (`LO_nofact` fails `NoUndo`; `co_writer_reseals_no_holder_left_out_spec_crypto_4`, `removal_alone_left_out_stays_out_at_co_writer_spec_crypto_4`). The record carries that one fact; everything else in it is the commits a co-writer needs to judge the rest itself.
  - Why causes are carried: so a node can judge a record void when what it shows is gone (a regrant, an invalidated departure), which a node that never follows M learns only from the carried commits and their evidence. A record whose cause a node lacks counts there: judging it void would re-seal M at a keyset made before the departure (`LO_voidunheld` fails `NoLeak`).
  - What a record does: M is left out at each recorded keyset while one of its causes stands or is not held: that keyset is blocked there by a box (a joint box naming it too), so Seal leaves M out at it. Every other keyset of M is judged by the ordinary rules over held commits, the carried departures included. Nothing about "past" the record: a fork of M made after the departure without knowing it, which the recorder did not hold, is sealed to where the order rule passes it, as any ignorant keyset is (the stated limit, spec/crypto.md §4).
  - Every block clause names its cause, so the rule has no exception: a departure its newest edge write a covering epoch must name; a box or maker departure (no epoch covers it) its holder's edge departure's write where its edge shows it leaving, else the rotation commit of each current epoch it holds a box at or made (the departed party holds that secret; only a rotation ends it; a snapshot's body where the rotation is not held, `members::keyset_commit`); a stale box the superseding keyset's rotation, or the invalid bodies of a keyset held invalid; a taint or keyless box the keyless grant. Tests: `a_box_departure_is_recorded_with_its_epochs_rotation_spec_crypto_4` (the co-writer churn, gone), `a_maker_departure_alone_is_recorded_with_its_rotation_spec_crypto_4`, `a_maker_departures_cause_is_its_rotation_spec_crypto_4`, `a_box_naming_an_invalid_keyset_is_recorded_with_its_rotation_spec_crypto_4`.
  - How a cause stands, by what the commit is: one writing a keyset stands while held, valid or invalid; any other while an edge write of it is a departure's there (not the node's own) or a grant of it is keyless there. Edge writes end by regrant or invalidity, which the node judges itself; a commit writing a keyset as well (a removal's own rotation) stands as a rotation, since a record does not say which write it names, so a regrant ends a record only where the removal rotated nothing. A keyset proves its block by existing: valid it supersedes what it lists or is an epoch a departed party holds; invalid it is a keyset no box may name, and a reader lacking what invalidates it finds it valid, so validity cannot end a record either way (a deep invalid keyset a reader cannot walk to would otherwise be re-sealed through). The cost: a rotation cause later invalidated, or a departed maker regranted, keeps M out until its re-key, one rotation of M (`left_out_on_an_invalidated_rotation_ends_with_one_rekey_spec_crypto_4`; `LO_rot`).
  - The re-key: a node that may rotate M rotates it at the end of a complete pass of A while M is left out of A at a current keyset of M. For an honest record the knowing re-key is due anyway where the rotator reads M; it is not where the rotator holds none of M's secrets and the recorded keyset is ordered after the departure (it judges by order), so this clause makes that rotator act (`LO_nodue` fails `Live`; under the rule it replaces, waiting for "the knowing re-key", `LO_ign_old` fails `Live` too). Only a current recorded keyset is due: re-keying for any counting record would rotate M at every pass while A's epochs keep the copy (`LO_anyrec` fails `HostileEnds`).
  - Records propagate: each rotation of A leaving M out records again what A's current keysets record, causes joined, less each keyset another recorded one descends from; a rotation boxing M records nothing of M. A rotation by a node judging every cause void seals M and so ends the record. A rotator adds its own causes to a keyset already recorded: else a false record naming a cause that stands nowhere hides the honest one, and a co-writer re-seals M there (`LO_noadd` fails `NoUndo`; `a_false_record_hides_no_honest_one_spec_crypto_4`). A copy keeps every cause, held or not: keeping only held ones, a co-writer lacking a cause drops it and re-seals M at the keyset made before the departure (`LO_copyheld` fails `NoUndo`; `left_out_waits_for_a_withheld_cause_spec_crypto_4`).
  - A's keyset does not wait for its causes (Held keyset waits for carried demotions only): copies would carry a cause nobody holds into every later keyset of A, none arriving, and its maker would rotate again at every pass (`LO_wait` fails `NoChurn`; `a_removed_writers_unheld_cause_keeps_no_keyset_from_arriving_spec_crypto_4`).
  - Hostile records: a writer of A may record any keyset of M with any causes. A record counts where a cause stands or is not held, so a false one costs at most one rotation of M per keyset it names, and once its writer is removed and M re-keyed past what it named nothing more (`LO_hostile`: `HostileEnds`; `hostile_record_forces_one_rotation_per_record_spec_crypto_4`, `a_hostile_record_on_a_rotation_ends_with_its_writers_removal_spec_crypto_4`). M boxed again at one node is not enough: a rotator of M still holding the writer's earlier epoch re-keys once for it.
  - A record names at least one cause: a writer records no keyset it holds no cause for (it could count nowhere), and a reader takes a `keys.0` body recording one with none as bad input (spec/wire.md §7; `a_keyset_with_no_cause_is_never_recorded_spec_crypto_4`, `a_record_with_no_cause_is_bad_input_spec_crypto_4`).
  - Rejected: no record, causes carried only (`LO_nofact`: the churn above); a record counting whatever its cause (`LO_inv_old`, `LO_ign_old`: a holder actor locked out of A for good once a cause turns invalid, and a node reading M's keysets by order never re-keys); publishing a keyset's names instead of recording the fact (its names grow with M's membership history and show it to every sealer of A).
  - Built (`rotation::members`: `left_out`, `left_out_at`, `stands`, `rekey_due`; `rotation::blocking` returns the causes its walk read, `departures::departure_causes` a departure's).
  - Not modelled: an invalidator of a cause voided in turn (validity not monotone): a node holding the invalidator but not its own invalidator judges the cause void and may re-seal M, as it judges any departure from A itself (Tail.tla `T_chain` for names). Several causes per record are tested in the engine.

## Box records and carried commits
- **Box records** (spec/crypto.md §4 Stale; Depth0 `RsPf`): every box to a holder actor is recorded where every node that seals A can see it, so a node holding A's keysets but none of A's boxes (an admin of G gone from A) finds A's epoch stale when M moves on (`every_box_is_recorded_beside_it_spec_crypto_4`). `osis_keys` `box_items` fills the record for every recipient, so every boxing path records by construction; the index joins the records of every valid `keys.0` body (`Indexes::sealed`) and `members::box_blocked` reads them beside the boxes. Union over bodies: a later write at a record's key hides nothing, it only adds (`a_false_record_hides_no_honest_one_spec_crypto_4`).
- A false record can only add staleness, which A's next rotation ends, and a writer of A can hand A's secrets out anyway. Only an admin's snapshot covering a record's body drops it from the index (`keys.0` is role-class, never compacted by the build; not defended).
- Records travel with the keyset they key: certificates carry the records at A's newest keysets (`sync_ids::certs::current_keys`); carried commits carry them with any keyset a commit's boxes name, the one way for a keyset below A's newest n (`a_keysets_records_travel_with_it_spec_crypto_4`).
- Cost: a grant or re-seal records its own boxes only (`a_grants_records_do_not_grow_with_the_actor_spec_crypto_4`). A's current `keys.0` commits grow by one per honest holder-actor grant or re-seal at the newest epochs, no more than A's next rotation writes, which resets them; at infinity a thing with thousands of grants to holder actors since its last rotation carries thousands of small commits in each certificate. A hostile writer grows them without bound, as it can with sibling rotations. The newest commit writing more than box records stays among them (`box_records_do_not_displace_the_newest_card_spec_permissions_4`). Rejected: judging by A's roster where no record exists (a rotation per honest grant seen so); rotating A on every holder-actor grant (a full rotation of a large thing per holder-actor grant). What the grant and re-seal records buy is `NoLeakDeep` alone (`D0_rs_off_req`, `D0_pf_off_req` pass the requirement as written without them); their cost in every unit is a tess-call (issues/account-evidence-per-unit.md).
- **Carried** (`rotation::carry`): a forged keyset is judged invalid only by a node holding its body and the removal, so whatever names a keyset logs its rotation commit beside it.
- Index: `Indexes::keysets` (per thing, each valid keyset with its list and KEM key, kept once dropped in `Dropped::keysets`, rebuilt on validity flips); held invalid keysets are looked up on demand (`rotation::members::invalid_writers`, their commits a stale box's causes).

## Carried demotions
**Carried demotions** (spec/crypto.md §4; Depth0 `Travel`; Tess chose this over accepting a wider leak): A, holding M's knowing keyset, holds the demotions it carries, and existing authority does the rest: each later commit of the demoted W on M or A that the demotion's frontier does not list is invalid at A, backdated ones included, so W's keysets of M are held invalid (a stale box) and W's rotations of A establish nothing. This closes the three removed-member leaks (W extending M past its knowing keyset, sealing M at a made-up keyset, backdating a rotation of A), leaving open only what spec/crypto.md §4 states: sealing to a keyset of M made without knowing the removal, until a knowing one arrives (`tests_carried`; planted fault: a keyset carrying nothing leaks in every path). `departures::carried` records a demotion on everything the lowered edge's target reaches, so those below M at any depth come for free.
- Cost: A's nodes, readers included, hold writer and admin demotions inside holder actors; readers' removals travel nowhere. The list is cumulative while the demotions stay in effect (a backdated commit in an earlier demoted window is invalid only by the demotion before it), so its worst case is the membership history of M and below (32 bytes per departed writer or admin per keyset, plus its edge writes under each parent's sync IDs), not the rotation count; a keyset whose carried commits are withheld counts as not arrived.
- A party removing its own route (f removes S from M, rotating M) makes a valid rotation, since a write never demotes its own author: it keeps the epoch it made until another rotator of M acts (`rotation_removing_its_own_route_leaks_nothing_spec_crypto_4`).
- Rejected: a possession proof per holder actor (a rotation counting its author's role through M only where it proves holding M's secret): a writer of M can always make the proof say what it wants, so it closed only the plain path, and with travel it is redundant (what the proofs also did stays, as box records). An unjudged proof as a stale box (`AsOf`): partial, and a cascade left a holder actor out for good. Carrying keyset metadata down the proof chain: stalls, and still needs `AsOf`.

## Destroyed holder actors
**Destroyed holder actors** (spec/crypto.md §4 Destroyed; Tess approved this over keeping a destroyed holder actor's `secrets.0` writable or accepting the leak): `Core::key_holders` puts the frozen holders in the destroyed thing's place (`expand_destroyed`, entering a destroyed thing again only at a greater role, so the result never depends on tid order), and everything about keys reads that one list, so nothing else changes: the destroyed thing's box at a current epoch is a box to a holder without a role, so the thing's admin rotates once and boxes the frozen holders. Key access runs along the same walk (`Core::keyed_holders`, `members::key_role`): the destruction gives no secret a keyless path withheld (`frozen_holder_keyed_at_the_role_of_its_keyed_paths_spec_crypto_4`). Readers meaning the edges on the thing itself use `edge_holders` (the record edge, `leaves_no_admin`, `grant_keyless`, lost's `own_leaving`). A node learning the destroyed thing later learns the destruction with its frozen history (notes/expiry-creation-design.md), so a newcomer admin keys the things whose authority runs through it alike. Model: Depth0 `D0_destroyed*` (controls `D0_destroyed_off`, `D0_destroyed_cycle_old`).
- A timed edge on a destroyed holder actor past its `until` stays a frozen holder for good (Tess chose it; revisit only if timed grants on things that get destroyed turn out common). Rejected: reading `until` at the destruction point (a smaller leak, still one); reading it by the clock (keys read no clock).

## The rotation property's checker
`tests_property::rotation_holds_*` reads a peer's access through holder actors over grants with key access only (`access_at`: counting a keyless grant put a secret opened only through an excused box among those held rightly). Its made-by-a-knowing-rotator check triggers on an epoch whose rotator held the commit that cost the peer the role, read two ways: its departure from that role of the thing itself (`Departure::of_change`), or the edge write, on any thing, after which it last read the role there by no path (`path_loss`; so a peer reading only through holder actors, or left sync-only, is checked too). Keying on the last departure from the thing alone left every party below the thing, and every earlier loss followed by a redundant removal, unchecked.
- It excuses hand-ons and the accepted leaks above, nothing else: a grant's hand-on of what its author held when writing it, including epochs a rotation the grant made first (`Run::handed`), and a handed keyset of a holder actor opening every box to it at that keyset, except a stale box (`Run::stale`: no honest node writes one, and counting it hid a planted Seal at a superseded keyset). A re-seal hands on nothing (a node missing a departure establishes no epoch naming it; excusing it would hide exactly that leak). Also excused: the history box of a later epoch the peer held while a holder in its rotator's view (`Made::carried`: access regained reads earlier content), and a box sealing a holder actor blind at a keyset the order rule passes whose rotation boxes no keyset the sealer held superseded (`Run::excused`; the order rule the spec's, `tail_departure_point`). An epoch's maker is never below it.
- An excuse applies only where such boxes are the peer's one way to the epoch (`Run::states`, per engine call; so a removal's cascade rotates none of the roles the removal rotates on its target, `rotation::Keep`, `a_removal_boxes_none_it_removes_spec_permissions_3`, else it boxed the removed party at a box the removal's rotation then dropped).
- Reach: with holder actors half the seeds have a deep peer. Engine leaks planted (a lowering's rotation boxing the lowered holder; due rotations boxing past holders) fail it with the newest-epoch check off; Seal past a blocked keyset fails only `tests_members` unit tests, not 120 seeds of it; Seal past a stale box one level down fails 4 of seeds 0..1000 and `writer_leaves_out_at_a_stale_box_below_spec_crypto_4`.
