# Account membership: nodes as holders on the ordinary rules
> Summary: Design (not built) for an account as an ordinary independent thing whose members are things holding roles on it, a node's root (minted with the node's owner key) among them: an audit of every mechanism of the built account system against the stolen-node attacks, visible mode (built first), what a removal keeps (the list in the removal, role writes kept only by it, what its writer leaves out, a remover that does not sync a thing, what the user is shown), how a removal reaches other things, hidden mode kept as a shared-key design, why the unit of removal is a key store, sessions, a record of node anonymity as built, and the model to check it.

**Principle: a node is a holder.** An account is an independent thing A; each node acts as a thing of its own (its node root, signed as by the node's owner key) holding a role on A; every event in an account's life (pairing, removal, theft, a fight between nodes) is an ordinary grant, removal, rotation or admin dispute on A, judged by the rules every thing has, with no rule naming accounts: core has no concept of an account (notes/write-path-design.md §5). Where the ordinary rules fall short they are fixed for every holder, never patched for accounts.

Marks: **[checked: ref]** read in the spec or code at that place; **(modelled: ref)** held by that model or hand world of roles-model `paths` as it stands; **(probed)** held by a throwaway run, to be cut into the model (notes/write-path-design.md §6); **(argued)** reasoning here; **(decided)** Tess (notes/handoff.md; issues/actors-one-mechanism.md Answers). Terms: notes/terms.md. The rules themselves (owner keys, paths, the walk, lists, first received, evidence) are notes/write-path-design.md; this note is their use for accounts.

## Assumed of the write-path design
- A commit is signed as a thing by an owner key that thing lists, states its path from that thing through each thing whose role it uses, and is judged along it alone (notes/write-path-design.md, Owner keys and §1).
- A reader of thing T holds, in T's sync unit, the grants and list writes on the paths of the commits on T it holds, and every later write on those hops, each removal whole with its list (notes/write-path-design.md §2).
- A removal reaches another thing where it is evidence of a held write and with that thing's next key rotation; nothing delivers it for its own sake (decided; notes/write-path-design.md §2, How a removal reaches a far thing).

## 1. Audit of the built account system
Built: spec/permissions.md §5.4-§5.5, spec/crypto.md §3 and §9, notes/engine-validity-epochs.md; models roles-model custody and epochs, core/test/tla/rotation Account.tla. Each mechanism: what it answers, then its fate: (a) an ordinary rule already answers it, (b) an ordinary rule with a gap to fix, (d) not needed.

- **Signing chain and epochs.** Answers: which key may sign for P; a removal stops the removed node signing; P shows readers one public key whatever its nodes. Fate (a): a node's root holds admin on A; the removal is an ordinary explicit removal on A, and the removed node's later writes fall by the role timeline and the list [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 steps 2-4, §5.2]. The built limit "a removed node's commits at an unaware sibling's open head count until a merge" [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.5 Carrying] has no counterpart (argued).
- **Seed custody.** Answers: handing the shared signing key and role secrets to the nodes; keeping judgment a function of held commits so a user's switch of side takes. Fate (a): a grant on A boxes A's secrets to the new node's root, a removal rotates A, and things A holds roles on re-key as for any holder's new keyset [checked: spec/permissions.md §3 Required rotation, Holder actors; spec/crypto.md §4]. A secret from an invalid commit is kept for reading only, so a node on the thief's side still holds the honest side's secrets and a switch takes [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Reading-only secrets]. No signing seed exists in visible mode.
- **Sealed inner signature.** Answers: only P's nodes learn which node made each commit. Fate (d): in visible mode the thing signed as is the node's; hidden mode has none (§3).
- **Merges of concurrent rotations.** Fate (a): two admins rotating A at once leave two current epochs; forks stand, holders are re-sealed at each and the next rotation supersedes both [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Forks].
- **Sides by first revocation seen.** Fate (a): the admin dispute: trust set, seeds, first received, switch [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Trust set; notes/disputes.md], with first received stated for the path walk (notes/write-path-design.md §1, First received).
- **`takes`** (a node that took the thief's revocation stands with that side). Fate (a) with gap (b): an honest node H2 on the thief's side writes valid rotations of A boxing X; at honest-side nodes those epochs do not cover X's departure, so they are blocked and re-keyed [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Departures and covering, Encrypting under]. At H2 the honest re-key is a rotation by a node it holds removed; under the withdraw rule as written the two sides of any admin dispute rotate at every complete pass while both stay online. **The rule, for every dispute**: an epoch covering a departure the node's own walk does not apply is the other side's: its secret is kept and read, it is never current, supersedes nothing, and is neither answered nor withdrawn. Each side rotates once; a node whose user switches withdraws its own epoch once and from then encrypts under the epoch of the side it joined (modelled: roles-model `paths`, `Withdraw::SideOnly`). No rule may act on a dispute being settled (notes/principles.md, Forks are permanent).
- **Thing lists, list trees, listings, names.** Answers: with a shared key a removal puts the whole account's history in question. Fate (d): honest nodes' writes are never in question in visible mode, and the removed node's are kept by the ordinary list (§4).
- **Re-adds.** Fate (a): the ordinary re-grant [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.2]. A node coming back from theft is paired as a new thing with a new key: a re-grant of the old one brings back the thief's writes too (argued). The collateral grantee (X paired N5, the remover never received that grant, so the removal drops it and N5's writes since) is lost until a member pairs N5 again: the list alone keeps a role write (§4).
- **Own cover at rotations.** Fate (a): the ordinary removal's own cover [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Own cover, §5.2 Own cover].
- **Cutoff and Carrying.** The cutoff is the list's "received as of T" (a). Carrying (d): it guarded open heads. What remains is ordinary: a writer that compacts before it holds the removal carries the removed node's late write in a valid snapshot, which stands [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.3; modelled: notes/path-model-findings.md 26, the accepted window].
- **Account root D, roster from the chain, seed re-seal.** (a): A's holders are its roster by the role timeline; re-seals are the ordinary re-seal [checked: spec/permissions.md §3 Re-seal]. D itself goes (notes/write-path-design.md §5).
- **Found in the built design** (§7): session-form dots under the account's shared tid let any holder of P's seed sign a commit in a sibling's session with an overlapping range; both commits are invalid [checked: spec/commits.md §4].

## 2. Visible mode (the one built)
- **The account** is an independent thing A, minted by its first node (with that node's root as mint admin, or with an owner key of A's own: the library's choice), no different afterwards from any thing [checked: notes/terms.md, Mint admin]. Whether a thing is an account is the schema's mark on it; nothing below reads it.
- **Members**: each node acts as a **node root**: an ordinary thing minted with the node's owner key, holding a role on A: admin for a node that may pair and remove, write for one that may not (decided). A node wanting two accounts unlinked uses a thing and an owner key for each (decided), so nothing links its activity across them, and a stolen node needs a removal per account.
- **A recovery key** is an owner key on A itself, written down (issues/recovery-redesign.md): it signs as A, so it removes any member and no member removes it. None by default.
- **Pairing**: the new node shows its root (tid and key card) to a member over the pairing connection; the member writes one role commit granting it on A, boxing A's secrets to it [checked: spec/permissions.md §2, a grant carries the grantee's boxes]. From A's secrets the new node opens A's dependents and every box to A. The pairer also hands over, locally, its trust entries for A: a node paired after a backdated fight would otherwise take the walk's side, under which its pairer may be no member (modelled: `a_node_paired_with_its_pairers_trust_entries_is_a_member_at_itself`). Pairing is two ordinary operations, done by a library above core.
- **Removing a node X** is one role commit R by a member H with admin on A: the explicit removal of X's grant, H's list, and the rotation of A's roles, in one commit [checked: spec/permissions.md §3]. Before R, H writes its own covers. What R lists and leaves out, what is lost and how R reaches other things: §4. R's result names the members X had added (decided), those it had removed or lowered, and what H's list left out. Things A holds roles on re-key lazily, as their boxes to A go stale [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Holder actors].
- **A node whose key store was copied, not lost**: its root lists a new key and removes the old one, as itself (notes/write-path-design.md, Owner keys, O6): the node keeps its grants. Where the thief may also hold the root's secrets, remove the root from A instead.
- **A node fight** (X removes H while H removes X) is an admin dispute on A [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Trust set; notes/disputes.md]. Under the plain walk the earlier-stamped removal applies and the later one finds its author without authority; each node sides by first received (a party holds its own write first), is told, and may switch. Two nodes leaving at once leave A with no admin, as a deliberate leave would (decided: notes/write-path-design.md §5, The last admin); a recovery key recovers it.
- **What each reader learns**: for each thing it reads, which node root of A wrote each change, when each was granted and by whom; a removal of a node of A wherever it is evidence of a write the reader holds. Roots differ per account, so nothing links one node's activity across accounts. A reader learns nothing inside A.
- **Per commit**: no epoch (32 B) and no inner signature (95 B) [checked: spec/wire.md §4 fields 14, 15 and Inner signature]; the path adds A's tid (16 B) (argued).

### Stolen node, step by step
Nodes H (honest, online), B (honest, offline), X (stolen: the thief holds X's owner key and every secret X held); readers Bob (reads T, which X wrote) and Carol (reads U, which X never wrote).
1. **H removes X** (R at t1). At H, and wherever R is held: X's commits that neither R nor an addendum names fall [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.2]; X opens no epoch of A made after R, and things A holds roles on re-key before their next write (argued).
2. **X writes T after t1, honestly stamped**: X held no role at its key, so invalid wherever R is held. Where R is not yet held it counts until R arrives (with T's next key rotation by a writer holding R, or by a node holding both); a writer of T that compacts meanwhile carries it for good [modelled: notes/path-model-findings.md 26, accepted]. Carol's unit, where X never wrote, holds no R until X's first write there meets a node holding R (notes/write-path-design.md §2, The gap).
3. **X backdates writes before t1**: named by nobody, so invalid at R's holders. A witness lying about receipt makes content count, within what the witness could write itself (§4).
4. **X backdates a removal of H and B before t1**: nodes that held R before it arrived trust R (first received), and R's seeds drop X's removals. B, coming back with no order to go by, takes the walk's side unless a node hands it trust entries; a thief feeding B first wins B until B's user switches [checked: notes/disputes.md, Detection and sides]. What B encrypts meanwhile X reads: the cost of the wrong default, accepted.
5. **X backdates a grant to a made-up thing Z, then Z removes H, or grants H anything**: R's seeds drop X's grant of Z, so Z holds nothing and no write of Z applies (nothing is exempt from a removal's drops). If H had received X's grant of Z before R, it is listed, Z stays a member and must be removed too: the accepted accomplice hole [checked: notes/disputes.md]; the member list shows Z.
6. **X rotates A boxing itself, backdated**: an unlisted write of X, invalid at R's holders, its secrets reading-only [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Reading-only secrets].
7. **X equivocates in its own old sessions** (overlapping counters): X's new commit is unlisted and invalid, and the overlap rule (spec/commits.md §4) voids with it X's pre-theft writes on those counters that R listed: a hole that stays (issues/overlap-among-valid-commits.md).
8. **B's unsent writes** made offline: never in question. X's pre-theft content that B received counts by B's addenda; its role writes only where H's list names them (§4).
9. **X withholds R from Carol**: availability only [checked: spec/requirements.md, Threat model].
10. **X removes H, honestly stamped, before H acts**; H receives it and removes X. H's node leaves X's removal out of R's list (§4, What a list leaves out), so R's seeds drop it. The same for X removing whoever paired H with a list that drops H's grant.
11. **X, heard before R, aims a write at H's own standing**: a grant to H with an `until` just past the removal, a demotion of H, an end on H by a made-up thing X granted. H's node leaves out each write with which R would not apply or H's path would end sooner (probed: notes/write-path-design.md §1, L3); backdated and heard after R, each is unlisted and falls with the rest.
12. **X answers with a removal of H that lists nothing**, or Z does: its drops take its own author's grant, so it is void: it drops nothing and raises no dispute (modelled).
13. **A key removed long before**: the thief also holds the key of N5, which X had honestly removed, and backdates N5's removal of B to before that removal. Every node that held X's removal of N5 before the backdated write arrived trusts it, and its seeds drop the write before it acts (probed: notes/write-path-design.md §1, First received; issues/removed-key-backdated-drop.md).
14. **X adds an owner key of its own to A**: refused while A lists a key (only an owner key writes the list). Where A lists none, the add is an admin write of X: listed by R if H had received it (the thief then holds A for good: remove the node at once, or keep a recovery key on A), dropped by R otherwise.

## 3. Hidden mode (a design, not built)
Kept for identities where privacy is the point and edits are few (decided). The same design with one shared owner key K on A itself, held by every node of the account: every commit is signed as A, and nothing says which node made it.
- **Distributing K**: K reaches a new node over the pairing connection. The **node list** (each node's KEM key) is a dependent of A read only by A's nodes.
- **Swap on theft**: a node holding K makes K1 and writes, as A, (1) a list write signed by K adding K1, then (2) a list write signed by K1 removing K, with its list (notes/write-path-design.md, Owner keys, O6) and the rotation of A. It boxes K1 to every node on the node list but the stolen one. One commit cannot do both: K removing itself is a self-removal, which audits nothing, so the thief's backdated writes under K would still count.
- **What the swap vouches for**: every commit signed by K that the swapper received: K is the whole account. Its size is the account's, not the node's.
- **Costs against visible**, each a property:
  - Every removal puts every node's writes and grants in question, with bytes and work in proportion.
  - An honest node's unsent work falls until that node vouches for its content under K1 by addenda; its role writes it makes again (§4).
  - Two honest swaps at once dispute each other, and the account is split until a user picks. In visible mode two removals of one node conflict with nothing.
  - No sessions: content uses commit form, since session dots under a shared key are forgeable by any holder (§1, Found in the built design).
  - A thief's writes before the removal cannot be told from the owner's.
- **What a swap shows**: no signature says which node made a swap, but a node not boxed the new key knows it was cut, so insiders judge a swap by whom it removes.
- **No design can tell a thief from an honest node**: with two nodes removing each other the answer is outside osis in visible mode, hidden mode and the built design alike (first received, the user switches).
- **Not known to be much simpler than what is built** (Tess): what goes is the parallel layer (chain, epochs, custody, account sides); vouching for the whole account's history at each removal is inherent to hiding and stays.
- **Nothing visible builds may rule it out**: no "a thing is signed as by one node" rule (own commits are known from the store; commit form allowed everywhere).

## 4. What a removal keeps
One rule for every removal of every holder and every key: **a write of the removed party is kept when the remover's list names it, or, for plain content, a remaining writer's addendum says it had received it by the removal**. A role write the list leaves out is discarded whoever else received it (decided, Tess: issues/role-vouch-contested.md). The rest is discarded, since nothing shows it was made before the removal [checked: spec/requirements.md Permissions]. The removal itself is judged in the world those statements make: once, at its key, and void where its own drops would take its author's authority (notes/write-path-design.md §1, Each lowering is judged once).
- **Who may keep what**: the keeper remains (it holds the needed right after the removal), and the write is another thing's. No reader's rule asks whom a kept write benefits or which edge it sets.
- **Nobody vouches for its own commit**: an addendum names no commit signed as the thing that signs it (modelled: `no_own_addendum`). A second writer colluding with it still can: nothing checks receipt.
- **Nothing is kept on a user's word** (decided): a removed node's writes that no remaining node had received are untrusted, with no exception.

### The list
- **Form**: the removal carries its list, as built: the commit IDs of what its writer had received through the removed hop, inline or linked, part of the commit and of every copy of it (decided direction, Tess's first candidate; notes/write-path-design.md §2, E4). A reader holding the removal holds the whole list, so a role write is listed or not at every reader alike and nothing waits on a part of a list.
- **What a list leaves out** (notes/write-path-design.md §1, L2 and L3; modelled but the `end` clause, which is probed): a remover names what it had received through the removed hop; of the removed party's role writes it keeps each, in the order it received them, only where the removal still applies with it and the remover's own path ends no sooner. So the thief's removal of the remover, or of whoever paired it, and its end date on the remover's admin are left out and R's seeds drop them; every other lowering X made is listed as received (decided: automatic), so a removal X made long before the theft stays made; the removal's result names what was kept and what was left out, and undoing one is an ordinary grant.
  - No reader checks this: every node reads the list as written, and a list keeping what unmakes its removal voids it. A remover that leaves out an honest earlier write has written a contest of it, which only nodes trusting that removal follow.
  - **In the order received, not in key order** (modelled: `a_remover_keeps_what_it_received_first`): the thief stamps as it likes, but cannot make the remover have received its writes before the honest history.
- **Size**: a removal costs in proportion to what it lists [checked: spec/requirements.md, Cost]. A list names only commits the remover still holds; forgotten ones are carried by its own cover [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Own cover]. Per aspect the remover may cover by an own cover in place of listing, except on aspects whose history is kept. A removal whose role commits alone pass the list bound cannot be written, as built.
- **Why commit IDs and not a tip per writer**: the thief can sign a commit overlapping a covered range; with counters both are covered and the overlap rule voids both (argued).

### Role writes: only the list keeps them
Decided (Tess, issues/role-vouch-contested.md): a role write of X that R's list does not name is dropped, whoever else received it. Two worlds can hold the same commits and need opposite answers, so no rule over the held set can keep it safely. A share, pairing or removal X made shortly before its removal that the remover had not received is undone until someone redoes it as their own write; a node in sync when it removes lists it. Where a node held such a write in R's window, the removal's result says so (the notice; not built).
- **What follows for the writes made under a fallen grant**: granted again, the grantee holds the role from the new grant; its earlier content is valid by the re-grant rule (modelled: `Regrant::FirstChained`), its earlier role writes stay dropped.
- **Addenda reach admin-only content**: an addendum by a witness holding admin at its key vouches for X's deltas on admin-only aspects that are not role-class (built: spec/permissions.md §5.2 Addenda).

### How a removal reaches other things
Decided (Tess): no eager delivery. R is evidence wherever a held write's path uses the removed hop, so any node holding R and such a write logs R with it; a thing's next key rotation by a writer holding R carries it; and R travels under A's own sync ID and those whose keys open it (notes/write-path-design.md §2). Nothing is logged for its own sake, nothing is owed per unit, and no node holds a unit for an errand.
- **What that leaves**: in a thing X could write and no remaining member writes, whose readers are served only by stores, X's later writes show until a node holding R syncs it: an open bug in an issue, not something built around (decided). Wanted if a model shows it simple: a reader holding both depositing R there (notes/write-path-design.md §2, D4).
- **Cost**: a removal costs nothing under a unit until a write it judges is held there, and leaves with the last of them.

### A remover that does not sync a thing
Nodes of one account hold different things. On a thing T the remover H does not hold, R lists nothing, so X's content there stands only by witnesses, a writer of T that had received it (addenda), and its role writes not at all. Where X was the only such party among those that held T, nothing honest can save them after the fact.
- **Core removes when told and never waits** (decided): no hear-out operation is tied to removal. A node still in its owner's hands is synced before it is removed (a general "wait until every connected node is fully synced" behind a sync button: UX); a lost or stolen node is removed at once. Everything the thief writes before R's `ts` that H collects is blessed with the rest, so waiting lengthens the thief's window.

### Nobody received it
- Where no remaining party had received a write of X before R, nobody honest can tell a real write from a backdated one. Core discards it and tells the app: per removal, the commits of the removed node that arrived after it and that nothing names, with the thing, the time each claims, and whether it is content, a grant (to whom) or another role write.
- **Nothing brings it back** (decided): no addendum is written on a user's word. A grant the removed node made that nobody received is made again by a member, and counts from the new grant.
- What the user is told: these were written by the removed node and seen by none of your other nodes or the thing's other members before it was removed; they may be from before it was lost, or made by whoever has it; they are discarded.

### Other gaps the audit found
- A holder of a removed node's key voiding its listed writes by overlapping their sessions (§2, step 7): open, issues/overlap-among-valid-commits.md.
- A thing with no owner key taking its first from an admin a later removal drops (§2, step 14; issues/owner-key-first-attach.md).

## 5. Why the unit of removal is a key store
- A thing signed as by one owner key is the unit of removal, so it should be as fine as what can be lost on its own: a key store. Coarser (a shared key) and a removal cannot tell members' writes apart (§3). Finer (a key per session or commit from one store) and stealing the store steals them all, so a removal must cover every key that store made or can make, which needs them linked to it: by a visible root (which is then the unit) or by a shared key (hiding again) (argued).
- A root per account (§2) is finer than a node only across accounts, where removals are separate anyway.
- A pseudonym per node per thing needs a grant on A for each, written by something readers of T cannot link to the node: a shared key. So it is hidden mode's cost again, per thing (argued).
- Per node root, paid at pairing and removal: a grant on A with its boxes (over 1 KB per role), the grant travelling to readers of what it writes, a box at each later rotation of A, and at removal R with its list wherever it is evidence. Against that, each commit drops 127 B (§2).
- **Large end**: thousands of nodes as members of one A cost a box per member at every removal's rotation; nest instead [checked: spec/requirements.md, Generality: nest rosters]. Thin clients do not count: a client's thing is minted with the account as admin and holds ordinary grants, the node signing for it [checked: notes/client-port.md §3]; its path is the node root, A, the client's thing.

## 6. Sessions
A session is one run of a node, a random nonce under the author's tid, with counters in memory only [checked: spec/crdt.md §2.1]. Its tid is the thing the node signs as, and only a holder of a key that thing lists can write in it. It stays a separate idea: a writer with durable counters would keep a counter per aspect it ever wrote and lose it with every released thing. Sessions do not help removals (§4: a list still names IDs). What they save is compact dots and one list entry per run in place of a 32 B source per commit. Kept as built, keyed to the thing signed as; commit form for a thing more than one key store signs as.

## 7. Node anonymity as it was built
Kept on purpose (Tess) so a later look at hiding nodes starts ahead; describes the design spec/permissions.md §5.4-§5.5 and spec/crypto.md §9 hold while they stand.
- **What it is**: an account is a key actor whose signing key changes by rotations of its signing chain (a DAG of epochs); each node holds every seed and signs as the account; a sealed inner signature (node tid and signature, 95 B, under a key from the epoch's seed) tells only the account's nodes who signed; a removal is a rotation (header shows only `revokes`) whose seed is boxed to the rest; commits at a revoked epoch count only if named by the rotation's thing lists (one leaf per thing in a blinded list tree), a later listing by the node that made them, or a writer's addendum, each by the cutoff (received before the epoch's earliest closer); honest concurrent rotations are joined by deterministic merges; each node sides by the first revocation it sees, reading who minted what from inner signatures; own covers carry forgotten writes.
- **Machinery**: two layers beside the role timeline (the chain: placing, rejects, following, standing, revocations taken, roster, heads, preferred head; the epoch rule: revoked, cutoff, closers that end, carrying, listings, answers), seed custody and re-seals, merge certificates, account sides apart from admin disputes; models roles-model custody and epochs, Account.tla; engine `flows/account.rs`, `custody.rs`, `lists.rs`, `signing.rs`.
- **Bytes**: per commit an epoch (32 B) and an inner signature (95 B) [checked: spec/wire.md §4]; per rotation seed boxes to every node and thing lists naming every commit of the whole account at the closed epochs; per node per rotation a listing per thing it wrote; rotation headers logged under every sync ID the account acts in.
- **Trade-offs it bought**: readers never learn node count, which node wrote what, or when nodes come and go (sessions leak runs, and connections show node identities). Paid for by: every removal puts every node's history in question; offline honest nodes' writes fall until they list them; a removed node's commits at an unaware sibling's head count until a merge; accounts are not ordinary holders (no grants on them, an account root standing in).
- **Hole**: session-form dots under the shared tid let a holder of the seed void a sibling's write by overlapping its session (§1).
- **Rejected inside that design** (notes/engine-validity-epochs.md, Rejected designs): removal and rotation apart, one account-wide list, per-node subkeys certified to readers, group signatures.
- **For a later look**: the costs come from one fact: with a shared key, writes cannot be told apart by signer, only by time, so every removal must vouch for everything. Any hiding design inherits that unless something only insiders read tells writes apart (the inner signature), and then sides differ inside and outside.

## 8. Model plan
roles-model `paths` is the oracle of this note and of notes/write-path-design.md §1; what it must be cut to before any build is listed there (§6, What a model must check before building). It holds, as it stands (notes/path-model-findings.md; core/test/roles-model/README.md, Paths): role writes kept by the list alone; addenda written by the state rule; nobody vouching for its own commit; pairing with the pairer's trust entries; the dispute's rotations; a removal judged in the world its list makes with the writers' leaving out, each lowering judged once at its key, a void one having no effect.
- **Instances**: A with members H (honest, removes), B (honest, offline, holds a thing T2 that H does not), X (stolen), N5 (paired by X, its grant received by B and not by H; or removed by X before the theft, its key the thief's) and N6 (paired by X, its grant received by nobody); a co-admin of T2 in another account; readers of T (X wrote it) and of U (X never wrote it).
