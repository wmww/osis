# Account membership: signers on the ordinary rules
> Summary: Design (not built) for an account as an ordinary independent thing whose members are signers: an audit of every mechanism of the built account system against the stolen-node attacks, visible mode (a key per node) and hidden mode (one shared key) over the ordinary role rules, the fixes the ordinary removal needs (a list per thing, no size limit, nothing kept per commit), why a signer is a node, sessions, a record of node anonymity as built, and the model to check it. Open questions: issues/actors-one-mechanism.md.

**Principle: a node is a holder.** An account is an independent thing A; each signer is a key holding a role on A; every event in an account's life (pairing, removal, theft, a fight between nodes) is an ordinary grant, removal, rotation or admin dispute on A, judged by spec/permissions.md §5.1-§5.2 and spec/crypto.md §4 with no rule naming accounts. Visible and hidden differ only in how many nodes hold each signer. Where the ordinary rules fall short, they are fixed for every holder, never patched for accounts.

Marks: **[checked: ref]** read in the spec or code at that place; **(argued)** reasoning here, not yet checked by a model or test. Terms: notes/terms.md.

## Assumed of the write-path design
- A commit states its path from its signer through each independent thing whose role it uses (signer, A, then any holder above), and is judged along it alone.
- A reader of thing T holds, as part of T's sync unit, the grants on the paths of the commits on T it holds, and every later edge write on those grants (so a removal reaches every reader that holds the grant it ends).
- R, a role commit on A, can reach a reader of T as evidence with the reader opening only what it needs (its header, the edge write on the grant, and the part of R's list about T).
- Whether A's private things are its dependents or a separate thing does not change anything here.

## 1. Audit of the built account system
Built: spec/permissions.md §5.4-§5.5, spec/crypto.md §3 and §9, notes/engine-validity-epochs.md; models roles-model custody and epochs, core/test/tla/rotation Account.tla. Each mechanism: what it answers, then its fate under the target: (a) an ordinary rule already answers it, (b) an ordinary rule with a gap to fix, (c) a requirement the target misses, (d) not needed.

- **Signing chain and epochs.** Answers: which key may sign for P now; a removal stops the removed node signing; P shows readers one public key whatever its nodes. Target: (a) a node's key is a key actor holding admin on A; the removal is an ordinary explicit removal on A, and the removed key's later writes fall by the role timeline and the frontier [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 steps 2-4, §5.2]. The removed node's writes after the removal never count at a node holding it, so the built limit "a removed node's commits at an unaware sibling's open head count until a merge" [checked: §5.5 Carrying] disappears (argued).
- **Seed custody** (seeds boxed to the roster; custody follows holding). Answers: handing the shared signing key and role secrets to the nodes; keeping judgment a function of held commits so a user's switch of side takes. Target: (a) for secrets: a grant on A boxes A's secrets to the new key, a removal rotates A, and things A holds roles on re-key as for any holder actor's new keyset [checked: spec/permissions.md §3 Required rotation, Holder actors; spec/crypto.md §4]. Custody following holding has its ordinary twin: a secret from an invalid commit is kept for reading only, so a node on the thief's side still holds the honest side's secrets and a switch takes [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Reading-only secrets] (argued that this suffices; the model must check "a switch takes"). No signing seed exists in visible mode.
- **Sealed inner signature.** Answers: only P's nodes learn which node minted each rotation and commit, so they can side by identity (a removal hits the removed node's rotations), name their own commits, and keep their account's sides apart from outsiders'. Target: (d) in visible mode, the signer is the node. In hidden mode it is the one thing the bare shared key cannot do, and its absence is costed in §3: insiders cannot tell an honest swap from a thief's.
- **Merges of concurrent rotations.** Answers: two nodes rotating at once fork the signing chain, and outsiders seal to one head, so the heads must be joined, deterministically, without a designated merger. Target: (a) two admins rotating A at once leave two current epochs; forks stand, holders are re-sealed at each and the next rotation supersedes both [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Forks]. No commit is signed at an epoch, so nothing else forks.
- **Sides by first revocation seen; answers to forks.** Answers: a stolen node fighting back (revoking honest devices, forking) has no right answer; each node sides, warns, lets its user switch. Target: (a) the admin dispute: trust set, seeds, first received, switch [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Trust set; notes/disputes.md]. A removed node's writes need no side at all (they fall by the frontier); only its removals of honest nodes make a dispute. Readers are told by holding both writes, which the remover's logging duty brings them [checked: spec/permissions.md §3, the node that removes a holder logs the removal under every sync ID the removed party could read].
- **`takes`** (a device that took the thief's revocation stands with that side). Answers: an honest node on the thief's side keeps boxing the thief, and the honest side must not follow and merge its rotations. Target: (a) with one gap to check. An honest node H2 on the thief's side writes valid rotations of A boxing X; at honest-side nodes those epochs do not cover X's departure, so they are blocked and re-keyed [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Covers, Encrypting under, the knowing re-key]. Gap (b): at H2 the honest re-key is a rotation by a node it holds removed, which it withdraws by rotating again [checked: spec/crypto.md §4, withdraws], so two sides of any admin dispute may rotate at every complete pass for as long as both stay online. The built account found the same loop and bounded it ("one answer per line", notes/engine-validity-epochs.md, Answers and the cutoff); under the target it falls on the ordinary withdraw rule, which must answer it for every dispute (to model; issue to open with the model's result).
- **Thing lists, list trees, listings, names.** Answers: with a shared key a removal puts the whole account's history in question, so the remover names everything it received at the closed epochs, cut per thing so a reader of one thing sees only its part (blinded tree), and every honest node names its own later (listings). Target: listings (d): honest nodes' writes are never in question in visible mode. The per-thing cut (b): the ordinary removal's frontier is one list over every thing (§4). Names are the engine's index of these lists: gone with them.
- **Re-adds** (a re-added node's own commits from before its re-add count by its own listing). Target: (a) the ordinary re-grant revalidates a holder's writes from the first point it held the role, those a frontier left out included [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.2, the re-grant rule]. Two notes. A node coming back from theft must be paired under a new key: a re-grant of the old key brings back the thief's writes too (today's re-add does the same, since the thief's commits carry that node's inner signature) (argued). Gap (b), the collateral grantee: X paired N5 (granted it on A), the remover never received that grant, so R's audit drops it; the user re-grants N5, but N5's writes between X's grant and the re-grant stay invalid, since in the settled walk N5 first holds the role at the re-grant. Scenario: X pairs N5 offline, N5 writes a week, X is stolen, H removes X; N5's week is lost. Fix: count the re-grant from the holder's first chained grant (spec/permissions.md §4 Chained writes: a write stays chained when a later audit drops it), not its first applied one. This applies to every holder whose grant fell with its granter (argued; to model).
- **Own cover at rotations.** Answers: a forgotten commit cannot be listed, so the remover vouches for its live writes by a snapshot. Target: (a) the ordinary removal's own cover [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Own cover, §5.2 Own cover].
- **Cutoff and Carrying.** Answers: what a lister may name (received before the earliest closer), and no snapshot lends a write standing it lacks. Target: the cutoff is the ordinary frontier's "received as of T" (a). Carrying (d): it guarded open heads, which do not exist. What remains is ordinary and accepted: a writer that compacts before it holds the removal carries the removed node's late write in a valid snapshot, which stands [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.3]; the thief's window is until each writer holds R.
- **Account root D, roster from the chain, seed re-seal.** (a) A's holders are its roster by the role timeline; re-seals are the ordinary re-seal [checked: spec/permissions.md §3 Re-seal]. Whether D merges into A: write-path.
- **Found in passing (applies to the built design, recorded in §7):** session-form dots under the account's shared tid let any holder of P's seed sign a commit in a sibling's session (its nonce is in every header) with an overlapping range; both commits are invalid [checked: spec/commits.md §4, two commits of one session overlapping]. A thief whose overlapping commit an honest lister received before the removal kills the sibling's write for good (argued). notes/crdt.md (Second dot form) says commit form is for shared keys, but spec/crdt.md §2.1 has every node of an account write session form under the account's tid.

What the simple picture misses (each argued above, to model):
1. A key swap in hidden mode is two commits (the old key grants the new, the new removes the old with a frontier), not a self-removal: a self-removal audits nothing, so the thief's backdated writes under the old key would still count [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1, a self-removal audits nothing].
2. Two honest swaps at once do not leave "two admins, nothing to merge": the earlier-stamped removal's audit drops the other swap's grant, and each node trusts its own removal, so the honest nodes dispute each other (§3).
3. A removed node granted by nothing a reader holds the removal of can still write where readers lack R, unless R reaches every unit the node could read; that is what Q1 in the issue asks.
4. A new node paired after a backdated fight takes the walk's side, under which its pairer is not a member, so it is not one either; pairing must hand over the pairer's trust entries for A (local, over the pairing connection).
5. The collateral grantee and the dispute rotation loop above.

## 2. Visible mode
- **The account** is an independent thing A, minted by its first node's key N0 (its mint admin, no different from any other admin afterwards [checked: notes/terms.md, Mint admin]). A is minted dependent and becomes independent at its first grant; until then it derives from N0's key, whose secrets never rotate [checked: spec/crypto.md §3 Key actor], which matters only to a one-node account, lost with its node anyway.
- **Members**: each node's signer is a key actor (its tid commits to its key) holding a role on A: admin for a node that may pair and remove, write for one that may not (a kiosk: it cannot start a fight). A recovery key is another member signer held offline. A node in several accounts holds one signer per account, derived from its seed (argued: the unit of loss is still the node, and signatures do not link the accounts; issue, Questions for Tess).
- **Pairing**: the new node shows its key card to a member over the pairing connection; the member writes one role commit granting it on A, boxing A's secrets to it (with history where it is to read A's past) [checked: spec/permissions.md §2, a grant carries the grantee's boxes]. From A's secrets it opens A's dependents and every box to A. The pairer also hands over, locally, its trust entries for A (§1, item 4).
- **Removing a node X** is one role commit R by a member H with admin on A: the explicit removal of X's grant, with H's frontier, and the rotation of A's roles that it requires, in one commit [checked: spec/permissions.md §3, the removal and its rotation are one role commit]. Before R, H writes its own covers (§4). R's frontier lists, as H received them by R's `ts`: X's role-class and admin-only commits on A and on every thing A reaches where X's class falls; for the demotion below write, X's commits on every aspect of those things and those acting for them; the purge requests resting on X's standing; the role commits of H's chain [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Frontier, Listed]. Every writer of each such thing that holds R writes an addendum for X's plain commits it received that R does not list [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.2 Addenda, Writing]. Things A holds roles on re-key lazily, as their boxes to A go stale [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Holder actors]. H logs R under every sync ID X could read [checked: spec/permissions.md §3]. Who can write R: any member with admin on A, and only such (argued: exactly the ordinary rule).
- **What a reader of T needs to judge X's writes**: X's grant on A and A's path to T (already held for X's earlier commits), R, R's list for T (§4), and H's grant on A for R's authority. Nothing about A's other members unless they wrote T (argued, given the assumptions above).
- **A node fight** (X removes H while H removes X) is an admin dispute on A [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Trust set; notes/disputes.md]. Trusting R seeds out every unlisted edge write X made on A and what it reaches before R, so X's backdated removals, grants and rotations go with it; nodes side by first received, are told, and may switch.
- **What each reader learns**: for each thing it reads, which of A's nodes wrote each change, when each was granted and by whom, and, linking keys across things, one node's activity across all it reads. Each removal of a node of A, with its remover, reaches the readers of everything that node could read (Q1). A reader learns nothing of nodes that never wrote what it reads, and nothing inside A.
- **Per commit**: no epoch (32 B) and no inner signature (95 B) [checked: spec/wire.md §4 fields 14, 15 and Inner signature]; the path adds A's tid (15 B) (argued, given the write-path design).

### Stolen device, step by step
Nodes H (honest, online), B (honest, offline), X (stolen: the thief holds X's key and every secret X held); readers Bob (reads T, which X wrote) and Carol (reads U, which X never wrote).
1. **H removes X** (R at t1). At H, Bob and Carol (R logged under every sync ID X could read): X's commits unlisted by R fall [checked: §5.2]; X opens no epoch of A made after R, and things A holds roles on re-key before their next write (argued).
2. **X writes T after t1, honestly stamped**: X held no role at its key, so invalid wherever R is held [checked: §5.2]. Where R is not yet held, it counts until R arrives; a writer of T that compacts meanwhile carries it for good [checked: §5.3].
3. **X backdates writes before t1**: not listed, so invalid at R's holders [checked: §5.1 Frontier, a write is covered iff listed].
4. **X backdates a removal of H and B before t1**: a dispute. Nodes holding R first trust it, and its seeds drop X's removals [checked: §5.1 Trust set, Seeds]. B, coming back, sides by what reached it first; a thief feeding B first wins B until B's user switches [checked: notes/disputes.md, Detection and sides]. B on X's side rotates A boxing X; honest nodes, finding that epoch not covering X's departure, re-key [checked: spec/crypto.md §4]. What B encrypts meanwhile, X reads: the cost of the wrong default, accepted [checked: notes/disputes.md, Decided].
5. **X backdates a grant to a made-up key Z, then Z removes H**: R's seeds drop X's grant of Z, so Z holds nothing and its removal applies nothing [checked: §5.1 Seeds: the unlisted writes by M or authors reaching M]. If H had received X's grant of Z before R, Z stays a member and H must remove Z too: the accepted accomplice hole [checked: notes/disputes.md, Hole: an accomplice that is a current admin raises nothing]; in visible mode H's member list shows Z, so the user sees it.
6. **X rotates A boxing itself, backdated**: an unlisted write of X, invalid at R's holders, its secrets reading-only [checked: spec/crypto.md §4].
7. **X equivocates in its own old sessions** (overlapping counters): with frontiers listing commit IDs, X's new commit is unlisted and invalid; the overlap rule must count only otherwise valid commits, or X kills its own pre-theft writes that R listed (gap (b), argued; spec/commits.md §4 does not say).
8. **B's unsent writes** made offline: never in question; they count when B syncs. X's pre-theft writes B received count by B's addenda [checked: §5.2 Addenda].
9. **X withholds R from Carol**: availability only; R reaches her by any other route [checked: spec/requirements.md, Threat model].

## 3. Hidden mode
The same design with one shared signer K as A's member, held by every node of the account; K is a key actor, so it changes only by replacement [checked: spec/crypto.md §3, `keys.0`: a key actor changes keys only by being replaced].
- **Distributing K**: K's seed reaches a new node over the pairing connection. The **node list** (each node's KEM key) is a dependent of K, so only K's holders read it: nodes box a new key to each other without a roster anyone else sees.
- **Swap on theft**: a node holding K mints K1 and writes (1) a commit signed by K granting K1 admin on A, then (2) a commit signed by K1 removing K explicitly, with its frontier and the rotation of A. It boxes K1's seed to every node on the list but the stolen one, and mints the next node list under K1. One commit cannot do both: a commit has one signer, and K removing itself is a self-removal, which audits nothing (§1, item 1).
- **What the removal vouches for**: every commit signed by K on everything A reaches that the swapper received, since K is the whole account at every key the stolen node held. Its size is the account's, not the node's.
- **Honest nodes' unsent writes**: an offline node's plain content under K falls and is saved by its own addenda under K1 once it has K1 (it counts its own commits as received) [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.2 Addenda]. Its role-class writes are not saved by addenda; its client writes them again under K1 (argued: grants and removals restate state, so writing them again is harmless).
- **Two honest swaps at once** (K→K1 by H1, K→K2 by H2): the earlier-stamped removal's audit drops the other's grant (unlisted, ordered before it); each node trusts its own removal first, so H1 and H2 dispute each other until a user picks, and both hold both keys [checked: §5.1 steps 4, Trust set; argued for the outcome]. The built design merges them.
- **A thief's swap** (K→Kt, Kt boxed to honest nodes or not) is indistinguishable to insiders from an honest one: no signature tells nodes apart. A node receiving it first sides with it. Built today, a thief's re-key that revokes no one is undone by the honest removal (the removal hits the rotations its node minted); here every swap is a removal of K, decided first-received. And the dispute the user sees is "K1 or Kt", which names no device.
- **Insiders not telling nodes apart**: content commits must use commit form; session dots under a shared signer are forgeable by any holder (§1, Found in passing).
- **Costs, as properties**: a swap puts the whole account's held history on everything it writes in question (bytes and work in proportion); offline honest nodes' writes fall until they vouch for them; honest concurrent swaps split the account until a user chooses; a thief's swap reads as honest; disputes are opaque to the user. Adding a sealed node signature on swap commits alone would let insiders tell swaps apart, but brings back sides that differ between insiders and outsiders, the carve-out the built design has (argued).
- **Building it later**: nothing visible builds needs to change, provided visible bakes in no "a signer is one node" rule: own commits known from the store, not the signer; the overlap rule only among valid commits; commit form allowed everywhere. Hidden then adds only the node list as a dependent of K, the two-commit swap, and the client's re-writing of fallen role writes (argued).

## 4. The ordinary removal's gaps
- **A frontier read by readers of one thing** [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Frontier: one list of commit IDs over every aspect in question, on every thing the demoted authority gave write on]. A reader of T fetches the whole list (up to `MAX_LIST_BYTES`, about 1.9M IDs [checked: spec/wire.md §2]) to judge X's few writes on T. It learns how many commits X made on things it cannot read, and, matching IDs, which of the things it does read X wrote. Commit IDs reveal no content. **Form**: the removal's header carries a list root; the list is a tree with one leaf per thing at a blinded position (the built list tree, spec/permissions.md §5.5 Rotation's list, depth fixed so it hides the number of things); a leaf names X's commits on that thing or acting for it; a reader holds R's header and its own things' leaves with their proofs. One form for both built lists.
- **No size where a removal cannot be written** [checked: §5.1, a removal whose role-class, admin-only and chain-certificate commits alone pass the bound cannot be written]. With a leaf per thing as a chunk tree, the bound falls on each wire object, never on the list; the removal costs in proportion to what it lists [checked: spec/requirements.md, Cost: removing a holder costs in proportion to its edits the other writers still hold]. Moving plain content into the remover's addenda stays as an option, not a requirement.
- **Nothing kept per commit for good.** A leaf names only commits the remover still holds; forgotten ones are carried by its own cover [checked: §5.1 Own cover]. Per aspect the remover may also cover by an own cover in place of listing (a performance switch between two correct strategies: list when X's held commits on the aspect are smaller than its state, snapshot otherwise, notes/arbitrary-values.md, the performance-switch class), except on aspects whose history is kept, where X's commits are listed so kept history keeps them. A node keeps a leaf only while it holds a commit the leaf names, as it keeps another actor's chain only while a held thing needs it [checked: spec/permissions.md §4, the last item]. So a leaf entry lives as long as the commit it names, and a node writing for 20 years keeps per removal only what it then held of the removed node (argued).
- **Why not a tip per writer**: a visible signer is sequential, so a removal could name one counter per (session, aspect). But the thief can sign a commit overlapping a covered range; with counters it is inside the range, both commits are covered, and the overlap rule voids both, killing the pre-theft writes. Telling the listed commit from the forgery needs a digest per commit or the IDs. IDs, bounded as above, are the simpler of the two (argued).

## 5. Why a signer is a node, never finer
Confirmed, with one correction.
- A signer is the unit of removal, so it should be as fine as what can be lost on its own: a key store. Coarser (a shared key) and a removal cannot tell members' writes apart (§3). Finer (a key per session or commit from one store) and stealing the store steals them all, so a removal must cover every key that store made or can make, which needs them linked to it: by a visible root (which is then the signer) or by a shared key (hiding again) (argued).
- Correction: a node in several accounts may hold one signer per account (§2). That is finer than a node only across accounts, where removals are separate anyway.
- A pseudonym per node per thing needs a grant on A for each, written by something readers of T cannot link to the node: a shared key. So it is hidden mode's cost again, per thing (argued).
- Per signer, paid at pairing and removal: a grant on A with its boxes (over 1 KB per role), the grant travelling to readers of what it writes, a box at each later rotation of A, and at removal R with its leaves. Against that, each commit drops 127 B (§2).
- **Large end**: a fleet of thousands of nodes as members of one A costs a box per member at every removal's rotation (megabytes) and, where readers hold every member's grant, the whole roster in each sync unit; nest accounts instead [checked: spec/requirements.md, Generality: nest rosters]. Thin clients do not count: a client's actor is minted with the account as admin and holds ordinary grants, the node signing for it [checked: notes/client-port.md §3, The client's actor]; under the target its path is node, A, client actor. A client actor is never a signer, so a thousand clients add nothing to A's members.

## 6. Sessions
A session is one run of a node, a random nonce under the author's tid, with counters in memory only [checked: spec/crdt.md §2.1]. With visible signers its tid is the node's signer, and only that node can write in it. It stays a separate idea: a writer with durable counters would keep a counter per aspect it ever wrote, and lose it with every released thing, so it would need a fresh run anyway. Sessions do not help removals (§4: a frontier still lists IDs). What they save is compact dots and one frontier entry per run in place of a 32 B source per commit; for many short runs (each CLI call is a session) they save nothing. **Recommendation**: keep sessions as built, keyed to the signer; require commit form for a signer several nodes hold; measure before removing them (no decision needed from Tess).

## 7. Node anonymity as it was built
Kept on purpose (Tess) so a later look at hiding nodes starts ahead; describes the design spec/permissions.md §5.4-§5.5 and spec/crypto.md §9 hold while they stand.
- **What it is**: an account is a key actor whose signing key changes by rotations of its signing chain (a DAG of epochs); each node holds every seed and signs as the account; a sealed inner signature (node tid and signature, 95 B, under a key from the epoch's seed) tells only the account's nodes who signed; a removal is a rotation (header shows only `revokes`) whose seed is boxed to the rest; commits at a revoked epoch count only if named by the rotation's thing lists (one leaf per thing in a blinded list tree), a later listing by the node that made them, or a writer's addendum, each by the cutoff (received before the epoch's earliest closer); honest concurrent rotations are joined by deterministic merges (seed and epoch a function of the tips); each node sides by the first revocation it sees, reading who minted what from inner signatures, a removal hitting the removed node's rotations off its line, a device standing with the revocations it took (`takes`); own covers carry forgotten writes.
- **Machinery**: two layers beside the role timeline (the chain: placing, rejects, following, standing, revocations taken, roster, heads, preferred head; the epoch rule: revoked, cutoff, closers that end, carrying, listings, answers), seed custody and re-seals, merge certificates, account sides apart from admin disputes; models roles-model custody and epochs, Account.tla; engine `flows/account.rs`, `custody.rs`, `lists.rs`, `signing.rs`.
- **Bytes**: per commit an epoch (32 B) and an inner signature (95 B) [checked: spec/wire.md §4]; per rotation seed boxes to every node and thing lists naming every commit of the whole account at the closed epochs, on every thing; per node per rotation a listing per thing it wrote; rotation headers logged under every sync ID the account acts in.
- **Trade-offs it bought**: readers never learn node count, which node wrote what, or when devices come and go (sessions leak runs, and connections show node identities). Paid for by: every removal puts every node's history in question; offline honest nodes' writes fall until they list them; a removed node's commits at an unaware sibling's head count until a merge; accounts are not ordinary holders (no grants on them, an account root standing in).
- **Holes found by this study**: session-form dots under the shared tid let a holder of the seed void a sibling's write by overlapping its session (§1).
- **Rejected inside that design**, and why (notes/engine-validity-epochs.md, Rejected designs): removal and rotation apart (one shows D's dot range), one account-wide list (shows every sync ID the others' IDs), per-node subkeys certified to readers (per-node data in every sync ID), group signatures.
- **For a later look**: the costs come from one fact: with a shared key, writes cannot be told apart by signer, only by time, so every removal must vouch for everything. Any hiding design inherits that unless something only insiders read tells writes apart (the inner signature), and then sides differ inside and outside.

## 8. Model plan
A new roles-model module, greenfield, an oracle of this note (not of code), before building. It joins the existing joint walk with trust sets (roles-model `trust`, `joint`), addenda and own cover (`addenda`, `forget`) to an ordinary keys layer for A and one thing T that A holds a role on.
- **Instances**: visible: A with members H (honest), B (honest, offline, unaware), X (stolen), and N5 (paired by X, its grant unreceived by H); readers R1 (reads T, X wrote it), R2 (reads U, X never wrote it, its user may switch). Hidden: the same nodes holding one K, swaps by H and by B at once, a thief's swap boxed to honest nodes or to none. Schedules random, lockstep and an exhaustive small family, as custody's.
- **Attacker moves** (X holds every header and every key and secret X held): backdated writes and removals of honest nodes, grants to a made-up key, rotations of A boxing itself, equivocation in its own sessions, covers and purge requests, withholding and choosing whom to deliver to first, re-seals; in hidden mode, swaps of K.
- **Properties**: safety (an X write after R shows on the honest side only where a writer carried it before holding R); liveness (every honest node's write shows on the honest side; X's writes H or a writer received before R show); secrecy (after a knowing re-key, X opens no new epoch of A or T, except where a node on X's side boxed it, counted); a switch takes; bounded rotations under a standing dispute (catches the withdraw loop); alike (same held set and trust set read the same); locality (a reader of T judges with T's leaf alone); the collateral grantee's writes count after its re-grant; a node paired after a fight is a member at its own node. Hidden: honest offline content saved by addenda; counted (not failures): honest swaps split, a thief's swap won first.
- **Negative controls**: leaf missing (safety fails), overlap among invalid commits (liveness of X's pre-theft writes fails), withdraw unbounded (rotations unbounded), re-grant from first applied grant (collateral grantee fails), pairing without the pairer's trust entries.
- **Replaces**: roles-model custody and epochs, and core/test/tla/rotation Account.tla, all of which check the signing chain. **Adapted from**: custody's parties, schedules and X's move menu; `trust`, `joint`, `addenda`, `forget` as they are; Depth0.tla for A as a holder actor of T; Rotation.tla for A's own keys.
