# Account membership: signers on the ordinary rules
> Summary: Design (not built) for an account as an ordinary independent thing whose members are signers, one per node per account: an audit of every mechanism of the built account system against the stolen-node attacks, visible mode (built first), what a removal keeps (a list per thing, role writes vouched for by a holder that could have written them, a remover that does not sync a thing, what the user is shown), hidden mode kept as a plain shared-key design, why a signer is a node, sessions, a record of node anonymity as built, and the model to check it. Decisions and open questions: issues/actors-one-mechanism.md.

**Principle: a node is a holder.** An account is an independent thing A; each signer is a key holding a role on A; every event in an account's life (pairing, removal, theft, a fight between nodes) is an ordinary grant, removal, rotation or admin dispute on A, judged by the rules every thing has, with no rule naming accounts: core has no concept of an account (notes/write-path-design.md §5). Where the ordinary rules fall short they are fixed for every holder, never patched for accounts.

Marks: **[checked: ref]** read in the spec or code at that place; **(modelled: ref)** held by that model or hand world of roles-model `paths`; **(argued)** reasoning here, not yet checked by a model or test; **(decided)** Tess, issues/actors-one-mechanism.md Answers. Terms: notes/terms.md.

## Assumed of the write-path design
- A commit states its path from its signer through each independent thing whose role it uses (signer, A, then any holder above), and is judged along it alone (notes/write-path-design.md §1).
- A reader of thing T holds, in T's sync unit, the grants on the paths of the commits on T it holds, every later edge write on those grants, and the vouches naming them (notes/write-path-design.md §2).
- A removal R of a signer on A reaches a reader of T with the reader opening only what it needs: R's header, its edge write, R's leaf for T, and R's entry for each role commit of the removed signer that T's unit holds as evidence (§4, The list).

## The built sync rules it rests on
The sync unit is the built one: a thing's readable closure, so a thing may be carried under several sync IDs (spec/sync-set.md §2; notes/sync-unit-scope.md). Used here:
- A grant naming a holder travels under the holder's sync ID with the target's boxes and `sync.0` [checked: spec/sync-set.md §2 Node closure, read; spec/permissions.md §4 Role changes travel], so A's sync ID lists every target A holds a role on, with its sync ID, and on through the sync IDs of the holders it reaches: this is how a removal finds the sync IDs it is owed under (§4, Where a removal is logged).
- Every reader of a unit syncs the own sync ID of each independent thing in its node closure [checked: spec/sync-set.md §2 Sync set], so a node reading an enclosing unit also holds what is logged under the inner thing's own sync ID.
- A node opens a sync ID only while it syncs it, and it syncs what its own references reach [checked: spec/sync-opens.md §4, the shared set; spec/sync-set.md §2]. Nothing fetches headers alone: a commit is one blob, header and parts [checked: spec/sync-ids.md §1]. So a node acts in a unit outside its closure (logging a removal, hearing the unit out) by holding it for that errand: a strong from its node's local part to the unit's thing, entered read by the boxes to A (argued from spec/sync-set.md §2), a complete pass, then the strong released (§3).

## Needs of the built sync rules
For the coordinator; stages in notes/redesign-plan.md.
- **A removal's copy kept for the unit's life** under every sync ID carrying a thing where the removed signer could act, whether or not it holds a commit by that signer, judged required by its readers from the copy and the edge writes it comes with (§4; A3). Built: a removal travels for `ROLE_KEEP` after its `ts` (spec/permissions.md §4 Role changes travel).
- **Steps listing signers**: a signer per node per account listed in A's steps by a claim under its own key (§2; A4). Built: claims for readers, a node or an account by its read-role KEM key, other key actors by static entry (spec/sync-ids.md §3).
- **Finding every enclosing sync ID of a thing** the removed signer could act on: the remover finds targets' own sync IDs from A's sync ID; enclosing units are reached only by the standing duty (§4), not by a list any node holds (argued; not modelled).

## 1. Audit of the built account system
Built: spec/permissions.md §5.4-§5.5, spec/crypto.md §3 and §9, notes/engine-validity-epochs.md; models roles-model custody and epochs, core/test/tla/rotation Account.tla. Each mechanism: what it answers, then its fate: (a) an ordinary rule already answers it, (b) an ordinary rule with a gap to fix, (d) not needed.

- **Signing chain and epochs.** Answers: which key may sign for P; a removal stops the removed node signing; P shows readers one public key whatever its nodes. Fate (a): a node's signer holds admin on A; the removal is an ordinary explicit removal on A, and the removed signer's later writes fall by the role timeline and the frontier [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 steps 2-4, §5.2]. The built limit "a removed node's commits at an unaware sibling's open head count until a merge" [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.5 Carrying] has no counterpart (argued).
- **Seed custody.** Answers: handing the shared signing key and role secrets to the nodes; keeping judgment a function of held commits so a user's switch of side takes. Fate (a): a grant on A boxes A's secrets to the new signer, a removal rotates A, and things A holds roles on re-key as for any holder's new keyset [checked: spec/permissions.md §3 Required rotation, Holder actors; spec/crypto.md §4]. Custody following holding has its ordinary twin: a secret from an invalid commit is kept for reading only, so a node on the thief's side still holds the honest side's secrets and a switch takes [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Reading-only secrets] (argued that this suffices; the model checks "a switch takes"). No signing seed exists in visible mode.
- **Sealed inner signature.** Answers: only P's nodes learn which node made each commit. Fate (d): in visible mode the signer is the node; hidden mode has none (§3).
- **Merges of concurrent rotations.** Fate (a): two admins rotating A at once leave two current epochs; forks stand, holders are re-sealed at each and the next rotation supersedes both [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Forks]. No commit is signed at an epoch, so nothing else forks.
- **Sides by first revocation seen.** Fate (a): the admin dispute: trust set, seeds, first received, switch [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Trust set; notes/disputes.md]. A removed node's writes need no side (they fall by the frontier); only its removals of honest nodes make a dispute.
- **`takes`** (a node that took the thief's revocation stands with that side). Fate (a) with gap (b): an honest node H2 on the thief's side writes valid rotations of A boxing X; at honest-side nodes those epochs do not cover X's departure, so they are blocked and re-keyed [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Departures and covering, Encrypting under]. At H2 the honest re-key is a rotation by a node it holds removed; under the withdraw rule as written H2 rotates again, and the two sides of any admin dispute rotate at every complete pass while both stay online [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Encrypting under, a node withdraws an epoch whose maker may not rotate]. **The rule, for every dispute**: an epoch covering a departure the node's own walk does not apply is the other side's: its secret is kept and read, it is never current, supersedes nothing, and is neither answered nor withdrawn. Each side rotates once; a node whose user switches withdraws its own epoch once and from then encrypts under the epoch of the side it joined, which boxed it all along (modelled: roles-model `paths`, `Withdraw::SideOnly`). It follows from the principle: the loop is a gap of the ordinary dispute, so it is closed for every thing's admins, and no rule may act on a dispute being settled (notes/principles.md, Forks are permanent).
- **Thing lists, list trees, listings, names.** Answers: with a shared key a removal puts the whole account's history in question. Fate: listings (d), since honest nodes' writes are never in question in visible mode; the per-thing cut (b): the ordinary frontier is one list over every thing (§4).
- **Re-adds.** Fate (a): the ordinary re-grant [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.2, a thing holding the required role in the final state is re-granted]. A node coming back from theft is paired under a new signer: a re-grant of the old one brings back the thief's writes too (argued). The collateral grantee (X paired N5, the remover never received that grant, so the removal drops it and N5's writes since) is answered by vouching, §4.
- **Own cover at rotations.** Fate (a): the ordinary removal's own cover [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Own cover, §5.2 Own cover].
- **Cutoff and Carrying.** The cutoff is the frontier's "received as of T" (a). Carrying (d): it guarded open heads. What remains is ordinary: a writer that compacts before it holds the removal carries the removed node's late write in a valid snapshot, which stands [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.3]; the thief's window is until each writer holds R [modelled: notes/path-model-findings.md 26, the accepted window].
- **Account root D, roster from the chain, seed re-seal.** (a): A's holders are its roster by the role timeline; re-seals are the ordinary re-seal [checked: spec/permissions.md §3 Re-seal]. D itself goes (notes/write-path-design.md §5).
- **Found in the built design** (§7): session-form dots under the account's shared tid let any holder of P's seed sign a commit in a sibling's session with an overlapping range; both commits are invalid [checked: spec/commits.md §4, two commits of one session overlapping].

## 2. Visible mode (the one built)
- **The account** is an independent thing A, minted by its first node's signer N0, its mint admin and no different from any other admin afterwards [checked: notes/terms.md, Mint admin]. Whether a thing is an account is the schema's mark on it; nothing below reads it.
- **Members**: each node's signer is a key thing (its tid commits to its key) holding a role on A: admin for a node that may pair and remove, write for one that may not. A recovery key is another member signer held offline. **A signer per node per account** (decided): each derived from the node's seed and a salt the node keeps, so a node in two accounts links them for no reader, and a stolen node needs a removal per account, as it would with one key (argued).
- **Pairing**: the new node makes a signer and shows its key card to a member over the pairing connection; the member writes one role commit granting it on A, boxing A's secrets to it [checked: spec/permissions.md §2, a grant carries the grantee's boxes]. From A's secrets the new node opens A's dependents and every box to A. The pairer also hands over, locally, its trust entries for A: a node paired after a backdated fight would otherwise take the walk's side, under which its pairer may be no member (argued). Pairing is two ordinary operations (make a signer, grant it), done by a library above core.
- **Removing a node X** is one role commit R by a member H with admin on A: the explicit removal of X's grant, H's frontier, and the rotation of A's roles, in one commit [checked: spec/permissions.md §3, the removal and its rotation are one role commit]. Before R, H writes its own covers. What R lists, who else vouches, where R is logged and what is lost: §4. R's result names the members X had added (decided) and those it had removed or lowered. Things A holds roles on re-key lazily, as their boxes to A go stale [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Holder actors].
- **A node fight** (X removes H while H removes X) is an admin dispute on A [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Trust set; notes/disputes.md]. Under the plain walk the earlier-stamped removal applies and the later one finds its author without authority; the later one is raised as a dispute, each node sides by first received (a party holds its own write first), is told, and may switch. Trusting R seeds out every unlisted edge write X made on A and what it reaches before R, so X's backdated removals, grants and rotations go with it. On neither side is A left without an admin (notes/write-path-design.md §5, The last admin).
- **What each reader learns**: for each thing it reads, which of A's signers wrote each change, when each was granted and by whom; each removal of a signer of A, with its remover, in every unit that signer could write (decided). Signers differ per account, so nothing links one node's activity across accounts. A reader learns nothing inside A.
- **Per commit**: no epoch (32 B) and no inner signature (95 B) [checked: spec/wire.md §4 fields 14, 15 and Inner signature]; the path adds A's tid (16 B) (argued).

### Stolen node, step by step
Nodes H (honest, online), B (honest, offline), X (stolen: the thief holds X's signers and every secret X held); readers Bob (reads T, which X wrote) and Carol (reads U, which X never wrote).
1. **H removes X** (R at t1). At H, and at Bob and Carol once R is logged under their units: X's commits that neither R nor a vouch nor an addendum names fall [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.2]; X opens no epoch of A made after R, and things A holds roles on re-key before their next write (argued).
2. **X writes T after t1, honestly stamped**: X held no role at its key, so invalid wherever R is held [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.2]. Where R is not yet held it counts until R arrives; a writer of T that compacts meanwhile carries it for good [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.3; modelled: notes/path-model-findings.md 26, accepted].
3. **X backdates writes before t1**: named by nobody, so invalid at R's holders [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Frontier, a write is covered iff listed]. A witness lying about receipt makes them count, within what the witness could write itself (§4).
4. **X backdates a removal of H and B before t1**: a dispute. Nodes holding R first trust it, and its seeds drop X's removals [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Trust set]. B, coming back, sides by what reached it first; a thief feeding B first wins B until B's user switches [checked: notes/disputes.md, Detection and sides]. What B encrypts meanwhile X reads: the cost of the wrong default, accepted [checked: notes/disputes.md, Decided].
5. **X backdates a grant to a made-up key Z, then Z removes H, or grants H anything**: R's seeds drop X's grant of Z, so Z holds nothing and no write of Z applies, whatever edge it sets (the remover's included: nothing is exempt from a removal's drops, notes/write-path-design.md §1). If H, or any node that could have paired Z itself, had received X's grant of Z before R, Z stays a member and must be removed too: the accepted accomplice hole [checked: notes/disputes.md, Hole: an accomplice that is a current admin raises nothing]; the member list shows Z.
6. **X rotates A boxing itself, backdated**: an unlisted write of X, invalid at R's holders, its secrets reading-only [checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Reading-only secrets].
7. **X equivocates in its own old sessions** (overlapping counters): X's new commit is unlisted and invalid, and the overlap rule (spec/commits.md §4, counting every held commit) voids with it X's pre-theft writes on those counters that R listed: a hole that stays (gap (b); issues/overlap-among-valid-commits.md: counting only otherwise valid commits makes a verdict read itself and leaves a shared dot with no owner).
8. **B's unsent writes** made offline: never in question. X's pre-theft writes that B received count by B's addenda and vouches (§4).
9. **X withholds R from Carol**: availability only; R reaches her by any other route [checked: spec/requirements.md, Threat model].
10. **X removes H, honestly stamped, before H acts**; H receives it and removes X. H's node leaves X's removal out of R's list, as it leaves out any write R would not apply with (§4, The list), so R's seeds drop it. Under the plain walk the earlier-stamped removal applies and R is raised as the dispute of step 4; trusted, R stands. The same for X removing whoever paired H with a list that drops H's grant.
11. **X backdates a write aimed at H's own standing**: a grant to H with an `until` (so that H, a timed admin, could not remove the last standing one), a demotion of H, its own leave stamped before role writes it made honestly. Each is a write resting on X's edge that R does not list, dropped by R's seeds like any other. Under the plain walk each is the dispute of step 4.
12. **X answers with a removal of H that lists nothing**, or Z does: its drops take its own author's grant, so it does not apply and is void: it drops nothing and raises no dispute (notes/write-path-design.md §1).

## 3. Hidden mode (a design, not built)
Kept for identities where privacy is the point and edits are few (decided). The same design with one shared signer K as A's member, held by every node of the account, and no node signature of any kind on any commit: nothing sealed for insiders. K is a key thing, so it changes only by replacement [checked: spec/crypto.md §3, a key actor changes keys only by being replaced].
- **Distributing K**: K's seed reaches a new node over the pairing connection. The **node list** (each node's KEM key) is a dependent of K, so only K's holders read it.
- **Swap on theft**: a node holding K mints K1 and writes (1) a commit signed by K granting K1 admin on A, then (2) a commit signed by K1 removing K explicitly, with its frontier and the rotation of A. It boxes K1's seed to every node on the list but the stolen one, and mints the next node list under K1. One commit cannot do both: a commit has one signer, and K removing itself is a self-removal, which audits nothing [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1, a demotion by self-removal audits nothing], so the thief's backdated writes under K would still count.
- **What the swap vouches for**: every commit signed by K on everything A reaches that the swapper received: K is the whole account. Its size is the account's, not the node's.
- **Costs against visible**, each a property:
  - Every removal puts every node's writes and grants in question, with bytes and work in proportion.
  - An honest node's unsent work falls until that node vouches for it under K1: its content by addenda, its role writes by vouches (§4), both once it holds K1.
  - Two honest swaps at once dispute each other: the earlier-stamped removal's audit drops the other swap's grant, each node trusts its own removal first, and the account is split until a user picks [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 step 4, Trust set; argued for the outcome]. In visible mode two removals of one node conflict with nothing.
  - No sessions: content uses commit form, since session dots under a shared signer are forgeable by any holder (§1, Found in the built design).
  - A thief's writes before the removal cannot be told from the owner's.
- **What a swap shows**: no signature says which node made a swap, but the node list minted under its new key says which nodes it leaves out, and a node not boxed the new key knows it was cut. So insiders judge a swap as they judge a removal in visible mode, by whom it removes, and a dispute can be shown as "one side drops the phone, the other drops the laptop".
- **No design can tell a thief from an honest node**: with two nodes removing each other the answer is outside osis in visible mode, hidden mode and the built design alike (first received, the user switches). Particular to hidden mode: a thief's swap that leaves nobody out still competes, first received, with an honest swap that drops the thief (argued).
- **Not known to be much simpler than what is built** (Tess): what goes is the parallel layer (chain, epochs, custody, account sides); vouching for the whole account's history at each removal is inherent to hiding and stays; merges and automatic settling are given up, not solved.
- Rejected: a sealed node signature on swap commits. It would settle the swap that drops nobody without the user, and brings back sides that differ between insiders and outsiders, the carve-out of the built design.
- **Nothing visible builds may rule it out**: no "a signer is one node" rule (own commits are known from the store, not the signer; commit form allowed everywhere).

## 4. What a removal keeps
One rule for every removal of every holder: **a write of the removed party is kept when a remaining party that could have written it itself says it had received it by the removal**: the remover by its frontier, a writer by an addendum (plain content), a holder of the right the write needed by a vouch (everything else). The rest is discarded, since nothing shows it was made before the removal [checked: spec/requirements.md Permissions]. The removal itself is judged in the world those statements make: once, at its key, and void, with no effect, where its own drops would take its author's authority (notes/write-path-design.md §1, Each lowering is judged once).
- **Who may keep what** is all of it: the keeper remains (it holds the needed right after the removal, with no vouch's help: Role writes, below), and the write is another signer's. Nothing asks whom the kept write benefits or which edge it sets.
- **Nobody vouches for its own signer's commit**: an addendum or a vouch names no commit signed by the key that signs it. Receiving is what a witness does; a signer saying it had received its own write adds nothing to the timestamp it chose, so a removed node that still writes the thing by another path cannot name its backdated writes through the cut one (modelled for addenda and vouches: roles-model `paths`, `no_own_addendum`, `no_own_vouch`). A second writer colluding with it still can: nothing checks receipt.
- **Nothing is kept on a user's word** (decided): a removed node's writes that no remaining node had received are untrusted, with no exception.

### The list: a leaf per thing
- **Form**: the removal's header carries a list root; the list is a tree of fixed depth (the built list tree, spec/permissions.md §5.5 Rotation's list; the depth hides the number of things) with one leaf per thing at a blinded position, naming X's plain-content commits on that thing, and one **entry per role commit** of X at a position blinded from its commit ID, so the path to a position proves presence or absence alike.
- **What a reader holds**: R's header, its own things' leaves, and the entry, present or absent, of each role commit of X its unit holds as evidence. So a grant X made on A that R lists counts at a reader of T as at A's members, a demotion X made is kept or dropped alike, and the reader learns of X's role commits only those it already holds (the reader seeing those entries is modelled: roles-model `paths`, finding 5; the position per commit is argued). A role commit whose entry is not held is unjudgeable, never unlisted (notes/write-path-design.md §2, Pending). It follows from the path principle: whether an edge write on a stated path was listed is part of what checking that path needs, so it is in the unit.
- **What a list leaves out** (modelled: roles-model `paths`, `Rules::stands`, the order received against key order in the search; on today's walk, roles-model `vouches`): a remover names what it had received through the removed edge by R's `ts`; where R, trusted, then does not apply in its own walk (a fight: something it received takes its own standing), it names the role writes one by one in the order it received them, leaving out each with which R would not apply. A witness builds its vouch the same way over R's list as written. So the thief's removal of the remover, or of whoever paired it, is left out and R's seeds drop it; every other lowering X made is listed as received, like any role write (decided: automatic), so a removal X made long before the theft stays made; the removal's result names them, and undoing one is an ordinary grant.
  - No reader checks this: every node reads the list as written, and a list keeping what unmakes its removal voids it (notes/write-path-design.md §1). A remover that leaves out an honest earlier removal has written a contest of it, which only nodes trusting that removal follow.
  - **In the order received, not in key order**: the thief stamps as it likes, but cannot make the remover have received its writes before the honest history. In key order a backdated demotion of the remover, repaired by a junk grant from a party X had removed, is kept first, and X's honest removal of that party is then the entry R cannot apply with: left out, and the removed party is back (`a_remover_keeps_what_it_received_first`).
  - Cost: one walk where R applies with everything named, which is every removal but one made in a fight; there, one walk per role write of X the writer received.
  - Today's frontier is one list over every thing [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Frontier], which a reader of T fetches whole, learning how many commits X made on things it cannot read.
- **No size where a removal cannot be written**: with a leaf per thing as a chunk tree, the bound falls on each wire object, never on the list [checked today: spec/permissions.md §5.1, a removal whose role-class commits alone pass the bound cannot be written]. The removal costs in proportion to what it lists [checked: spec/requirements.md, Cost].
- **Nothing kept per commit for good**: a leaf names only commits the remover still holds; forgotten ones are carried by its own cover [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Own cover]. Per aspect the remover may cover by an own cover in place of listing (a performance switch between two correct strategies, notes/arbitrary-values.md), except on aspects whose history is kept. A node keeps a leaf only while it holds a commit the leaf names (argued).
- **Why commit IDs and not a tip per writer**: the thief can sign a commit overlapping a covered range; with counters both are covered and the overlap rule voids both, killing the pre-theft writes (argued).

### Role writes: vouched for like content
Wanted (Tess): removing a node from an account must not undo the membership of what was shared from it. Today a frontier lists the removed party's role-class commits only as the remover received them, and addenda vouch for plain content only [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Frontier, §5.2 Addenda], because third-party witnesses for edge writes judged by the roles the walk ends with never settle and let a grant's own beneficiary witness it [checked: notes/role-timeline.md, Findings].
- **A vouch** is an entry in a role commit on the thing the vouched write is on (a role-class aspect beside `roles.0`; name open), keyed by the removal R's commit ID, listing commit IDs of the removed party X. It says: R's list is to be read as naming these too.
- **It counts** iff, in the walk made with every vouch disregarded (**the first walk**): R applies as an explicit write; the named commit is X's and ordered before R; the vouch is ordered after R; and its author W held, just before the vouch's key, every right the named commit's parts on that thing needed (admin for an edge write, a change of settings or a rotation of every role; the invite an invite grant needed, with a value W's own invite allows; write for a rotation of the lower roles; the purge right for a purge request), W's own vouch surviving W's later removals as any role-class write of W does, by their frontiers in the first walk.
- **The timeline is the second walk**: the ordinary walk over the same held writes with each counting vouch's commits added to its removal's frontier. Nothing judges a vouch again in the second walk.
- **No circle** (modelled: `Vouch::BaseWalk`): a vouch's standing reads only the first walk, which reads no vouch; the second walk reads vouches as fixed input. Two walks, no fixed point. A fallen grant gives its grantee no authority in the first walk, so no grant vouches for itself and two forged grants cannot vouch for each other. A holder whose authority only a vouch restores cannot itself vouch (one stage only: a second stage would be the fixed point that does not settle).
- **It gives nobody a right they lacked** (argued): the second walk is exactly the walk R's author would have caused by listing those commits, a thing the rules already let a remover do unchecked [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1, nothing but the frontier decides what X's writes before T survive]; a vouched write applies at its own key as X's write, under the authority X held then; and the voucher could have written the same write itself at the vouch's key. A witness lying about receipt makes X's backdated role writes count, within X's own tenure before R and within what the witness may write: the same bargain as an addendum [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.2, nothing checks when W received what it lists]. An admin gains nothing by it that an empty or padded frontier of its own would not give.
- **A vouch is part of its removal's list, trusted or not**: a counting vouch keeps what it names out of R's audit and R's seeds (modelled), so witnesses' vouches keep X's honest role writes in a fight too. The removed party and any key it made up hold nothing in the first walk, so vouch for nothing. A party holding admin after R that vouches for a write R does not apply with (X's removal of the remover) voids R: the accomplice that is a current admin, which the remover removes too (notes/disputes.md, Hole); an honest witness leaves such a write out (The list).
- **What follows for the writes made under a fallen grant**: vouched for, the grant applies at its own key, so the grantee's content and its own role writes since then were never without authority: nothing more is written. Granted again with no vouch, the grantee holds the role from the new grant; its earlier content is valid by the re-grant rule (from its edge's first chained grant, modelled: `Regrant::FirstChained`), its earlier role writes stay dropped [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.2, edge writes in the gap stay dropped]. A vouch may still come later, from any holder of the right that had received the grant by R, and its bodies are fetched again as for any write turned valid [checked: spec/permissions.md §6 Invalid deltas].
- **A grantee granted again vouches for its own fallen grant** (modelled: `a_re_granted_grantee_vouches_for_its_own_fallen_grant`): the grant is X's commit, the grantee had received it, and once an admin has granted it again it is a remaining holder of the right like any. Its writes from the gap then stand, role writes included. This is the same terms as content, where a writer granted again writes the addendum that keeps what it had received. It gives a hostile admin nothing: one that lies about receipt already makes any backdated grant of X to any key count. A rule against vouching for a write that sets the voucher's own edge would bar a node keeping X's demotion of it, which only it may have received, and would not reach a member vouching through a holder thing for X's grant to that holder.
- **Writing** (modelled as the state-based rule, `Rules::rewrite`; answering only the first time a node's walk applies R fails liveness): whenever a node's own walk applies R and shows, on a thing where it holds the right in the first walk, a commit of X it had received by R's `ts` that R's list leaves out, that it could write itself, that R still applies with (The list), and that no counting vouch of its own names, it writes one vouch there; and likewise the addendum it writes today for plain content. So it writes on applying R, when it first holds the right after R (granted again, or its user switched side), and whenever its earlier vouch stops counting: what it had received by R is a local fact that does not age. R's author does the same for what it had received and its list does not name; the writes it left out because R would not apply with them fail the same test again.
- **Addenda reach admin-only content**: an addendum by a witness holding admin at its key vouches for X's deltas on admin-only aspects that are not role-class (settings). They are judged in layer 2 and nothing in the timeline reads them, so the addenda fixed point is unchanged (argued; today they stay with the frontier, spec/permissions.md §5.2 Addenda).
- **Evidence**: a unit's evidence holds the vouches on its path things that name a held removal (notes/write-path-design.md §2).

### Where a removal is logged
- R is logged under every unit where an act of the removed signer could be valid (decided): where a path through the lowered edge gave write or an invite, or gave read on a thing that takes a reader-level act (a history add). A unit the signer could only read gets no copy: nothing it signs is judged valid there, and the readers of a unit the account only reads learn nothing of the account. Additions travel as their writes need them; removals travel at once.
- It stays there for good (decided): the thief can bring its own grant as evidence with its first write in a unit, and nothing but a held R shows that grant ended. A removal that left a unit after a time would turn the window into a hole.
- **Finding the units** (argued from the built sync rules above): A's sync ID carries the grants A holds; those at write or with an invite name their targets and the targets' sync IDs. A target that is itself a holder is followed the same way through its own sync ID, and a dependent target is reached through its independent's. The remover logs R under each such sync ID where it holds the unit and its path gives the stamp; a unit it does not hold it holds for the errand (above); where the removed signer's right was a reader-level act R goes as a deposit, promoted by a writer. **A thing under several sync IDs**: the target is also carried under the sync ID of each enclosing unit whose thing can read it, which the remover does not list. Each reader of such an enclosing unit syncs the target's own sync ID too (above), so holds R once it is logged there, and logs it under the enclosing sync ID by the standing duty below; R's copy there is required because the enclosing unit carries the target's role data, A's grant on it included.
- **A standing duty, not one pass**: any node holding R, a unit U that carries R, and a unit V that carries a thing U's thing reaches at write or with an invite by edges standing now (V may be the thing's own unit or one enclosing it), logs R under V where V lacks it [checked: spec/permissions.md §4, any node holding such a commit logs it]. One rule covers a holder's unit the remover does not hold (a member of that holder walks on), a unit out of reach at removal time, a thing promoted while R was on its way, and a holder raised from read to write after R.
- **Judged by the unit's readers**: R's copy under V comes with the edge writes joining A to the thing V carries, so V's readers hold what shows the removed signer reached V and judge the copy required; a copy without that chain is outside the required set (argued).
- **Cost, stated**: one entry and one stamp per such unit, kept for the unit's life: removing a node of an account in an organization that holds write on 10^5 things is 10^5 entries, more where those things are also carried under enclosing units (one entry per sync ID carrying them), the walk passing through each holder's sync ID once. spec/requirements.md Cost allows it ("in proportion to the rosters of what it could read"); it is the largest cost of this design.
- **Owed**: for a unit the remover knows and cannot reach, it keeps the pair (R, sync ID) durably and logs when a source appears; the debt ends when a source shows the copy. Until then the thief's writes count at that unit's readers: availability, which no rule closes.
- **To model**: a holder raised from read to write after R. Between the raise and the first member of that holder syncing the unit, the removed signer, holding the unit's older read secrets, may offer a write there; whether readers take content sealed under a superseded epoch decides whether that is a window at all (spec/crypto.md §4 Floors, not checked here).

### A remover that does not sync a thing
Nodes of one account hold different things. On a thing T the remover H does not hold, R's leaf is empty, so X's writes there stand only by witnesses: a writer of T that had received them (addenda), a holder of the needed right that had (vouches). Where X was the only such party among those that held T, nothing honest can save them after the fact.
- **H may hear the units out first**: it holds each unit X could write for the errand (a strong from one of its node's own things, the read secret through A), runs a complete pass, lists what it finds, and lets the strong go. A commit counts as received once its header is read and its signature checked, bodies or not [checked: spec/permissions.md §5.1 Received]; a commit is one blob, so the pass brings the unit's commit blobs whole, and not the chunk, tree and page blobs they link (assumed: a linked blob is fetched for a part the node applies). X's signer is sealed in the header, so this costs in the commits of those units, not in X's edits there (a node keeping a unit unread must not learn authors, spec/requirements.md Data a node cannot read).
- **It is never required and never waited for by core** (argued): removing completes with nobody online [checked: spec/requirements.md, Offline and network], and everything the thief writes before R's `ts` and H collects is blessed with the rest, so collecting lengthens the thief's window. Core offers two plain operations: remove (local, at once, listing what the node has received) and hear out a set of units (a wait the caller may drop). A unit that cannot be reached is not collected; the removal goes ahead and R is owed there.
- **Which comes first is the caller's choice by why the node is removed**: a node still in its owner's hands syncs before it is removed (decided); a lost or stolen node is removed at once. The library that understands accounts offers both.

### Nobody received it
- Where no remaining party had received a write of X before R, nobody honest can tell a real write from a backdated one. Core discards it and tells the app: per removal, the commits of the removed signer that arrived after it and that nothing names, with the thing, the time each claims, and whether it is content, a grant (to whom) or another role write.
- **Nothing brings it back** (decided): no vouch or addendum is written on a user's word, and spec/requirements.md keeps "vouches for having received". The remedy comes before the removal: a node still in its owner's hands syncs first. A grant the removed node made that nobody received is made again by a member, and counts from the new grant.
- What the user is told: these were written by the removed node and seen by none of your other nodes or the thing's other members before it was removed; they may be from before it was lost, or made by whoever has it; they are discarded.

### Other gaps the audit found
- A holder of a removed node's key voiding its listed writes by overlapping their sessions (§2, step 7): open, issues/overlap-among-valid-commits.md.
- Pairing handing the new node its pairer's trust entries (§2).

## 5. Why a signer is a node, never finer
- A signer is the unit of removal, so it should be as fine as what can be lost on its own: a key store. Coarser (a shared key) and a removal cannot tell members' writes apart (§3). Finer (a key per session or commit from one store) and stealing the store steals them all, so a removal must cover every key that store made or can make, which needs them linked to it: by a visible root (which is then the signer) or by a shared key (hiding again) (argued).
- A signer per node per account (§2) is finer than a node only across accounts, where removals are separate anyway.
- A pseudonym per node per thing needs a grant on A for each, written by something readers of T cannot link to the node: a shared key. So it is hidden mode's cost again, per thing (argued).
- Per signer, paid at pairing and removal: a grant on A with its boxes (over 1 KB per role), the grant travelling to readers of what it writes, a box at each later rotation of A, and at removal R with its leaves and an entry under every unit it could write. Against that, each commit drops 127 B (§2).
- **Large end**: thousands of nodes as members of one A cost a box per member at every removal's rotation; nest instead [checked: spec/requirements.md, Generality: nest rosters]. Thin clients do not count: a client's thing is minted with the account as admin and holds ordinary grants, the node signing for it [checked: notes/client-port.md §3]; its path is signer, A, the client's thing.

## 6. Sessions
A session is one run of a node, a random nonce under the author's tid, with counters in memory only [checked: spec/crdt.md §2.1]. With visible signers its tid is the node's signer, and only that node can write in it. It stays a separate idea: a writer with durable counters would keep a counter per aspect it ever wrote and lose it with every released thing. Sessions do not help removals (§4: a list still names IDs). What they save is compact dots and one frontier entry per run in place of a 32 B source per commit. Kept as built, keyed to the signer; commit form for a signer several nodes hold.

## 7. Node anonymity as it was built
Kept on purpose (Tess) so a later look at hiding nodes starts ahead; describes the design spec/permissions.md §5.4-§5.5 and spec/crypto.md §9 hold while they stand.
- **What it is**: an account is a key actor whose signing key changes by rotations of its signing chain (a DAG of epochs); each node holds every seed and signs as the account; a sealed inner signature (node tid and signature, 95 B, under a key from the epoch's seed) tells only the account's nodes who signed; a removal is a rotation (header shows only `revokes`) whose seed is boxed to the rest; commits at a revoked epoch count only if named by the rotation's thing lists (one leaf per thing in a blinded list tree), a later listing by the node that made them, or a writer's addendum, each by the cutoff (received before the epoch's earliest closer); honest concurrent rotations are joined by deterministic merges; each node sides by the first revocation it sees, reading who minted what from inner signatures; own covers carry forgotten writes.
- **Machinery**: two layers beside the role timeline (the chain: placing, rejects, following, standing, revocations taken, roster, heads, preferred head; the epoch rule: revoked, cutoff, closers that end, carrying, listings, answers), seed custody and re-seals, merge certificates, account sides apart from admin disputes; models roles-model custody and epochs, Account.tla; engine `flows/account.rs`, `custody.rs`, `lists.rs`, `signing.rs`.
- **Bytes**: per commit an epoch (32 B) and an inner signature (95 B) [checked: spec/wire.md §4]; per rotation seed boxes to every node and thing lists naming every commit of the whole account at the closed epochs; per node per rotation a listing per thing it wrote; rotation headers logged under every sync ID the account acts in.
- **Trade-offs it bought**: readers never learn node count, which node wrote what, or when nodes come and go (sessions leak runs, and connections show node identities). Paid for by: every removal puts every node's history in question; offline honest nodes' writes fall until they list them; a removed node's commits at an unaware sibling's head count until a merge; accounts are not ordinary holders (no grants on them, an account root standing in).
- **Hole**: session-form dots under the shared tid let a holder of the seed void a sibling's write by overlapping its session (§1).
- **Rejected inside that design** (notes/engine-validity-epochs.md, Rejected designs): removal and rotation apart, one account-wide list, per-node subkeys certified to readers, group signatures.
- **For a later look**: the costs come from one fact: with a shared key, writes cannot be told apart by signer, only by time, so every removal must vouch for everything. Any hiding design inherits that unless something only insiders read tells writes apart (the inner signature), and then sides differ inside and outside.

## 8. Model plan
A new roles-model module, greenfield, an oracle of this note (not of code), before building. It joins the path-judged walk (notes/write-path-design.md §6) with trust sets, addenda and own cover (roles-model `trust`, `addenda`, `forget`) and an ordinary keys layer for A and things A holds roles on.
- **State**: roles-model `paths` holds this note's rules through its second round (notes/path-model-findings.md; core/test/roles-model/README.md, Paths). Modelled: vouches in two walks and their writing by the state rule; nobody vouching for its own signer; the list's entry per role commit seen by a reader; the last-admin rule naming its remaining admin, timed admins and the mark; descent, `until`, self-removal by path end and a leave put in question by a removal of its edge; pairing with the pairer's trust entries; the dispute's rotations; and, as its one rule set (`Rules::fixed`) at full size, a removal judged in the world its list makes with the writers' leaving out (§4, The list), each lowering judged once at its key, a void one having no effect (notes/path-model-findings.md 16). Owed before a build: the open points of notes/path-model-findings.md (Open); bodies and refetching after a cover falls (invites, finalizes, purge authors and covers by copies are modelled: notes/path-model-findings.md 23-26); sessions and overlapping dots; the raised holder (§4); secrecy beyond the dispute loop.
- **Instances**: visible: A with members H (honest, removes), B (honest, offline, holds a thing T2 that H does not), X (stolen), N5 (paired by X, its grant received by B and not by H) and N6 (paired by X, its grant received by nobody); a co-admin C of T2 in another account; readers R1 (reads T, X wrote it) and R2 (reads U, X never wrote it, its user may switch). Hidden: the same nodes holding one K, swaps by H and by B at once, a thief's swap boxed to honest nodes or to none. Schedules random, lockstep and an exhaustive small family, as custody's.
- **Attacker moves** (X holds every header and every key and secret X held): backdated writes and removals of honest nodes, grants to a made-up key, rotations of A boxing itself, equivocation in its own sessions, covers and purge requests, withholding and choosing whom to deliver to first, re-seals, forged vouches and addenda by X, by a grantee of X, and by a lying witness of each role; in hidden mode, swaps of K.
- **Properties**:
  - Safety: an X write after R shows on the honest side only where a writer carried it before holding R, or a party that could write it itself vouched for it.
  - Liveness: every honest node's write shows on the honest side; X's writes that H, a writer (content) or a holder of the needed right (role writes) received before R show; N5 stays a member with every write it made; grants X made on T2 that B or C received stand, with what was written under them.
  - Vouching: the result is a function of the held set in any order; two walks suffice; a vouch by a party without the right in the first walk changes nothing (the beneficiary, two forged grants vouching for each other, a writer vouching for a grant); a lying witness gains no write it could not make itself beyond X's backdating within X's tenure; removing an honest vouch never makes a write valid; a trusted removal's seeds are not lifted by a vouch.
  - N6: its grant and writes fall; a new grant restores only from the new grant; nothing written on a user's word counts.
  - Lists: a removal by an honest node that had received the thief's earlier removal of it (or of its granter) applies under a trust set holding it, and every other lowering the removed node made stays made; nothing is dropped by a write that does not apply; no addendum or vouch counts for a commit of its own signer; a re-granted writer's addendum written after its new grant keeps what it had received before R.
  - Secrecy: after a knowing re-key, X opens no new epoch of A or T, except where a node on X's side boxed it, counted.
  - A switch takes; bounded rotations under a standing dispute (catches the withdraw loop); alike (same held set and trust set read the same); locality (a reader of T judges with T's leaf, the entries of the role commits it holds as evidence, and T's vouches alone); a node paired after a fight is a member at its own node; R logged under a unit X could write and never wrote makes X's first write there invalid; R absent from a unit X could only read changes no verdict there; a holder raised to write after R.
  - Hidden: honest offline content saved by addenda and role writes by vouches; counted (not failures): honest swaps split, a thief's swap won first.
- **Negative controls**: leaf missing (safety fails); role-commit entries missing (a reader and a member read differently); the remover listing all it received (the removal is void); a removal whose drops take its own authority applied; the grants a removal rests on exempt from its drops (a made-up key the thief granted keeps its role); an own-signer addendum counted; withdraw unbounded; vouches judged in the walk they change (does not settle, or the beneficiary vouches); vouches without the right check (a writer and an ex-admin hold admin power); R logged only where X wrote (X's first write in U counts for good); pairing without the pairer's trust entries.
- **Replaces**: roles-model custody and epochs, and core/test/tla/rotation Account.tla, all of which check the signing chain. **Adapted from**: custody's parties, schedules and X's move menu; `trust`, `addenda`, `forget`; Depth0.tla for A as a holder of T; Rotation.tla for A's own keys.
