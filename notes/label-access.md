# Label access: steps, claims, re-seal
> Summary: Why reading a label is gated by its sync key and how a holder that fell behind gets back in: registration steps, claim keys and the step-at-a-time walk, rotation commits (wrapped under the superseded epoch, logged before the step), opens ending at a new sync key, refused Register and Deposit learning only the code, re-seal, entry boxes and static entries; which group owns a label; the accepted lockout and stranding limits. Rules: spec/sync-ids.md §2, spec/sync-ids.md §6; spec/permissions.md §3-§4. Neighbours: notes/sync.md (index), notes/inbox.md (re-seals and grants travel by inbox), notes/crypto.md (epochs, history boxes).

## Why the gate and the claim
- **Reads are gated by the sync key**: without it an ex-holder, or anyone who learned a label ID, keeps fetching ciphertext old and new (counts, timing, laggards' old-epoch wraps, a wider surface against future key breaks). Tess: nothing is served under a superseded key, not even entries the holder could have fetched before. The price: a holder that missed a rotation cannot download the commit carrying its new box; claim keys pay it.
  - Rejected: a grace window after re-registration; "old key serves old entries" (no gating against ex-holders); serving only rotation entries to old keys (every ex-holder watches roster size and rotation timing forever, and the flag is unverifiable); no gating at all (download-before-logging closes most of the laggard-wrap leak at hosts but not via relays or bundles, and ex-holders watch activity and harvest ciphertext forever; Tess rejected).
- **Claim keys** (decided with Tess): a laggard and an ex-holder hold the same shared keys, so the gate needs what only the laggard has, its own private key, and the registrar is the one party that knows the roster a step is for. So the step itself is the delivery: a fresh step key and one pseudonymous Ed25519 claim key per reader, derived by DH; recovery is a pure function of the laggard's private key and the chain the host stores anyway. Nobody delivers, drains or budgets anything.
  - A signature, not a hash preimage: a dishonest host cannot replay it elsewhere.
  - One sync key at a time (a Behind item by a stale sync key, a claim or a static entry of step j yields the steps after j through the first under another sync key): an ex-holder removed at a rotation learns that one rotation exists and nothing after it, no count or timing, as much as a bare "stale" reply tells it, however long ago it was removed (a sync key never stops being a credential; claims and static entries end with the claim set, `ROLE_KEEP`). A current reader asks on by its credential at the step given (its rotation box's sync key, its claim). Rejected: answering every step to the head (one item per catch-up): every holder of any old sync secret, a removed reader or a used link, would watch the rotations and learn the head's sync key, whose beacon tells it which peers keep L, forever.
  - The reply is the signed step, verified under the previous delete key, never a bare key: a host handing out a chosen step key could otherwise recognise a claimant by the pseudonym it signs with.
  - The claim set comes from the epoch's read boxes, not the registrar's roster: the registrar may hold the epoch without the removal commit and would list the holder the rotation just removed, and boxes name the first admin and plain-group readers the roster does not.
  - Nested actors chain claims: the node key on the account's home label, then the account's read key on each group label. The registrar uses M's newest `keys.0` it holds, so M tries old keysets as for boxes; a node M removed can claim until a registrar that saw M's new keyset issues a step (metadata only, M's boxes are under its new key), the same staleness class as the epoch-covers rule.
  - Steps are not deterministic (random step key and padding), so concurrent admins make sibling steps and hosts that saw different ones first keep different branches for good: steps form a tree by `prev`, a host accepts only a child of its head, and the next rotation gets one child per branch (same role keys, different parent signature). `label.0` holds the set of steps, so any admin can relay a host's branch; claim keys bind to the step key, not a sequence number, since a branch has no number of its own. A node that cannot match a host's head walks from the newest step it shares with it.
  - Cost: 32 B per reader per step, about 3% on top of the rotation commit's boxes (whose padded size already leaks the roster bucket). The chain itself (role keys, step key, signature) stays for good; claim sets only until the rotations they lead to are compactable (`ROLE_KEEP` after superseded): beyond that a walk reaches nothing readable anyway. The gate is classical like the Ed25519 open; a quantum ex-holder could fetch hybrid-protected ciphertext.
  - Claim-set padding keys are public keys of random seeds (valid points), not random bytes, or a host testing point validity would count the real keys. Static entries (hashes) stay distinguishable from points about half the time, which says only how many key actors read the label.
- Grants still need a way in before the grantee holds any key of the target: the inbox blob (notes/inbox.md).
- Rejected: **mailboxes** for rotation delivery (every actor names a mailbox label in `keys.0`, key actors derive theirs from the seed, every rotation deposits one doubly boxed projection per holder, about 2.5 KB per holder per rotated actor): a stored, budgeted side channel someone else must feed; a key actor's mailbox has no writer to promote deposits, so a key actor offline through enough rotations finds the newest deliveries refused; clients need a mailbox-first bootstrap across nesting levels; a node's mailbox is a stable per-node label, so the host counts an account's nodes for life; deposits over one connection link the recipients' mailboxes. Claim-key variants rejected: the registrar's own read key as the DH key with the holder guessing among admins it knows (fails once the registrar's keyset changed since the holder last synced); an ephemeral key served to anyone or in a public chain (every ex-holder watches rotation cadence and roster bucket).

## Rotations and opens
- **Rotation commits are wrapped under the epoch they supersede**: a rotation hands out the new read secret, so a wrap under it would be readable by the rotator alone. Under the old wrap a laggard reads the rotations it missed in order, each under the one before. What an ex-holder gains from that blob (an honest host serves it under the old sync key until the step is registered; a dishonest host or stray bundle for ever): the box map of the rotation that removed it (tids and roles at the new epoch) and the removal about itself with its frontier, which it gets by inbox anyway; never a secret. Under a fork the rotator picks the newest superseded epoch; a holder granted during the fork under the other branch only cannot open it and needs a re-grant (rare).
- **Rotation commits go up before the step** (TLC R9). "Register before logging anything" (needed: the host checks stamps against its head's write key, so content stamped under rotated keys waits for the step) leaves a window where a head names an epoch whose rotation commit is nowhere: every listed holder reaches the head, cannot open it, reports itself stranded and is re-sealed with a secret it has no commit for. So the registrar logs the actor's role commits the host lacks (the rotation chain) stamped under the head's keys, registers once the host has pulled them, then logs the rest. A step at a host implies the chain behind it is there (until compaction, where the `secrets.0` snapshot is), and "stranded" means not boxed.
- **An open ends at a head with another sync key**: an open kept across steps would push a removed reader that stays connected every new entry for the connection's life. So an open holds under the sync key of the head it was made at; reads check it lazily (one head lookup), and a push ends with the head event that ends it. Tied to the sync key, not the step: a step without a read rotation removes no reader (an epoch's readers only grow), so its opens stay, and a claim or static open at step j is held by a reader of j's epoch. The registrar's own open ends too: Register is relayable and proves only that the step is signed, so a departed node relaying a step it was pushed must not keep its open by it; the registrar reopens under the new key (one round trip, cursor kept). The remover's rotation commit still reaches a connected departed node: it is logged before the step. Co-reader-proof opens at co-reader peers are not step-bound.
- **Refused Register and Deposit learn the code alone**: a refused Register answered with the head, or a badly signed Deposit with the budget, would let anyone with a label ID (ex-holder, link holder) watch rotations and deposit activity forever. Nothing needs them: the node reopens on any refused Register and re-signs a stale deposit from its own seed; heads reach it through its open. Delete's Status carries no head for the same reason (an ex-admin would watch rotations by re-sending an old delete); the node deletes only where the label is open, so the head comes by the stream's head events.
  - Code 9 stays apart from 1: it tells a signer its key was superseded, not bad, so a stale deposit is re-signed at once with the current deposit key, and an ephemeral entry or delete waits for the newer key instead of being dropped or looping. It tells a holder of an earlier key only that fewer than `OLDER_KEYS` (8) rotations passed, which it mostly knew. A Behind item's 13 vs 14 still says whether a node keeps a label (a key-derived ID; accepted).

## Re-seal, entry boxes, static entries
- **Re-seal is the one recovery path** for a holder with the right private keys that cannot reach the current epoch: its missed rotations compacted (`ROLE_KEEP` after replaced), its grant unknown to the rotator, a fork branch it never received. Nothing a co-reader serves helps by itself (every current commit's wrap is under the epoch it lacks, and a P2P peer serves wraps, not keys), so someone who may rotate seals the current secret to it: a role commit with its boxes and the epochs' history boxes (without them a re-sealed holder or grantee could decrypt the history box but not fetch it: it sits in a rotation commit wrapped under the epoch they lack, TLC R3). A sibling node of the same account is usually a writer, not an admin, of the group, so "who may rotate" matters.
  - It goes as an inbox blob, not under the label: such a holder cannot open the label (its claim was refused or the rotator never listed it), and a wrap would have to be under an epoch it holds.
  - Its own private keys never change, so a node stranded on its account's home label is re-sealed the same way; when the inbox is the very label it cannot open (a node offline past `ROLE_KEEP`), a sibling node re-seals it over the pairing channel (a direct connection, same flow and node identity as pairing), cheaper than a restore from the card.
  - Nothing unattended re-seals (a PQ box per reader per step was rejected for size). Triggers, both SHOULD: a current holder seen without a box at a current epoch, and a stranded report (spec/permissions.md §3, §4); a client may also offer it by hand (`Engine::reshare`). A node reports itself stranded as soon as the open runs out of keys, wherever the walk stopped and whether or not a claim was tried, not only at the head: a holder whose next step's set was dropped never reaches the head (TLC R2), and one reaching a step where it holds no named actor (its grant not received, a key actor's static entry dropped) has nothing to claim with, yet is in the same place. One rule: stranded = no key it holds opens the label's current epoch there.
  - **The report**: a Stranded message to each connected peer that proved an account holding write or above on the label's actor, else a stranded notice (an inbox blob naming the label and the holder) to the inbox of each of the actor's admins, each once per sync key of the newest step reached and recipient (in memory: once more per launch). Neither needs a key of the label: an inbox is the admin's own label under its own deposit seed, so an admin read rotation refreshing the label's deposit seed changes nothing. Both name the holder the node reads through as it judges it: its account, or a non-key actor its account holds a role on that holds the actor; else its account (a grant it has not received). A link key it holds is never named: it reads by its static entry and has no inbox. Both carry the holder's keys (`HolderKeys`: its current `keys.0` commits projected to that delta, with their authors' chains): a node that left the holder out and no longer follows it (gone from it, or never in it) learns its next keyset and deposit seed only so (spec/crypto.md §4, Reported); a receiver that may re-seal the actor takes them (as chain certificates) only for a current holder of it and only its `keys.0` (with the cards of its writers on its authority chain: a commit can name anyone as author), so a report plants no other actor's keyset; of records and `roles.0` deltas only those on the holder's authority chain (the holder, each one's first admin and current holders, transitively, recomputed as each round of taking extends it), so what a report can make its receiver keep is bounded by the holder's real authority history, not by the sender (a notice has no sender to admit: anyone with an admin's deposit seed can send one). A commit taken there that applies with a part judged invalid or an edge write not applied is rolled back whole (`Engine::ingest_valid`) and tried again next round (its granter's edge may come later), so a removed admin of a chain thing, or any other author reaching one without the right, stores nothing by reports (the engine otherwise keeps invalid role commits whole). What a report can leave is the chain's valid history, which only right holders on the chain extend: the reach of an admitted inbox payload's chain. Rejected: a current-role check on the author (it drops the grants a since-removed admin made that the honest chain runs through). Rejected: taking a chain commit only as a carried `keys.0` commit awaits it (`Ingested::Awaits` names only records; a missing role shows as no reach, not as which edge). Rejected: inferring a message's holder from the connection's proved actors up their record chains: it finds only account and node holders, so a member stranded through a team while connected to a writer (which then sends no notice) is never heard. The cost: a few KB of `keys.0` per report, and the holder's deposit seed handed to the actor's writers (a holder actor's refreshed seed keeps its departed out of its inbox; a writer of a thing it reads may deposit there, within the inbox's deposit budget). Not new in kind: the holder's rotation commits, fresh seed included, reach every reader of the actor as Carried certificates once it is sealed there (spec/crypto.md §4). The receiver maps the label to the actor itself (only a label it syncs), checks that the named holder is a current one (nothing of the sender: the re-seal is due whoever asks), and re-seals once its view of the actor's labels is complete, once per (actor, holder) at the actor's current read epochs. Where the stranded node's inbox is the very label it cannot open (its account's D home label), only a connected sibling reaches it (the message, and the re-seal over the direct connection).
- **Entry boxes and static entries** for link keys: a key actor entering cold holds no epoch to unwrap anything with, and the account's rotations get compacted, so its box in `secrets.0` is not reliably reachable. So the current step boxes the read secret to it (one hybrid box, 1.2 KB, per step of a label listing it; sealed form only: the symmetric form carries no epoch to check, spec/crypto.md §4), and it claims the current step directly by a static entry (a hash of its signing key and the label ID): no walk, no old step. The registrar cannot do DH for it without a step private key it would have to keep, and a bare signing key in the set would show the host a public identity; the hash shows nothing until the key claims, which is rare. The entry is stable across steps, which says only that one slot is the first admin's or the link's. A key actor granted after step 0 is listed by a fresh step (an admin may step without rotating).
  - Rejected: the read secret boxed to every reader (1.2 KB per reader per step, permanent, in `label.0`; it would spare laggards past compaction the re-grant); deriving the step key from the admin secret for determinism (an ex-admin plus a dishonest host could identify late claimants from its time as admin).
- Re-seal soundness rests on established epochs (notes/permissions.md): within `ROLE_KEEP` the departures travel in the epoch chain a re-sealer must hold; after compaction they travel in the role history and the removal commit apart from the `secrets.0` snapshot, so a node with an incomplete view (a host that never received the removal, an interrupted first sync) can box a removed holder at a snapshot-established epoch by an eager rotation or a grant (re-seals themselves wait for a complete pass and chain-established epochs). Healed by the next rotation of a node that knows the departure; accepted.

## Label owner
- **A label is g's only by g's sync key** (spec/sync-ids.md §4 owner, `labeling::actors::owns`): any admin of g writes g's `label.0` and can name any label there, so every reader of `label.0` (`Engine::labels_read`, `flows::labels::labels_of`, `own_label`, `keep_labels`) keeps an entry only when a step on a verified path from its genesis bears a sync key of a sync secret of g held here. Steps chain by signature to the genesis delete key the label ID commits to, so only the owner's admins make a step bear g's key. Ignored, never judged invalid: the check turns on held secrets. Forgetting g (lost access, its secrets already gone) keeps the labels a kept group owns.
  - While this node holds no sync secret of g's own (`Core::owns_label`: no `own_record` sync secret; a reader through g's first admin before the commit giving g's own arrives, `sync_more::label_published_under_home_label_spec_sync_2`; a first admin's new node holding K's keyset before its box, osis `sharing::new_node_gets_k_keyset_before_its_box_spec_permissions_4`), g's entries are taken unjudged: it reads by derived secrets no step bears, and fetches that commit by claim under the very label. An entry naming another group's label counts there until then, and for good on a thing whose own secrets never reach it (a plain thing: its `label.0` and the window look alike, as a cross-published `label.0` arrives without `keys.0`). Bounded: unjudged g holds no secret deriving that label's keys, so it registers, re-registers and deletes nothing at a host, and `keep_labels` needs g's own secrets; what is left is the label synced under g as well as its owner and, forgetting its owner, kept while g names it (destruction `a_label_named_in_another_groups_label_0_is_ignored_spec_sync_2`: the owner's label still syncs).
  - The sync key, not the genesis delete key (admin secret): every reader holds a sync secret, only admins the admin secret. Every holder holds the older secrets by history boxes, so some step always matches.
  - Limit: an admin of t that also holds sync on u can give t u's sync secret as a new epoch (nothing checks a rotation's secrets are fresh) and so list u's label as t's. Hostile admins are out of scope.

## Limits (accepted)
- A removed writer keeps its delete keys, but they alone delete nothing; its power ends when the sync key is re-registered, which every removal does. Until an admin steps, writers stamp and open with the keys of the epoch the host's head registers (they hold it by history boxes) and wrap under the newest they hold; a writer's rotation changes no delete key, so a label whose admins are all offline keeps its old step while content under it is already unreadable to the removed holder.
- A removed admin holds the current delete key until re-registration and can win the race at a peer that cannot read the group by registering keys only it knows. A reader registers nothing first-come, and such a peer is by default the user's own node, so the remedy is local and manual (nothing detects it): drop L's entries there, after which a reader registers the recorded path again (code 13), or the group's readers stop syncing with that peer. No sync ID is ever replaced. Hostile admins are out of scope.
- A holder listed in no step since its last sync (a grant a rotation raced) is locked out until a later step lists it or a holder re-seals it (spec/requirements.md, Permissions); the rotation left it without boxes anyway, and the SHOULD re-seal (a holder without a box at a current epoch) repairs it at the next write.
- A holder whose missed rotations were compacted is refused at the step whose set the host dropped and is reachable only through its inbox; its stranded report tells a connected writer or the admins. A walk costs one round trip per missed step. A node offline past `ROLE_KEEP` on its account's home label is refused at the first dropped set and has no inbox of its own: a sibling re-seals it over a direct connection, or it is restored from the card; the official host may keep home-label sets longer by policy. A dropped set also strands a holder whose next rotation is still readable (it cannot walk past the step).
- Re-sealing is triggered, never guaranteed: a stranded notice is lost when the admin's inbox refuses it (its budget full, or an account's deposit seed rotated since this node last read its `keys.0`) and no writer is connected; the holder then waits for a manual re-share (`Engine::reshare`, offered by clients).
- Link keys claim the current step, so compaction and set retention never strand them.
- **Accepted limit (Tess):** a hinted-label walk (a link joiner reaching a label it knows only by ID) claims on the host's step before it can verify it, with the keys of the hint's actor only, so a dishonest host handing out its own step can recognise that one pseudonym at join time. Closing it needs `Path` to answer a claim-key holder (a wire change), declined.

- **Accepted (hostile host, traced, not run):** a host holding a hinted label under another thing can swap the real host's wraps before `label.0` is held: availability only, which the threat model trusts hosts for. If ever seen: never replace a wrap that opens under any held group.
- **Holder actors at depth 0** (spec/requirements.md, Permissions; notes/crypto.md): a party removed from a holder actor M of A reads A's content sealed to a keyset of M written without knowing the removal (an ignorant fork, or a forgery where the removal or the forgery is not held) until M's knowing rotation reaches A's writers; a grant some sealer has not seen lets a cycle form there until the writer holds the grants.
- **Deposit-only re-registration** (the head's deposit key is not the one this admin's seed derives, no newer epoch) waits until nothing under the label it can open, at a connected host that has not said it lacks it, is unopened, and no `keys.0` write deciding the label's keys is held unapplied: one of the actor pending and signed by a current admin, or a held commit's valid `keys.0` part missing on a thing from the label's group up to that actor (its body may give a newer seed, or the group secrets of its own) (`Engine::keys_write_unapplied`). Unsigned or unreachable ones never count: anyone can send a pending commit, and an entry only a gone host lists never opens; either would hold the step back for good (an admin-signed write that never applies holds it back while held). Why: a co-admin's refresh step arrives before its `keys.0` write when blobs are not inline (or come from another host), and the laggard would step back to the old key, re-admitting a departed party's deposits until it applied the write.
- `label.0` growth is about 250 B per step per group per rotation, plus claim sets (32 B per padded reader per step, plus entry boxes) until an admin drops them `ROLE_KEEP` after superseded (steps are a map keyed by step ID, so the drop is a field write); a host registered from a trimmed chain serves no claims at the trimmed steps. If growth ever matters, a root step carrying the current keys signed by the genesis delete key would let old steps be dropped.
