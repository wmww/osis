# Label access: steps, claims, re-seal
> Summary: Why reading a label is gated by its sync key and how a holder that fell behind gets back in: registration steps, claim keys and the step-at-a-time walk, rotation commits (wrapped under the superseded epoch, uploaded before the step), opens ending at a new sync key, refused Register and Deposit learning only the code, re-seal, entry boxes and static entries, replacement labels; the accepted lockout and stranding limits; node sync's open, re-registration and replaced-label handling. Rules: spec/sync.md §2, §5; spec/permissions.md §3-§4. Neighbours: notes/sync.md (index), notes/inbox.md (re-seals and grants travel by inbox), notes/crypto.md (epochs, history boxes).

## Why the gate and the claim
- **Reads are gated by the sync key**: without it an ex-holder, or anyone who learned a label ID, keeps fetching ciphertext old and new (counts, timing, laggards' old-epoch wraps, a wider surface against future key breaks). Tess: nothing is served under a superseded key, not even entries the holder could have fetched before. The price: a holder that missed a rotation cannot download the commit carrying its new box; claim keys pay it.
  - Rejected: a grace window after re-registration; "old key serves old entries" (no gating against ex-holders); serving only rotation entries to old keys (every ex-holder watches roster size and rotation timing forever, and the flag is unverifiable); no gating at all (download-before-upload closes most of the laggard-wrap leak at hosts but not via relays or bundles, and ex-holders watch activity and harvest ciphertext forever; Tess rejected).
- **Claim keys** (decided with Tess): a laggard and an ex-holder hold the same shared keys, so the gate needs what only the laggard has, its own private key, and the registrar is the one party that knows the roster a step is for. So the step itself is the delivery: a fresh step key and one pseudonymous Ed25519 claim key per reader, derived by DH; recovery is a pure function of the laggard's private key and the chain the host stores anyway. Nobody delivers, drains or budgets anything.
  - A signature, not a hash preimage: a dishonest host cannot replay it elsewhere.
  - One step at a time (a stale sync key or a claim on step j yields only step j+1): an ex-holder removed at step k+1 learns that one step exists and nothing after it, no count or timing, as much as a bare "stale" reply tells it. Serving the current step to anyone would leak the cadence to every ex-holder forever.
  - The reply is the signed step, verified under the previous delete key, never a bare key: a host handing out a chosen step key could otherwise recognise a claimant by the pseudonym it signs with.
  - The claim set comes from the epoch's read boxes, not the registrar's roster: the registrar may hold the epoch without the removal commit and would list the holder the rotation just removed, and boxes name the first admin and plain-group readers the roster does not.
  - Nested actors chain claims: the node key on the account's home label, then the account's read key on each group label. The registrar uses M's newest `keys.0` it holds, so M tries old keysets as for boxes; a node M removed can claim until a registrar that saw M's new keyset issues a step (metadata only, M's boxes are under its new key), the same staleness class as the epoch-covers rule.
  - Steps are not deterministic (random step key and padding), so concurrent admins make sibling steps and hosts that saw different ones first keep different branches for good: steps form a tree by `prev`, a host accepts only a child of its head, and the next rotation gets one child per branch (same role keys, different parent signature). `label.0` holds the set of steps, so any admin can relay a host's branch; claim keys bind to the step key, not a sequence number, since a branch has no number of its own. A node that cannot match a host's head walks from the newest step it shares with it.
  - Cost: 32 B per reader per step, about 3% on top of the rotation commit's boxes (whose padded size already leaks the roster bucket). The chain itself (role keys, step key, signature) stays for good; claim sets only until the rotations they lead to are compactable (`ROLE_KEEP` after superseded): beyond that a walk reaches nothing readable anyway. The gate is classical like the Ed25519 open; a quantum ex-holder could fetch hybrid-protected ciphertext.
  - Claim-set padding keys are public keys of random seeds (valid points), not random bytes, or a host testing point validity would count the real keys. Static entries (hashes) stay distinguishable from points about half the time, which says only how many key actors read the label.
- Grants still need a way in before the grantee holds any key of the target: the inbox blob (notes/inbox.md).
- Rejected: **mailboxes** for rotation delivery (every actor names a mailbox label in `keys.0`, key actors derive theirs from the seed, every rotation deposits one doubly boxed projection per holder, about 2.5 KB per holder per rotated actor): a stored, budgeted side channel someone else must feed; a key actor's mailbox has no writer to promote deposits, so a key actor offline through enough rotations finds the newest deliveries refused; clients need a mailbox-first bootstrap across nesting levels; a node's mailbox is a stable per-node label, so the host counts an account's nodes for life; deposits over one connection link the recipients' mailboxes. Claim-key variants rejected: the registrar's own read key as the DH key with the holder guessing among admins it knows (fails once the registrar's keyset changed since the holder last synced); an ephemeral key served to anyone or in a public chain (every ex-holder watches rotation cadence and roster bucket).

## Rotations and opens
- **Rotation commits are wrapped under the epoch they supersede**: a rotation hands out the new read secret, so a wrap under it would be readable by the rotator alone. Under the old wrap a laggard reads the rotations it missed in order, each under the one before. What an ex-holder gains from that blob (an honest host serves it under the old sync key until the step is registered; a dishonest host or stray bundle for ever): the box map of the rotation that removed it (tids and roles at the new epoch) and the removal about itself with its frontier, which it gets by inbox anyway; never a secret. Under a fork the rotator picks the newest superseded epoch; a holder granted during the fork under the other branch only cannot open it and needs a re-grant (rare).
- **Rotation commits go up before the step** (TLC R9). "Register before any upload" (needed: the host checks stamps against its head's write key, so content stamped under rotated keys waits for the step) leaves a window where a head names an epoch whose rotation commit is nowhere: every listed holder reaches the head, cannot open it, heartbeats stranded and is re-sealed with a secret it has no commit for. So the registrar uploads the actor's role commits the host lacks (the rotation chain) under the head's keys, then registers, then the rest. A step at a host implies the chain behind it is there (until compaction, where the `secrets.0` snapshot is), and "stranded" means not boxed.
- **An open ends at a head with another sync key**: an open kept across steps would push a removed reader that stays connected every new entry for the connection's life. So an open holds under the sync key of the head it was made at; reads check it lazily (one head lookup), and a push ends with the head event that ends it. Tied to the sync key, not the step: a step without a read rotation removes no reader (an epoch's readers only grow), so its opens stay, and a claim or static open at step j is held by a reader of j's epoch. The registrar's own open ends too: Register is relayable and proves only that the step is signed, so a departed node relaying a step it was pushed must not keep its open by it; the registrar reopens under the new key (one round trip, cursor kept). The remover's rotation commit still reaches a connected departed node: it is uploaded before the step. Co-reader-proof opens at co-reader peers are not step-bound.
- **Refused Register and Deposit learn the code alone**: a refused Register answered with the head, or a badly signed Deposit with the budget, would let anyone with a label ID (ex-holder, link holder) watch rotations and deposit activity forever. Nothing needs them: the node reopens on any refused Register and re-signs a stale deposit from its own seed; heads reach it through its open. Upload and Delete Status carry no head for the same reason (an ex-writer or ex-admin would watch rotations by re-sending an old stamped entry); the node uploads and deletes only where the label is open, so the head comes by push, or by the next resync's Sync when unsubscribed.
  - Code 9 stays apart from 1: it tells a signer its key was superseded, not bad, so a stale deposit is re-signed at once with the current deposit key, and an ephemeral entry or delete waits for the newer key instead of being dropped or looping. It tells a holder of an earlier key only that fewer than `OLDER_KEYS` (8) rotations passed, which it mostly knew. Open's UNKNOWN_LABEL vs REFUSED still says whether a host hosts a label (a key-derived ID; accepted).

## Re-seal, entry boxes, static entries
- **Re-seal is the one recovery path** for a holder with the right private keys that cannot reach the current epoch: its missed rotations compacted (`ROLE_KEEP` after replaced), its grant unknown to the rotator, a fork branch it never received. Nothing a co-reader serves helps by itself (every current commit's wrap is under the epoch it lacks, and a P2P peer serves wraps, not keys), so someone who may rotate seals the current secret to it: a role commit with its boxes and the epochs' history boxes (without them a re-sealed holder or grantee could decrypt the history box but not fetch it: it sits in a rotation commit wrapped under the epoch they lack, TLC R3). A sibling node of the same account is usually a writer, not an admin, of the group, so "who may rotate" matters.
  - It goes as an inbox blob, not under the label: such a holder cannot open the label (its claim was refused or the rotator never listed it), and a wrap would have to be under an epoch it holds.
  - Its own private keys never change, so a node stranded on its account's home label is re-sealed the same way; when the inbox is the very label it cannot open (a node offline past `ROLE_KEEP`), a sibling node re-seals it over the pairing channel (a direct connection, same flow and node identity as pairing), cheaper than a restore from the card.
  - Nothing unattended re-seals (a PQ box per reader per step was rejected for size). Triggers, both SHOULD: a current holder seen without a box at a current epoch, and the stranded heartbeat (spec/permissions.md §3, §4); a client may also offer it by hand (`Engine::reshare`). The stranded heartbeat goes as soon as the open runs out of keys, wherever the walk stopped and whether or not a claim was tried, not only at the head: a holder whose next step's set was dropped never reaches the head (TLC R2), and one reaching a step where it holds no named actor (its grant not received, a key actor's static entry dropped) has nothing to claim with, yet is in the same place. One rule: stranded = no key it holds opens the label's current epoch there. It is a deposit wrapped under the newest secret the node holds, so it needs no open. What the triggers miss needs a manual re-share.
- **Entry boxes and static entries** for link keys: a key actor entering cold holds no epoch to unwrap anything with, and the account's rotations get compacted, so its box in `secrets.0` is not reliably reachable. So the current step boxes the read secret to it (one hybrid box, 1.2 KB, per step of a label listing it; sealed form only: the symmetric form carries no epoch to check, spec/crypto.md §4), and it claims the current step directly by a static entry (a hash of its signing key and the label ID): no walk, no old step. The registrar cannot do DH for it without a step private key it would have to keep, and a bare signing key in the set would show the host a public identity; the hash shows nothing until the key claims, which is rare. The entry is stable across steps, which says only that one slot is the first admin's or the link's. A key actor granted after step 0 is listed by a fresh step (an admin may step without rotating).
  - Rejected: the read secret boxed to every reader (1.2 KB per reader per step, permanent, in `label.0`; it would spare laggards past compaction the re-grant); deriving the step key from the admin secret for determinism (an ex-admin plus a dishonest host could identify late claimants from its time as admin).
- Re-seal soundness rests on established epochs (notes/permissions.md): within `ROLE_KEEP` the departures travel in the epoch chain a re-sealer must hold; after compaction they travel in the role history and the removal commit apart from the `secrets.0` snapshot, so a node with an incomplete view (a host that never received the removal, an interrupted first sync) can box a removed holder at a snapshot-established epoch by an eager rotation or a grant (re-seals themselves wait for a complete pass and chain-established epochs). Healed by the next rotation of a node that knows the departure; accepted.

## Replacement labels
- A label is typically lost when a removed admin re-registers first (below). The replacement takes the next index with keys from the rotated admin secret the removed admin lacks; the index keeps its keys and ID apart from the old label's, and two admins replacing concurrently derive the same ID. Cost O(entries). The old label keeps its role commits and the `label.0` commit marking it replaced, so a node knowing only the old label finds the new one, and only a key actor that knows no honest host is stranded (a removed admin squatting every host a card names).
- As built (`Engine::replace_label`): it rotates the admin secret first when the newest admin epoch is one a step of the old label registers (whoever took it may hold it; a plain group gets secrets of its own), then writes the new label and marks the old one replaced in one commit. Holders stop syncing a label once they read it is replaced; an admin keeps it (`labels`) until done: its passes there hold role-class bodies only (`bodies_under`), so content entries judge must-not-hold (outside) and go once settled, the replacement's `label.0` commit goes up, and certificates (chain certificates, cards, heartbeats) stay to verify them; none are placed there anew. A replaced label is never stepped again (`reregister` skips it), but at a host that lost it the admin registers its recorded path again (`register_path` on code 13) so the replacement's `label.0` commit is found there.
- Retiring: a settled pass with nothing left to upload and no content entry present marks the label done at that host (`retire_at`, in memory; deletions there mark the label dirty for that pass); a host where no key opens it (`Phase::Failed`) counts as done. Done at every host, or `ROLE_KEEP` after this admin first saw it replaced (a host never reached), it is retired for good (`y/q`: empty value = retired, else the time first seen). Unopened entries there that only content commits list (chunks, tree nodes, pages: `replaced_leftovers`) are deleted by delete key too, and the label is not done at a host while one is present; their deletion never queues a chunk repair (`relink_pending` skips replaced labels: the replacement holds the content).

## Limits (accepted)
- A removed writer keeps its delete keys, but they alone delete nothing; its power ends when the sync key is re-registered, which every removal does. Until an admin steps, writers stamp and open with the keys of the epoch the host's head registers (they hold it by history boxes) and wrap under the newest they hold; a writer's rotation changes no delete key, so a label whose admins are all offline keeps its old step while content under it is already unreadable to the removed holder.
- A removed admin holds the current delete key until re-registration and can win the race at a host by registering keys only it knows. Remedy: a replacement label (above). Hostile admins are out of scope.
- A holder listed in no step since its last sync (a grant a rotation raced) is locked out until a later step lists it or a holder re-seals it (spec/requirements.md, Permissions); the rotation left it without boxes anyway, and the SHOULD re-seal (a holder without a box at a current epoch) repairs it at the next write.
- A holder whose missed rotations were compacted is refused at the step whose set the host dropped and is reachable only through its inbox; its stranded heartbeat tells co-readers. A walk costs one round trip per missed step. A node offline past `ROLE_KEEP` on its account's home label is refused at the first dropped set and has no inbox of its own: a sibling re-seals it over a direct connection, or it is restored from the card; the official host may keep home-label sets longer by policy. A dropped set also strands a holder whose next rotation is still readable (it cannot walk past the step).
- **Accepted limit (Tess):** when the stranded heartbeat's deposit is refused (the admins refreshed the deposit seed, or the label's budget is full) nobody learns the holder is stuck and it waits for a manual re-share (`Engine::reshare`, offered by clients). Accepted as rare, within "re-sealing is triggered, never guaranteed"; the alternative, a host claim log mapping slots to holders (a new log event kind), adds a third mechanism to cover the overlap of two. The rotation model never refreshes the deposit seed for this reason (its AMBIGUITIES.md residue 2), but the built heartbeat makes it common (below) until step 8. A stranded heartbeat is a deposit under the head's deposit key, sent only when the holder's deposit seed derives the head this node last opened there; every admin read rotation writes a fresh seed (spec/crypto.md §3, `refreshes_deposit`), so a holder that missed one deposits nothing the host accepts, and the heartbeat reaches admins only after rotations by non-admins or ones whose `keys.0` the holder read. Its re-seal also maps the heartbeat's node to a holder only up the node's record chain, so a node reading as its account is not re-sealed by it. Both go with the target's Stranded message and inbox notice (issues/account-signing.md step 8), which need no label deposit key and name the holder.
- Link keys claim the current step, so compaction and set retention never strand them.
- **Accepted limit (Tess):** a hinted-label walk (a link joiner reaching a label it knows only by ID) claims on the host's step before it can verify it, with the keys of the hint's actor only, so a dishonest host handing out its own step can recognise that one pseudonym at join time. Closing it needs `Path` to answer a claim-key holder (a wire change), declined.

- **Accepted (hostile host, traced, not run):** a host holding a hinted label under another thing can swap the real host's wraps before `label.0` is held: availability only, which the threat model trusts hosts for. If ever seen: never replace a wrap that opens under any held group.
- **Holder actors at depth 0** (spec/requirements.md, Permissions; notes/crypto.md): a party removed from a holder actor M of A reads A's content sealed to a keyset of M written without knowing the removal (an ignorant fork, or a forgery where the removal or the forgery is not held) until M's knowing rotation reaches A's writers; a grant some sealer has not seen lets a cycle form there until the writer holds the grants.
- **Deposit-only re-registration** (the head's deposit key is not the one this admin's seed derives, no newer epoch) waits until nothing under the label it can open, at a connected host that has not said it lacks it, is unopened, and no `keys.0` write of the actor signed by a current admin is pending (`keys_caught_up`, `Engine::keys_write_pending`). Unsigned or unreachable ones never count: anyone can send a pending commit, and an entry only a gone host lists never opens; either would hold the step back for good (an admin-signed write that never applies holds it back while held). Why: a co-admin's refresh step arrives before its `keys.0` write when blobs are not inline (or come from another host), and the laggard would step back to the old key, re-admitting a departed party's deposits until it applied the write (`lagging_admin_keeps_refreshed_deposit_key_spec_sync_5`). Left: a head registered while its `keys.0` upload was refused (rate limit, cap: `reregister` sends Upload then Register without waiting) still gets one step back, until the refresher's next pass uploads the write and steps again.
- `label.0` growth is about 250 B per step per group per rotation, plus claim sets (32 B per padded reader per step, plus entry boxes) until an admin drops them `ROLE_KEEP` after superseded (steps are a map keyed by step ID, so the drop is a field write); a host registered from a trimmed chain serves no claims at the trimmed steps. If growth ever matters, a root step carrying the current keys signed by the genesis delete key would let old steps be dropped.

## In node sync (`sync/open.rs`, `pass/register.rs`)
- **Open order**: the known head's sync key; the recorded steps deepest first by sync key; then by claim key (tried only once the sync keys failed, `Try::Rest`, filtered by a recorded claim set) as each **claimant** (`claimants`): an actor this node is or holds that a held edge write names on the thing, of any validity and whatever followed (`Held::holders_of`), or that its applied `secrets.0` boxes; its own X25519, its accounts' epoch KEM keys, the read-role KEM of every held read epoch of a held actor. Not the node's judgment of the roster: the claim set is the registrar's, and the open may bring what changes the judgment (a link's race winner holding the loser's earlier redemption but not the finalize that drops it judges itself out until K's new step, which it reaches only by claim: `a_node_judging_itself_out_claims_as_an_actor_a_held_write_names_spec_sync_5`). Not every held actor either: a walk costs the keys of the actors named on the thing it holds, per step (`stale_walk_claims_are_flat`, counted by `PassStats::claim_keys`); then held key actors by static entry, only those the group's `roles.0` lists (a hinted label: the key actor the hint follows for, `Hint::via`, kept when a later inbox hint names the label, and those the thing's held `roles.0` lists), since the signing key in a static proof names the key to the host and links labels. No key left: the label is `Failed` on that connection, places a stranded heartbeat (`stranded`; whatever was tried), and is started again from scratch after `resync_ms`; the retry recomputes the same tries, so a key missing from them stays missing until a re-seal or a commit naming it arrives (`walk_out_of_keys_is_stranded_spec_permissions_4`). Inbox-named actors join hinted walks only: on a recorded label a grant held only in a pending inbox commit heals by that re-seal instead (no inbox index per walk).
- A host's path is verified page by page (a bad page fails the label at once). `Step` replies are verified under the step tried and recorded in `label.0` by admins; a sync-key open names the step it is for (one try per held sync key, at the deepest recorded step bearing it: steps sharing a key are one run of the path, so a host refusing the deepest lacks the rest), since a step without a rotation (a key actor listed) shares its parent's sync key and a host matching by key alone answers the newest such step's child, which does not verify under an earlier one (a holder granted at the earlier step stranded; `sync_key_open_at_its_step_spec_sync_5`). Only a hint, knowing no step, opens by key alone (its walk is the host's word until the path verifies); a head not in `label.0` is checked against the host's path. `accept_head` asks `Path` after the opened head and walks it, since a step registered between an Open reply and the Subscribe is in neither pushes nor a cursor-less replay. Pushes for labels not started on the connection are dropped. Code 13 (unknown label) registers the recorded path (deepest branch).
- **Hinted walks** (no recorded step to verify a `Step` reply under, `LConn.walk`) claim only as the claimants, plus those the pending commits of the inbox blobs naming the thing grant or box, that this node is or holds (`Engine::hint_actors`; the boxes cover reading through an actor a rotation boxed with no grant held, `hinted_walk_claims_for_boxed_actor_spec_sync_5`), never every held actor's: a host handing out a step of its choosing would otherwise recognise the claimant by every pseudonym it signs with (`hinted_walk_claims_only_for_its_actors_spec_sync_5`). The hint's own grantee's pseudonym is still offered (the accepted limit above). The pending part of that actor set comes only from the commits of inbox blobs naming the thing, decoded unverified: a sender knowing another held actor's tid can widen it (accepted).
- A held key actor opens by static entry and learns the read secret from the head's entry box (`follow(via, thing, label, hosts)` for a card's or link's label, `via` the held key actor, index 0: a link's K and a card's account are created with that label); the static-entry open verifies the host's path first and takes the box's secret only when it derives the head's sync key. A hint opens at exactly the index it names (inbox payloads carry the label index, spec/wire.md §9; guessing indexes 0..4 would leave a group replaced 4+ times unopenable from an inbox).
- **A pass with no read epoch of the head held** does only two things: it opens or fetches every unopened entry there that opens under a held read secret (`read_first`: else one listed before that secret arrived is never fetched; seen in member_churn seeds, mostly on depth0 where merges make more forks: an admin never read the head's rotation, never registered or uploaded again, its label open and idle), then places a stranded heartbeat, only when the held deposit seed derives the head's deposit key (else it is signed with the wrong seed and refused, code 1: a sharer's other node opening a link's fresh K label before K's own `keys.0` reached the host derived K from the account).
- **Re-registration**: an admin holding a newer established epoch uploads the secret actor's role commits the host lacks under the head's keys, registers a child, and sends the rest after `Registered`. It registers a recorded child of the host's head before issuing anything (`recorded_child`), and `issue_step` reuses a recorded child of the head at the current registration epochs.
- **One step per rotation**: a node issues a new step only once another node's rotation of the actor (a remote role commit writing its `secrets.0`, `rotation_at`) is `SETTLE_MS` (30 s) old (`step_settled`; the author registers first). Without it each admin and each connection added a sibling step per rotation (`one_step_per_rotation_without_host_spec_sync_5`). The trigger counts only `secrets.0` writes: counting every edge write, an admin's `label.0` record of the previous step held each next rotation's step back, so two quick rotations shared one step and `stranded_reseal` failed on every seed.
- A Register refused for now (4, 12, 14) keeps the open and registers again at a later pass; the rest of that pass waits (nothing goes under the old keys). Steps queued behind a refused one each wait an interval (notes/serving.md, policy). An admin registers another node's rotation at once when that node is no admin (`used_link_refused_at_once_spec_permissions_7`).
- Rotations and re-seals: `join_late` runs the established-epoch hooks with the commit's held `keys.0`. Re-seals also go under the actor's labels (else later removals of their box entries never establish elsewhere), and `reseal_due` runs at the end of a complete pass; a stranded heartbeat under a label makes a node that may rotate re-seal the stranded node's actor (`reshare`, once per heartbeat commit) then too.
- Every encrypting path first runs flows' `ensure_rotated` on the label's actor (`uncovered`): an uncovered read role places nothing, an uncovered write stamps nothing, an uncovered admin steps nothing. This node's own self-removals (a held key actor's included) uncover nothing until placed (notes/engine-roles.md, Own self-removals).
