# Addenda
> Summary: Why writers answer a removal with an addendum listing the removed node's content commits they had received (spec/permissions.md §5.2), the rules and their reasons, what lying about receipt allows, how they are judged and indexed, measured cost, and the roles-model addenda results. Revocation: notes/revocation.md.

Principle: **a writer is a witness for what it had received**: after a removal, each writer of a thing may vouch for the removed node's plain-content commits that reached it, so a write the remover missed survives if any writer had it.

## Rules
- A node writes addenda by the state rule, on the vouch flow (`flows/vouches.rs` `addenda_owed`, notes/engine-roles.md Vouches, Write): for each explicit demotion R in its view taking X (not itself) below write on N, X's plain content commits on N or acting for N (and below admin, X's admin-only content on N that is not role-class, for a witness holding admin), ordered before R, in R's window (by its `ts` and before R itself, `vouches::window`) and not listed by its frontier are candidates; while it holds the role one needs on N now and no counting addendum of its own vouches for it, it writes one `addenda.0` entry on N keyed by R listing every candidate. So it writes on applying R, when it first holds write after (granted again, kept by a vouch), and whenever its own addendum stops counting; nothing durable (an open re-derives it). R's own node writes them too, for what its frontier left out (`receipt.rs`, over `MAX_LIST_BYTES`). An addendum is written as `put_new` (a new entry whatever is live): a plain put at a key another writer already answered writes into that writer's entry and says nothing. Every writer writes on its own, duplicates accepted. A write that fails (`OwnWriteFailed`) or waits for delete keys leaves the removal due. An addendum is content: it moves no component, so no flood of them makes a removal due.
- Judgment (osis-perm `Timeline::vouching`, the engine's `Judge::vouching`): the least fixpoint over the held addenda on a thing (those valid as their author's plain delta count first, then those they vouch for), kept to authors with write at their key; a part is valid when the base rule says so or a counting addendum lists its commit after a demotion of its author below the role it needs ordered after it: write, or admin for admin-only content that is not role-class, whose witness then held admin at its addendum's key (`Vouching::witnesses` with the role; the engine's `listable_part`). A commit's Acting check is its part on its header actor, vouched for there by a writer of the actor (every osis commit acts for its account, so asking the witness for write on the actor would let nobody vouch for anything).
- Why each rule: writers only (a reader could combine with an ex-writer); after the removal, about earlier commits (a removed writer cannot backdate one, nor two vouch for each other in a cycle); content only (role-class and purge writes stay with the frontier; admin-only content needs a witness holding admin, as it needs an admin to write it: a partly vouched role commit splits, and role-write witnesses never settle, notes/role-timeline.md); an addendum is an ordinary write audited by its author's removals (and may list a fallen addendum: Dave vouches for Carol's). Earlier addenda keep counting after a re-removal.
- Receipt time is local and unprovable: a writer of N lying about receipt makes the removed side's backdated writes on N count, attributed to it; the writer could write the content itself, not as the removed side. Per part, the removed side of a holder T removed from N includes T's current members: a writer of N vouches for the part on N and the member still writes T, so nothing more is needed (`tests_addenda::lying_writer_vouches_for_a_removed_teams_write_spec_perm_5_2`); a member removed from T needs a writer of T as well (`acting_unvouched_invalidates_every_part_spec_perm_5_2`). Whether this squares with spec/requirements.md "rights held by different parties never combine into a right neither has alone" is Tess's call.
- Addenda list content commits only: a removed writer's compaction snapshot the frontier left out falls, and what it covered is fetched again (the sim relies on that).
- Addenda are decoded whole like role parts (`whole_part`), indexed per (thing, commit) from their bodies (`Indexes::addenda`, derived at open), never compacted, dropped while invalid like any body (a body-less or purged addendum is in the fixpoint listing nothing, so a later addendum can still vouch for it and its body is fetched again). A thing with addenda is re-judged whole whenever its author- or commit-restricted re-judging could change an addendum (`rejudge_once`); measured (`scale::addenda_rejudge_cost_*`, release, 10^4 commits on the thing): a member joining a holder of it 0.65 ms without an addendum, 12.3 ms with one (about 1.1 µs per commit on the thing), within budget.
- Differential: osis-perm `differential::addenda_model_agrees` (the judge against the model's `ideal` under `addenda::GAPS` with admin-only content, half the worlds with forged addenda). Its random worlds rarely hold a writer's addendum listing admin-only content (disabling the admin-witness check fails no world in the large run), so hand tests pin it: `tests_addenda::admin_only_content_is_kept_by_an_admin_witness`, `admin_only_content_is_not_kept_by_a_writers_addendum`. Addenda against a rotation (spec/permissions.md §5.5) still read plain content without admin-only (`plain_part`).

## Addenda model
`core/test/roles-model/src/addenda.rs` (`roles-model -- N addenda`, `-- 0 addenda-exh` (2.83M worlds, 11 min)): layer 2 with several authors and things, acting commits, special (unlistable) writes, snapshots; honest witnesses publish from their own view at receipt of the removal.
- 1M random worlds and the exhaustive family pass: one result for every input order, monotone (removing an honest addendum never makes a write valid), the least fixpoint settles in at most 4 rounds and equals the greatest, the role layer unchanged, forged addenda by nodes of any role (any removal, thing, `ts`, list, up to three) gain nothing beyond the forger's own write power.
- Counting every removal's frontier and any later removal's addenda (the rule) is a superset of newest-only and differs in about 1 in 40,000 random worlds (Dave vouched for the first removal; Bob re-granted and removed again while Dave is offline); bounding frontiers to earlier writes differs in 0.6% (receipts may precede an item's `ts`).
- Bodies dropped at once and fetched from any holder: nodes agree and read no more than the no-drop result, but lose writes: a re-grant restores nothing every holder dropped (27k of 1M worlds), and 8% of the writes valid only through addenda (the witness judged the write or its own addendum invalid before vouching and dropped it); keeping what held addenda list cut it to 208, only keeping invalid bodies to none.
- Cover rules (`addenda-covers`: two admins, a snapshot by any node carrying the leaver's edit, its author removed or not, 38,656 worlds): leaving out what another writer's snapshot covers loses a visible write in 544 family worlds (3 in 1M random); leaving out only what the leaver's own snapshot covers, the list naming it, loses none there, in 1M random worlds or in `addenda-exh`; addenda naming a snapshot gain no attack and stay monotone.
- S3 brief 3 (`Gaps`: addenda reaching admin-only content that is not role-class, and a witness writing when it first holds the role after the removal): core/test/roles-model/README.md, Addenda gaps. Holds in 1M random worlds and a 2.83M-world family; each rule's control fails. Late addenda lengthen the window in which every node drops the body (2,741 of 33,349 such commits lost with drop-at-once).
- Cost: random worlds 0.06 addenda per removal; one removal, each commit received by each node with probability 1/2: listed IDs grow as writers × commits / 4, nearly all duplicates (30 writers × 100 commits: 752 IDs in 30 addenda, about 24 KB).
