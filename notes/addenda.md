# Addenda
> Summary: Why writers answer a removal with an addendum listing the removed node's content commits they had received (spec/permissions.md §5.2), the rules and their reasons, what lying about receipt allows, how they are judged and indexed, and what was measured. Revocation: notes/revocation.md.

Principle: **a writer is a witness for what it had received**: after a removal, each writer of a thing may vouch for the removed node's plain-content commits that reached it, so a write the remover missed survives if any writer had it.

## Rules
- A node writes addenda by the state rule (`flows/addenda.rs` `gather`, `owed`; notes/engine-roles.md Addenda): for each cut an applied lowering R in its view makes (its hop taken below write: plain content; below admin: admin-only content that is not role-class, for a witness holding admin), the content commits not its own with a delta stated along a path through that hop (`Paths::rests`, read off the walk brought up to the held set first: it takes a content commit applied with nothing else moving only at its next update), ordered before R, in R's window (by its `ts` and before R itself, `addenda::window`) and not listed by its frontier are candidates; while it holds the role one needs on N now and no counting addendum of its own vouches for it, it writes one `addenda.0` entry on N keyed by R listing every candidate. So it writes on applying R, when it first holds write after (granted again), and whenever its own addendum stops counting; nothing durable (an open re-derives it). R's own node writes them too, for what its frontier left out (`receipt.rs`, over `MAX_LIST_BYTES`). An addendum is written as `put_new` (a new entry whatever is live): a plain put at a key another writer already answered writes into that writer's entry and says nothing. Every writer writes on its own, duplicates accepted. A write that fails (`OwnWriteFailed`) or waits for delete keys leaves the removal due. An addendum is content: it moves no edge, so no flood of them makes a removal due.
- Judgment (spec/permissions.md §5.2 Content, Addenda; osis-perm `paths`, notes/perm-paths.md): a delta is valid where no cut of its stated path refuses it; a cut is answered by its own list, or by a counting addendum against it listing the commit, by a writer of the thing at its key (a witness holding admin for admin-only content that is not role-class), and answers for itself alone (notes/a1-spec/README.md Open 24). Addenda are judged as one least fixpoint over those held on the thing; no addendum vouches for a role-class delta, a snapshot, a commit carrying a purge request or a role commit's addendum.
- Why each rule: writers only (a reader could combine with an ex-writer); after the removal, about earlier commits (a removed writer cannot backdate one, nor two vouch for each other in a cycle); content only (role-class and purge writes stay with the frontier; admin-only content needs a witness holding admin, as it needs an admin to write it: a partly vouched role commit splits, and role-write witnesses never settle, notes/permissions.md); an addendum is an ordinary write audited by its author's removals (and may list a fallen addendum: Dave vouches for Carol's). Earlier addenda keep counting after a re-removal.
- Receipt time is local and unprovable: a writer of N lying about receipt makes the removed side's backdated writes on N count, attributed to it; the writer could write the content itself, not as the removed side. Per part, the removed side of a holder T removed from N includes T's current members: a writer of N vouches for the part on N and the member still writes T, so nothing more is needed (`tests_addenda::lying_writer_vouches_for_a_removed_holders_write_spec_perm_5_2`); a member removed from T needs a writer of T as well. Whether this squares with spec/requirements.md "rights held by different parties never combine into a right neither has alone" is Tess's call.
- Addenda list content commits only: a removed writer's compaction snapshot the frontier left out falls, and what it covered is fetched again (the sim relies on that).
- Addenda are decoded whole like role parts (`whole_part`), indexed per (thing, commit) from their bodies (`Indexes::addenda`, derived at open), never compacted, dropped while invalid like any body (a body-less or purged addendum is in the fixpoint listing nothing, so a later addendum can still vouch for it and its body is fetched again). The walk judges addenda again only where an update reaches them (notes/perm-paths.md, addenda by reach); the engine judges a thing with addenda whole where a role moved on it or an addendum's own commit is judged again (`rejudge_once`); counted at n and 10n commits on the thing (`scale::addenda_rejudge_spec_permissions_5_2`): a member joining a holder of it re-judges nothing per commit without an addendum, linearly in the thing's commits with one held.
- Its random worlds rarely hold a writer's addendum listing admin-only content (disabling the admin-witness check fails no world in the large run), so hand tests pin it: `tests_addenda::admin_only_content_is_kept_by_an_admin_witness`, `admin_only_content_is_not_kept_by_a_writers_addendum`. Addenda against a rotation (spec/permissions.md §5.5) still read plain content without admin-only (`plain_part`).

## Measured
Results of a model of the addenda rules over the role rules before stated paths (honest witnesses publishing from their own view at receipt of the removal); the `paths` model judges addenda along stated paths (notes/path-model-findings.md). Kept as records of what the rules give:
- 1M random worlds and an exhaustive family: one result for every input order, monotone (removing an honest addendum never makes a write valid), the least fixpoint settles in at most 4 rounds and equals the greatest, forged addenda by nodes of any role (any removal, thing, `ts`, list, up to three) gain nothing beyond the forger's own write power.
- Counting every removal's frontier and any later removal's addenda (the rule) is a superset of newest-only and differs in about 1 in 40,000 random worlds (Dave vouched for the first removal; Bob re-granted and removed again while Dave is offline); bounding frontiers to earlier writes differs in 0.6% (receipts may precede an item's `ts`).
- Bodies dropped at once and fetched from any holder: nodes agree and read no more than the no-drop result, but lose writes: a re-grant restores nothing every holder dropped (27k of 1M worlds), and 8% of the writes valid only through addenda (the witness judged the write or its own addendum invalid before vouching and dropped it); keeping what held addenda list cut it to 208, only keeping invalid bodies to none.
- Cover rules (two admins, a snapshot by any node carrying the leaver's edit, its author removed or not, 38,656 worlds): leaving out what another writer's snapshot covers loses a visible write in 544 family worlds (3 in 1M random); leaving out only what the leaver's own snapshot covers, the list naming it, loses none; addenda naming a snapshot gain no attack and stay monotone.
- Addenda reaching admin-only content that is not role-class, and a witness writing when it first holds the role after the removal: held in 1M random worlds and a 2.83M-world family, each rule's control failing. Late addenda lengthen the window in which every node drops the body (2,741 of 33,349 such commits lost with drop-at-once).
- Cost: 0.06 addenda per removal in random worlds; one removal, each commit received by each node with probability 1/2: listed IDs grow as writers × commits / 4, nearly all duplicates (30 writers × 100 commits: 752 IDs in 30 addenda, about 24 KB).
