Each a question about the model, with what a user would observe.
1. **How long a far document shows a removed person's new writes.** A removal reaches a document the removed person could write through the removed membership (however many grants down) when some device holding the removal and able to read that document next syncs and reaches its servers. Until then its readers show the removed (or stolen) device's new writes there as valid, and a complete sync of the document does not end that (finding 29). Where no such device exists or comes online, the writes stay shown. Today a far document learns of the removal when a writer of each thing in between next writes (each rotates its keys, carrying the removal), or when a device syncing both carries it; with no such writer or device, never. A3 is as fast or faster than today in every world built (The delivery duty, When a far reader learns). Is "until a device holding the removal and reading the document next reaches it" within "only propagation waits"?
2. **A remover's device lost right after removing.** If the removal reached any server that another live device holding read on the organisation syncs, that device delivers it (no designated device), with the list of what the removed person wrote, kept with the removal under the organisation's own sync ID for good. If it reached no such server, it is lost like any unsynced change. Acceptable?
3. **What every device pays per removal.** Every member device holding a removal checks, once, each document sync ID the removal reaches and it can read, until it sees the removal there, and keeps a small record per (removal, document) for good. In an organisation of 1,000 member devices writing 1,000 documents one removal is about 2,000 stored entries in all, and about 1,000 sync ID checks per device, a million across the organisation, all but one per document redundant. Restricting the checks to the remover's devices would make the delivery fail when those devices are lost first (modelled). Is that cost acceptable, or is losing the removal with the remover's devices acceptable?
4. **What a document stores per removal of a writer above it.** For good, at every document the removed writer could reach: the removal and the grant it ends (about 1.6 KB) plus 64 bytes for each thing the removed writer had written that the remover held (the list root); while a write the list judges is kept, that document's own part of the list (32 bytes per listed write there); and the key rotation the removal forces (about 21 KB, as today). A team that wrote 1,000 documents makes the root 64 KB, stored at every document the team could reach, per removal (issues/zero-growth.md table). A tree in place of the flat root makes it about 32 bytes at the cost of a small part per document read (OPEN 1). Which?
5. **Who can keep a removal from arriving.** Anyone who can write a document (the removed device included, until the document's keys change after the removal) can delete the removal's copy at its servers once the devices delivering it saw it there: they do not deliver it again (finding 36). Readers that pulled it before keep it and put it back; readers served only by those servers that had not pulled it never see it. Today such a document gets the removal from no one at all. Is that within "authenticated malicious writers are bounded"?
6. **A removed person granted again keeps old lists alive.** A document keeps a removal's list part as long as the person writes it through the same membership again; possibly only until the next re-grant (finding 34, not modelled; OPEN 4).
7. **A way cut before the removal arrives** (OPEN 6). A stolen device writes a document through the organisation; the document's admin then removes the organisation (keeping that write, which it had received); the organisation's admin then removes the device. No member device can deliver the removal to the document any more, so the write stays shown there unless a device that syncs both carries it. Accept, or must the document's admin, by removing the organisation, take on something?
8. **Admin-only content of a demoted admin** (OPEN 7, finding 35). An admin demoted to writer had written admin-only content (settings, say) on documents below the organisation. The demotion reaches those documents only with their next key rotation; where it does, the demoted admin's earlier admin-only content, which the remover had received and listed, then waits at those documents' readers for good (the list part is not delivered with it); where it does not, the demoted admin's later admin-only writes show as valid. Should a demotion from admin travel to every document below as a removal does (a copy per document per admin demoted), or is one of those outcomes acceptable?
9. **A valid write lost after a complete sync** (OPEN 12, finding 33). A removal delivered to a document while an earlier removal that cancels it (its writer had been removed first) has not arrived yet makes readers drop a write as invalid; if every reader dropped it and its writer's device is gone, the write is lost though it is valid. Waiting for a complete sync narrows this to "the cancelling removal had not reached the document's servers", not to never. Accept, or keep every write dropped for a removal until ... (it is a removed writer's junk too)?
10. **An organisation keeps every list it ever made.** The list parts of a removal for the documents below it stay with the removal in the organisation's own sync ID for good (any device must be able to deliver them: finding 30); with 30-day history that is about 96 KB per team removal (issues/zero-growth.md). And a document's part stays whole while any one of its listed writes is kept, so one kept delete by the removed person keeps it for good. Is that growth "with role changes made on it"?
11. **Invitations by a removed member** (finding 37). A removed member who could invite (not write) through the organisation can, with a stolen key, keep inviting people onto documents below it, shown valid until a device carries the removal there: A3 delivers removals that take write away only. Today alike. Must removals that take an invite away travel too?

## The list form
- **Root** (the removal's own commit, field 12): the role entries (commit IDs of the role commits it lists, any thing) and one (key, hash) pair per thing on which it lists anything else. Nothing per thing in clear: the key is `kdf("list leaf", dedup secret of the thing, blind ‖ tid)`, so only that thing's readers find their pair. The root travels in every copy of the removal.
- **Leaf** (a blob per (removal, thing), wrapped per sync ID as a certificate copy): the thing, the commit IDs listed there (content, purge requests, addenda, snapshots, own covers: every commit with a part on that thing resting on the lowered edge that the remover had received), a salt. An entry names its commit's part on that thing alone (finding 24), so a commit is listed in each thing's leaf it has such a part on.
- **When a leaf is read** (finding 27): only for a part its removal cuts, which for content (needing write) is where the removal takes write away on its edge: a held chained write giving write or above ordered before it, or a record edge. A reader then decides from what it holds: no pair for its thing in the root: nothing listed; its leaf held: listed or not; else it waits for the leaf. A removal that cuts nothing is read by no verdict, so no reader waits for its leaf. For admin-only content the cut is a lowering below admin (finding 35, OPEN 7).
- **Where leaves go**: under each sync ID with a body on the leaf's thing that the removal cuts (finding 6: evidence of every commit its verdict reads), and a leaf for a thing other than the removal's own with its removal wherever the removal is a body, for good (finding 30: so a node holding the removal holds the leaf a write it judges elsewhere needs; finding 34: the own thing's leaf leaves with the last body it reads). A leaf is one blob, kept whole while any commit it reads is held (finding 34 (b)).
- **The writers' leaving out** (The list): reads the role entries alone; a leaf names no edge-class part and never decides whether the removal applies, so every leaf is written whole.
- **A remover that does not hold a thing** lists nothing there (no pair): every cut part there stands only by an addendum (content); a role write cut there falls (issues/role-vouch-contested.md). No hear-out (Tess: core removes when told).

## Far delivery
A removal reaches a thing other than its own as today: with that thing's next key rotation by a writer holding it (its keyset carries the demotion: spec/crypto.md §4 Carried demotions; A3 places that copy by the required set, notes/a3-spec/crypto.md), and wherever a node holds both the removal and a write it judges (the removal is on the write's path, so in the evidence of the write's sync IDs). Nothing logs a removal anywhere for its own sake (Tess: no eager delivery, notes/handoff.md). Where neither happens (a thing no remaining member writes, its readers served only by stores), its readers show the removed key's later writes there.
- **Open modelling question** (wanted if simple and good; nothing built): a node that only reads the thing deposits the removal there when it holds a write the removal cuts, the copy leaving with the writes it judges. Model it first in held-model; if it is trouble, the gap above is an open bug in an issue, not something to build around.
- **The model**: held-model has no keysets, so it cannot check far delivery by rotation. Its properties that hold everything (the family slice, `one_sync_id_reader_judges_as_everything_*`, `evidence_along_joined_chained_paths_*`, `evidence_reach_and_generator_gaps_*`) run over `Rule::fixed` and `Stage::A3`, which still carry the drafted remover's errand (`Errand`) and reach (`Rule::reach`); without them they fail in finding 17's class, where today too delivers only by rotation or a bridge. They are restated for lazy delivery at notes/redesign-plan.md, D. The design restated (a keyset per rotation, or the class stated), and the errand and reach leave the model then. The engine derivation and its differential use `Stage::A2`, which has neither.

## Where the model overrode the design notes
1. **A leaf is read only where its lowering takes write away** (finding 27): the growth study and A2's draft had every leaf a held root shows waited on; a removal cutting nothing then strands its readers.
2. **A removal's leaves for other things stay with it wherever it is a body** (findings 30, 34): the growth study had leaves only where a commit they judge is; a node holding the removal then lacks the leaf a write it judges elsewhere needs. Its own thing's leaf leaves with what it reads.
3. **A complete pass does not end the window in which a removal written earlier arrives** (finding 33): A2's Provisional had acts after a complete pass reversed only by later writes.
4. **Role changes travel stays whole** (finding 31): read access through a holder thing reads its keys-open half.
5. Earlier, kept: copies a function of holdings (2), a copy carrying its leaf for its own thing (4), a leaf evidence of every commit its verdict reads (6), an entry naming its part on its leaf's thing (24).

## The two parked leaks
Both wait for the account redesign (notes/priorities.md item 3); A3 closes neither.
- **A removed device reads later grants' role parts** (issues/removed-device-reads-later-grants.md): unchanged by A3, which wraps its copies and leaves as any certificate copy (under the sync ID's current wrap), so they show a removed party nothing it lacks. Smallest scenario after A3: X removed from S at the rotation minting e; Y's grant of read to Z on S, wrapped under the epoch e supersedes; X, still opening S at a store that has not taken S's new step, opens the grant's role parts (Z's name, the box keys). Re-judged at A5: its candidate "the plain rule with inbox delivery" fails today because a recipient may hold only an older epoch and have no inbox (a link left out of a profile rotation); A5 gives every enabled thing an inbox as a card aspect and publishes KEM keys only for holders and enabled things, which is what that candidate lacks [assumed: whether a link becomes such a thing is A5's spec].
- **A departed party reads the fresh deposit seed** (issues/fresh-deposit-seed-readable-by-departed.md): A2 closes its third route (a thing's current `keys.0` no longer travels as evidence); A3 closes nothing more (the rotation commit's wrap, as above, and keysets carried as keys stay; A3's keyset seed moves only demotions, no `keys.0`). Smallest scenario after A3: X removed from M; M's rotation writes a fresh seed in M's `keys.0`; A's next rotation, sealing M at its new keyset, logs M's rotation commit (seed included) under A's sync IDs, which X still reads as a direct holder of A. Re-judged at A5: the issue's only clean fix moves the seed out of certificate-visible `keys.0`, and A5 replaces `keys.0`'s inbox field by the card aspect and the admin-only aspect naming a thing's inbox and cards (notes/redesign-plan.md A5 Changes), where the seed can live sealed under the new epoch.
