## For Tess
Each a question about the model, with what a user would observe.
1. **How long a far document shows a removed person's new writes.** A removal reaches a document the removed person could write through the removed membership (however many grants down) when some device holding the removal and able to read that document next syncs and reaches its servers. Until then its readers show the removed (or stolen) device's new writes there as valid, and a complete sync of the document does not end that (finding 29). Where no such device exists or comes online, the writes stay shown. Today a far document learns of the removal when a writer of each thing in between next writes (each rotates its keys, carrying the removal), or when a device syncing both carries it; with no such writer or device, never. A3 is as fast or faster than today in every world built (The delivery duty, When a far reader learns). Is "until a device holding the removal and reading the document next reaches it" within "only propagation waits"?
2. **A remover's device lost right after removing.** If the removal reached any server that another live device holding read on the organisation syncs, that device delivers it (no designated device), with the list of what the removed person wrote, kept with the removal under the organisation's own sync ID for good. If it reached no such server, it is lost like any unsynced change. Acceptable?
3. **What every device pays per removal.** Every member device holding a removal checks, once, each document sync ID the removal reaches and it can read, until it sees the removal there, and keeps a small record per (removal, document) for good. In an organisation of 1,000 member devices writing 1,000 documents one removal is about 2,000 stored entries in all, and about 1,000 sync ID checks per device, a million across the organisation, all but one per document redundant. Restricting the checks to the remover's devices would make the delivery fail when those devices are lost first (modelled). Is that cost acceptable, or is losing the removal with the remover's devices acceptable?
4. **What a document stores per removal of a writer above it.** For good, at every document the removed writer could reach: the removal and the grant it ends (about 1.6 KB) plus 64 bytes for each thing the removed writer had written that the remover held (the list root); while a write the list judges is kept, that document's own part of the list (32 bytes per listed write there); and the key rotation the removal forces (about 21 KB, as today). A team that wrote 1,000 documents makes the root 64 KB, stored at every document the team could reach, per removal (issues/zero-growth.md table). A tree in place of the flat root makes it about 32 bytes at the cost of a small part per document read (OPEN 1). Which?
5. **Who can keep a removal from arriving.** Anyone who can write a document (the removed device included, until the document's keys change after the removal) can delete the removal's copy at its servers once the devices delivering it saw it there: they do not deliver it again (finding 36). Readers that pulled it before keep it and put it back; readers served only by those servers that had not pulled it never see it. Today such a document gets the removal from no one at all. Is that within "authenticated malicious writers are bounded"?
6. **A removed person granted again keeps old lists alive.** A document keeps a removal's list part as long as the person writes it through the same membership again; possibly only until the next re-grant (finding 34, not modelled; OPEN 4).
7. **A way cut before the removal arrives** (OPEN 6). A stolen device writes a document through the organisation; the document's admin then removes the organisation (keeping that write, which it had received); the organisation's admin then removes the device. No member device can deliver the removal to the document any more, so the write stays shown there unless a device that syncs both carries it. Accept, or must the document's admin, by removing the organisation, take on something?
8. **Admin-only content of a demoted admin** (OPEN 7, finding 35). An admin demoted to writer had written admin-only content (settings, say) on documents below the organisation. The demotion reaches those documents only with their next key rotation; where it does, the demoted admin's earlier admin-only content, which the remover had received and listed, then waits at those documents' readers for good (the list part is not delivered with it); where it does not, the demoted admin's later admin-only writes show as valid. Should a demotion from admin travel to every document below as a removal does (a copy per document per admin demoted), or is one of those outcomes acceptable?
9. **A valid write lost after a complete sync** (OPEN 12, finding 33). A removal delivered to a document while an earlier removal that cancels it (its writer had been removed first) has not arrived yet makes readers drop a write as invalid; if every reader dropped it and its writer's device is gone, the write is lost though it is valid. Waiting for a complete sync narrows this to "the cancelling removal had not reached the document's servers", not to never. Accept, or keep every write dropped for a removal until ... (it is a removed writer's junk too)?
10. **An organisation keeps every list it ever made.** The list parts of a removal for the documents below it stay with the removal in the organisation's own sync ID for good (any device must be able to deliver them: finding 30); with 30-day history that is about 96 KB per team removal (issues/zero-growth.md). And a document's part stays whole while any one of its listed writes is kept, so one kept delete by the removed person keeps it for good. Is that growth "with role changes made on it"?
11. **Invitations by a removed member** (finding 37). A removed member who could invite (not write) through the organisation can, with a stolen key, keep inviting people onto documents below it, shown valid until a device carries the removal there: A3 delivers removals that take write away only. Today alike. Must removals that take an invite away travel too?

## The list form
- **Root** (the removal's own commit, field 12): the role entries (commit IDs of the role commits it lists, any thing) and one (key, hash) pair per thing on which it lists anything else. Nothing per thing in clear: the key is `kdf("list leaf", dedup secret of the thing, blind ‖ tid)`, so only that thing's readers find their pair. The root travels in every copy of the removal, so every reached sync ID stores it for good (OPEN 1).
- **Leaf** (a blob per (removal, thing), wrapped per sync ID as a certificate copy): the thing, the commit IDs listed there (content, purge requests, addenda, snapshots, own covers: every commit with a part on that thing resting on the lowered edge that the remover had received), a salt. An entry names its commit's part on that thing alone (finding 24), so a commit is listed in each thing's leaf it has such a part on.
- **When a leaf is read** (finding 27): only for a part its removal cuts, which for content (needing write) is where the removal takes write away on its edge: a held chained write giving write or above ordered before it, or a record edge; the same test reach's witness reads, so a leaf waited on is one the duty carries. A reader then decides from what it holds: no pair for its thing in the root: nothing listed; its leaf held: listed or not; else it waits for the leaf. A removal that cuts nothing is read by no verdict, so no reader waits for its leaf. For admin-only content the cut is a lowering below admin, which no duty carries (finding 35, OPEN 7).
- **Where leaves go**: under each sync ID with a body on the leaf's thing that the removal cuts (finding 6: evidence of every commit its verdict reads), and a leaf for a thing other than the removal's own with its removal wherever the removal is a body, for good (finding 30: so every holder of a removal holds what any runner carries; finding 34: the own thing's leaf leaves with the last body it reads). A leaf is one blob, kept whole while any commit it reads is held (finding 34 (b)).
- **The writers' leaving out** (The list): reads the role entries alone; a leaf names no edge-class part and never decides whether the removal applies, so every leaf is written whole.
- **A remover that does not hold a thing** lists nothing there (no pair): every cut part there stands only by an addendum (content) or by a vouch (role writes; under `remake`, by being written again). No hear-out (Tess: core removes when told).

## The delivery duty
- **Who**: every honest node holding the removal R, for each target it holds read or above on by a path (finding 32): the remover, any other member's device, a reader of a reached thing (it runs the duty there and below, whether or not it reads R's own thing). No designated device: a remover's node lost right after removing hands the duty to any holder (`the_errand_runs_at_any_honest_holder_when_the_removers_node_is_lost`; the family with the remover lost: `Runners::Remover` fails, `AnyHolder` holds; property `a_lost_removers_duty_falls_to_another_holder_*`). Who holds R besides the remover: the syncers of R's own thing, of the removed holder's own sync ID and of every thing whose keys open it (Role changes travel, kept whole: finding 31), and of every target it already reached. The runner checks chained-ness from its own view (the closure takes chained writes only: finding 16): an unchained "removal" (a non-admin's) never enters a duty.
- **Where**: the own sync ID of t and of every thing reached from t by chained grants giving write or above and record edges, at any time, followed to the end (finding 1), each reached thing's grants read under its own sync ID; enclosing units get it from the target's own sync ID by their readers' required set (the standing duty of spec/sync-set.md §2).
- **When**: standing (finding 3): whenever the node's view shows a target without a record that a source showed the copy: a holder raised after the removal (Risk 4), a backdated grant, a new holder of a reached thing, a retag; between, in the runner's passes. The delay a far reader sees is a runner's time to its next pass reaching a source of that sync ID; a complete pass there before that does not end it (finding 29: P1-window holds at every step, P1 only once the copy lands).
- **When a far reader learns, against today.** Today a far document D (below the hub A through grants) learns of a writer's removal from A by a rotation cascade: A's rotation carries the demotion in A's new keyset; a writer of each thing in between, holding the keyset above, finds its box to that holder stale and rotates before its next write there, logging the keyset with its carried demotions under that thing's sync IDs (spec/crypto.md §4 Encrypting under, Carried, Carried demotions); or by a bridge (a node syncing D and holding the removal logs it as a chain certificate of a body there). Under A3 the keyset route stays (the keyset seed, below) and the duty and reach are added. Worlds:
  - **No live holder of R can reach any source of D**: never under A3; never today (each of today's routes is a holder of R, or of a keyset carrying it, reaching D). Equal.
  - **D synced only by readers that hold no R; a holder reads D and reaches its stores**: A3, at that holder's next pass reaching a store of D (readers pull deposited entries from a store, spec/sync-streams.md §4 Fill order); today, never (no honest writer of D holds the removal; no bridge). A3 better.
  - **D's only writers hostile (the thief's accomplice); the runner only reads D**: A3, the runner deposits; readers that pull it before the accomplice deletes it learn and keep it (and deposit it again); the runners stop once a source showed it (finding 36), so readers served only by stores the accomplice cleans first never learn; today, never. A3 better or equal.
  - **An honest writer of D holds R**: A3, at its next sync of D (its view requires R there: logged at once); today, when it next writes D (it rotates first). A3 better or equal.
  - **The remover lost before its first pass; R's only live holders sync a thing whose keys open the removed holder** (finding 31's world): today, at that holder's next sync (keys-open travel); as drafted before review, never; with Role changes travel kept whole, as today.
  - **A cut way** (OPEN 6): no runner reads D any more; equal to today (a bridge only).
  So with Role changes travel whole, A3 delivers in every world built at least when today does.
- **By what right and key**: a holder of read or above on a target holds the keys the way's grants box to their holders, which travel under each holder's own sync ID with the target's `sync.0` (Role changes travel). So it opens each target's own sync ID as a reader of a holder does, stamps where its role on the target gives write, else deposits; a deposited copy is not promoted (A2: no certificate copy is), it is logged by any writer of the target whose view requires it as its own copy, at once (the required set reads no verdict), and the target's readers judge with it as soon as they hold it. A deposit can be cleared (`DEPOSITS_WAITING`) or deleted by a hostile writer (finding 36). A runner whose way no longer gives read cannot open the target: OPEN 6.
- **What a runner stores, and for how long**: per target, while the hold lasts: the target's current step (its keys to open and deposit or stamp), the grants naming the target as holder under its own sync ID (what the duty follows), and in its own store of that sync ID the entries it logs (R, its witness, reach and their evidence closure, the leaves it carries there); nothing of it enters the node closure. The hold ends once a source shows the copy; its own store's entries of that sync ID then go (spec/commits.md §5 What a held thing needs). A target with no source online stays held, retried at every pass: attributable to the synced sync ID holding R (unsyncing it ends the duty and the hold). Kept for good: a record per (removal, target) that a source showed the copy, so the duty does not reopen it (OPEN 2's cost). The copies stay at the target, required there for good while its unit holds a thing the removal reaches (attributable to the target's sync ID; gone when it is unsynced everywhere).
- **Idempotence and races**: several runners log one entry (copies are (commit, thing): `several_runners_log_one_entry_per_copy_*`, entries equal at every step); a thief racing the duty is shown until the copy lands (finding 29); a removal voided later by an earlier-ordered removal of its writer stays chained (chained-ness disregards removals), so its copies stay where it reached; it is harmless to verdicts once the voiding removal (taking write away from the voided remover on the same or an enclosing edge, so reaching at least as far) lands, and wrong in between (finding 33). A removed admin's key can sign any number of such chained, void removals, each run by every holder (OPEN 13).
- **Cost** per removal taking write away: one entry and stamp per reached own sync ID and per enclosing sync ID carrying a reached thing (the removal, its witness, the grants on the ways not already there), stored as one blob per copy at each store; per runner, one hold of each target sync ID it reads until it sees the copy, and a record per target for good. An organisation's hub of 1,000 documents: about 2,000 entries in all (removal and witness per document; A2 OPEN 16 measured the copies at 782 B each, plus the root: For Tess 4), and up to 1,000 opens per member device holding the removal: N devices × D documents across the organisation (For Tess 3, OPEN 2). Counted at A3 brief 8 (n and 10n things, under one and two sync IDs).

## One mechanism or two (carried demotions and reach)
Not folded: two mechanisms still deliver demotions. A3 changes only where the keyset route's copies are placed: the demotions a keyset S requires carries become a seed of S's required set, the same (removal, thing) copy reach requires where both do (one blob, logged once), logged by any node whose view requires it, in place of "placed with the keyset by whoever places it" (notes/a3-spec/crypto.md). Each keeps its own reader and its own trigger:
- **reach and the duty**: validity where the removed party could write; triggered by the removal, delivered to every reached document whether or not anyone rotates.
- **the keyset seed**: sealing and judging a holder's keyset (spec/crypto.md §4 Held keyset: a keyset is held only with the demotions it carries); triggered by a rotation of the thing (made by its writers, lazily before their next write); it alone carries what reach never does: a demotion on M where M only reads the thing, and a demotion from admin to write (which takes no write away: finding 35 shows it then delivers a demotion whose admin-only leaves it cannot carry).
Costs, unchanged by A3 except the shared copy: per writer removal, a reader of one document downloads about 21 KB on a first read (A2 OPEN 16), almost all of it the rotation's key material, which follows every grant whichever mechanism carries the demotion; reach adds the removal and its witness (1.6 KB), logged once where the keyset route also requires the removal. Whether one mechanism can serve both readers is OPEN 8; nothing here builds toward it.

## The two parked leaks
Both wait for the account redesign (notes/priorities.md item 3); A3 closes neither.
- **A removed device reads later grants' role parts** (issues/removed-device-reads-later-grants.md): unchanged by A3, which wraps its copies and leaves as any certificate copy (under the sync ID's current wrap), so they show a removed party nothing it lacks. Smallest scenario after A3: X removed from S at the rotation minting e; Y's grant of read to Z on S, wrapped under the epoch e supersedes; X, still opening S at a store that has not taken S's new step, opens the grant's role parts (Z's name, the box keys). Re-judged at A5: its candidate "the plain rule with inbox delivery" fails today because a recipient may hold only an older epoch and have no inbox (a link left out of a profile rotation); A5 gives every enabled thing an inbox as a card aspect and publishes KEM keys only for holders and enabled things, which is what that candidate lacks [assumed: whether a link becomes such a thing is A5's spec].
- **A departed party reads the fresh deposit seed** (issues/fresh-deposit-seed-readable-by-departed.md): A2 closes its third route (a thing's current `keys.0` no longer travels as evidence); A3 closes nothing more (the rotation commit's wrap, as above, and keysets carried as keys stay; A3's keyset seed moves only demotions, no `keys.0`). Smallest scenario after A3: X removed from M; M's rotation writes a fresh seed in M's `keys.0`; A's next rotation, sealing M at its new keyset, logs M's rotation commit (seed included) under A's sync IDs, which X still reads as a direct holder of A. Re-judged at A5: the issue's only clean fix moves the seed out of certificate-visible `keys.0`, and A5 replaces `keys.0`'s inbox field by the card aspect and the admin-only aspect naming a thing's inbox and cards (notes/redesign-plan.md A5 Changes), where the seed can live sealed under the new epoch.

## Where the model overrode the design notes
1. **A leaf is read only where its lowering takes write away** (finding 27): the growth study and A2's draft had every leaf a held root shows waited on; a removal cutting nothing then strands its readers.
2. **A removal's leaves for other things stay with it wherever it is a body** (findings 30, 34): the growth study had leaves only where a commit they judge is; a leaf only the lost remover held then strands what it lists. Its own thing's leaf leaves with what it reads.
3. **Any honest holder runs the duty, per target it reads** (the lost-remover worlds, finding 32): the design notes' "the remover's errand".
4. **A complete pass does not end the duty's window** (findings 29, 33): A2's Provisional had acts after a complete pass reversed only by later writes.
5. **Role changes travel stays whole** (finding 31): the draft dropped its keys-open half.
6. Earlier, kept: the errand to the end (1), standing (3), copies a function of holdings (2), a copy carrying its leaf for its own thing (4), a leaf evidence of every commit its verdict reads (6), an entry naming its part on its leaf's thing (24).
