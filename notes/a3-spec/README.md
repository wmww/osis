## For Tess
Each a question about the model, with what a user would observe.
1. **How long a far document shows a removed person's new writes.** A removal reaches each document the removed person could write through the removed edge (however many grants down) when some device that holds the removal next syncs and reaches that document's servers. Until then the document's readers show the removed (or stolen) device's new writes there as valid, and a complete sync of the document does not end that: things done on those writes (shown as settled, a deposit promoted) are undone when the removal arrives (finding 29). If no device holding the removal ever comes online, they stay shown. Today the same holds, worse: no device carries the removal there at all unless one syncs both. Is "until a device holding the removal next reaches it" within "only propagation waits"?
2. **A remover's device lost right after removing.** If the removal reached any server, every other member device that holds it delivers it (no designated device), with the list of what the removed person wrote, which is kept with the removal on the organisation's own sync ID for good, as today's removal keeps its whole list inside itself (finding 30). If the device was lost before the removal left it, the removal is lost like any unsynced change. Acceptable?
3. **What every device pays per removal.** Every member device holding a removal checks each sync ID the removal reaches until it sees the removal there: in an organisation whose members write 1,000 documents, one removal is 1,000 entries (stored once each) and, per member device, up to 1,000 sync IDs opened once. Accept that every device does this, rather than one device (which would be a designated device)?
4. **What a reader of one document downloads per removal of a writer above it.** About 21 KB as today (keys: the rotations the removal forces), plus the removal and the grant it ends (about 1.6 KB, from A2), plus 64 bytes per thing the removed person had written that the remover held (the list root's pairs), plus that document's own part of the list (about 32 bytes per listed write, only where the person wrote that document). A removal from a big organisation listing writes on 1,000 things shows every reader of any of them a 64 KB root, and the number of things listed (OPEN 1: a tree would make it about 1 KB at the cost of a small blob per document even where nothing was listed). Acceptable, or is the tree wanted?
5. **Who can keep a removal from arriving.** Anyone who can write the document (the removed device included, until the document's keys change after the removal) can delete the removal's copy at servers; honest devices put it back once, and readers that already hold it keep it. And nobody online who holds it. Is that within "authenticated malicious writers are bounded"?
6. **A removed person granted again keeps old lists alive.** The list part for a document stays as long as the person writes that document through the same grant again (re-granted): one list part per such removal, for good (OPEN 4).
7. **A way cut before the removal arrives** (OPEN 6). A stolen device writes a document through the organisation; the document's admin then removes the organisation, keeping that write because it had received it; the organisation's admin then removes the device. No member device can deliver the removal to the document any more (the organisation lost access to it), so the write stays shown there unless a device that syncs both carries it. Accept, or must the document's admin, by removing the organisation, take on something?

## The list form
- **Root** (the removal's own commit, field 12): the role entries (commit IDs of the role commits it lists, any thing) and one (key, hash) pair per thing on which it lists anything else. Nothing per thing in clear: the key is `kdf("list leaf", dedup secret of the thing, blind ‖ tid)`, so only that thing's readers find their pair.
- **Leaf** (a blob per (removal, thing), wrapped per sync ID as a certificate copy): the thing, the commit IDs listed there (content, purge requests, addenda, snapshots, own covers: every commit with a part on that thing resting on the lowered edge that the remover had received), a salt. An entry names its commit's part on that thing alone (finding 24), so a commit is listed in each thing's leaf it has such a part on.
- **When a leaf is read** (finding 27): only for a part its removal cuts, which for content (needing write) is where the removal takes write away on its edge: a held chained write giving write or above ordered before it, or a record edge; the same test reach's witness reads, so a leaf waited on is one the duty carries. A reader then decides from what it holds: no pair for its thing in the root: nothing listed; its leaf held: listed or not; else it waits for the leaf. A removal that cuts nothing (finding 27's world: Z never held write) is read by no verdict, so no reader waits for its leaf and no empty leaf need exist.
- **Where leaves go**: under each sync ID with a body on the leaf's thing that the removal cuts (finding 6: evidence of every commit its verdict reads), and every leaf with its removal wherever the removal is a body (finding 30). So every holder of a removal holds its leaves, any runner of the duty carries them, and a reader of one thing elsewhere holds only its own thing's leaf.
- **The writers' leaving out** (The list): reads the role entries alone; a leaf names no edge-class part and never decides whether the removal applies, so every leaf is written whole.
- **A remover that does not hold a thing** lists nothing there (no pair): every cut part there stands only by an addendum (content) or by a vouch (role writes; under `remake`, by being written again). No hear-out (Tess: core removes when told).

## The delivery duty
- **Who**: every node holding the removal and read or above on its target t by a path: the remover, any other member's device, a reader of t (deposits). No designated device: a remover's node lost right after removing hands the duty to any holder (`the_errand_runs_at_any_honest_holder_when_the_removers_node_is_lost`; the family with the remover lost: `Runners::Remover` fails, `AnyHolder` holds; property `a_lost_removers_duty_falls_to_another_holder_*`). Several runners log one entry: copies are (commit, thing) (`several_runners_log_one_entry_per_copy_*`: entries equal at every step).
- **Where**: the own sync ID of t and of every thing reached from t by chained grants giving write or above and record edges, at any time, followed to the end (finding 1), each reached thing's grants read under its own sync ID; enclosing units get it from the target's own sync ID by their readers' required set (the standing duty of spec/sync-set.md §2).
- **When**: standing (finding 3): whenever the node's view shows a target without a record that a source showed the copy: a holder raised after the removal (Risk 4), a backdated grant, a new holder of a reached thing, a retag; between, in the runner's passes. The delay a far reader sees is the runner's time to its next pass reaching a source of that sync ID; a complete pass there before that does not end it (finding 29: P1-window holds at every step, P1 only once the copy lands).
- **By what right and key**: a holder of read or above on t holds, through each way's write grants, read or above on every reached thing (the lowest role along the way, at most write), and the keys those grants box to their holders, which travel under each holder's own sync ID with the target's `sync.0` (Role changes travel). So it opens each target's own sync ID as a reader of a holder does, stamps where its role through the way gives write, else deposits; a deposited copy is not promoted (A2: no certificate copy is), it is logged by any writer of the target whose view requires it as its own copy, at once (the required set reads no verdict), and S's readers judge with it as soon as they hold it. A deposit can be cleared (`DEPOSITS_WAITING`) or deleted by a hostile writer before a writer logs its own (A2 For Tess 4; here For Tess 5). A runner whose way no longer gives read (the target removed the holder since) cannot open the target: OPEN 6.
- **Storage**: the hold ends once a source shows the copy; the node then keeps of the target nothing but what a synced sync ID needs (spec/commits.md §5 What a held thing needs). The copies stay at the target, required there for good while its unit holds a thing the removal reaches (attributable to the target's sync ID; gone when it is unsynced everywhere).
- **Cost** per removal taking write away: one entry and stamp per reached own sync ID and per enclosing sync ID carrying a reached thing (the removal, its witness, the grants on the ways not already there), stored as one blob per copy at each store; per runner, one hold of each target sync ID until it sees the copy. An organisation's hub of 1,000 documents: 1,000 targets, about 2,000 entries (removal and witness per document; A2 OPEN 16 measured the copies at 782 B each, held once per store), and up to 1,000 opens per member device holding the removal (For Tess 3). Counted at A3 brief 6 (n and 10n things, under one and two sync IDs).

## One mechanism or two (carried demotions and reach)
One delivery mechanism: the required set. Carried demotions (spec/crypto.md §4) stop being placed "with the keyset" by whoever places it: the demotions a keyset S requires carries become a seed of S's required set, the same (removal, thing) copy reach requires where both do, logged by any node whose view requires it (notes/a3-spec/crypto.md). Two seeds remain because they have two readers: reach serves validity where the removed party could write; the keyset seed serves sealing where a holder's keyset is judged (a writer or admin demoted on M where M only reads S, which reach never carries since nothing of M writes S; a demotion from admin to write, which takes no write away). The judging rule (a keyset held only with the demotions it carries) stays. Not modelled (held-model has no keysets): OPEN 8 says what would check it.
- **Bytes per writer removal for a reader of one document** (A2 OPEN 16's measurement: 21 KB on a first read, most of it the rotation's key material). Under A3: the key material unchanged (keys still follow every grant); the removal and its witness 1.6 KB (A2's reach, kept); the root in place of the whole frontier (today every listed commit ID on every thing, 32 B each, travels with the removal: under A3 32 B per role entry plus 64 B per thing listed); its own thing's leaf where the removed party wrote it. A removal listing few commits on many things costs a reader more than today's flat list (64 B per thing against 32 B per commit); one listing many on few, less (OPEN 1).

## The two parked leaks
- **A removed device reads later grants' role parts** (issues/removed-device-reads-later-grants.md): unchanged by A3. It is the wrap of a commit giving read epochs under the epoch it supersedes (spec/crypto.md §5), a keys rule A3 does not touch; A3's copies and leaves are wrapped as any certificate copy (under the sync ID's current wrap), so the removed party opens them only while it still opens the sync ID, and they show it nothing it lacks (its own removal, its own listed writes). Smallest scenario as the issue states; still Tess's (a stated limit proposed there).
- **A departed party reads the fresh deposit seed** (issues/fresh-deposit-seed-readable-by-departed.md): A2 closes its third route (M's `keys.0` no longer travels as evidence: Evidence of a commit takes no `keys.0`). A3 closes nothing more: the rotation commit's wrap (as above) and carried keysets (spec/crypto.md §4 Carried: keysets travel as keys, with M's `keys.0` and its seed) stay; A3's fold moves only the carried demotions (role parts, no `keys.0`) into the required set. Smallest scenario: X removed from M, M's rotation writes a fresh seed, A's next rotation carries M's keyset under A's sync IDs, which X still reads. Re-judged at A5 (cards hold the seed), as the plan says.

## Where the model overrode the design notes
1. **A leaf is read only where its lowering takes write away** (finding 27): the growth study and A2's draft had every leaf a held root shows waited on; a removal cutting nothing then strands its readers.
2. **Leaves stay with their removal wherever it is a body** (finding 30): the growth study had leaves only where a commit they judge is; a leaf only the lost remover held then strands what it lists.
3. **Any honest holder runs the duty** (the lost-remover worlds): the design notes' "the remover's errand".
4. **A complete pass does not end the duty's window** (finding 29): A2's Provisional had acts after a complete pass reversed only by later writes.
5. Earlier, kept: the errand to the end (1), standing (3), copies a function of holdings (2), a copy carrying its leaf for its own thing (4), a leaf evidence of every commit its verdict reads (6), an entry naming its part on its leaf's thing (24).
