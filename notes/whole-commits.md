# Whole commits
> Summary: Why a commit arrives whole and leaves whole (Tess's direction): one blob per commit with one section per thing under that thing's read secret, under every sync ID of every thing it touches; one expiry per commit; apply never waits for a part (a part waits alone only for what it itself needs); a part whose body goes for good is stripped by rewriting that one blob, judged per blob. The decisions and their reasons, the strip and its model, the accepted exposures (overlap visible to nodes keeping data unread), the one rule for per-sync-ID copies, sealed sections (only held things open), costs, and what is left to build.

## Decisions (Tess)
- One shared blob under every sync ID of every thing a commit touches, not a fresh blob per sync ID: logged once, and nodes syncing several of those sync IDs dedup it. Cost: a node keeping them unread learns that one blob sits under two sync IDs that share no thing. A per-sync-ID blob hides the overlap at k sends and k fetches for a node syncing k of the sync IDs.
- A part on a thing whose record the node lacks waits alone, whatever the node syncs; the commit's other parts apply. Holding the whole commit for a synced thing's unknown record would keep a cross-sync-ID wait and a store withholding B's record would hold A's part back. Cost: a node syncing SA and SB fresh may show A's part before SB delivers B's record (one first-sync window; afterwards records precede their commits, and a commit creating B carries B's record). The author's unknown record still holds the whole commit (unverifiable).
- Purge stays per part by rewrite, not per commit and not "multi-thing commits are unpurgeable": rights are per thing, requests name `(commit, tid, ak)`, and the strip rewrite is the one mechanism compaction needs anyway (a mixed-retention commit). Whole-commit purge would not be simpler: dropping the rewrite needs the API to split every write by purge-by class and key set, today's projections moved into the API, losing atomicity across them.
- Mixed retention in one commit (a message kept forever beside a purge-on-replace `last_modified` register) costs one more logging of the blob when the short-lived part dies: accepted ("slightly more expensive"). A schema mixing the two in one write pays it.

## Design
- **Why**: per-reader projections made a commit on A (sync ID SA) and B (sync ID SB) two blobs on two sync IDs, so atomic apply had to decide whether to wait for B's part, and since B's part may have left for good (expiry, keep at most, purge, compaction, a frontier) it needed clauses inferring "it would be gone anyway" from unrelated held data, plus three exceptions in spec/requirements.md. The header already tells every reader of any part which other things the commit touched, so arriving whole leaks nothing new to readers.
- **Wire** (spec/wire.md §4, §5): one blob per commit; a section per thing under the thing's read secret (own or derived by tid), with the record chain in the clear beside it. Per thing, not per independent: a dependent thing made independent at its first grant hands new holders only its derived history, never its mint admin's secret. The section wrap carries a random nonce (a rewrite seals the same section again). A node that cannot open a section copies it verbatim into a fresh encryption of the blob. No size split: a blob stays within `MAX_BLOB` by paging deltas (spec/crdt.md §8).
- **Expiry per commit**: the header carries one optional `expires` (blob prefix `T`); every store drops the whole commit at T unattended. A write whose parts want different expiries, or whose things' settings fill different expiries, is refused (`Error::Clash`, naming them); so is an undo needing two. A commit with a purge request and `expires` reaches only deltas expiring no later (spec/commits.md §5). A commit creating a thing gives its `created.0` the commit's expiry, so one commit is a whole thing that expires (spec/commits.md §6).
- **Delete keys**: the clear prefix lists one commitment group per distinct delete-key set among the parts; a Delete needs one signature per group. Requiring a signature for every commitment instead would let no single right holder delete even a one-part `writer` blob (it commits to three levels). Nodes keeping it unread see how many distinct key sets a blob has.
- **Apply** (spec/commits.md §4): every held part that does not wait applies in one transaction; a part waits alone for its thing's or a chain record, the author's reach, pages, a linked frontier, or parking; a commit pends whole only for its author's epoch, drift or unresolved header lists, never for its parts' waits; one with no part held, or only waiting ones, applies as its header. A blob with more parts than the node applied joins the extra ones as late parts; one with fewer changes nothing locally. A blob holding only bad parts is bad input.
- **Must-hold and carriers** (spec/sync-judgement.md §2): judgments compare the parts the sync ID's actor may read, of every entry alike. A blob carrying more makes another lose as needed only when its extra deltas hold one only a blob carries. A kept part's carrier is always the rewrite (a snapshot only for a dropped part's live writes); otherwise every drop in a mixed blob snapshots every kept aspect.
- **Covered parts are no blob's alone**: a deleted body stays out while other blobs hold every such delta of it, counting a delta only before keep-at-least or with a live write, and no snapshot under the sync ID covering it. Why: when a body was replaced is judged from the bodies each node holds, so a right holder can delete a body as past keep-at-least while a node holding other bodies judges it replaced later and still keeps it; letting the later node log it again loops delete and log again until its own bound, a day away. Accepted cost: after a delete a node keeping S unread keeps only the snapshot's live state of a covered part, not its history before keep-at-least; once the covering snapshot falls (coverer demoted) holders log the part again.
- **Section record chains**: a chain listing a tid twice is bad input (honest chains never repeat; a held tid listed 64 times would cost about 2000 epoch derivations per section). Opening derives each member's held epochs down the nearer part of the chain once per chain and reuses the previous section's answer when its chain is the same (`Engine::chain_epochs`). Bound left: a chain of distinct tids the node holds epochs of costs the sum of their depths per section (up to about 2000 derivations at 64 such things), only for nodes holding that many.
- **Ephemeral commits**: one blob too, held in memory, its pages with it; the paged commit must fit `MAX_BLOB` whole (relay-only deltas never page).
- Role commits keep applying part by part; certificate copies (one blob for every sync ID needing the same parts) are copies by design.

## Per-sync-ID copies: one rule
spec/sync-judgement.md §1: S's body of a commit is the parts S's actor may read plus the parts S's certificates name; it is the shared blob where S's readers can open every section, else one per-sync-ID copy. Code: `blobs::bodies_under` (`Body::to_sync_id`: a role part elsewhere, or a part not derivable through the sync unit), `bodies::with_certificates` (certificate parts beyond the closure body's make it a copy of both), `Engine::seal_body` (a copy sealed by `seal_to` or `seal_certificate`: one section under the blob key, so the wrap alone gates it); received ones marked by `OpenedBlob::copy` (a section the blob key opens) so their one section is never copied into a fresh encryption. A copy refused as tombstoned under a sync ID goes there again as a fresh encryption. Why each section can fail to open:
- A role part on a thing other than S's actor: its boxes are what give S's readers that thing's secrets (a referenced thing's first rotation, a grant to S's thing, a cross-published `sync.0`), so the thing's own section is under a secret they do not hold yet.
- A part whose read secret derives from neither S's actor nor a live independent thing of its sync unit: a cross-published `created.0`, a thing added from among another independent thing's dependents, a dependent of a destroyed independent thing (its `secrets.0` boxes went with it, so a newcomer cannot derive its secret). This can start after the shared blob is logged (the independent thing destroyed later): only a copy holds or carries such a body (the facade's `carried_by`); where the body is a copy, a shared blob of the same delta set is the copy's duplicate whatever its ID, elsewhere the two are never duplicates (a node not yet seeing the independent thing destroyed must not delete the copy).
- A part S's certificates name beside its body (an account's `keys.0` in the commit making its profile, under the profile's sync ID): outside the sync unit, so the body is a copy of both.
A part whose thing's secret the writer lacks (a key actor's own card) stays out of the shared blob and travels as a certificate copy. Fewer copies would need role parts to ride the certificate path and S to carry the rotations of the independents outside it.

## What lingers
- A right holder that does not write a sync ID (a B-writer that cannot open SA) cannot strip B's part under SA; the blob lingers at nodes keeping SA unread until a writer of SA that holds the request or kill syncs, readable only by nodes holding B, which hold the request (Sealed sections, below).
- A node holding a sync ID stores the sealed sections of things it cannot read.

## Strip (spec/sync-judgement.md §2 Per blob)
Principle: per (sync ID, commit) a node's own store holds one blob: the parts it opens whose bodies it still holds, plus the sections it cannot open that every blob of the commit it opened carries. Everything follows from that.
- **What strips** (`bodies::strips`, `Engine::blob_gone`): a part whose body is gone here other than while invalid: a purge's leftover, dropped past bounds, destroyed, or dropped as covered, on an aspect the current setting or its floor may put under purge on replace (`Kept::purges`), while a held valid snapshot of its aspect reaches it by its tables (`bodies::reachers`, `snapshot::reaches`). The body's verdict is unchanged: the rest of it is still must-hold.
- **A cover's proof lasts by construction**: a snapshot's tables list, beside the sources occurring in it, every commit its writer holds with a part on the aspect (spec/crdt.md §8; `snapshot::held_sources`), so the snapshot covering a reacher still reaches the part while the commit is held, and a source goes with the last commit held (no table grows with history). Listing only what occurs lost it: the reacher's cover named no session with no write left, so a new reader counted the stripped part never received, for good (`deletes::a_reader_counts_a_stripped_part_covered_by_its_covers_cover_spec_crdt_8`). Rejected: a cover keeping every source its covered snapshots list (grows with history); a reacher kept beside its cover until the cover reaches all it reaches (order-dependent at a reader that dropped it before the commit came). Left: a cover written by a node holding the reacher but not yet the commit (issues/cover-before-commit.md).
- **Why no cover strip without purge on replace**: that left case; unstripped, a new reader joins the part and judges its cover itself (`deletes::a_reader_receives_a_part_whose_cover_was_covered_whole_*`). Purge on replace must strip.
- **Covered, never received** (spec/commits.md §5 Covered): a valid part of a held commit that the node never received (a new reader of a rewrite) drops as covered by the ordinary rule (`covered_drops` takes missing parts too, past their keep bound; `snapshot::covered` judges a body not held by the snapshot's tables), under another node's own cover too: its writer-only rule keeps held bodies, and a missing part holds none (a rotation's own cover covering the strip's cover, which sync then does not count must-hold: `deletes::a_new_reader_of_a_cover_stripped_commit_misses_nothing_spec_commits_5`): it reads as received and dropped under that cover, `uncover` makes it missing again once no coverer carries it, and it is forgettable as any covered part. Before the snapshot arrives it counts missing and is fetched; the snapshot travels under the same sync ID, so the fetch ends. Why tables on the strip side: a snapshot covering by the join but not by its tables (its writer held the body behind a gap) would leave a new reader missing the part for good. Why no strip under own replaces alone: they do not cover a body not held, and a commit goes under them only whole.
- **Rewrite**: one act (`Act::Rewrite`): the rest logged (a held blob that is it verbatim, else `seal_body`), then the old entries leave. One act, not Log beside DropOwn: a Log already called for before the entry went stale is no new act, and read acts only on what a judgement adds.
- **At sources**: a key holder deletes a stripped blob once the source shows, for each part of it still held here, another entry not stripping carrying it, and for each part stripped as covered an entry of a snapshot reaching it, from `MAX_DRIFT` past that part's keep bound (`Engine::strip_of`: `Strip::covers`, `from`; judge's `deletes`); also while the body is unjudged. Role entries first holds: the purge request reaches the source before the delete. The model shows the snapshot wait is needed: without it a non-writer key holder deletes the old blob at K before any snapshot is there and a covered live part is lost at K.
- **Losing** (`Engine::blob_loses`, one pairwise judgement): A loses to B (neither stripping, B of A's kind, carrying every part of A held here) as needed when B also carries A's parts and unopened sections and a part only a blob carries that A lacks (`blob_only`): must-not-hold, deleted at a source showing B; as spare when B may stand (holds the body, no deposit awaiting promotion) and A carries an unopened section B lacks, or the same sections under a higher ID, or under any ID where A may not stand: own store only, never a delete at a peer. Only a standing entry beats as spare, else a loser's fresh rewrite is beaten again forever; a non-standing one loses whatever its ID, else whether a deposit lingers beside the standing entry turns on blob ID order. This also makes readers that log one rewrite at once settle on one blob in their own stores.
- **Unopened sections** (decided): a node never judges stripping from a section it cannot open; its fresh encryption keeps such a section only where every shared blob of the commit it opened lists its aspects, its bytes held or not (`held_sections` reads the `bp/` rows). Without that, two strips of different parts, each by a node that opens only its own, never meet at a node that opens neither (held-model `strip`: 13 of 3000 with only held bytes' lists; `deletes::strips_of_two_parts_by_nodes_apart_meet_at_a_node_reading_neither_spec_sync_judgement_1`). A held blob opens again as the node gains secrets or comes to hold the thing (`blob_awaits_secrets`, Sealed sections below).
- **Invalid is no strip reason**: an invalid part's blobs are its refetch source (spec/permissions.md §6), and nodes judging it valid would see the rewrite as losing as needed and delete it, a delete/log-again churn bounded only per change of ground.
- **Cost per strip**: one re-encryption of what remains, per sync ID, at each node that logs the body; one Delete per source per sync ID. A commit whose parts go at k separate times is logged up to k+1 times. A cover strip costs a mixed commit one more logging when its covered part drops (the accepted mixed-retention cost).
- **Hostile nodes**: a right holder can purge anyway; a false rewrite lacking a live part loses as needed to the old blob at nodes holding that part, who keep the old blob and log it again; deleting the old blob at a store needs the delete keys of each of its commitment groups (the right over every part). A writer without the right can add a rewrite lacking a section it opens (storage within caps; nodes that do not open it prefer it in their own stores and leave the section out of their fresh encryptions, stores keep the old blob). A store can withhold or lie about showing the rewrite and so lose both copies, which it could do anyway.
- **Model**: held-model `strip` (one commit under one sync ID, readers with different opened parts and delete keys, late readers holding nothing, a store K, parts going for good or covered by a snapshot at random nodes and times, later snapshots covering earlier ones, reaching the part only where their writer holds the commit, snapshots travelling apart from blobs; only writers log at K; oracle at quiescence from the history alone: nothing missing anywhere, K never without a live part, one blob per own log, nothing left at K a key holder could delete): 1 of 3000 histories fails (2006: the left case, a cover by a late node holding no blob, so the part is never stripped at K). Off, each rule: tables listing occurring sources only 104 (missing for good, blobs keeping a covered part), carrier wait 145 (K loses a live part), snapshot wait at K 139 (K loses a covered live part), covered-counts-missing 25 (a late reader misses a part for good), no cover strip 907 (the leak), lists of held blobs only 13, fewer-wins 2; the per-ground stand and the no-delete-on-sealed rule never matter there (gone is final in the model, and a section outside S's body is never needed under S: the second guards body parts sealed below a reader's floor, not modelled).

## Sealed sections: only held things open (spec/sync-judgement.md §1 Sealed sections)
- Rule: a section under a thing's read secret opens only where the node holds the thing (node closure, any context: `Closure::reaches`), the thing is a key actor, or it is a roles section (`Engine::section_opens`). A thing entered on a ticket's word counts: the node syncs its sync IDs, so takes its purge requests. Without it a commit on such a thing and its dependents opens to nothing, is forgotten whole at the pass end, and its entry, waiting for S alone, never opens again once the thing is independent here (engine `tickets::a_ticket_enters_its_target_at_a_strong_spec_sync_set_2`). Why: opening a thing merely held by secret (a dependent thing whose mint admin the node reads, outside its closure) let a purged part be read and kept for good from a sync ID its purge request never reaches; the node then even kept the old blob and logged it again against the strip, as a rewrite lacking a part it held (`holding::a_section_of_a_thing_not_held_stays_sealed_spec_sync_judgement_1`).
- Roles sections open regardless: a purge request never names a role commit (spec/commits.md §2), and what a node needs of an unheld thing is its record and role-class state (`retention::needed`). Key actors are never in a closure; their sections are certificate material.
- The record of a thing whose record the node lacks is taken alone from its sealed section (`OpenedBlob::records`, learned before the ingest): a commit making a thing and the strong to it carries the record there, and the closure cannot enter the thing without it. The created.0 part stays missing on the commit and the section sealed, so a fresh encryption copies it whole rather than sealing the record alone and dropping the content.
- Opening again, one mechanism (store `Follow`): an entry with a sealed section on a missing part is parked under `Awaits::Thing(t)`, whose value is `Engine::section_keys` (whole or not, and t's read secrets while it opens t at all), so it opens again as t enters the closure or gains a secret. A pull anew opens every parked entry of S, whatever it awaits: a strong newly reaching a thing whose record went (forgotten at a pass end as not needed) refetches, and the held blob gives the record again (`holding::a_thing_made_beside_its_strong_opens_once_held_spec_sync_judgement_1`, both arrival orders and a strong added after the minting). Cost: every reader opens a minting blob twice (record, then whole once the strong brings the thing in).

## Left to build
- The reference model (core/test/held-model) models atomic waits, not a part waiting alone (`retention/pending.rs`, `PendingExpiry`: a part waiting for pages or parking expires and is purged).

## Consequences of spec/commits.md §5
- Purge on replace only under `writer`: whoever replaces a body must be able to cover it.
- Dropping a body destroys every local copy so that later compromise of the node recovers nothing (mechanism: notes/local-store.md).
- Cost per commit is linear in its things (`commits::commit_work_linear_in_things_spec_requirements_decentralization` counts it at 50 and 500): `meta: parts indexed` 100 / 1000 on each of the write and the apply, perm's role walks 106 / 1006 things reached on the write (53 / 503 on the apply). Per-part work reads its part, never the whole commit: a part found a whole replace is marked once per commit (`atomic::note_whole`, one `put_meta`), aspects and parts are found by search (`View::aspect`, `Header::aspect_index`, `CommitBlob::part`: header aspects and blob parts are sorted), the meta is not cloned to read one part. Debug checks keep that: `first_held`'s replay check goes on per (node, role) like the replay it checks; a kept record's stored-bytes check runs at its hits 1, 2, 4, ... The floor test (2 000 things beside a value past `MAX_BLOB`) is a default test, under a second in the test profile.
- Sizes: values of any size (a tree of 48 levels, u64 lengths: nothing lists its blobs); about 950 000 aspects per commit by its aspect list (64 MiB at about 70 B per entry), but the commit blob (`MAX_BLOB`, every delta's root and a section per thing) holds far fewer: about 150 000 changes on one thing or about 6 500 things (spec/wire.md §4; Tess: more than enough, much lower would do; splitting a commit across blobs to raise it is not wanted); a removal listing at most about 1.9 million writes (a frontier is one 64 MiB header list at about 34 B per commit ID).
