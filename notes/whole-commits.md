# Whole commits
> Summary: Why a commit arrives whole and leaves whole (Tess's direction): one blob per commit with one section per thing under that thing's read secret, under every label of every thing it touches; one expiry per commit; apply never waits for a part (a part waits alone only for what it itself needs); keep-at-most and purge act per part by rewriting that one blob. The decisions and their reasons, the accepted exposures (host-visible overlap, junk Replace), the closed list of per-label copies, costs, and what is left to build (issues/whole-commits.md).

## Decisions (Tess)
- One shared blob under every label of every thing a commit touches, not a fresh blob per label: one upload, hosts and multi-label nodes dedup. Cost: a host learns that one blob sits under two labels that share no thing. A per-label blob hides the overlap at k uploads and k downloads for a node syncing k of the labels.
- A part on a thing whose record the node lacks waits alone, whatever the node syncs; the commit's other parts apply. Holding the whole commit for a synced thing's unknown record would keep a cross-group wait and the hiding-host limit. Cost: a node syncing LA and LB fresh may show A's part before LB's first pass delivers B's record (one first-sync window; afterwards records precede their commits, and a commit creating B carries B's record). The author's unknown record still holds the whole commit (unverifiable).
- Purge stays per part by rewrite, not per commit and not "multi-thing commits are unpurgeable": rights are per thing, requests name `(commit, tid, ak)`, and the strip rewrite is the one mechanism compaction needs anyway (a mixed-retention commit). Revisit only if whole-commit purge would be significantly simpler in requirements, API or implementation; checked once, it would not: dropping the rewrite needs the API to split every write by retention class and key set, today's projections moved into the API, losing atomicity across them.
- Mixed retention in one commit (a message kept forever beside a purge-on-replace `last_modified` register) costs one re-upload of the blob when the short-lived part dies: accepted ("slightly more expensive"). A schema mixing the two in one write pays it.

## Design
- **Why**: per-reader projections made a commit on A (label LA) and B (label LB) two blobs on two labels, so atomic apply had to decide whether to wait for B's part, and since B's part may have left for good (expiry, keep at most, purge, compaction, a frontier) it needed clauses inferring "it would be gone anyway" from unrelated held data, plus three exceptions in spec/requirements.md. The header already tells every reader of any part which other things the commit touched, so arriving whole leaks nothing new to readers.
- **Wire** (spec/wire.md §4, §5): one blob per commit; a section per thing under the thing's read secret (own or derived by tid), with the record chain in the clear beside it. Per thing, not per reader actor: a plain group given secrets of its own at its first grant hands new members only its derived history, never the base actor's secret. The section wrap carries a random nonce (a rewrite seals the same section again). A node that cannot open a section copies it verbatim into a fresh encryption of the blob. No size split: a blob stays within `MAX_BLOB` by paging deltas (spec/crdt.md §8).
- **Expiry per commit**: the header carries one optional `expires` (blob prefix `T`); hosts delete the whole commit at T unattended. A write whose parts want different expiries, or whose things' `required.0^` durations differ, is refused (`Error::Clash`, naming them); so is an undo needing two. A commit with a purge request carries no `expires`. Consequence: `created.0` never carries `expires`, so creating a thing with expiring content takes two commits, the record (with memberships) first, then the content, written by the app (issues/creation-record-ordinary.md would make it one). Chat messages are writes on an existing thing, so one commit.
- **`nobody` parts** share a commit only with `nobody` parts, except a thing's record (`created.0`) and `required.0^`, which ride with whatever the creating commit carries (a thing made by its record alone carries it in a role commit). So apps' own `nobody` data keeps the property that no key deletes its blob; a blob holding a record is replaceable by its other parts' key holders, and nodes that must keep the record re-upload it.
- **Delete keys**: the clear prefix lists one commitment group per distinct delete-key set among the parts; a Delete needs one signature per group (a `nobody` part's empty group blocks deletion by key), a Replace one signature from any group. Requiring a signature for every commitment instead would let no single right holder delete even a one-part `writer` blob (it commits to three levels). Hosts see how many distinct key sets a blob has.
- **Apply** (spec/commits.md §4): every held part that does not wait applies in one transaction; a part waits alone for its thing's or a chain record, the author's reach, pages, a linked frontier, or parking; a commit pends whole only for its author's record, drift, unresolved header lists, or every held part waiting; one with no part held applies as its header. A blob with more parts than the node applied joins the extra ones as late parts; one with fewer changes nothing locally. A blob holding only bad parts is bad input.
- **Must-hold and carriers** (spec/sync.md §6): judgments compare the parts the label's actor may read, of every entry alike. A superset makes a blob a strict subset only when its extra deltas hold one only a blob carries. A kept part's carrier is always the rewrite (a snapshot only for a dropped part's live writes); otherwise every drop in a mixed blob snapshots every kept aspect.
- **Covered parts are no blob's alone** (`SyncEngine::must_carry`): a deleted body stays out while other blobs hold every such delta of it, counting a delta only before keep-at-least, `nobody`, or with a live write, and no snapshot under the label covering it. Why: when a body was replaced is judged from the bodies each node holds, so a right holder can delete a body as past keep-at-least while a node holding other bodies judges it replaced later and still keeps it; letting the later node re-place it loops delete/re-place every pass until its own bound, a day away (`negative::covered_drop_stays_out_across_local_dating_spec_sync_6`, sim `snapshot_rights`). Accepted cost: after a delete or a junk Replace the host keeps only the snapshot's live state of a covered part, not its history before keep-at-least; once the covering snapshot falls (coverer demoted) holders place the part again (`negative::covered_drop_coverer_falls_reuploaded_spec_sync_6`, `deletion::stale_drop_reads_alike_spec_commits_5`).
- **Section record chains**: a chain listing a tid twice is bad input (honest chains never repeat; a held tid listed 64 times would cost about 2000 epoch derivations per section). Opening derives each member's held epochs down the nearer part of the chain once per chain and reuses the previous section's answer when its chain is the same (`Engine::chain_epochs`). Bound left: a chain of distinct tids the node holds epochs of costs the sum of their depths per section (up to about 2000 derivations at 64 such actors), only for nodes holding that many.
- **Ephemeral commits**: one blob too, held in memory; the commit must fit `MAX_BLOB` whole (ephemeral deltas never page).
- Role commits keep applying part by part; certificate copies and inbox blobs are per-label copies by design.

## Per-label copies: the closed list
spec/sync.md §3; `Body::to_label`, `Engine::seal_to`; received ones marked by `OpenedBlob::copy` so their one section is never copied into a fresh encryption. A copy holds exactly the parts the label's actor reads and is judged as any blob of the commit. Each case is needed (dropping it fails tests):
1. A role commit with a readable part on a thing other than the label's actor: its boxes are what give the label's readers that thing's secrets (a subgroup's first rotation, a grant to the label's group, a cross-published `label.0`), so the thing's own section is under a secret they do not hold yet (`sync_review::self_removal_shared_subgroup`, tied shares, tags, routes, `sharing::removing_a_device_lists_its_links`).
2. A part on a thing whose read secret derives from neither the label's actor nor an actor in its closure: a cross-published `created.0` (the group's own secret, under the home label) and a thing added from another actor's tree (`sync_more::label_published_under_home_label`, `ephemeral::second_label_gets_held_state`, `ephemeral::moved_thing_leaves_old_label`).
3. A part whose thing's secret the writer lacks (a key actor's own card) stays out of the shared blob and travels as a certificate copy.
Shrinking further would need case 1 to ride the certificate path and case 2 the label to carry the outside reader actor's rotations.

## Junk Replace
A Replace needs one group's delete signature and the host cannot see what the replacing blob holds, so any holder of one part's delete key (a writer of t under `writer`, an author under `author`) can remove the whole commit's blob at a host, other things' parts and other retention classes included, replacing it with junk, a rewrite without them, or a copy whose sections of other things carry corrupted ciphertext under their genuine wraps. Accepted as the bounded malicious writer (spec/requirements.md Out of scope). Healing: every node that must hold a part no snapshot under the label covers (keep-at-least and `nobody` included, dead writes too) and finds no other genuine held blob of the commit holding it uploads a fresh encryption (`delete_stands` via `must_carry`, `carried_by`); a reader of the corrupted thing marks the copy bad (`genuine`) and copies none of its sections. With no such holder online a later reader misses those parts until one comes. A node that cannot read a section takes a corrupted one as it is (it checks only what it opens) and may copy it into its own rewrite; that rewrite is then bad for the section's readers and healed the same way. Tests: `hostile_host::replace_by_one_group_healed_by_holders`, `replace_by_corrupted_section_healed_by_its_readers`.

## Stated limits
- A right holder that does not write a label (a B-writer that cannot open LA) cannot strip B's part under LA; the blob lingers at LA's hosts until a writer of LA that holds the request or kill syncs, readable only by B's readers that sync LA, who hold the request.
- A node holding a label stores the sealed sections of things it cannot read.

## Left to build (issues/whole-commits.md)
- Step 4, the per-part strip rewrite: a part whose body must go (applied purge request, covering compaction, invalidity under `required.0^`) is dropped locally and, by a right holder, uploaded again as the commit's blob without that part, naming the old entry as replaced; must-hold per blob on readable parts; a node never uploads or deletes on the strength of sections it cannot open. Hosts on their own act only at the commit's expiry.
- Step 5, cleanup and one open call: whether a node opens sections only of things it syncs. Opening sections of things one merely has the secret for would let a purged part be read for good from a label where no request ever arrives; but the rule cannot be judged at open time, since a commit adding a new thing to a synced group carries the thing's record in a section whose thing the receiver does not sync until that membership applies, so it needs held blobs reopened as the sync set grows.
- `retention/pending.rs` and `PendingExpiry` stay: parts still wait alone for records, chains, pages and parking, and a waiting part still expires and is purged. The reference model (core/test/held-model) still models atomic waits.
- A part on a thing whose record never arrives: a purge naming it cannot be judged (the right is on that thing). Rule available: drop (not strip) a waiting part a held request names when its right cannot be judged; when the record comes the part counts as never received (issues/purged-values-in-waiting-copies.md, issues/pending-unbounded.md).

## Consequences of spec/commits.md §5
- Purge on replace only under `writer`: whoever replaces a body must be able to cover it.
- Dropping a body destroys every local copy so that later compromise of the node recovers nothing (mechanism: notes/local-store.md).
- Derived limits (spec/requirements.md): about 140 GB of chunks per commit (64 MiB of list at about 38 B per chunk entry, chunks about 80 KB); a removal listing at most about 1.9 million writes (a frontier is one 64 MiB header list at about 34 B per commit ID).
