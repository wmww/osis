# Whole commits
> Summary: Design study (no code) for Tess's direction that a multi-thing commit arrives whole and leaves whole: one blob per commit carrying every part, each part sealed to its thing's readers, under every label of every thing it touches; one expiry per commit; keep-at-most and purge act per part by rewriting that one blob; a node applies every part it can read and whose thing it holds, and never waits for a part. What it deletes (the cross-thing gating, the "missing part counts as held" stand-ins, two of the three requirement exceptions, the late-parts harness), what it costs, what stays hard, the choices for Tess and a build order.

## 1. Today, in one paragraph
A commit is one signed header (every `(tid, ak)` with its delta hash, retention and `expires` per aspect) plus parts `(ai, di, delta)`. It is uploaded as **projections**: one blob per (reader actor, retention class), size-split past `MAX_BLOB`, wrapped per label (spec/sync.md §3; `labeling/blobs.rs` `Body`). A label carries only the projections its actor may read, so a commit on A (label LA) and B (label LB) is two blobs on two labels, and a node that syncs LA sees A's part with a header saying "there is also a part on B". Atomic apply (spec/commits.md §4) then has to decide whether to wait for B's part, and since B's part may have left for good (expiry, keep at most, purge, a compaction, a frontier), six clauses infer "it would be gone anyway" from unrelated held data (`apply/gates.rs::counts_held`: expired, `at_most_bound`, `counts_as_purged`, `receipt::listed` with `Dropped::stands_in`, `removed_author`, `breaks_a_held_setting`, each on `Held::ever_holders`), plus `waits_whole`, `never_whole`, `held_units`, the `WaitDeltas` half of `apply/waits.rs`, `retention/pending.rs` (parts held while the commit waits: expiry at T, purge strip), `suspect::Why::PendingExpiry`, session-sources rule 5, and three exceptions in spec/requirements.md. The header already tells every reader of any part which other things the commit touched; "arrive whole" leaks nothing new to readers.

## 2. The design

### 2.1 Wire: one blob per commit
- `CommitBlobPlaintext := [Header, signature, [Section*]]`, `Section := [[ai*], wrap, ciphertext]`: one section per reader actor among the commit's parts (spec/sync.md §3 entitlement, `labeling/actors.rs`), holding that actor's parts (`[Part*]` as today) under a fresh random section key; `wrap` = AEAD of the section key under `kdf("section", reader actor's read secret)` at the newest epoch the writer holds, nonce from (commit ID, section index), AD = commit ID. A reader tries the read secrets of the actors of the tids the header names for that section (bounded; the header is readable by anyone who opens the blob). A section is self-contained: a node that cannot open it copies it verbatim into a fresh encryption of the blob.
- The blob key and per-label wrap are unchanged (spec/crypto.md §5). The common case, a commit whose parts have one reader actor, is one section; the blob costs one more AEAD and about 80 bytes over today's.
- **No size split**: a blob stays within `MAX_BLOB` by paging deltas (spec/crdt.md §8) until the whole blob fits, largest first, instead of splitting into blobs. Pages are fetched before apply, as today. A single blob over 8 MiB of unpaged delta roots is a shape no app has today.
- **Expiry per commit**: the header carries one optional `expires`; aspects lose theirs. The blob prefix `T` is it; hosts delete the whole commit at T unattended (spec/sync.md §5). A header-list chunk's expiry is the commit's.
- **Labels and hosts**: every label of every thing the commit touches carries the one blob (its label's wrap). Hosts dedup blob bytes by ID as today; a node holding several of its labels fetches and stores it once (`state.rs::labels_of`). Hosts see one more thing than today: a blob under two labels that share no thing. Readers of one section see the header (as today) and the sizes of the other sections.
- Certificate copies and inbox blobs stay as they are: projections of role commits, fresh per label (spec/sync.md §3). Role commits keep applying part by part (§2.4).

### 2.2 Apply: never wait for a part
- A node applies, in one transaction, every part it can open whose thing's record it holds (author's chain as today). A part on a thing whose `created.0` it does not hold waits alone, in the held blob, and joins when the record arrives (today's late-part join, `apply::takes_late`). Nothing waits for a part: a part absent from the blob is absent (stripped, §2.3), and the commit reads as today's commit with a dropped body does (spec/commits.md §5 "The commit reads as a held commit without that delta's body").
- A blob of a commit arriving with more parts than the node applied (a holder re-uploaded a body a kill no longer covers) joins the extra parts as late parts; one with fewer changes nothing locally (drops are the node's own judgment, as today).
- What is deleted from spec/commits.md §4: the whole "atomic apply" clause list (every "counts as held" rule), "a part waits and holds it", "the wait of a thing the node syncs holds the whole commit", "a thing's content applies whole or not at all", the sync-set exception and its roster impact, "with no such commit the commit waits...". What stays: signature, dedup, drift, header lists by link, `created.0` records (author, owner chain, the walk), pages, parking, judged-alone, copies.
- Sealed sections of things the node does not sync: kept opaque, never opened. A node reads a part only on a thing it syncs (or holds by its own label); a thing you sync has a label you sync, which is where its purge requests, killers and snapshots live. Opening sections of things one merely has the secret for would let a purged part be read for good from a label where no request ever arrives.

### 2.3 Lifetimes: one container, per-part fate, one rewrite mechanism
- **Expiry**: per commit (§2.1). A write whose parts want different expiries, or whose things' `required.0^` durations differ, is split by the API into separate commits (`Write::commit` does it; the app gets two `Committed`, or an `Invalid` naming the split if it asked for atomicity explicitly). A commit with a purge request carries no `expires` as today.
- **Keep at least / keep at most / purgeable-by**: per aspect on the wire as today (rights are per thing, schemas per AK). What changes is the act: a part whose body must go (keep-at-most bound passed, purge request applied, a compaction covering it, its own invalidity under `required.0^`) is **stripped**: the node drops it locally as today (shredded pages, value-less residue, `Meta.dropped`) and a right holder uploads the commit's blob again without that section's part (the other sections copied verbatim), naming the old entry as replaced (spec/sync.md §5 Replace). This is today's purge rewrite ("a node dropping a body for a purge keeps the commit as a blob without the purged deltas") made the one mechanism; a blob with nothing left is a header-only blob, kept only as a lasting header, as today.
- **Hosts on their own**: expiry (whole commit at T) and a timed delete of a blob all of whose parts are dead (spec/sync.md §5). A part under D-after-replaced in a commit with a live part elsewhere leaves hosts when a right holder next syncs after its bound, not unattended. Keep at most 0 at nodes is as prompt as today (the killing transaction); at hosts it is the rewrite in the same sync, as today's carrier-less delete.
- **Delete keys**: the prefix commits to every level key of every part (as today for a class). A **Delete** (timed or not) needs a delete signature for every commitment; a **Replace** needs one. So a holder with the right over one part can strip it but not delete others' parts; a dishonest one can replace the blob with a junk rewrite, which every holder of a must-hold part heals with a fresh encryption (bounded malicious writer, spec/requirements.md Out of scope); today such a writer could not touch another thing's blob at all. Rule for `nobody`: a `nobody` part shares a commit only with `nobody` parts (the API splits), so a `nobody` blob keeps today's property that no key deletes it.
- **Must-hold** (spec/sync.md §6) per blob, judged on the parts the node reads: must-hold if one of its readable parts is must-hold and no other held blob of the commit under the label carries that part; must-not-hold if a readable part must not be held (purged, past its bound, covered with the right); else may-hold. A node neither uploads nor deletes a blob on the strength of sections it cannot open; it relays what it holds and copies opaque sections verbatim into its own fresh encryptions. The "strict subset" rule becomes "fewer parts of the same commit wins while nothing the node reads is missing from it".
- **Carriers**: a rewrite that strips a dead part is itself the carrier of the live ones. A part with a live write past its keep-at-most grace still needs a covering snapshot before it is stripped, as today.

### 2.4 Partial readers, role commits, ephemeral
- A device reading some of a commit's things applies the parts it can open on things it holds; the rest stay sealed in the blob it holds. "Apply whole" means: all applicable parts at once, from whichever label delivered the blob first.
- Role commits, roster snapshots: unchanged. Certificate copies and inbox blobs are projections by design, so "every part of a role commit waits alone" stays, and so does the requirements' roster line ("a holder may look removed ... until the rest of a roster snapshot arrives").
- Ephemeral commits: one blob too (one section per reader actor, creation records beside parts as today), held in memory; the commit must fit `MAX_BLOB` whole, where today each delta had to (ephemeral deltas never page). A stated limit; presence and cursors are tiny.

## 4. What remains hard
- **Unknown thing**: a part on a thing whose `created.0` the node lacks waits alone (synced or not). The commit's other parts apply. Cost: a node syncing LA and LB fresh may show A's part before LB's first pass delivers B's record (one first sync; afterwards records precede their commits, and a commit creating B carries B's record). The alternative, today's "a synced thing's unknown record holds the commit", keeps the cross-group pend and the hiding-host limit for one short window; not recommended. The author's unknown record still pends the whole commit (unverifiable), as today.
- **Validity flips per part**: unchanged; a flip un-merges one part, the blob stays, re-judging reads the held parts. Nothing cross-thing was ever inferred here.
- **Moves and imports**: an import is single-thing by spec (spec/permissions.md §7); `moved.0`/`was.0` are role-class. Unaffected.
- **Ephemeral**: the whole-commit size limit above; the memory lane's `part_charge` counts one blob per commit, with opaque sections charged by bytes.
- **Mixed retention in one commit** (the reason projections had retention classes, notes/sync.md): a commit writing a message (keep forever) and a `last_modified` register (keep at most 0) is now rewritten once when the register is replaced, i.e. one extra upload of the message's size per message. Tess accepts this ("slightly more expensive"); a schema that mixes the two in one write pays it, and the API could split by retention class on request. Watch the chat schema for this shape.
- **A right holder that does not write a label** (a B-writer that cannot open LA) cannot strip B's part under LA; the blob lingers at LA's hosts until a writer of LA that holds the request or kill syncs, readable only by B's readers that sync LA, who hold the request. Today the same holds for a thing in a group its writer does not read. Stated limit.
- **Roster snapshots** keep their exception (§2.4).

## 5. Choices for Tess
1. **One shared blob under every label (recommended)** or a fresh blob per label. Shared: one upload, hosts and multi-label nodes dedup, "cross once"; a host learns that one blob sits under two labels that share no thing (today it learns this only for shared things). Per label: k uploads and k downloads for a node syncing k of the labels, no new host knowledge. The requirement line changes under the first only.
2. **A part on an unknown thing waits alone, whatever the node syncs (recommended)**, or a synced thing's unknown record holds the whole commit as today. The first deletes the last cross-thing wait and closes the receipts-only cross-group pend; its cost is one first-sync window (§4).
3. **Purge stays per part by rewrite (recommended)**, not per commit and not "multi-thing commits are unpurgeable": rights are per thing, requests already name `(commit, tid, ak)`, and the rewrite is the one mechanism keep-at-most and compaction use too. Making multi-thing commits unpurgeable would buy nothing the rewrite does not.

## 6. The coordinator's extra case: purged values in waiting copies, and the pending bound
A part on a thing whose record never arrives: its values sit in the held blob, and a purge naming it cannot be judged (the right is on that thing). Whole commits **shrink** this: only the part on the unheld thing waits (not the commit's other parts), and the pending copy is the label entry the node holds anyway (no second copy), so what pends is bounded by what the node synced. They do not close it: the right still needs the record, and stripping an unchecked copy would still let a junk copy pose as the part. The clean rule, available today and unchanged by this design: drop (not strip) a waiting part a held request names when its right cannot be judged; when the record comes the part counts as never received and its non-value writes return through a snapshot or a holder's rewrite. issues/pending-unbounded.md likewise shrinks to "label entries the node holds whose author's record never comes" (a writer's word, under the host's per-label cap and the deposit budget) and still wants its per-label bound with oldest-first eviction; nothing here replaces that call.

## 7. Build order (no migration; formats change in place)
1. **Spec and API rule** (S): spec/wire.md §4 `Section`, header `expires`, Delete/Replace commitments; spec/commits.md §4 rewritten, §5 body = the commit's blob, strip rule; spec/sync.md §3, §5, §6, §7; spec/requirements.md lines above. Written first so the steps below have a target; each step keeps the spec true for what it lands.
2. **Wire, labeling, label open** (M): osis-commit blob plaintext with sections; header expiry; `labeling/blobs.rs` one `Body` per commit, section sealing and verbatim copy; osis-label section open and the Replace rule in osis-host; API split by expiry and `nobody`; commit/label/host unit tests. The engine still applies per part as before (sections open to parts), so this merges alone.
3. **Engine apply** (L, the gnarly one): delete gating and the `WaitDeltas` path; parts wait only on records; drop `pending.rs`, `PendingExpiry`, `stands_in`, `listed`; pending as index rows over held blobs; harness: remove late-parts orders and `parted`, rewrite parts.rs around unknown-thing joins and sealed sections; order sweeps and `cargo test-net` green.
4. **Retention and node-sync** (M-L): per-part strip rewrite for keep-at-most, compaction and purge through one path; must-hold per blob on readable parts; timed deletes only with every commitment; carrier rule per blob; sim-sweep and net oracles.
5. **Cleanup** (S): close the issues in §3, delete session-sources rule 5, update notes/sync.md, notes/core-triage.md §2.1.
Steps 2-4 each a task with review; 3 is a candidate for `fable`.

## 8. Plain words for Tess
A change that touches several things now travels as one package: every place any of its things syncs to carries the whole package, with each part locked so only that thing's readers can open it. A device that may read several of the things gets all of them from wherever the package arrives first, and never waits for a missing piece; a piece it may not read is luggage it carries and passes on. For app writers: one write that wants different expiries for different parts becomes separate commits (the API does it and tells you), and a write mixing "keep forever" with "delete when replaced" costs one re-upload when the short-lived part dies. For users: nothing visible, except that a shared-with-you change can no longer show half of itself. The cost: a package under two labels tells the host those labels had a change in common, and a device holding a label stores the locked parts of things it cannot read. It deletes the hardest paragraph of the spec, two of the three written exceptions, about a thousand lines of engine and harness code and six open issues. Two calls for you: share one package across labels (I recommend yes; the alternative hides the overlap from hosts at the cost of one upload per label), and let a part on a thing the device does not know yet wait on its own rather than holding the whole change (I recommend yes).
