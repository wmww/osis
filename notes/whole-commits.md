# Whole commits
> Summary: Why a commit arrives whole and leaves whole (Tess's direction): one blob per commit with one section per thing under that thing's read secret, under every sync ID of every thing it touches; one expiry per commit; apply never waits for a part (a part waits alone only for what it itself needs); a part whose body goes for good is stripped by rewriting that one blob, judged per blob. The decisions and their reasons, the strip and its model, the accepted exposures (overlap visible to nodes keeping data unread), the one rule for per-sync-ID copies, costs, and what is left to build (issues/whole-commits.md).

## Decisions (Tess)
- One shared blob under every sync ID of every thing a commit touches, not a fresh blob per sync ID: logged once, and nodes syncing several of those sync IDs dedup it. Cost: a node keeping them unread learns that one blob sits under two sync IDs that share no thing. A per-sync-ID blob hides the overlap at k sends and k fetches for a node syncing k of the sync IDs.
- A part on a thing whose record the node lacks waits alone, whatever the node syncs; the commit's other parts apply. Holding the whole commit for a synced thing's unknown record would keep a cross-sync-ID wait and a store withholding B's record would hold A's part back. Cost: a node syncing SA and SB fresh may show A's part before SB delivers B's record (one first-sync window; afterwards records precede their commits, and a commit creating B carries B's record). The author's unknown record still holds the whole commit (unverifiable).
- Purge stays per part by rewrite, not per commit and not "multi-thing commits are unpurgeable": rights are per thing, requests name `(commit, tid, ak)`, and the strip rewrite is the one mechanism compaction needs anyway (a mixed-retention commit). Revisit only if whole-commit purge would be significantly simpler in requirements, API or implementation; checked once, it would not: dropping the rewrite needs the API to split every write by retention class and key set, today's projections moved into the API, losing atomicity across them.
- Mixed retention in one commit (a message kept forever beside a purge-on-replace `last_modified` register) costs one more logging of the blob when the short-lived part dies: accepted ("slightly more expensive"). A schema mixing the two in one write pays it.

## Design
- **Why**: per-reader projections made a commit on A (sync ID SA) and B (sync ID SB) two blobs on two sync IDs, so atomic apply had to decide whether to wait for B's part, and since B's part may have left for good (expiry, keep at most, purge, compaction, a frontier) it needed clauses inferring "it would be gone anyway" from unrelated held data, plus three exceptions in spec/requirements.md. The header already tells every reader of any part which other things the commit touched, so arriving whole leaks nothing new to readers.
- **Wire** (spec/wire.md §4, §5): one blob per commit; a section per thing under the thing's read secret (own or derived by tid), with the record chain in the clear beside it. Per thing, not per reader actor: a plain thing given secrets of its own at its first grant hands new holders only its derived history, never the base actor's secret. The section wrap carries a random nonce (a rewrite seals the same section again). A node that cannot open a section copies it verbatim into a fresh encryption of the blob. No size split: a blob stays within `MAX_BLOB` by paging deltas (spec/crdt.md §8).
- **Expiry per commit**: the header carries one optional `expires` (blob prefix `T`); every store drops the whole commit at T unattended. A write whose parts want different expiries, or whose things' settings fill different expiries, is refused (`Error::Clash`, naming them); so is an undo needing two. A commit with a purge request and `expires` reaches only deltas expiring no later (spec/commits.md §5). A commit creating a thing gives its `created.0` the commit's expiry, so one commit is a whole thing that expires (spec/commits.md §6).
- **`nobody` parts**: only `created.0` (by choice) and `retention^.0` (fixed) carry `nobody`, and they ride with whatever the creating commit carries (a thing made by its record alone carries it in a role commit); a blob holding them is replaceable by its other parts' key holders, and nodes that must keep them log them again.
- **Delete keys**: the clear prefix lists one commitment group per distinct delete-key set among the parts; a Delete needs one signature per group (a `nobody` part's empty group blocks deletion by key). Requiring a signature for every commitment instead would let no single right holder delete even a one-part `writer` blob (it commits to three levels). Nodes keeping it unread see how many distinct key sets a blob has.
- **Apply** (spec/commits.md §4): every held part that does not wait applies in one transaction; a part waits alone for its thing's or a chain record, the author's reach, pages, a linked frontier, or parking; a commit pends whole only for its author's epoch, drift or unresolved header lists, never for its parts' waits; one with no part held, or only waiting ones, applies as its header. A blob with more parts than the node applied joins the extra ones as late parts; one with fewer changes nothing locally. A blob holding only bad parts is bad input.
- **Must-hold and carriers** (spec/sync-judgement.md §2): judgments compare the parts the sync ID's actor may read, of every entry alike. A superset makes a blob a strict subset only when its extra deltas hold one only a blob carries. A kept part's carrier is always the rewrite (a snapshot only for a dropped part's live writes); otherwise every drop in a mixed blob snapshots every kept aspect.
- **Covered parts are no blob's alone**: a deleted body stays out while other blobs hold every such delta of it, counting a delta only before keep-at-least, `nobody`, or with a live write, and no snapshot under the sync ID covering it. Why: when a body was replaced is judged from the bodies each node holds, so a right holder can delete a body as past keep-at-least while a node holding other bodies judges it replaced later and still keeps it; letting the later node log it again loops delete and log again until its own bound, a day away. Accepted cost: after a delete a node keeping S unread keeps only the snapshot's live state of a covered part, not its history before keep-at-least; once the covering snapshot falls (coverer demoted) holders log the part again.
- **Section record chains**: a chain listing a tid twice is bad input (honest chains never repeat; a held tid listed 64 times would cost about 2000 epoch derivations per section). Opening derives each member's held epochs down the nearer part of the chain once per chain and reuses the previous section's answer when its chain is the same (`Engine::chain_epochs`). Bound left: a chain of distinct tids the node holds epochs of costs the sum of their depths per section (up to about 2000 derivations at 64 such actors), only for nodes holding that many.
- **Ephemeral commits**: one blob too, held in memory, its pages with it; the paged commit must fit `MAX_BLOB` whole (relay-only deltas never page).
- Role commits keep applying part by part; certificate copies (one blob for every sync ID needing the same parts) are copies by design.

## Per-sync-ID copies: one rule
spec/sync-judgement.md §1: S's body of a commit is the parts S's actor may read plus the parts S's certificates name; it is the shared blob where S's readers can open every section, else one per-sync-ID copy. Code: `blobs::bodies_under` (`Body::to_sync_id`: a role part elsewhere, or a part not derivable through the sync unit), `bodies::with_certificates` (certificate parts beyond the closure body's make it a copy of both), `Engine::seal_body` (a copy sealed by `seal_to` or `seal_certificate`: one section under the blob key, so the wrap alone gates it); received ones marked by `OpenedBlob::copy` (a section the blob key opens) so their one section is never copied into a fresh encryption. A copy refused as tombstoned under a sync ID goes there again as a fresh encryption. Why each section can fail to open:
- A role part on a thing other than S's actor: its boxes are what give S's readers that thing's secrets (a referenced thing's first rotation, a grant to S's thing, a cross-published `sync.0`), so the thing's own section is under a secret they do not hold yet.
- A part whose read secret derives from neither S's actor nor a live actor of its sync unit: a cross-published `created.0`, a thing added from another actor's tree, a dependent of a destroyed actor (its `secrets.0` boxes went with it, so a newcomer cannot derive its secret). This can start after the shared blob is logged (the actor destroyed later): only a copy holds or carries such a body (the facade's `carried_by`); where the body is a copy, a shared blob of the same delta set is the copy's duplicate whatever its ID, elsewhere the two are never duplicates (a node not yet seeing the actor destroyed must not delete the copy).
- A part S's certificates name beside its body (an account's `keys.0` in the commit making its profile, under the profile's sync ID): outside the sync unit, so the body is a copy of both.
A part whose thing's secret the writer lacks (a key actor's own card) stays out of the shared blob and travels as a certificate copy. Fewer copies would need role parts to ride the certificate path and S to carry the outside reader actors' rotations.

## Stated limits
- A right holder that does not write a sync ID (a B-writer that cannot open SA) cannot strip B's part under SA; the blob lingers at nodes keeping SA unread until a writer of SA that holds the request or kill syncs, readable only by B's readers that sync SA, who hold the request.
- A node holding a sync ID stores the sealed sections of things it cannot read.

## Strip (spec/sync-judgement.md §2 Per blob)
Principle: per (sync ID, commit) a node's own store holds one blob: the parts it opens whose bodies it still holds, plus the sections it cannot open that every held blob of the commit carries. Everything follows from that.
- **Must-not-hold per blob**: a blob carrying an opened part whose body is gone here for good (`Meta::body_gone` but not dropped as covered or invalid: a purge's leftover, dropped past bounds, destroyed; `Engine::blob_gone`). The body's verdict is unchanged: the rest of it is still must-hold.
- **Rewrite**: one act (`Act::Rewrite`): the rest logged (a held blob that is it verbatim, else `seal_body`), then the old entries leave. One act, not Log beside DropOwn: a Log already called for before the entry went stale is no new act, and read acts only on what a judgement adds.
- **At sources**: a key holder deletes a gone blob once the source shows, for each part of it still held here, another entry not gone carrying it (`Engine::strip_of`, judge's `deletes`), also while the body is unjudged (an entry gone here goes whatever the body is). Role entries first holds: the purge request reaches the source before the delete.
- **Fewer sections wins** (`Engine::blob_beaten_by`): a blob lacking an unopened section another carries, or carrying the same sections under a lower blob ID, while carrying all the other holds here, beats it in the own store. Only an entry holding the body and not an unpromoted deposit beats anything, else a loser's fresh rewrite is beaten again forever. Never a ground for a delete at a peer. This also makes readers that log one rewrite at once settle on one blob in their own stores.
- **Sealed sections** (decided): a node never judges gone-ness from a section it cannot open; its fresh encryption keeps such a section only where every held blob of the commit lists its aspects (`held_sections`). Without that, two strips of different parts, each by a node that opens only its own, never meet at a node that opens neither (held-model `strip`). A held blob opens again as the node gains secrets (`blob_awaits_secrets`), as before.
- **Invalid is no strip reason**: an invalid part's blobs are its refetch source (spec/permissions.md §6), and nodes judging it valid would see the rewrite as a strict subset and delete it, a delete/log-again churn bounded only per change of ground.
- **Cost per strip**: one re-encryption of what remains, per sync ID, at each node that logs the body; one Delete per source per sync ID. A commit whose parts go at k separate times is logged up to k+1 times.
- **Hostile nodes**: a right holder can purge anyway; a false rewrite lacking a live part is a strict subset to nodes holding that part, who keep the old blob and log it again; deleting the old blob at a store needs the delete keys of each of its commitment groups (the right over every part). A writer without the right can add a rewrite lacking a section it opens (storage within caps; nodes that do not open it prefer it in their own stores, stores keep the old blob). A store can withhold or lie about showing the rewrite and so lose both copies, which it could do anyway.
- **Model**: held-model `strip` (one commit under one sync ID, readers with different opened parts and delete keys, a store K, parts going at random nodes and times; oracle at quiescence from the history alone): 0 of 3000 histories fail. Without the carrier wait K loses a live part; without the every-blob rule for unopened sections own stores keep two blobs; the per-ground stand (spec/sync-judgement.md §4) and the no-delete-on-sealed rule never matter there (gone is final in the model, and a section outside S's body is never needed under S: the second guards body parts sealed below a reader's floor, not modelled).

## Left to build (issues/whole-commits.md)
- **Cover strips**: a part dropped as covered does not strip yet. A node that never held it (a new reader) receives the rewrite with the part listed in the header and no body, counts it missing and fetches it from no one. Needed first: a part not held that a held valid snapshot of its aspect reaches by its tables (spec/crdt.md §8 Frontiers, Absorbed; `retention::snapshot::reaches`) counts as covered, not missing, and missing again once no such snapshot is held. Then cover drops strip too: a purge-on-replace part beside a live one leaves the sync stores (open leak: `deletes::a_part_purged_on_replace_is_unrecoverable_from_the_sync_store_spec_commits_5`, ignored), and a mixed commit's covered part stops costing storage. The strict-subset clause then reduces to fewer-sections-wins plus "a blob lacking a part this node still needs loses".
- Whether a node opens sections only of things it syncs: opening sections of things one merely has the secret for would let a purged part be read for good from a sync ID where no request ever arrives; it cannot be judged at open time (a commit adding a new thing and the strong to it carries the thing's record in a section whose thing the receiver does not sync until that strong applies), so it needs held blobs reopened as the sync set grows.
- `retention/pending.rs` and `PendingExpiry` stay: parts still wait alone for pages and parking, and a waiting part still expires and is purged. The reference model (core/test/held-model) still models atomic waits.

## Consequences of spec/commits.md §5
- Purge on replace only under `writer`: whoever replaces a body must be able to cover it.
- Dropping a body destroys every local copy so that later compromise of the node recovers nothing (mechanism: notes/local-store.md).
- Cost per commit is linear in its things (`commits::commit_work_linear_in_things_spec_requirements_decentralization` counts it at 50 and 500): `meta: parts indexed` 100 / 1000 on each of the write and the apply, perm's role walks 106 / 1006 things reached on the write (53 / 503 on the apply). Per-part work reads its part, never the whole commit: a part found a whole replace is marked once per commit (`atomic::note_whole`, one `put_meta`), aspects and parts are found by search (`View::aspect`, `Header::aspect_index`, `CommitBlob::part`: header aspects and blob parts are sorted), the meta is not cloned to read one part. Debug checks keep that: `first_held`'s replay check goes on per (node, role) like the replay it checks; a kept record's stored-bytes check runs at its hits 1, 2, 4, ... The floor test (2 000 things beside a value past `MAX_BLOB`) is a default test, under a second in the test profile.
- Limits (spec/requirements.md asks only for at least 2 000 changes per commit, of values of any size): values of any size (a tree of 48 levels, u64 lengths: nothing lists its blobs); about 950 000 aspects per commit by its aspect list (64 MiB at about 70 B per entry), but the commit blob (`MAX_BLOB`, every delta's root and a section per thing) holds far fewer: about 150 000 changes on one thing or about 6 500 things (spec/wire.md §4; Tess: more than enough, much lower would do; splitting a commit across blobs to raise it is not wanted); a removal listing at most about 1.9 million writes (a frontier is one 64 MiB header list at about 34 B per commit ID).
