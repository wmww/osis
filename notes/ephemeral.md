# Ephemeral data
> Summary: Design (proposal, from the owner's round-13 direction) for ephemeral data: things and schema-declared aspects held only in memory, an axis independent of sync; every thing starts ephemeral and is persisted with its owner chain at its first durable write, keeping its tid; ephemeral placements; local cost about an in-memory structure; synced ephemeral data by signed envelopes under labels with poke liveness (a value lives while a route to its writer does), direct re-sends to joiners and a long lease backstop; signals folded in as event aspects; the API delta, engine, wire, bounds, deletions, spec edits and a five-step plan. Also kept: expiry and the all-nodes predicate. Tracking: issues/ephemeral-data.md.

## Owner direction (round 13, decided)
- Ephemeral = cheap and disposable, no never-touches-disk promise. Independent of sync: durable or ephemeral, local or synced (four cells). All of an app's state lives in osis; the UI drives and is driven by it.
- Both units: ephemeral things (creation record only in memory, same tid derivation and rights) and ephemeral aspects on durable things, declared in the schema.
- `create` takes no placement; group ops set groups (same batch for atomic create-and-place). Every thing starts ephemeral and is persisted at its first durable write together with its owner chain's creation records, keeping its tid. No persist call. A thing never given durable data vanishes on restart.
- Local ephemeral writes: no signing, hashing, encryption or journaling; typed reads, writes, watches, merge, undo alike, in memory. Synced ephemeral writes are signed.
- Synced delivery is the poke model: a value lives while its writer is online; a new subscriber gets current values re-sent, ideally to it alone through the server or meshed device.
- Fold signals in, fewest concepts.

## Model
**Durable vs ephemeral aspect.** An aspect is ephemeral by its key: the schema flag `ephemeral` (a value with lasting state) or `event` (an ephemeral write with no lasting value: the former signal). The writer's key decides the path; merge needs no schema. Core aspects are durable. Retention does not apply to ephemeral aspects; expiry does.

**Ephemeral thing.** A thing whose creation record no durable commit holds at this node. Tid derivation, creation record and owner are as for any thing. `create(owner)` mints the record and keeps it in memory; nothing is written. Invariant: **the durable set is closed under owner**, so a durable thing never has an ephemeral owner.

**Persistence (promotion).** The first commit with a durable delta on an ephemeral thing T (a durable aspect of T, or a `/group` write on T as a group, by any node that holds T's record and may write T) also carries `/created` of T and of each ephemeral thing on T's owner chain, top down, and T's ephemeral placements as `/group` adds (below). Same tid; ephemeral aspects of T stay ephemeral; ephemeral values never become durable (the app copies a draft's content into a durable aspect in the same batch: `set`). Reason: one aspect state never mixes lifetimes, since a durable write anchored at a session dot would hide after restart and a durable delta cannot carry foreign session stamps. A membership operation on an ephemeral thing first persists it by its own content commit (membership commits cannot carry `/group`), then runs. A durable thing never becomes ephemeral again.

**Ephemeral placements.** A group op (`place`, `unplace`, subgroup add or remove) whose item is ephemeral is ephemeral: an overlay entry `(item, group, kind)` held in memory, needing write on the group like the durable op. It shows in the group's `/group` read and the item's `/groups`, routes the item's synced ephemeral data under the group's labels, and becomes a durable `/group` write (with its expiry) when the item persists. A group op with a durable item is a durable write on the group and persists the group like any durable aspect. So an ephemeral thing can be a group of ephemeral items (a scratch collection), and drafts placed in a folder land there at save, with no ghost tids after a restart. Only the placer removes its overlay entry; another node's `unplace` resets durable entries only. A durable `/group` naming an ephemeral tid (a raw path op, a remote record) is a reference that may dangle; it still routes that thing's ephemeral data.

**Rights.** An ephemeral thing has no membership-class aspects (writing one persists it), so a node's role on it is its role on a new thing owned by the nearest durable thing on its chain: the rule already used for things created in the same batch. Ephemeral writes need what the durable write would need (write). Readers cannot write ephemeral data, presence included (spec/requirements.md: readers only read). Remote ephemeral parts are judged by current roles (no timeline, no frontier: they have no history); a roster change re-judges held contributions and drops those no longer allowed.

**Deletion.** `delete(T)` on an ephemeral thing drops it entirely: record, own state, overlay entries, undo entries, and the ephemeral things it owns; the tid then reads unknown (an app creating many short-lived things frees them). Remote copies get the resets and drop the record with the last contribution naming it.

**Reads.** An aspect reads the join of its durable state and the live ephemeral contributions (spec/crdt.md §4.1). Normally only one side exists; both exist only when schemas disagree, and the join is still defined. `/aspects` and `/groups` include ephemeral data. Event aspects always read absent.

**Contributions.** Ephemeral state of an aspect is kept per writer session: the own one (joined with each local batch) and one per remote session (joined from its bodies). Each holds only that session's stamps. The read is their join; dropping a session is un-merge of that author (spec/crdt.md §4.6: its writes go, its sequence records stay as hidden anchors, so others' inserts after them stay placed). An expiring ephemeral write follows spec/crdt.md §4.7 (values go value-less at T); a contribution keeps one fragment per expiry class.

## API delta (notes/api.md, applied with step 1)
- `w.create(owner) -> Tid`; `Place`, `create_expiring` go (expiring placements stay `place_expiring`). Placement is `w.place(..)` in the same batch.
- Schema DSL: `aspect /doc/cursor: map<tid, Cursor> [ephemeral];` `aspect /call/ring: Ring [event];` (`flag` cases 4 `ephemeral`, 5 `event`; `signal` items, `signal_def` and schema field 4 go). `AspectDefaults` gains `ephemeral: Durable | Ephemeral | Event`; untyped `Ak::ephemeral()` / `Ak::event()` for generic tools and the CLI.
- Writes: unchanged calls. One `Write` may mix durable and ephemeral ops: validated as a whole, applied locally at once (one dispatch); the commit and the envelope travel separately, so a peer may see one without the other. `retention(..)` on an ephemeral write and any op but `set` on an event aspect are `Invalid`. `commit()` returns `Committed { id: ChangeId, ts }`, `ChangeId = Commit(CommitId) | Ephemeral(EphemeralId)`; a batch with both halves is one change (one `ts`; revert of either reverts both).
- Watches: unchanged. Each event write reaches every `on_change` callback once (never coalesced, never in state; a `Watch` on an event aspect stays `None`). `Source` keeps its variants; `CommitInfo.id` becomes `ChangeId`; a remote value dropped at its writer's departure fires `Remote` naming that writer.
- `created(t)`: `Created.commit: Option<CommitId>`, `None` while ephemeral (the "is it persisted" query; `ts`, `node`, `actor` from the creating batch or body, `received` from its arrival).
- Undo, redo, revert, query: ephemeral batches of this node are changes (`CommitKind::Ephemeral`). Thing and Group scopes cover durable aspects and ephemeral things; an ephemeral aspect of a durable thing (cursor, selection) is in scope only when named by `Scope::Aspects`, so document undo never steps through cursor moves. Remote ephemeral changes have no log: revert is `Invalid`. `Config.ephemeral_history` (default 16 MiB) bounds the log; older batches are `NotUndoable{Compacted}`.
- Gone: `send_signal`, `on_signal`, `on_signal_in`, `Target`, `SignalEvent`, `Signal<T>`/`SignalKey`. Device targeting is dropped (no user; a value can name a device). A group-wide event goes on the group thing.
- Errors: none new. `UnknownThing` for a vanished tid. Diagnostics: `EphemeralTooLarge { tid, ak }` (a part not sent), `EphemeralDropped { label, node }` (a remote writer over its cap). `NotSynced` stays only for a durable placement into a closure with no label. Footgun line changes from "placement explicit at creation" to "a thing never written durably vanishes at restart".
- Text sessions on an ephemeral `text` aspect apply each edit at once (no idle commit).

## Local engine
- Module `osis-engine` `ephemeral/`, owned by `Engine` beside `Core`, never in the store or the journaled indexes; under the handle's one lock. `Eph { session: [u8; 32] random per open, next counter, things: Tid → (record, own | remote refcount), aspects: (Tid, Ak) → { own, remote: session → classes, read cache }, sessions: session → { node, actor, routes, live_until, bytes per label }, overlay: (item, group, kind) → per session (stamp, present, expires), log, timers }`.
- Stamps: `ts` from the engine's HLC (receipt of an envelope advances it like a commit, same acceptance window); dots of source `Commit(session)` (spec/crdt.md §2.1; no new source kind) with counters from the session counter. Node-form counters are never used (they must stay contiguous and survive restarts). Ops resolve against the read view with the same `osis_crdt` code as durable writes and join into `own`. No store transaction, signature, hash or encryption: a local ephemeral write is an HLC tick, an op, a join and a watch event.
- `Write` splits ops by key: durable ops into the `Mutation` (plus chain records and placements of the ephemeral things it persists), ephemeral ops into an `EphBatch`. The mutation commits first; if it fails, nothing applies. Then the batch applies (cannot fail after validation) and the engine queues `Event::Ephemeral { changes, info, events }` (replaces `Event::Signal`), dispatched like commit events.
- Reads (`read.rs`): join the durable state with the read cache when both exist; synthetic aspects and `groups_of` include ephemeral data; `created` reports records without a commit. Perm: an ephemeral thing's role through its nearest durable owner (above).
- Undo: the log holds own batches (deltas, `ts`, linked commit). The undo buffer is computed as on the durable path, over the base plus the batches before it instead of held bodies, with remote contributions' writes stamped before it; the inverse writes follow spec/crdt.md §4.6 and apply as a new batch with `undoes`. Past `ephemeral_history`, the oldest batches fold into the base.
- Restart (the database closes or the process dies): everything ephemeral is gone, and the next open draws a new session. Durable references to ephemeral tids dangle.

## Synced ephemeral data
### Envelope (replaces the signal envelope)
- Same framing as today: a blob with no expiry and no commitments, at most `MAX_EPHEMERAL` = 64 KiB, wrapped under the label, publish auth under the label's write or deposit key (domain renamed `"osis v1 ephemeral publish"`). Plaintext: padded canonical `[EphemeralBody, signature]` (sender node, domain `"osis v1 ephemeral"`).
- `EphemeralBody := [node, actor, ts, session, records, places, parts]`. `records`: creation records of the ephemeral things the parts and places name and of their ephemeral chains, plus durable ones whose creation commit is not yet placed under the label. `places`: `[item, group, kind, ts, ctr, present, Opt(expires)]`. `parts`: `[0, tid, ak, Opt(schema), Opt(expires), fragment]` (state-form fragment, spec/crdt.md §3 with the Commits table, every stamp of source `Commit(session)`; others' dots only as element identities and anchors) or `[1, tid, ak, Opt(schema), ctr, value]` (event).
- Entitlement as for blobs (spec/sync.md §3): one envelope per label holding the parts whose things are in its closure (durable groups plus overlay) and readable by its actor; a part too big for one envelope is not sent (diagnostic).
- Receiver: decrypt, verify the node signature and its chain like a commit (drop the body when the chain is missing), check each record hashes to its tid, then judge each part and place on its own (write on the thing or group), dropping failures.

### Delivery: routes, poke, direct re-send, lease
- Forwarding stays one hop (spec/sync.md §9): a serving node (server or member peer) forwards a body from a client to its other subscribers; clients do not re-forward. Servers stay blind and keep nothing but routing state: per (connection, label) a random 8-byte **handle**, unique per label.
- Messages (replacing `Signal`): `Ephemeral [24, 0, label_id, blob, wrap, auth, to, from]`: from a client, `to` is `Opt(handle)` (a re-send for one joiner, delivered only to it) and `from` is none; from a serving node, `from` is the publishing route's handle (its own for its own bodies). `Poke [25, 0, label_id, from]`: a client sends it after Subscribe; the serving node forwards it to its other subscribers with the poker's handle and answers it with its own contributions. `Left [26, 0, label_id, handle]`: sent by the serving node when a connection that published or poked under the label closes or unsubscribes. Poke needs the label opened; Ephemeral needs auth as above.
- **Liveness.** A remote session's contribution is live while it has a route: (connection, handle) for bodies a serving node delivered, or the connection itself for bodies a client published to this node. `Left`, or the loss of the connection, removes routes; a session with none is dropped (un-merge, watches fire). So a value lives while a path from its writer to the reader does. Backstop against a path that never reports (a hostile server, a half-open connection): a contribution also dies at `min(ts, arrival) + MAX_LEASE` of its newest body, and a writer with live values re-sends its whole contribution to every route every `MAX_LEASE / 2`, which also heals bodies lost to rate limits.
- **Joiners.** After (re)subscribing, a node broadcasts its whole contributions (others dropped them when it left) and sends `Poke`. A writer receiving `Poke(h)` re-sends its whole contribution under the label addressed `to = h`, coalescing pokes for `POKE_COALESCE` = 1 s: one poke gets an addressed re-send, several get one broadcast. Direct P2P is the same with the peer as serving node.
- **Writer outbox.** Per label, at most one pending body: new batches join into it until the connection takes it (latest wins: a fast drag sends what the link can carry). Events queue beside it, at most `EVENT_QUEUE` = 256 per label (the oldest are dropped, diagnostic).
- Offline writer: its own state stays local; remote copies go at `Left` or at the lease; reconnecting re-broadcasts. Never in bundles, never deposited, never stored by a server.

### Ordering, duplicates, replay
- A body is accepted when `ts` is within `[now − MAX_LEASE, now + MAX_DRIFT]` (corrected clock); a captured body is useless after `MAX_LEASE`. Fragments join idempotently, so duplicates over two servers and reordering are harmless. Events are delivered in counter order per session, at most once (a counter at or below the last delivered one for that session is dropped). A wrong local clock harms only that writer: its values die early.

### Bounds (notes/arbitrary-values.md)
| value | class | at 0 / at infinity |
|---|---|---|
| `MAX_EPHEMERAL` 64 KiB | resource bound (existing) | nothing sent / unbounded frames |
| `MAX_LEASE` 5 min (proposed) | owner-agreed protocol constant | constant re-sends / a hostile path keeps a departed writer's value forever |
| remote contribution cap 1 MiB per (writer node, label), 16 MiB per label | resource bound: a body that would exceed it is dropped, `EphemeralDropped` (deduped), other writers unaffected | no remote ephemeral data / a hostile writer fills memory |
| `POKE_COALESCE` 1 s | minor consequence: joiner latency | a re-send per poke (server poke rate still bounds it) / joiners wait |
| `EVENT_QUEUE` 256 | resource bound | no events / unbounded queue on a slow link |
| `ephemeral_history` 16 MiB (config) | resource bound: older batches not undoable | no ephemeral undo / log grows with edits |
| server rates for 24 and 25 per label (renamed `signals` knob plus `pokes`) | resource bound: over-rate messages dropped | best effort anyway / flooding |
Local ephemeral memory is the app's; no cap.

### Leaks
A blind server sees ephemeral traffic size and timing per label (as for signals) and nothing new. Co-readers see handles join and leave per label (connection timing, the same class as presence) and a node's session change at restart.

## Expiry
- Expiry is death by timer (spec/crdt.md §4.8): the values die at T and the body follows, whatever the deltas' keep bounds. "Keep at least until T" is a retention bound (spec/commits.md §5), a different thing.
- Covers disappearing messages (write-based timer in core; read-based timers and earlier deletion are app writes; core expiry is the hard upper bound and lets a server wipe them unattended) and buffered events (a list aspect with a one-hour default). Cheaper than keep-at-most 0: nothing has to be deleted by hand, and the server does it unattended.
- Leak accepted by owner: a blind node learns which entries expire and when.
- Not provided: server-enforced minimum retention (legal hold). Keep-at-least binds honest nodes; a blind node deletes whatever a delete key or its label delete key tells it to.
- On ephemeral writes expiry runs in memory (typing indicators: `expires_in(5 s).set(true)`); presence needs none, liveness covers it.
- Nothing is kept "until every receiver has seen it": the receiver set is open and nodes die. What must outlive a receiver being offline is durable state: the inbox pattern (receiver-owned list, senders append, owner deletes, expiry as backstop) or a list with expiry.

## The all-nodes predicate
"All nodes a thing syncs to" is a state predicate computable at trusted nodes, never a protocol condition. Per label: the nodes with a valid chain that published `/node` under the thing's label (not "every node of every member", which needs rosters readers never see).
- Recency: each node places an expiring `/seen` heartbeat under each label it tracks (spec/permissions.md §4); the live one's value is its last sync of L, absent means unknown. `/seen` is one aspect of the node thing, so its joined read is "last seen anywhere"; the per-label answer comes from the sync layer's record of which heartbeat commits arrived under L, not from the aspect. (A `map<label ID, timestamp>` would give aspect semantics but leave a value-less entry record per expired heartbeat forever.) The heartbeat also carries `stranded`, the one signal a device that cannot open a label's current epoch, or cannot walk to the head because a claim set was dropped, can still send (it deposits without opening the label, under the newest established read secret it holds, which co-members open by history boxes), and what triggers a re-seal; it is placed only after a complete pass at that server (the rotation commits are uploaded before the step, notes/sync.md, so "cannot open" means not boxed, never "not fetched yet"); it fails only when the deposit key changed under it (issues/reseal-triggers.md). Membership (the node card) and recency are separate aspects because an expired register winner reads as absent and the older write does not show through: an expiring re-put of `/node` would drop the node from the membership view when it lapsed.
- Why a durable heartbeat and not a node-card re-put or ephemeral data: a reader's re-put is a deposit that sits in the label's budget until a writer promotes it, so readers × cadence × promotion delay had to fit a budget the spec never sized, and a full budget refuses the deposits that matter (grants into the group, a reader's self-removal, a revoke only readers hold). Expiry frees the budget unattended (no tombstone, no delete, no writer), so a reader holds at most ceil(lifetime / interval) entries at once and the budget is sized from the claim set (spec/sync.md §5). Rejected: deposit new card plus token-delete the old one (a delete and a 30-day tombstone per refresh, reader-side delete flows, rate-limited deletes); ephemeral writes (the asker is rarely online with the reader, and readers cannot write ephemeral data); server arrival times (untrusted, per server).
- Leak accepted by owner: a blind node sees each reader's heartbeat rhythm per label (same class as deposit and expiry times it already sees). Clock skew between the heartbeat's writer and the asker shifts the reading by the skew; lifetimes are days, so no coarse policy notices.
- A trusted node keeps its own local record of the last heartbeat it saw per node, for policies wanting a longer memory than the lifetime; it is a lower bound, not knowledge of the gap.
- Uses: write a new schema form once every node reads it (fixes the read-only old app the header heuristic cannot see), drop old-form history after that, clean up an inbox once every own device has acked.
- Dead nodes: a lost phone's heartbeat lapses and it drops out of "all nodes seen recently" on its own; a policy that needs it gone from membership too waits for an explicit node removal. Never gates deletion at blind nodes.
- "Buffer until all have seen it" = inbox pattern plus this predicate plus expiry as backstop. Fine as a core helper later; not sync machinery.

## Implementation plan
Each step merges alone and passes the gate; notes/api.md and the spec follow each.
1. **Local ephemeral things and aspects.** Schema flags and `AspectDefaults.ephemeral`, `Ak::ephemeral()/event()`; engine `ephemeral/` (session source, records, own state, expiry classes, timers); reads joined; `Write` split; persistence with chain records; `create(owner)` (Place gone at every call site); `created().commit: Option`; rights through the nearest durable owner; ephemeral `delete`; event aspects to callbacks; `Event::Ephemeral`. Interim: a group op on an ephemeral item is a durable group write. Signals untouched. Tests (osis `tests/it/ephemeral`): write and watch with no commit and no store write; first durable write persists T and its ephemeral owner chain in one commit, same tid; restart on a `Dir` store: T unknown, durable data intact, a durable thing's ephemeral aspect gone; permission on another actor's ephemeral thing; ephemeral expiry under `ManualClock`; events once each, never in state, path op `Invalid`; a mixed batch whose durable half fails applies nothing. Release bench by hand: 10^5 local ephemeral sets against a `BTreeMap` baseline.
2. **Ephemeral placements.** Overlay, `/group` and `/groups` reads, conversion at persistence (with expiry), cascade on delete, membership ops persisting first. Tests: an ephemeral item placed in a durable group lists there with no commit; persisting writes `/created` and the `/group` add in one commit; a restart before persisting leaves no ghost; a durable item placed in an ephemeral group persists the group; another node's `unplace` leaves the overlay.
3. **Synced ephemeral data, signals removed.** Envelope module, body codec and vectors; message 24 with `to`/`from` (server forwards, ignores `to` until step 4); node outbox, entitlement, receive and verify, contributions, lease and renewal, caps, acceptance window; signals and their spec go. Tests (tier 1 `World` with `Hub`s and `ManualClock`): a cursor on a synced doc reaches B's watch, B's store unchanged; an ephemeral thing syncs with its records, then persists to the same tid at B; a stopped writer's value dies at the lease, renewal keeps a live one; a non-writer's or revoked writer's parts dropped; events once and in order across two servers; replay outside the window dropped (`hostile_server` raw frames); a body over the cap dropped with `EphemeralDropped`, another writer unaffected; a part over the size limit gives `EphemeralTooLarge`.
4. **Routes, poke, direct re-send.** Handles, `Left`, `Poke`, addressed delivery in proto server, Hub and node serving; route tracking and drop on loss; poke coalescing; server poke rate. Tests: a joiner gets current values at once and other subscribers receive no frame for it (count at the Hub); `Left` drops a value without a clock advance; with two servers the value stays until both routes leave; three pokes in one interval give one broadcast; a member peer serving with no server delivers and reports `Left`.
5. **Ephemeral undo.** Log, `CommitKind::Ephemeral`, `ChangeId`, the scope rule, mixed batches as one change, history cap. Tests: undo and redo of edits on an ephemeral thing; Thing scope skips a durable thing's cursor; `NotUndoable{Compacted}` past the cap; a mixed batch undone in one step; no history after restart.
