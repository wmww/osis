# Sync access: steps, claims, re-seal
> Summary: Why opening a sync ID needs a credential of the head and how a holder gets in: steps, the step key per sync epoch, claims in the boxing commit, an older step's proof served to that step's end, rotation commits (wrapped under the superseded epoch, logged before the step), refusals carrying only a code, re-seal and stranded reports; which thing owns a sync ID; the lockout and stranding it accepts. Rules: spec/sync-ids.md §2-§6, spec/sync-opens.md §4-§6, spec/permissions.md §3-§4. Neighbours: notes/stubs.md (claims, the decided design and rejected ones), notes/sync-reasons.md, notes/keys.md (epochs), notes/keys-establishment.md (history boxes), notes/sync-opens.md (the code).

**Principle:** a grant's box to its holder is also what lets the holder in (Tess): the commit boxing a holder carries its claim, found by a claim key only that holder and the thing's readers compute. A credential of the head opens S whole; an older step's sync proof opens it only up to that step's end, so nothing written past a step's end is served under that step's key.

## Why the gate and the claim
- **Opens need a credential of the head** (spec/sync-opens.md §4): without the gate an ex-holder, or anyone who learned a sync ID, keeps fetching ciphertext old and new (counts, timing, laggards' old-epoch wraps, a wider surface against future key breaks). An older step's proof is served up to that step's end: a removed holder reads its removal (the rotation is wrapped under an epoch it holds) and a laggard the rotations it missed, and nothing written past them.
- **The step key is per sync epoch, its secret derived by readers alone** (`kdf("step key", sync secret, read roles secret)`): a laggard and an ex-holder hold the same shared keys, so the gate needs what only the holder has, its own private key, and DH with a key every reader derives lets any reader write the holder's claim with the box. The read roles secret keeps stores (sync secrets only) from testing a known KEM key against the claim keys they show. Per sync epoch, not per step: a step without a rotation changes no claim key (notes/stubs.md, Rejected).
  - A signature, not a hash preimage: a dishonest node cannot replay it elsewhere.
  - Claims pad to a power of two, at least one dummy, so a store learns a padded count per role commit, and a role commit boxing nobody looks like one boxing one holder.
  - Nested holders chain claim keys: the node key on its account's sync ID, then the account's read key on each other thing's. A writer uses M's newest `keys.0` it holds, so M tries old keysets as for boxes.
  - Concurrent admins make sibling steps (one sync epoch each): steps form a tree by `prev`, `sync.0` holds every branch, and readers take the deepest (`head_of`).
  - **A store moves only deeper, and only by its head's delete key** (spec/sync-ids.md §6 Register): off a step bearing that key, or to a step that key endorsed. A Register carries a branch: the recorded steps after the deepest step the store's path shares with the recorded path, through the first deeper than its head (a child of the head is the one-step case), or through the first deeper recorded step endorsed by the head's delete key, with the endorsement. So a store on a sibling the recorded path does not take is moved on once a step lists past the other; equal-depth siblings stand.
    - Why deeper: Register is relayable and proves only signatures, so any relay (a removed node included) holds real steps of every branch it saw. Depth keeps it from flipping a store between siblings (each flip to a sibling whose sync key the store lacks is a Stops drop and a refetch) or rolling it back to a step whose keys it still holds.
    - Why the delete key: an admin removed at a step under a new admin secret still holds every delete key before it, and could branch off any of them, as deep as it likes, onto keys of its own. By the head's key it can do no more than a child of the head, which it could while it held that key anyway (spec/sync-hostile.md §2).
    - Why endorsements: a store on a sibling whose own step rotated the admin secret (its issuer removed an admin while another admin stepped) bears a delete key no step of the recorded branch leaves from. An endorsement (a signature by delete key d over a step ID, its own domain) grants nothing a child of the head does not: its signer could make that child with any keys. It names the target step, whose ID binds the sync ID and every step above it, so it moves no other sync ID's store and endorses no other branch; it binds no head, since every store whose head bears d is one d's holders could move anyway. The admin removed at the losing step lacks d. A store keeps S unread, so its evidence is a signature it checks under the head's own key; following the recorded `sync.0` is not open to it.
    - Endorsements live in `sync.0`, on the endorsed step's record keyed by d, so any reader relays them; Ed25519 is deterministic, so concurrent endorsers write the same value. Owed (`sync_ids::actors::endorsements_owed`) by an admin holding d's admin secret while a recorded off-path step bearing d, shallower than the head, forks from a recorded step under another key and no deeper recorded step carries one by d: one endorsement of the head covers every such step of d, and stays valid while that head stays on the recorded path. Written beside the step due (`Engine::endorse_due`, through sync's step trigger), with no rotation commit to log first: the registrar still registers each step only once the source holds its role commits.
    - What stays: a store on such a step while no admin holds its admin secret (a dispute in which the winners removed every admin of that epoch) stays there (spec/sync-hostile.md §2).
    - Rejected: one child per tip at each step due (branches never merge, so every later step costs one step per tip, growing with every concurrent issue); a single-step sideways Register at equal depth (any relay flips a store between siblings).
- A grantee holding no key of the target gets in by a reference whose unit carries the target's stub, or a held grant signal (spec/sync-set.md §2 First entry), then Head and its claim key at any node holding the sync ID.

## Costs
- **A claim per boxed role per holder per boxing commit** (a read holder's read and sync claims, a sync holder's one): 1,233 bytes each in the prefix (a rotation adds 2.4 to 4.9 KB per read holder, half that per sync holder, a grant to a reader 4.9 KB, a role commit boxing nobody two dummies); a rotation roughly doubles. A read holder's sync claim is what a node holding only its sync secrets takes the thing's sync secret by (a sync chain through it, notes/stubs.md). Sharing the box's encapsulation instead: issues/claim-encapsulation.md.
- **Claim pages** (spec/wire.md §5): a block of more than `CLAIM_PAGE` (32) claims goes as pages of 32, each a blob of about 39.5 KB under S beside its commit blob, the block in the prefix 32 B per page, so a commit boxes any number of holders (bounded by its header's piece hashes, `MAX_LIST_BYTES`, about 2 million). Up to 32 claims (16 read holders) nothing changes. Pages are per encryption, not per delta (each W carries its blob key), so they go with their blob at the store layer, never as a body's pieces: the judgement never sees them. A page can reach a node after its blob (pull order is not log order): the store fills the blob's claim keys as it comes, and a parked blob is opened again by it.
- **Every node holding S shows its head claim keys**: the filter grows linearly with the holders boxed at the head's epoch, the open at a store stays flat. Measured (osis-sync `grants::an_open_at_a_store_costs_at_most_linear_in_holders_*`, 4 against 40 holders): filter entries for S 10 against 66; claim keys indexed and blocks verified at the store 2 and 1 at both; at the grantee blocks verified 9 against 45, beacons tested 101 against 312, decapsulations 7 at both.
- **Head** shows anyone naming S that the node keeps S, and the head's public values (rotation timing).
- A sync-only granter with may-grant holds no step secret: its grant carries dummies alone, so its grantee gets in only once a reader re-seals it.

## Rotations and opens
- **Rotation commits are wrapped under the epoch they supersede** (spec/crypto.md §5): a rotation hands out the new read secret, so a wrap under it would be readable by the rotator alone. Under the old wrap a laggard reads the rotations it missed in order, each under the one before. What an ex-holder gains from that blob (an honest node serves it under the old sync key until the step is registered; a dishonest node for ever): the box map of the rotation that removed it (tids and roles at the new epoch) and the removal about itself, which it gets by inbox anyway; never a secret. Under a fork the rotator picks the newest superseded epoch; a holder granted during the fork under the other branch only cannot open it and needs a re-seal (rare).
- **Rotation commits go before the step** (TLC R9; spec/sync-ids.md §6 Steps). The source checks stamps against its head's write key, so content stamped under rotated keys waits for the step; but a head naming an epoch whose rotation commit is nowhere strands every listed holder (it reaches the head, cannot open it, reports itself stranded and is re-sealed with a secret it has no commit for). So the registrar logs the owning thing's role commits the source lacks stamped under the source's head, registers once the source's stream shows them held, then logs the rest. A step at a source implies the rotation commits behind it are there (until compaction, where the `secrets.0` snapshot is), and "stranded" means not boxed. Except at a source on a step past its keys (a store granted after the epochs moved on, registered genesis, holding a later sync key S opened by): it takes nothing, so it could never show them; steps go at once until it stands at the step bearing that key, then the wait applies again.
- **An open ends at its step's end when the head moves past its sync key** (spec/sync-opens.md §6): an open kept across steps would push a removed holder that stays connected every new entry for the connection's life. Tied to the sync key, not the step: a step without a sync rotation removes no holder, so its opens stay. The registrar's own open ends too: Register is relayable and proves only that the step is signed, so a removed node relaying a step it was pushed must not keep its open by it; the registrar opens again under the new key (its record keeps the cursor). The remover's rotation commit still reaches the removed node: it is logged before the step, so it lies within the old step's end.
- **A held sync secret newer than the head's is of the head** (spec/sync-opens.md §4 One open rule): a reader's path records a step only by the `sync.0` write logged after it, under the step's keys, which it pulls only open whole; its older proof is served only up to the rotation's entries in the store. Judged by the step's own head alone, its open by the new proof would end at once and it would never record the step.
- **An ended member at a store source stays while a Register is owed there** (spec/sync-opens.md §6); a genesis Register refused while the source's step was not yet known owes the Register still (it waits for the head event), else the member closed before the head came and the store was never moved: the registrar (`inbox::register`) registers only at open store members, so closing at `until` would strand a store on an older step. The registrar reports each judged source as owed or not (`Inbox::take_owed`, a `Plan` of `Send` or `Wait`; `Stuck`, refused at the source's step, owes nothing) and opens closes an ended store member at its `until` only once told none is owed.
- **`until` reached is per class pair**: a Hit on an older step's proof opens nothing on a connection where a member open with the same classes reached its `until` (no loop of a removed reader re-opening by its old proof), but a class moved since (a reader that came to keep S unread) opens anew, so the store it became is moved on.
- **Head is asked after a refused Hit** (not only when nothing hit), unless another Hit of that Offer opened S (a holder's read and sync claim keys both shown; a filter's false positive beside a real Hit): a stub or reader testing an older step's credential hits a beacon the peer's stored filter still holds (nothing is removed from a stored filter) and is refused; the Head answer moves its claim keys to the peer's head.
- **A filter arrival tests again the sync IDs its new entries may show a tested credential of** (`Opens::retest_named`, from a per-connection index of tested beacons built at the first arrival needing it, kept by each test): a claim written after a holder tested (a re-grant at the head) is hit then, though nothing moved the holder's `changed_at`.
- **Refusals carry a code alone**: a refused Register answered with the head, a refused Deposit with the budget, or a Delete's Status with the head would let anyone with a sync ID (ex-holder, link holder, an ex-admin re-sending an old delete) watch rotations and deposit activity forever. Nothing needs them: heads reach a node through its open (the stream's head events) or by Head; a stale deposit is signed again from the node's own seed.
  - Code 9 stays apart from 1: it tells a signer its key was superseded, not bad, so a stale deposit is signed again at once with the current deposit key, and an ephemeral entry or delete waits for the newer key instead of being dropped or looping. It tells a holder of an earlier key only that it was superseded, which it mostly knew. Head answers anyone (spec/sync-hostile.md §1: it says that the node keeps S and its path, public values its filter's beacons show to a holder anyway).

## Re-seal and stranded reports
- **Re-seal is the one recovery path** (spec/permissions.md §3 Re-seal) for a holder with the right private keys that cannot reach the current epoch: its missed rotations compacted (`ROLE_KEEP` after superseded), its grant unknown to the rotator, a fork branch it never received. Nothing a co-reader serves helps by itself (every current commit's wrap is under the epoch it lacks, and a peer serves wraps, not keys), so someone who may rotate seals the current secret to it, with the epochs' history boxes (without them a re-sealed holder could decrypt the history box but not fetch it: it sits in a rotation commit wrapped under the epoch it lacks, TLC R3). A sibling node of the same account is usually a writer, not an admin, of the thing, so "who may rotate" matters.
  - The re-seal carries the holder's claim at the current sync epoch (spec/sync-ids.md §3), so the holder opens S by its claim key at any node holding S and takes the re-seal there. Its own private keys never change, so a node stranded on its account's sync ID is re-sealed the same way.
  - Nothing unattended re-seals. Triggers, both SHOULD: a current holder seen without a box at a current epoch, and a stranded report.
- **Stranded = no key it holds opens S's current epoch** (spec/permissions.md §4 Stranded), whether or not a claim key was tried: a holder no claim at the head names (its grant raced by a rotation, a rotation compacted) has nothing to claim with.
- **The report** (a Stranded message to a connected writer, else a stranded notice sealed to S's thing's newest keyset this node holds, which a writer acts on as it opens it: `Engine::open_sealed_fire`, `take_stranded`) needs no key of S's current epoch: a sealed fire goes under the thing's public key record and deposit seed. It names the holder the node reads through and carries that holder's current `keys.0` commits: a node that left the holder out and no longer follows it learns its next keyset and deposit seed only so (spec/crypto.md §4, Reported). A link key is never named: it has no inbox; its claim in its grant and in each rotation lets it in.
  - A receiver takes only the holder's authority chain from it, each commit rolled back whole unless valid with every edge write applied (`Engine::ingest_valid`), so what a report can make its receiver keep is bounded by the holder's real authority history, not by the sender (a notice has no sender to judge: anyone with an admin's deposit seed can send one).
  - Rejected: a current-role check on the author (it drops the grants a since-removed admin made that the honest chain runs through); taking a chain commit only as a carried `keys.0` commit awaits it (`Exam::Awaits` names only records; a missing role shows as no reach, not as which edge); inferring the holder from the things the connection proved, up their record chains (it finds only account and node holders, so a member stranded through an organization while connected to a writer would never be heard).
  - The cost: a few KB of `keys.0` per report, and the holder's deposit seed handed to the thing's writers. Not new in kind: the holder's rotation commits, fresh seed included, reach every reader of the thing as carried certificates once it is sealed there.
- Re-seal soundness rests on established epochs (notes/keys-rotation.md, Re-seals): within `ROLE_KEEP` the departures travel in the epoch chain a re-sealer must hold; after compaction they travel in the role history and the removal commit apart from the `secrets.0` snapshot, so a node with an incomplete view (one that never received the removal, an interrupted first sync) can box a removed holder at a snapshot-established epoch by an eager rotation or a grant (re-seals themselves wait for a complete pass and chain-established epochs). Healed by the next rotation of a node that knows the departure; accepted.

## Owner of a sync ID
- **A sync ID is g's only by g's sync key** (spec/sync-ids.md §4 Owner; `Core::named_sync_id`): any admin of g writes g's `sync.0` and can name any sync ID there, so an entry counts only when a step on a verified path from its genesis bears the sync key of a sync secret of g held here. Steps chain by signature to the genesis delete key the sync ID commits to, so only the owner's admins make a step bear g's key. Ignored, never judged invalid: the check turns on held secrets.
  - A node holding the genesis seed (an admin) takes the seed's sync ID alone; others check the sync key, since every holder holds a sync secret and only admins the seed. The genesis step bears no sync secret's key; every holder holds the older secrets by history boxes, so some step below it always matches.
  - While this node holds no sync secret of g's own (it reads g by derived secrets no step bears), g's entries are hints. Bounded: such a g holds no secret deriving that sync ID's keys, so it registers and deletes nothing.
  - An admin of t that also holds sync on u can give t u's sync secret as a new epoch (nothing checks a rotation's secrets are fresh) and so list u's sync ID as t's. Hostile admins are out of scope.

## What removal, compaction and re-sealing leave
- A removed writer keeps its delete keys, but they alone delete nothing: a Delete also needs the head's sync or write key, which the step its removal requires replaces. Until an admin steps, writers stamp and open with the keys of the epoch the source's head registers (held by history boxes) and wrap under the newest they hold; a writer's rotation changes no delete key, so a sync ID whose admins are all offline keeps its old step while content under it is already unreadable to the removed holder.
- A removed admin holds the current delete key until the next step and can register a child of its own first at a node keeping S unread. Hostile admins are out of scope; the remedy is local and manual (nothing detects it): restart S at that node (dropped and synced again, spec/sync-ids.md §6), after which a reader registers the recorded path again, or S's readers stop syncing with it. No sync ID is ever replaced.
- Re-sealing is triggered, never guaranteed: a stranded notice is lost when S's stores refuse it (the deposit budget full, or the thing's deposit seed rotated since this node last read its `keys.0`) and no writer is connected; the holder then waits for the next trigger: a writer connecting (the report goes to it as a Stranded message), or a newer step or a move of the holder's `keys.0` (a new report key, the notice fired again; osis-sync `inbox::stranded`).
- **Holders at depth 0** (spec/requirements.md, Permissions; notes/holder-actors.md): a party removed from a holder M of A reads A's content sealed to a keyset of M written without knowing the removal (an ignorant fork, or a forgery where the removal or the forgery is not held) until M's knowing rotation reaches A's writers; a grant some sealer has not seen lets a cycle form there until the writer holds the grants.
- **A deposit-only step** (a new deposit key, no other role newer) waits for a complete pass of S begun after it came due (osis-sync `Read::step_since`, `StepReady::AfterPass`; status runs the pass through `passes_wanted`) and while the engine holds an unapplied `keys.0` write of the thing signed by a current admin or misses a valid `keys.0` part (`flows::sync_ids::step_due`, `rotation::keys_write_unapplied`; spec/permissions.md §3). The pass place is the one it first came due at, so a co-admin refreshing again and again delays it by one pass at most. Why: a co-admin's refresh step can arrive before its `keys.0` write, and the laggard would step back to the old key, letting a departed party deposit again until it applied the write. Unsigned or unopenable ones never count: anyone can send a pending commit, and an entry that never opens would hold the step back for good.
- A holder a rotation raced (granted concurrently) has no claim at the new epoch until a re-seal writes one; one whose missed rotations were compacted opens S by its older proof and finds no rotation past its step's end to read, so it is stranded until re-sealed. Re-sealing is the one recovery.
- `sync.0` growth is about 290 B per step per sync ID per rotation, for good. If growth ever matters, a root step carrying the current keys signed by the genesis delete key would let old steps be dropped.
