# External holds
> Summary: The expiring external hold (Tess, Gimbal) as a design for issues/unit-dependent-release.md: a reference from another unit to a dependent thing X keeps X in its independent's unit through an expiring strong on that independent, renewed by nodes holding both units that may write it. Stated as a design, then checked against the sync unit design, the spec and the requirements: what holds, every difference between a dependent and an independent target that remains (one hard: a referring unit none of whose holders may write X's independent), three rules the proposal needs to be sound (renewal by a well-founded closure, reads gated by it, purges kept past a lapse), costs, the open choices, and the model cases for held-model `spec_unit`. Nothing here is built or modelled.

Marks: **[checked: source]** read in the spec or note named; **[argued]** reasoned here, not modelled. Words: notes/terms.md. Throughout: X is a dependent thing whose independent is H; G is a thing of another unit, whose independent is J (J ≠ H); X' is the same arrangement with X independent, minted with H, holding no grant of its own, so that the same parties reach it (H's holders by the record edge, notes/sync-unit-design.md §5).

## The rule, if adopted
- **A hold is an expiring strong on H.** A core aspect held.0 on H: a map from a 16-byte **source tag** to a strong reference to X. Each entry is written in a commit of its own carrying `expires` (spec/commits.md §6: one expiry per commit, and a write mixing expiries is refused, so the hold never rides in the referring commit) [checked]. Purgeable by `writer`; schema default expiry E (below); H's admins may set another for held.0 in H's retention setting, which already gives a writer its default expiry per AK [checked: spec/commits.md §6, §5 The setting].
- **H's unit is unchanged in form**: H and the dependents of H reached from H by strongs through things of the unit (notes/sync-unit-design.md §6). An unexpired hold is such a strong, so X and what X reaches inside the unit are in it; an expired one is a value-less write, which references nothing [checked: spec/data-model.md §8 What counts, "a value-less write never"]. No new membership rule, blob class, wire object or store row: a hold is one ordinary expiring commit under S(H) [argued from the above]. Handles are untouched: X is inside H's unit, so a hold lists none [checked: design §2 Handle blobs].
- **Source tag**: `kdf("hold", sync secret of J at the epoch the writer holds as current, tid of X)`, cut to 16 bytes. One entry per (referring unit, target): every strong from J's unit to X shares it, every other unit has its own.
- **Placed** by the node writing a strong from J's unit to X, in the same storage transaction, where it may write H (as a node writes D's tickets beside what it enters, spec/sync-set.md §1 The account's tickets [checked]); and by any **maintainer** that finds one missing.
- **Maintainers**: nodes that read J's unit, hold H's unit and may write H. Each, for each strong from J's unit to a dependent of H that is not destroyed, keeps one hold: it **renews** when the standing one has less than E/2 left on its clock (a write at the same key with a fresh expiry, under its current tag; the entry under an older tag of J ends in that commit), and **ends** it when no strong of J's unit reaches the target: a write of the entry with an expiry 1 ms after its `ts`, never a map removal, so no removal tombstone stays [argued: spec/crdt.md §5.6, a removal record stays until a reset]. One commit renews or ends every hold a maintainer keeps on H.
- **Well-founded**: a maintainer counts a strong of J's unit only while its own node closure reaches the strong's thing **with each hold counted as an edge from its source's strong, not from H** (the least fixpoint: a hold is used only once the thing holding its source strong is reached). A hold is no reason to renew itself.
- **Reads follow the same closure**: a thing a node holds only because a hold put it in an entered unit, and that its own closure (holds counted from their sources) does not reach, reads as not held; the node stores it for the unit, as for a unit kept unread.
- **Lapse**: with no renewal, the hold expires at T. Writers of S(H) then judge X's bodies must-not-hold and delete them at stores keeping S(H) unread from T + `MAX_DRIFT` on their clocks (as keep bounds wait, spec/commits.md §5 Keep at least [checked]). Every node releases X as any thing leaving its unit (spec/sync-set.md §3).
- **A referrer's own copy**: a node whose closure still reaches X by a strong it holds, while no entered unit carries X, keeps X local, under no sync ID, as the local part keeps its dependents (spec/sync-set.md §2 Node closure [checked: the local part is the only place a dependent outside every unit is held today]). A maintainer holding such a copy that may write H **restores**: it writes a fresh hold and logs the bodies it holds under S(H) (verbatim under a new wrap, or a fresh encryption; spec/sync-judgement.md §3 Own store, design §5 [checked]). A node that only reads H restores nothing (it cannot stamp under S(H)).
- **Purges outlive a lapse**: a purge request or destruction on a dependent of H stays in S(H)'s required set as a header-only lasting blob after the thing leaves the unit, so a restore cannot bring back what it removed (Restoring, below).
- **Hidden like keying**: held.0 is no known reference (no backlink of X shows it), is never listed, and reads as the role-class aspects do to the API.

## 1. Fit with the design
- **What a hold is in §2's terms**: a strong of H's own unit, so a function of S(H) alone; every reader of H computes the same unit from it, the clock aside (below) [argued]. It is not a home (that is delta metadata on G's strong, which still brings H's unit, design §2 A strong's home), not a handle (nothing is listed: X is inside), not a key blob, not a member blob. G's strong keeps its home and its handle as now.
- **Who reads it**: every reader of H, who learns the number of outside units referencing each dependent of H and, for a referring unit whose sync secret it holds, that it references X. A node keeping H unread sees an expiring commit appear under S(H) about every E/2 per maintained hold; it deletes the blob at T unattended (prefix expiry, spec/commits.md §6 [checked]).
- **A node keeping H's unit unread** keeps X's bodies until a writer of H deletes them, as for any thing leaving a unit (spec/sync-set.md §3 A thing leaving a sync unit [checked]). Nothing new is judged there.
- **Added**: one core aspect, its schema default expiry, a kdf label, and engine logic (placement, renewal, ending, the well-founded closure for renewal and reads, the referrer's local copy, restore, purges kept past a lapse). Nothing on the wire beyond the aspect's value [argued].
- **Spec sections that change**: spec/data-model.md §8 (held.0 counts for the closure and is no known reference) and §9 (the aspect; schema/core.osis); spec/sync-set.md §2 (reads and renewal by the well-founded closure; the referrer's local copy) and §3 (lapse, restore); a new part for placement and maintenance beside §1's account tickets; spec/sync-judgement.md §2 (purges kept past a lapse; deletes for a lapse from T + `MAX_DRIFT`); spec/crypto.md §6 (the tag's label); spec/requirements.md Data model ("kept while something references it strongly") states the weaker property the differences below leave, for Tess.

## 2. Differences between a dependent and an independent target
Each: same, or different and whether a rule fixes it. X' never needs a hold: S(X') is its own unit, kept while anything enters it.
1. **Strong written by a writer of H**: hold placed; same while maintained [argued]. After a lapse: the accepted gap.
2. **Strong written by a node that reads H and may not write it**: no hold from it. A maintainer (any node holding J's unit that may write H) places one. **Where J's unit has no holder that may write H, no hold is ever placed**: when H drops X, X leaves S(H) at once with every node online, and G's other holders lose it for good; X' would be kept. Not an availability gap (no lapse, no return), and no rule inside holds fixes it: a reader may not write H (spec/requirements.md Permissions, "Readers only read"). Ways out, each with its cost: an external signal on H by which a reader asks for a hold, converted into one by the next writer of H that syncs (needs H's admins to enable it, spec/commits.md §5 The setting; default-on for every thing is a carve-out; a hostile reader can then pin any dependent of H it can name for E, which the threat model allows: "Malicious readers can only waste storage"; turns this case into the lapse class); or carrying X under J's unit for such a referrer (the built model's per-unit carriage, design §1). Tess's call [argued].
3. **Strong written by someone with no role on H**: it cannot read X, so it writes from a bare tid with no home (design §2, issues/unit-reference-home.md); a maintainer still places a hold, so X stays in S(H); G's holders reach H's unit only by another route, as for X' (whose key is in S(H) only). Same as X' apart from the existing home corner [argued].
4. **H drops its own reference**: the hold keeps X: same.
5. **H's writers want X gone**: removing H's strong does not; ending the hold does not for long (the next maintainer places it again); they destroy X (purge `created.0`, its admins being H's), the same act as for X' [checked: spec/commits.md §5 Destruction]. **Different**: any writer of H can end a hold, and X then leaves S(H) until a maintainer places it again; nobody can do that to X'. Availability class (a writer of H can also delete any entry of S(H)); no rule fixes it short of a hold no writer of H may end, which leaks.
6. **X destroyed**: maintainers place and renew nothing for a destroyed target, so the hold lapses; reads absent at once either way. Same.
7. **X's dependents and X's references**: the hold is a strong from H, so H's unit holds X and every dependent of H that X reaches, and lists the handles of X's strongs leaving the unit; X' keeps its own dependents and lists its own. A strong from X' to a dependent W of H needs its own hold (X' is another unit); from held X, W is reached inside. Both keep W while maintained. Same [argued].
8. **G released by every node**: no maintainer is left; the hold lapses at T. X' is released at the next pass of each node. **Different**: X stays in S(H), at every holder of H, up to E longer. Within "Release is not prompt" (spec/requirements.md Data model) but a difference; only a smaller E shortens it.
9. **The referrer's writer loses write on H, or read on J**: it stops maintaining; another maintainer goes on; with none, lapse: the accepted gap.
10. **A writer of G that may not write H removes the reference**: maintainers end the hold; with none, X stays up to E: as 8.
11. **Promotion with a hold standing**: after P the hold is a strong from H to the independent X, so H's holders enter S(X) while it stands: up to E of extra holding (reads gated, so no read differs). Maintainers stop renewing a hold on an independent target and do not end it early, so a void within E finds it standing. Against issues/unit-promotion.md:
    - **1 (a promotion drops what H's unit reached past X)**: holds **help**. Z, a dependent of X that H reaches, becomes a dependent of X's unit referenced from H's; the promoter, holding both and writing X, places a hold on Z in X in P's transaction, and re-homes H's strong to Z (home X) so H's holders find X's unit. That replaces "adds a strong from X", which writes into X's app-visible data. Z is then exposed to a lapse, as it would be had X been independent from its minting [argued].
    - **2 (a voided promotion strands what was written under S(X))**: neutral; a hold standing at the void keeps X in S(H) for the move back, which needs the way out of the issue all the same.
    - **3 (a forked promotion's lesser sync ID)**: neutral.
    - **Races**: a lapse racing P loses nothing (the promoter holds X's bodies and logs them under S(X), design §5 carrier rule); a renewal by a node lacking P adds an E of moot hold.
12. **X promoted, then the promotion judged invalid**: X is dependent again; a hold standing keeps it; one lapsed meanwhile is placed again by the first maintainer to hold the void. Availability class.
13. **A node keeping both units unread**: cannot maintain (it reads no strong of J), keeps X's bodies until a writer's delete; same as for any entry [argued].
14. **A new reader of G**: while the hold stands, G's home brings H's unit and X is there: same. After a lapse: the accepted gap; but see Restoring.
15. **Undo and revert of the referencing write**: an undo removing G's strong ends the hold as a removal does; a redo places it again. Where X was released in between, it is gone as X' would be once nothing referenced it. Same [argued]. Holds are never listed, so nobody undoes one.
16. **Several referring units**: one hold each, maintained apart; X stays while any stands. A shared hold cannot be ended by a maintainer that sees only some referrers. Same as X' [argued].
17. **G itself a dependent of another unit**: the source is G's unit J whatever keys G; when G leaves J's unit its strongs stop counting and the hold ends. Same.
18. **Cycles across units**: X -> Y, Y -> X, Y a dependent of J, a hold in H on X (source J) and one in J on Y (source H), neither H nor J referencing them. Counted from H, each hold reaches its target from a root-reached H, so each source is reached and renews the other: **kept for good with nothing outside referencing the cycle**, against "a cycle nothing outside it references is released whole" (spec/requirements.md Data model). Counting each hold from its source (the well-founded rule) fixes it: neither source is reached without the other's hold, so neither renews and both lapse. Counting no hold at all instead breaks chains (R -> Y with Y held in J, Y -> X: X's hold would lapse with every maintainer present). With the rule: released up to E late, as 8.
19. **What `NotCarried` reports** (spec/sync-set.md §2): a strong whose target is not held. While the hold stands, nothing; after a lapse, or in case 2, the strong, as for a dangling X'. A write in case 2 cannot be warned of at write time (X is held then).
20. **What H's holders hold**: a node holding H and not G holds X and enters what X references, as nodes keeping S(H) unread must (they cannot tell X's handles apart, design §3, so a reader skipping them would enter less than a keeper, against P2). **Different in cost only** with reads gated; without the gate a read of X there returns content where X' reads not held (spec/requirements.md API: "Nothing an app can observe depends on how osis stores or keys a thing").
21. **Visible data**: without hiding, an untyped read of H shows held.0 and X's backlinks show H; X' shows neither. Hidden as keying aspects are (spec/data-model.md §9: X and X' already differ in `keys.0`, `secrets.0`, `sync.0`), the same.

Found on the way, not the proposal's: a strong to X' written after its promotion carries no home (design §2: "A strong to an independent thing needs none"), so G's holders whose only role on X' is through H enter S(X') only where something else in their closure holds H (design §4); a strong to dependent X brings H's unit by its home. A home for every strong to a thing whose mint admin's independent is another unit (15 B a delta) would make the two alike [argued].

## 3. The expiring write
- **As commits** [checked: spec/commits.md §6, §2]: a hold is its own commit, a content commit of one map write with `expires`, in the transaction of the referring write; never a role commit (those carry no expiry). A renewal is a new write at the same key ("an edit keeps the expiry it was written with" binds an edit, not a new write); the renewed write supersedes the old, which expires at its own T anyway.
- **Bytes** [checked: notes/thing-cost.md, Floors]: a small commit is about 990 B on the wire and 1.7-1.9 KB stored per node today, 470 B and 600 B as the survey targets; a batched renewal adds about 60 B per further hold [argued]. Paid at every holder of S(H) and at stores keeping it unread.
- **Growth**: flat per hold [argued]. At most the standing write and the one it superseded are valued; each goes at its T; the leftovers (map writes value-less, keys erased, spec/crdt.md §4.7) are covered by "Cover once quiet" (spec/sync-compaction.md §1) and the commits then forgotten (spec/commits.md §5 Forgetting). Ending by an immediately expiring write, not a removal, leaves no tombstone (issues/zero-growth.md: set and map removals keep one). To be counted at n and 10n renewals in a test.
- **Clocks**: a writer whose clock is behind gives a shorter hold, ahead a longer one (receivers park its commit until within `MAX_DRIFT`, spec/crdt.md §2.2 [checked]); E is months, drift is not. A writer of H whose clock runs more than `MAX_DRIFT` ahead deletes X's entries at stores keeping S(H) unread before T, and honest writers log them again (spec/sync-judgement.md §4), so it churns while its clock is wrong: it harms others' data, against "A wrong local clock harms only that node's own writes" (spec/requirements.md Decentralization), the same exposure a fast right holder already has over history at keepers (spec/commits.md §5 Keep at least). Readers whose clocks differ disagree on the unit within the drift, as on any expiry [argued].
- **"Whether others are alive is never a protocol condition"** (spec/requirements.md Ephemeral data): kept in letter: a lapse is the expiry of data, nothing waits for or tests anyone; it is the pattern that section states for a value "that should live only while its author is there", applied to a durable write. In substance X's retention follows whether a maintainer was there within E: the gap Tess accepts [argued].

## 4. Maintenance
- **Who**: nodes reading J's unit, holding H's and with write on H (an account's nodes are one author: they renew one entry). Renewal at under E/2 left, by own clock, so once one has renewed the others see a fresh T and skip; maintainers out of contact each renew: at most one write per maintainer per E/2, per H [argued].
- **Early end**: by a maintainer seeing no strong of J's unit to X (case 10: the remover may not write H, so a maintainer, or the lapse, ends it). The removal and the end are separate commits, judged apart: a removal later judged invalid leaves the hold ended until the next maintainer pass places it again (availability class).
- **Idle cost**: one small commit per maintained H per E/2, growing by about 60 B per outside reference to a dependent: work while nothing changes, per reference. Against notes/principles.md Fast by construction ("each change does work in proportion to what changed") it is a standing cost in the wrong thing; the cheap route is a reference to an independent thing (no hold). It is bounded and flat in storage. Tess's to accept [argued].

## 5. Restoring after a lapse
- **Logging others' bodies**: a writer of S(H) logs any held body under S(H) with its own stamp (spec/sync-judgement.md §3 Own store; design §5) [checked]; it needs the sealed blob or a fresh encryption under H's current epoch (spec/crypto.md §5 Passing on). The referrer's local copy must keep X's blobs or be able to encrypt again.
- **Purged meanwhile**: a purge or destruction on X made before the lapse, missed by a maintainer that was away longer than E, has left S(H) with X (lasting commits are held "under every sync ID whose sync unit holds t", spec/commits.md §5 Purge [checked]). Restoring would bring the purged content back, against "Expired and purged content never returns while the purge counts" (spec/requirements.md Deletion). X' still referenced keeps its requests under S(X'). Fix: such requests on a dependent stay in S(H) as header-only lasting blobs, for good: about 300 B per purge or destruction of a released dependent at every holder of H (purges are outside the churn rule, issues/zero-growth.md) [argued].
- **Two returning nodes with different stale states** both log; the bodies merge as any partial receipts (spec/requirements.md Convergence). Same as two stale copies of X' [argued].
- **A node that only reads H** keeps its local copy and restores nothing.

## 6. Naming the source
- **The tid of G or J** (Tess's first form): every reader of H learns that a thing of another unit references X and, knowing that tid from anything it reads (a holder in a roster, a weak), confirms the value of another thing (spec/requirements.md Confidentiality between readers: "Sharing one thing reveals no value of another"). Rejected.
- **Recommended**: the source tag above. Every holder of H counts the holds (map keys); only a node holding J's sync secret matches one, the same parties who can already test J's handles (design §3) [argued]. Keyed by J's current sync epoch, so a holder removed from J matches nothing written after J's rotation; a renewal moves the entry to the new tag in the same commit, so a rotation of J costs no write of its own. A maintainer matches the tags of J's epochs it holds (design §2 Sealing carries superseded sync secrets).

## 7. The expiry E
- **At 0**: no hold; rule 1 of issues/unit-dependent-release.md. **At infinity**: a hold never lapses, and cases 8, 10, 18 and a lost write on H keep X for good: rule 2's growth, by references instead of mintings.
- **It trades**: how long every maintainer may be away before X leaves (the gap) against how long an unseen release is late (cases 8, 10, 11, 18) and how much released churn S(H) holds at once (rate times E); renewals per period are 2 per E.
- **Class**: minor consequence on both sides (notes/arbitrary-values.md): neither side breaks correctness, both are availability or delay. **Recommended**: a schema default of `ROLE_KEEP` (90 days, the existing bound on how long a node may be away and come back cleanly, spec/sync-judgement.md §2), overridable per thing by H's admins in its retention setting (it is H's storage). Not the referrer's choice: the referrer pays nothing for a long hold.

## 8. Alternatives
- **A hold with no expiry**: leaks in cases 8, 10, 18 and on a lost write on H; nothing repairs them. Worse.
- **Promoting X when referenced from outside**: needs admin on X (H's), costs a sync ID and its 9.4 KB (notes/thing-cost.md), and a reader cannot do it either. Fine as the app's cheap route, not as the rule.
- **Carrying X under the referrer's unit** (the built model): no gap and no reader case, but brings back per-unit carriage, retag and copies the redesign removes (design §1). The only alternative that fixes case 2.
- Of its kind (keep X in its own unit by state of that unit) the expiring hold is the best found: the only form that releases when nobody is left to judge.

## 9. Verdict
The sync unit design stands with the rule plus three additions it needs (well-founded renewal, reads gated by the same closure, purges kept past a lapse), **except for case 2**: a referring unit none of whose holders may write H keeps X only at its own nodes, and loses it everywhere else at once and for good when H drops it. That is no availability gap and holds cannot fix it; Tess decides between accepting it (the requirement then says so), an external hold signal, or carrying for that case. Remaining with the additions:
- the accepted gap after a lapse, including a writer of H ending a hold (5), a lost write on H (9, 12);
- release late by up to E where no maintainer sees the reference go (8, 10, 11, 18);
- cost only: H's holders store X and what it reaches (20); idle renewals per outside reference (§4);
- a writer's clock far ahead harms others' entries at keepers (§3);
- found on the way: the missing home on strongs to an independent thing minted with another unit's thing (§2).

## 10. Model cases for held-model `spec_unit`
Each with its negative control; the oracle compares what every node reads with X dependent and with X' independent over the same history, equal except X's availability after a lapse.
1. H, X, J with J -> X (home H): H drops H -> X; a maintainer renews: X stays at every holder of H and J. No renewal past T: X leaves S(H), J's nodes keep a local copy; a maintainer returning restores. Control: no hold (rule 1) loses X at once.
2. J's strong removed by a writer of J that may not write H: a maintainer ends the hold; with none, X leaves at T and not before.
3. Reader case: J's holders read H, none writes it; H drops X: X lost at J's other nodes (pinned as a finding; X' kept).
4. Cycle: X -> Y, Y -> X across H and J, holds both ways, no outside reference: with renewal from sources, both lapse; control (renewal from H) keeps both for good.
5. Chain: R -> Y (Y a dependent of J, hold in J), Y -> X (hold in H): both renewed; control (no hold counted) lets X lapse with maintainers present.
6. Reads at a node holding H alone: X stored, read not held; control without the gate reads it.
7. Promotion: P of X with a hold standing: no loss, hold not renewed, gone at T; void within E: X back in S(H) by the hold; issues/unit-promotion.md 1's worlds with the promoter's holds and re-homing: Z kept.
8. Purge then lapse, a maintainer away longer than E returns: restore brings back nothing purged; control (request leaving with X) brings it back.
9. Two maintainers out of contact renew at once: one entry stands, at most one write each per E/2, settles.
10. Growth: n and 10n renewals and ends of one hold: stored rows flat.
