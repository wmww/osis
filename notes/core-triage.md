## 2. Requirement sinkholes

Method: for each requirement, the merged items whose subject it was, the open issues it still generates, whether periodic runs still find new instances, and the concrete relaxation. The order harness got teeth twice (d24, 256) and each time found a batch; that is the harness catching up, not the product regressing, but it is also why the stream has not stopped.

### 2.1 Atomic apply across things: "A node applies all the parts it may read and syncs at once, or none"
- **Rounds**: 85 atomic-replace, 187 pending-expiry, 229 pending-drops, 256 harness-clock, d22 atomic-carrierless (two reviews, one attempt removed), d25 chainless-parts (three reviewers), d31 parts-holes (four rounds), d33 atomic-first-sync (three rounds), d35 parts-seeds (three rounds), d44 roster-exception: about 10 items, most multi-round.
- **Exceptions already written into spec/requirements.md**: a part still away after a thing entered the sync set (Decentralization), a holder looking removed until the rest of a roster snapshot arrives (same line), a commit shown without a part when a later snapshot of that aspect is held (API). Three exceptions on one sentence is the sign.
- **Open**: atomic-wait-no-later-commit, pending-unbounded and purged-pending-unchecked (pending exists because parts wait), linked-header-junk-record, order-seed-871301 (late parts), the cross-group pend in revoke-needs-whole-tree: 8 of 99.
- **Slowing?** Somewhat: d35 and 256 each closed a batch and left one stated limit. But every new drop mechanism (expiry, purge, keep bounds, receipts-only) re-opens "does a part that left still count", and the rule now has stand-ins (`Dropped::stands_in`, `Held::ever_holders`, `at_most_bound`, `spent_copy`) to make a missing part count as held.
- **Relaxation: atomic per thing, advisory across things.** A part applies when its own thing's record is held and it verifies; a commit's other parts apply when theirs do. Nothing waits on another thing.
  - Deletes: the cross-thing wait (`waits`, `UnknownCreated` for other things), the "missing part counts as held" stand-ins and rule 5 of session-sources, the `PendingExpiry` flag's reason to exist, most of the pending store's growth problem (#1, #2 shrink to "a part on a thing I do not hold"), the three requirement exceptions, the harness's `parted`/late-parts machinery, and the whole class of "which part arrived first" seeds. Several hundred lines in engine apply/waits/retention plus harness code.
  - Keeps: atomicity within one thing (all of a commit's aspects on one thing apply together: the record is one unit), signatures over the whole commit (a part is still provably part of a commit), the API's one `Write` call.
  - User-visible cost: an app that moves an item between two groups, or writes a reference on A and the target on B, can see the A half before the B half at a node that got A's label first, until B's part arrives (the same window the Atomic exception already allows when a snapshot of that aspect is held). App invariants across things already have to hold under merge, so this adds "and under partial arrival". Requirements change: the "all or none" sentence becomes "all parts on one thing at once; across things each part applies as its thing arrives".
  - Honest read: this is the one relaxation that removes machinery rather than a check. It is also the one Tess may care most about keeping; the case for it is that the guarantee is already holed three ways and apps cannot build on it today.

### 2.2 Arrival-order independence under keep-at-most, expiry, purge and validity flips: "a node's state and its judgment of every write are functions of the data it holds"
- **Rounds**: the largest stream. Round 13 items 21, 25, 26, 29, 31, 34, 36, 42, 43, 49, 51, 56, 59, 73, 113, 117, 131, 134, 139, 182, 184, 185, 221, 222 (R1 plus suspect repair), 226, 228, 231, 233, 234, 237, 241, 242, 244, 246, 247, 251, 256; devbox d24, d38, d39, d42, d45, d55: about 45 items, 11 of the last 28.
- **Open**: expired-entry-key-kept, wide-purge-order, vouched-key-erased (honest half), import-body-never-live, covered-body-new-label. Relaxation A and the expiry exception below are done (notes/order-study.md); at-most-map-snapshot-drop and reopen-char-fallen-delete closed under B (keep-two).
- **Slowing?** Periodic runs 4-7 and 10 found nothing; runs 8-14 found about one seed each; the batches of 231-251 came from the harness growing (d24 teeth, item 256 clock mode, purge mixes). The remaining honest shape is always the same: keep-at-most or expiry on an aspect, plus a validity flip or a purge on it (notes/convergence-study.md: the class is flip x non-vouching drop). R1 made the common case converge by delivery; the suspect repair covers what no copy can bring back. What is left is mostly the tail: nested maps, text chars, snapshots carrying only dead writes, and internal rows.
- **Relaxation A, cheap: the oracle checks reads and held bodies, not internal state.** Tess already approved per-node death dating ("may be approximate, never early") in round 13, so death dates, `bounded_held` counts, dormancy flags and which of two covered-equal snapshots a node kept are not requirements. Today the harness compares them (`dating`, `register_deaths`, `bounded_held`, index lines) and the open seeds 93104 and 74305, body-class-moves, expiry-no-death-on-snapshot and the honest half of vouched-key-erased are exactly those comparisons.
  - Deletes: those harness checks and about five issues; no product code.
  - Cost: none visible to users. Risk: a death dated early at one node drops a body early there; keep "never early" as the one death check (the oracle node that keeps every body already gives it).
- **Relaxation B: on an aspect with keep bounds or expiry, agreement after a writer's repair, not on delivery.** State in requirements: nodes holding the same bodies read alike, except an aspect where a body was dropped for its bound or expiry and a validity flip or purge then changed what it would have joined; such an aspect reads alike once a writer of it has compacted after the flip (the suspect repair, built in 222). Today the spec claims convergence on delivery and the repair is the backstop.
  - Deletes: the `drop_flip`/`both_dropped_flips` tolerances become one rule; closes at-most-map-snapshot-drop, wide-purge-order, reopen-char-fallen-delete, expired-entry-key-kept's keep-at-most half, applied-by-part-before-expiry as stated limits, and future seeds of the shape. Little product code goes (the residues `v/`, `w/` stay: without them every un-merge would be a repair).
  - Cost: a divergence on such an aspect is visible until a writer is online and heartbeats (6 h cadence; sooner on any write), and for good if no writer ever returns. In practice: a chat with disappearing messages where a member was kicked with a backdated revoke may show one extra or missing old message at one device until anyone writes. Oracles keep running with repair off so bugs still show up as seeds; they just stop being bugs in the honest tail.
- **Relaxation C, bigger: keep bounds act only through compaction.** Every non-vouching drop is the root of the class, so make keep-at-most drops vouching: a body under a bound is dropped only once a valid snapshot covers it, and the writer that replaces a bounded write compacts in the same commit (a replace of a bounded aspect is a snapshot). Removes the class by construction and the suspect repair with it. Cost: keep at most 0 ("delete right after a change", which Tess wants prompt) then depends on the replacing writer compacting, which is O(aspect state) per write: fine for registers and messages, wrong for a large bounded map. Not recommended unless A and B prove insufficient.
- **Honest read**: the requirement is sound and R1 was the right fix; the tail is the harness checking more than the requirement (A) and the spec promising delivery-convergence where the design already has a repair (B). Do A now; B is Tess's call and costs users almost nothing.

### 2.3 Kick cost: "Removing a holder costs ... one entry per aspect it wrote, never to content or history"
- **Rounds**: 1 apply-revoke-order, 45/49 snapshot receipts, 69, 89, 93 frontier-inflation, 128, 145 receipts-only, 159 backdated-revoke, 206, 213, 219 session-1, 224 frontier-f2, 227 lockout, 239 session-25, d36/d41 keep after removal, d42 purge-rejudge: about 17 items. Correctness converged (notes/convergence-study.md: no removal rule removes the R x D class; the frontier model stays). What keeps coming back is cost: frontier-clobbers-history (F3/F4 permanent heads unbuilt), frontier-certificate-cost, long-aspect-cost (a kick past about 1.9e6 uncompacted commits fails at the author), session-sources step 3 (compact before removing; a roster snapshot past 64 MiB is refused for good).
- **Open**: frontier-certificate-cost, session-sources step 3, revoke-needs-whole-tree, fork-held-by-non-rotators: 5 (until-ending-drop done; repudiation-takes-honest-rotation now a stated limit).
- **Slowing?** The correctness side yes (last rule change 239; purge-rejudge was the fourth round and ended under Tess's simple-rules line). The cost side has not started: F3/F4 (permanent heads with per-session predecessor links) is a design with wire changes, and frontier-certificate-cost says the frontier is re-encrypted under every label the device's grantees write to.
- **Decided (Tess): IDs only, kept history a stated limit (spec/requirements.md Cost); no heads, links or hash DAG.** Was the relaxation: a kick lists what the demoter's compaction cannot drop; the demoter compacts first. Keep F2 (built) as the rule, build session-sources step 3 (the demoting admin compacts the aspects the demoted device wrote under keep-forever before writing the frontier, when it can), and do not build permanent heads. The Cost item becomes "proportional to the demoted holder's uncompacted writes".
  - Deletes: notes/frontier-study.md F3/F4 (unbuilt, so nothing in code), the predecessor-link header field it would add, and the per-session chain walk.
  - Cost: a keep-forever aspect nobody compacts (chat E compacts per-year aspects; a document under keep forever may not) makes the kick carry 32 B per commit the device wrote there: 10^5 messages is 3 MB per label it is re-encrypted under, and past about 1.9e6 the kick fails until someone compacts. A stated limit with a number.
  - Honest read: the requirement as a goal ("Cost (goal)") already allows this; it became hard in frontier-clobbers-history. The strongest case for relaxing is that nothing built today meets it and the fix is a second frontier format.

### 2.4 Prompt purge: "no honest node keeps purged content once it has seen the deletion"
- **Rounds**: 51, 229, 236 purge-imports (two reviews), 242, 244, 247 (security), d32 purge-rights (three rounds plus a rule call), d39, d40, d42 (five rounds): 10 items.
- **Open**: purge-keeps-repeated-key (hostile), purged-pending-unchecked (#2), purged-snapshot-residue (an option), import-own-source (hostile), wide-purge-order (0.4%, reads alike): 5.
- **Slowing?** Yes. The honest path is closed (imports, pending copies, rights, tombstones, re-judging each got one rule); every open item needs a writer crafting its own leftovers or an unbounded pending copy.
- **Relaxation: purge is exact for content as honest writers write it; a writer's own crafted residue is swept by the next entry removal or reset.** Already what the code does; state it in spec/crdt.md and close purge-keeps-repeated-key, import-own-source, forged-key-erased-reset and the misstated half of vouched-key-erased as that limit. Deletes nothing; costs nothing for honest users. The requirement looks fine.

### 2.8 What this says overall
- Two requirements generate the open honest-path tail: atomic apply across things (2.1) and delivery-convergence on bounded aspects under flips (2.2). Both have a relaxation that deletes machinery or checks rather than adding them. 2.2's cheap half (oracle on reads and held bodies) needs no requirement change.
- One requirement is unmet with no cheap path: kick cost on keep-forever aspects (2.3). Say so and bound it instead of building a second frontier format.
- Purge, keys, inbox and ephemeral have converged to stated residuals; their open issues are wording and two small fixes.
- The A-list is short and concrete: a pending bound (#1, #2), lost-access drop (#3), absolute bounds (#4), roster privacy (#5). None is a requirement problem; #3 needs a simple rule chosen up front.
