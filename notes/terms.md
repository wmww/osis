# Terms
> Summary: The one set of words for osis's concepts (Tess): each word, what it means, what it is never used for, and the old words it replaces; which concepts are built and which are the design being moved to; how to write about them. Read before writing any spec, note, issue, comment or API doc.

The concepts and their properties are spec/requirements.md (Concepts). This note fixes the words. Old words still in the repo, and what removing each takes: issues/deprecated-terms.md.

## Words
- **Thing**: anything with an identity. A role is **on a thing**; say "thing" wherever any thing will do, never "actor".
- **Role** (sync, read, write, admin) and **holder**: a thing with a role on something. "Holder" is relative to what the role is on; no thing is "a holder" by nature.
- **Mint admin**: the admin a thing's creation record names, fixed in its tid (replaces *first admin*). It is an ordinary admin, removable like any other, and of no significance once the thing is independent: nothing an app can observe may treat it specially (issues/behaviour-depends-on-own-keys.md). A thing is **minted with admin X**; say that, or "one of X's dependents". Never *made under X*, *created under X*, *under X*: the positional picture is not kept to elsewhere, and what holds a thing (strong references) is another matter. No "parent" or "child".
- **Dependent thing**: one whose secrets derive from its mint admin's (replaces *plain thing*). **Independent thing**: one with secrets of its own (replaces *thing with own keys*, *keyed thing*, *thing with secrets of its own*). A thing is minted dependent and becomes independent when it needs to (its first grant, its own sync ID); that promotion is implicit and one-way. These two words are internal: they never appear in the `osis` API or its docs, and no API behaviour depends on which a thing is, only cost (notes/api-design.md, Public docs).
- **Its independent**: for any thing, the nearest independent thing up its chain of mint admins, itself if independent (replaces *reader actor*). A dependent thing's *base actor* is its mint admin: say mint admin.
- **Actor**: a part played in a commit, not a kind of thing. A commit's actors are the things it is made through: the thing it is signed as, then each thing whose role it uses, in order, ending before the things it writes (as built: starting at the key actor that signs). A write acts only as things on that chain (Tess). No thing "is" or "is not" an actor outside a commit. Never write "an actor" for a thing that holds roles (say thing or holder), for a thing with its own secrets (independent thing), or as a kind things belong to.
- **Signing key** (a thing's **key**): a key that signs as one thing, by a grant it holds on that thing. A value, never a thing: no tid or aspects, and nothing is sealed to it. A commit it signs is that thing acting, and the commit's actors start at that thing. Its grant is a grant like any (a role, `until`, may-grant), written and removed by the thing's admins, and it outranks nothing: an admin key is one admin among the others (Tess: a key is an admin backed by a key). A thing is minted with a mint admin or with a key, which then holds admin on it. Never *owner key*, and no key is a "recovery key": recovery is not designed (issues/recovery-design.md).
- **Signed as**: the thing a commit's key signs as, the first of its actors. Never "signer" for a thing or a member: a key signs, a thing is signed as. (The built header field `signer` names the key actor that signs; in the design it names the thing signed as.)
- **Key hop**: the pair of a signing key and the thing it signs as, the first hop of every path of the commits that key signs: an edge like any, giving the role of the key's grant.
- **May-grant**: a flag on a grant (Tess): its holder may add new holders up to the grant's own role, or may not. No levels: a lower level is an arrangement (a thing holding the lower role with the flag, the person a writer of it). The built form is the `invite` level.
- **Lowering**, **end**, **list**: a lowering is an explicit write giving its hop less than it gave (an edge write below admin, a key's grant among them); an end is an explicit write leaving an admin edge with an `until`, which is a grant and no lowering; a lowering's list names the commits resting on its hop that its writer had received (the spec's frontier).
- **Node**: a running osis instance with its own key and store. Never "device" in core (app UI text may say device).
- **Account**: the identity nodes act as and where others reach it. In the design being moved to, an account is a kind of realm, and core has no concept of it.
- **Profile**: the public face of a thing (a name, a picture), what a write is shown as: the schema's, not core's. A separate, publicly readable thing; a writer references its own from what it writes in.
- **Realm**: a place holding a body of things in ways users reach: by convention the mint admin of what is made in it, with members that are its holders. Every account's home is one. **Tag**: a collection that points at things by strong references and administers nothing. The line between them is convention apps follow, not a rule of core (notes/realms.md).
- **Home**: the user's realm, one per account, described by the schema: neither core's nor any one app's.
- **External signal**: a signal that parties holding no role on the thing may fire (not *open*: opening already names what a node does to a sync ID, a blob or a sealed fire; a fire of one is an **external fire**). A thing's admins **enable** each external signal it takes; none is taken otherwise. A member fires one the ordinary way; a party that does not hold the thing sends a **sealed fire** (the fire as a commit sealed to the thing's public read key and deposited). **Externally grantable**: a thing that has enabled the external signal by which grants arrive (tickets), so parties outside it can grant it roles.
- No **team**: it is not a term and names no kind of thing. For an example say "an organization" or "a shared space"; for the concept, a holder, or a realm.
- Sync words (sync unit, sync ID, closure, ticket, release, store, peer, source): spec/requirements.md Concepts and spec/sync-set.md, with the rows of issues/deprecated-terms.md.

## Built, and being moved to
Some concepts in the repo are what is built today and are being redesigned (issues/actors-one-mechanism.md, designed in notes/account-membership-design.md and notes/write-path-design.md; first on notes/priorities.md). Until that lands:
- **Built**: an account is a key actor whose nodes are kept by its signing chain (epochs, custody, merges, sides, thing lists); the **account root** is the independent thing beside it that its nodes share; a commit header names its `signer` and the `path` it writes along (until A1 step 3, only the path's last listed thing is read, as the one thing it acts for); the engine finds any chain of grants that gives a write its right; a commit's evidence travels as **chain certificates** logged where its author writes; an account's profile and inbox are things minted with the account.
- **Moving to**: an account is an ordinary independent thing whose members are things holding roles on it (a node's root thing, minted with the node's key, among them); a commit is signed as a thing by a key holding a grant on it, states its actors and is judged along them alone; the evidence for the paths a unit's held commits state is part of the sync unit; public is a well-known thing nobody can sign as; a thing is opened to messages and grants from strangers by its admins.
- **Never call an old concept by a new name** (Tess). The account root is not "the home realm", a key actor is not "a thing with a key", chain certificates are not "the sync unit's evidence": where the built thing is still the old concept, keep its word, and make only the edits that are right looking forward. A section may be marked for deletion or rewriting once a named change lands; that is allowed, not required.

## Writing about them
- A rename reads as if the text was written that way: no aliases, no "formerly X", no "(was Y)".
- No **Limit** items, in the requirements or anywhere (Tess): a fact that makes a stated property untrue means the property is the weaker one, so state that; a fact that contradicts nothing is stated plainly as what holds, or dropped where it is nobody's concern. Keep every fact a subsystem spec needs; lose only the framing.
- Requirements state concepts and properties, never the build (notes/principles.md, Requirements state concepts and properties).
- API docs state a cost only where it matters to the reader's choice, hedged and comparative, in what the reader can see (notes/api-design.md, Public docs).
