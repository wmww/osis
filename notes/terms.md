# Terms
> Summary: The one set of words for osis's concepts (Tess): each word, what it means and what it is never used for; accounts, signing and paths as built; how to write about them. Read before writing any spec, note, issue, comment or API doc.

The concepts and their properties are spec/requirements.md (Concepts). This note fixes the words. Old words, those still in the repo with what removing each takes and those gone: issues/deprecated-terms.md.

## Words
- **Thing**: anything with an identity. A role is **on a thing**; say "thing" wherever any thing will do, never "actor".
- **Role** (sync, read, write, admin) and **holder**: a thing with a role on something. "Holder" is relative to what the role is on; no thing is "a holder" by nature.
- **Mint admin**: the admin a thing's creation record names, fixed in its tid. It is an ordinary admin, removable like any other, and of no significance once the thing is independent: nothing an app can observe may treat it specially (issues/behaviour-depends-on-own-keys.md). A thing is **minted with admin X**; say that, or "one of X's dependents". Never *made under X*, *created under X*, *under X*: the positional picture is not kept to elsewhere, and what holds a thing (strong references) is another matter. No "parent" or "child". Nothing *makes* a thing: a thing is minted with a mint admin (by its **minting**, the commit carrying its creation record), and an account **admins** a thing that may have been minted with it as admin.
- **Who did it**, each by its own word (one word for all of them is refused: issues/deprecated-terms.md, Gone): an epoch's **rotator** (signed as in its rotation commit, spec/crypto.md §4), a link's **sharer** (who made it, notes/api.md Links), a fire's **sender**, a write's **author** (the thing it is signed as), an app's **developer**.
- **Dependent thing**: one whose secrets derive from its mint admin's. **Independent thing**: one with secrets of its own. A thing is minted dependent and becomes independent when it needs to (its first grant, its own sync ID); that promotion is implicit and one-way. These two words are internal: they never appear in the `osis` API or its docs, and no API behaviour depends on which a thing is, only cost (notes/api-design.md, Public docs).
- **Its independent**: for any thing, the nearest independent thing up its chain of mint admins, itself if independent.
- **Actor**: a part played in a commit, not a kind of thing. A commit's actors are the things it is made through: the thing it is signed as, then each thing whose role it uses, in order, ending before the things it writes . A write acts only as things on that chain (Tess). No thing "is" or "is not" an actor outside a commit. Never write "an actor" for a thing that holds roles (say thing or holder), for a thing with its own secrets (independent thing), or as a kind things belong to.
- **Signing key** (a thing's **key**): a key that signs as one thing, by a grant it holds on that thing. A value, never a thing: no tid or aspects, and nothing is sealed to it. A commit it signs is that thing acting, and the commit's actors start at that thing. Its grant is a grant like any (a role, `until`, may-grant), written and removed by the thing's admins, and it outranks nothing: an admin key is one admin among the others (Tess: a key is an admin backed by a key). A thing is minted with a mint admin or with a key, which then holds admin on it. No key is a "recovery key": recovery is not designed (issues/recovery-design.md).
- **Signed as**: the thing a commit's key signs as, the first of its actors. Never "signer" for a thing or a member: a key signs, a thing is signed as. The header field `signer` names the thing signed as.
- **Key hop**: the pair of a signing key and the thing it signs as, the first hop of every path of the commits that key signs: its grant is a grant like any.
- **Grant** (a role grant): a holder's role on a thing, written in the thing's `roles.0` (a key's in `signers.0`): `Grant` in perm and the schema. A **grant write** grants, changes or removes one (`GrantWrite`); its parts are the **grant-class** parts. A mint admin's **record grant** is the admin its thing's creation record gives it, a grant no write made (so "record", as in record chain and record's key), which grant writes change or remove like any. A grant's **departure** for keys is a **grant departure** (spec/crypto.md §4). Never "edge" for any of these (Tess: not a word for a permission).
- **Edge**: only a graph's, where code or a model treats things as nodes and the (holder, thing) pairs grants sit on as edges: perm's `Edges` and `EdgesAt`, the evidence module's pairs, the models' closure graphs, `crdt`'s linearization. Never in the spec or the API.
- **May-grant**: a flag on a grant (Tess): its holder may add new holders up to the grant's own role, or may not. No levels: a lower level is an arrangement (a thing holding the lower role with the flag, the person a writer of it). Built (`may_grant` in `roles.0`, the `osis` API and the CLI).
- **Lowering**, **end**, **list**: a lowering is an explicit write giving its hop less than it gave (a grant write below admin, a key's grant among them); an end is an explicit write leaving an admin grant with an `until`, which is a grant and no lowering; a lowering's list names the commits resting on its hop that its writer had received (the spec's frontier).
- **Node**: a running osis instance with its own key and store. Never "device": one machine may run many nodes ("device" is the machine, or a store's storage).
- **Account**: the identity nodes act as and where others reach it. In the design being moved to, an account is a kind of realm, and core has no concept of it.
- **Profile**: the public face of a thing (a name, a picture), what a write is shown as: the schema's, not core's. A separate, publicly readable thing; a writer references its own from what it writes in.
- **Realm**: a place holding a body of things in ways users reach: by convention the things in it are minted with it as mint admin, with members that are its holders. Every account's home is one. **Tag**: a collection that points at things by strong references and administers nothing. The line between them is convention apps follow, not a rule of core (notes/realms.md).
- **Home**: the user's realm, one per account, described by the schema: neither core's nor any one app's.
- **External signal**: a signal that parties holding no role on the thing may fire (not *open*: opening already names what a node does to a sync ID, a blob or a sealed fire; a fire of one is an **external fire**). A thing's admins **enable** each external signal it takes; none is taken otherwise. A member fires one the ordinary way; a party that does not hold the thing sends a **sealed fire** (the fire as a commit sealed to the thing's public read key and deposited). **Externally grantable**: a thing that has enabled the external signal by which grants from outside arrive (`grant@~0`), so parties outside it can tell it of the roles they grant it.
- No **team**: it is not a term and names no kind of thing. For an example say "an organization" or "a shared space"; for the concept, a holder, or a realm.
- **Roster**: who holds which role on a thing: the final state of its role timeline (spec/permissions.md §5.1), read from `roles.0`.
- **Role entries** of an independent thing: its role-class data (roster, `signers.0`, `keys.0`, `secrets.0`, `sync.0`): who holds what role on it, the boxes handing its keys to them, its sync ID and steps. A grant is a role entry of the thing granted, never of its holder (spec/permissions.md §4 Role entries).
- **Chain**: a sequence of grants by which one thing reads another through holders: T holds read on H1, H1 on H2, and so on, the last on X, each grant with key access. The **holders on the chain** are H1 to Hn; T's role on X along it is the lowest along it (spec/sync-set.md §2 T's keys open). Never a chain of mint admins (say "record chain") nor a commit's path (say "path").
- **Stub**: a thing's role entries alone, cut to the readers of the unit carrying it: what a node needs to start and keep syncing a thing without its content (spec/permissions.md §4 Stub). Its **sync-only cut** is what a unit holding only sync on the thing carries of it: the boxes to that unit's thing and the steps, no header (same place).
- **Carry**: a unit (a thing's sync unit, or the local part) carries a thing whole or as a stub by the references in it and the holders on its chains (spec/sync-set.md §2 Stubs); what a sync ID carries is what nodes have logged under it. A **bridge** is a node that holds a dependent thing otherwise and logs it under a unit carrying it (spec/sync-set.md §2 Carriers).
- **Log**: a node logs something under sync ID S when it writes it into its own store as an entry of S, stamped if it may write S, deposited otherwise; peers syncing S pull it from there. Nothing is under S that no node logged (spec/sync-set.md §2 Carriers).
- **Grant signal**: a `grant@~0` fire on a holder's inbox telling the holder's nodes of a grant by values alone: the thing granted, its sync ID, nodes to dial, the holder (spec/sync-inbox.md §5). It proves nothing and gives no role (the grant is its target's data); sending one is the granter's option. A node **follows** a held signal while it reads the holder: it follows the thing's sync ID and holds the thing's stub. In the requirements the "grant's notice" is this signal.
- **Follow** a sync ID: keep it in the sync set and sync it, for a stub, a held grant signal, a holder the node reads through or a locator being redeemed, though the node closure need not hold its thing (spec/sync-set.md §2 Sync set, §5 Source).
- **Claim**, **claim key**, **step key**, **head claim keys**: a commit boxing a holder carries the holder's **claim**: its **claim key** (a key pair the holder and the thing's readers compute from the sync epoch's step key and the holder's KEM key) together with W, the commit's keys sealed to the holder (what it needs to open its boxes there); claims sit in the blob's store-visible **claim block**, signed by the sync key, paged into **claim pages** past a page. A sync epoch's **step key** is the X25519 pair whose public half every step bearing its sync key carries; its private half is the **step secret**, which readers alone derive. A node's **head claim keys** are the claim keys of the blocks it holds bound to its head's sync key: what it shows (spec/sync-ids.md §3). **Entry** is a store's alone: a blob held under a sync ID (spec/sync-ids.md §5). Never "entry" for a claim, nor "claim set" (issues/deprecated-terms.md, Gone).
- **Head** (`Head(S)`): asking any node holding S for its path; answered without a credential (spec/sync-ids.md §6 Head).
- **Locator**: a link holding no secret and granting nothing, which names a thing and where to find it: the thing, its independent's sync ID and the nodes to dial (spec/wire.md §10). The grant signal's values and pairing's hand-over are one. It grants the public nothing, whatever the old name said (issues/deprecated-terms.md). It **reaches** its target once the redeemer holds a role on it, the public's counting as every party's (spec/sync-set.md §4).
- **Stub class**: the source class of a peer holding S's thing as a stub: it is served S's role entries alone (spec/sync-opens.md §4 Source; wire value 2).
- Sync words (sync unit, sync ID, closure, release, store, peer, source): spec/requirements.md Concepts and spec/sync-set.md.

## API words
The `osis` API's names for the concepts above (notes/api-design.md: one word per concept):
- A thing holding a role is a **holder**; the API names it as a thing: `Holder::thing`, `HolderKind::Thing` (any thing but a link's thing, a key or the public), `LinkId::thing`.
- A link's **thing** K: the thing a link's joiners hold through (spec/permissions.md §7), never an actor.
- **Keys** (`Osis::keys`, `Keys`): a thing's public keys, which a grant to it needs. A thing gets them at its first grant or card, or at its minting by `Write::create_with_keys`; a thing that has none known here is `Error::UnknownKeys`. Never "keys of its own": that is independence, an internal word.
- A **client's thing** (`Client::thing`): the thing `add_client` mints, which the node acts as for the client in a **client session**; a **main session** (the node's unix socket) acts as **the node itself**. Neither is an actor.
- `act_as`, "the thing a handle acts as": "actor" in its commit sense (Words, Actor) fits the acting thing; the API says "thing" for it in docs.

## Accounts, signing and paths
An account is an ordinary independent thing whose members are things holding roles on it, set up by a library (osis-account, notes/accounts-switch-brief.md; spec/permissions.md §8): a node's **node thing**, minted with the node's key, among them; the node's strongs sit on its local **node root** (spec/sync-set.md §1). A node's **own things** are those its keys hold grants on. A commit is signed as a thing by a key holding a grant on it, that grant the first hop of the path it states; its header names its `signer` (the thing signed as) and states, per thing it writes, a path from the signer through each thing whose role it uses, and it is judged along that path alone; its evidence is certificate copies per (commit, thing) under the sync IDs that need them (notes/evidence-derivation.md). A thing is public by a grant to the public, a holder that is no thing (notes/public-and-cards-brief.md); a thing is opened to messages and grants from strangers by its admins.

## Writing about them
- A rename reads as if the text was written that way: no aliases, no "formerly X", no "(was Y)".
- **Never call an old concept by a new name** (Tess): where code still builds an old concept, keep its word until the change lands.
- No **Limit** items, in the requirements or anywhere (Tess): a fact that makes a stated property untrue means the property is the weaker one, so state that; a fact that contradicts nothing is stated plainly as what holds, or dropped where it is nobody's concern. Keep every fact a subsystem spec needs; lose only the framing.
- Requirements state concepts and properties, never the build (notes/principles.md, Requirements state concepts and properties).
- API docs state a cost only where it matters to the reader's choice, hedged and comparative, in what the reader can see (notes/api-design.md, Public docs).
