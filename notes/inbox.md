# Deposits, the inbox and deliveries
> Summary: Why what must reach an actor from outside goes as a boxed inbox blob, and why deposits work as they do: the deposit budget, readers' deposits and copies under a superseded stamp, invite requests (Tess: leaking an IP takes a click), one clearing rule under every sync ID, promotion once, stamping in place, accepted limits of what a stranger with `keys.0` can do; no placement order, why a copy is not judged by its author's chain. Rules: spec/sync-inbox.md, spec/sync-judgement.md §1, §3. The code: notes/sync-inbox.md. Neighbours: notes/sync.md (index), notes/sync-access.md (re-seal, claims), notes/permissions.md (grants, certificates).

## Why an inbox
- **Inbox blobs**: a non-writer cannot stamp, and a node that does not read a sync ID cannot wrap anything under it (a granter does not read the grantee's home sync ID), so everything that must reach an actor from outside goes boxed to its read KEM key: grants, grant changes and removals, re-seals, rotations boxing it, each with the sync IDs it needs. Not the target's `sync.0` commit: a non-holder cannot wrap it, and it joins many commits. Each carries an expiry (`INBOX_LIFETIME`) so an unconsumed one frees the budget by itself, and the inbox thing's admins may delete consumed ones. A node gets its grant by pairing only: it has no inbox.
- Rejected: plain (unboxed) grants under a shared sync ID: every reader of it would read them, against entitlement.
- **A reader's deposits** (its account's chain certificates, its invite grant, self-removal, card) sit deposited in its own store until a writer promotes them; co-readers pull and read them meanwhile.
- **A reader's copy under a superseded stamp** (any author's, ephemeral or durable) goes as a fresh encryption deposited where a source keeping S unread lacks it (relayed under its stamp only while the source's head write key verifies it): after a write-key rotation, with that source's copy gone (an ephemeral entry at each of its restarts; a durable one on a new or wiped node) and no writer holding it, it is the only way back. The promoting writer judges it by its author's rights at its `ts`. A reader deposits a body only while it judges it valid: a demoted writer's own writes refused under the rotated key are invalid at it too (kept for its user, spec/permissions.md §6); deposited, no writer would promote them and they would sit in the budget until cleared. It costs no new room: one budget per sync ID holds durable and ephemeral deposits alike, an ephemeral one also within the ephemeral cap, and any deposit-key holder could fill that budget already. Rejected: taking stamps under a superseded write key on relay (refused, code 9): a removed writer keeps the old write key and could fill S's room.
- **The budget is local policy**, not a format constant: senders never compute it, they see refusals (code 5). The recommended minimum (spec/wire.md §2: 64 + 4 per claim of the current step, 32 KiB each) is sized per reader *actor*, while deposits come per *node*: a plain group under a team has a claim set of one, and thirty reader nodes' cards can approach it, so a node keeping S unread for a large read-only audience raises it (`caps^.0` policy `deposit_entries`, `deposit_bytes`). Deposits are unlinkable by design, so per-reader limits are impossible; the peer budget (spec/sync-ids.md §5) charges only entries that give nothing held.
- Reasons behind spec rules (spec/sync-inbox.md):
  - Re-send (§2) ends only on the recipient taking the blob (a commit writing a thing it names, an open of a sync ID it names): the recipient acting elsewhere proves nothing, as the blob may have been cleared.
  - A grant copy (§5) is what lets the actor's other and future nodes, its readers and a link's redeemers find the grant once its inbox blob expired (a sender reading the inbox ends its delivery once logged). It carries what the inbox blob carries and no more: no `keys.0` but its author's and, when it boxes the actor, the thing's current keyset (a verifier judges by creation records and edge writes, and a fresh deposit seed is not for that actor's readers); to a holder of sync alone, its box alone (spec/sync-ids.md §2). One per (actor, thing), the newest applied edge write, so copies never pile up; a removal is its own copy, telling the actor's other nodes the grant ended. Every reader of the inbox holding it logs it (a certificate in the required set), not only the node that took it: the rule then needs no memory of how a commit arrived.
  - A holder below read gets a boxes payload, not a projection of the commit (spec/wire.md §9): the `secrets.0` part must stay whole to match the header's hash, so its box keys named every holder the commit boxes (each rotation showed a sync holder the roster) and the header every thing the commit touches; nor could the holder check the part against the header (the delta hash is keyed by a dedup secret it never gets). The author's signature binds the boxes to the recipient and the commit ID. A payload whose author's key is not held here is taken only by accept or, for a listed thing's boxes alone, by the listing: a forged box there gives only a secret naming an epoch of the forger's (an epoch names its secret), which no step uses (but see issues/unverified-boxes-sync-ids.md).
  - A node keeps offering its own deposit after losing read access (§3 Sending): other nodes may hold parts of that commit from elsewhere and wait for the rest.
  - Relay-only blobs are refused as deposits (§3, code 14): held by nobody, a reader's would be a write of its own, and readers write no ephemeral data.

## Invite requests (spec/sync-inbox.md §2 Taking; Tess: leaking an IP takes a user click)
- An untaken blob fetches, follows and dials nothing: its hints are a stranger's word, and dialing them shows this node's IP.
- A commit already held is taken (a sibling node that took it logs its grant copy under the inbox), so a sibling's accept wins over this node's untaken blob: refusing it would leave this node holding a valid grant under home it never applies while its siblings join. Judged per node: contacts are per node, and an accept on one node reaches the others only through the grant copy.
- What an attacker can still do:
  - Fill: requests are inbox entries, bounded by `DEPOSITS_WAITING` per store (clearing, below) and gone with their blobs. A declined blob is stamped out of the budget; an unanswered one holds a slot until it expires or is cleared.
  - Spoof: a request never comes from a node known as connected (no forged signature passes); the API shows nothing about its sender or thing (header and payload are the sender's word; a known name there would be a spoof or a node not seen yet: notes/api.md, Attribution). An app can show only "someone invites you" (left: a payload field pointing at the sender's card, verified once fetched).
  - Vouching: anyone holding a role on a thing in this node's sync set (or up its record chain, or on an account whose card it imported) is a connected sender, so its valid grant is taken. Accepted: they share a group already.
  - Dial hints are unsigned: whoever holds a connected sender's genuine grant commit (a reader of the target's sync ID) can seal it again naming its own dial hint, and the node dials it. Accepted: that reader holds a role on the target.

## Clearing
- **One clearing rule, every sync ID**, for all a stranger (inbox) or a reader (any sync ID: it holds the deposit key) can put in the budget (junk that opens to nothing, requests, re-seals, commits it sealed and signed that open but are invalid, pending or parked commits, record blobs, far or no expiry): a delete-key holder leaves at most `DEPOSITS_WAITING` (32) and 1 MiB of them per store and deletes the rest. Exempt only while it would act on it: a deposit it would promote, an inbox blob it would stamp; a duplicate or a commit S does not need counts. No "this is junk" verdict: a blob sealed to a key this node lacks yet looks like junk and stays while under the bound, and what stays blocks nothing, half the recommended budget being free.
  - Counted at blob size once its bytes arrived, whether or not the node still holds them (it drops the bytes of what it judged invalid).
  - Cost of judging: under a validity fork (permanent) a clearing node may clear what a writer on the other side would promote, only past the bound; an honest deposit pending at the clearing node past the bound goes the same way. A reader's own must-hold body is not lost: it deposits a fresh encryption at each judgement finding the source lacks it, so past the bound it cycles until judged.
  - Rejected: **eviction by the node keeping S unread** (a full budget takes a deposit by dropping the oldest unpromoted one): judgement-free, but a taken deposit is answered, and an inbox blob is not sent again for `INBOX_LIFETIME`, so any holder of the actor's `keys.0` (or honest traffic past the budget while the actor's admins are away) erases pending grants instead of delaying them; readers' re-deposits of evicted entries would also evict each other. **Age-out** of unstamped entries: at 0 nothing waits for a writer, at infinity the hole stays.
  - Order (spec/sync-inbox.md §4): opened requests with an honest expiry last, earliest to expire first (junk that opens to nothing never displaces one, so requests are lost only past 32 of them); before them everything else, far or no expiry first, then earliest. Displacing requests only by junk would not do: anyone with `keys.0` can seal requests that open, and 72 of them would hold the budget for `INBOX_LIFETIME` again. Keeping the oldest instead lets one fill refuse every later invite. Rejected: a rule on deposit expiry at nodes keeping S unread: certificate copies and other deposits carry no expiry, and those nodes cannot tell them apart.

## Promotion and stamping
- **Promotion once** (spec/sync-inbox.md §4): one stamped copy per delta, however many encryptions are deposited: any reader can seal any commit it holds again, and role commits and cards outside the closure are may-hold (a writer cannot tell which of them another node's chain needs), so every duplicate stamped would be kept for good. Chain certificates are not promoted: the promoter logs the author's chain of a promoted commit as its own certificate copies, so a reader's deposited chain certificates are duplicates left to clearing. Rejected: promoting certificate-class deltas on anything upstream of the closure: the reader's own account is upstream, so every edge it writes on its account and every thing it mints and grants there would be stamped.
  - Never an entry whose bytes the writer dropped: a purged or superseded part's (shredding cleared its stamp, and promoting it would bring back a destroyed thing's record for a newcomer to read).
  - Limits: a key actor's newest own card is promoted because co-readers dial by it and a group's writer cannot tie a node to its account, so a reader minting key actors gets each one's card stamped (issues/promotion-minted-key-actor-cards.md). A role commit naming a closure thing as holder is promoted at any thing, so a reader sharing things it mints with the group, or rewriting one such edge, gets each grant stamped (issues/promotion-readers-grants-to-the-group.md; sharing does not put the thing in the closure, so its content is not promoted). Chain churn (grant, revoke, grant on the chain of a commit S must hold) goes as copies when needed and is may-hold: bounded by the commits S must hold.
- **Stamping in place** (spec/sync-inbox.md §2): a handled inbox blob leaves the budget by a writer's stamp (an account otherwise took 72 grants per 90 days). Only the first blob of a commit under the inbox, and only one expiring within `INBOX_LIFETIME + 2 × MAX_DRIFT`: a stranger can seal any commit he holds in any number of blobs, so without both rules his re-seals of a held or once-declined commit, or of the actor's own card, would be stamped past the budget.
- **Accepted limit (Tess):** consumed inbox blobs are not deleted early (the admins' delete is a MAY): later holders (a link's later joiners, an account's later nodes) find the named thing's sync IDs by the blob while the copied `sync.0` still waits for the granter's chain. They expire after `INBOX_LIFETIME`; the cost is sealed storage for 90 days per grant.

## What a stranger with `keys.0` can still do (accepted; spec/requirements.md, Data a node cannot read)
- Fill the budget while no delete-key holder of the inbox is online: grants wait at their senders (offered again after back-off) until one comes back. A group's readers can fill any of its sync IDs the same way: readers' deposits (cards, self-removals, readers' copies) wait until a delete-key holder clears.
- Flood without pause: no deposit rate limit is built (spec/sync-ids.md §6 allows one, code 4), so slots go as they free, an honest deposit races him, and each junk blob costs the node its bytes until he stops.
- Push out unanswered requests with requests of his own (they open): more than 32 displace the earliest. No flood needed: 40 real invites from unconnected people while the user is away lose the first 8 at her next sync, before she sees them, and their senders are not told; each re-sends after `INBOX_LIFETIME`. Any constant has this shape (the budget itself holds 68-72); a larger one is Tess's call. A node that does not know a connected sender yet (fresh, or behind) sees its grant as a request too, so past 32 it can lose that blob at this store, though a sibling that took it holds the commit.
- Past the bound, flood or not, what goes may be real: a reader's deposited linked blob (filler wrap, no expiry) or record blob (a promoter lacking it logs its own), a commit still pending here, or a blob sealed to a key the node holds only later (a team's, whose secrets come from another sync ID). A node holding the head's read epoch holds its history, so readers' valid commits (cards, certificate copies) open and never count.
- A decline frees a slot; a new deposit seed needs a new `keys.0` and step.
- Any sender holding a role on a thing the actor syncs is connected, and its fresh grants of its own things to the actor are valid, so they leave the budget (stamped), bounded only by S's caps and expiry: once the inbox is full at a node keeping it unread, that node takes nothing more there (code 12) for up to 90 days, with no node path to delete, its blobs on each node too and each grant's copy under home for good (unstamped they would fill only the budget). Removing the sender stops new ones only.

## Delivery
- **No placement order** (Tess's decision): projections of a node's own commit (certificate copies, inbox blobs) go at once. A receiver holding any part of a role commit applies it and joins the rest on arrival (spec/commits.md §4), so nobody waits for a body that never reaches K's sync ID (author's disk lost, no source ever taking it); copies waiting for the full commit would need a timeout, and receivers would still wait forever past it. Accepted cost: a K-syncer may see a grant before its boxes (an admin may re-seal needlessly).
- **A copy is never judged by its author's chain** (spec/sync-inbox.md §4: never deleted for an incomplete chain): the chain moves after a copy (the granter's grant lowered, his node's key), so copies of a settled grant would be deleted for good, and a judge cannot tell a chain that will not come from one still on its way. Nothing needs it since promotion completes chains. Two writers (or a writer and the author) may log the same chain parts concurrently: the duplicates are may-hold.
