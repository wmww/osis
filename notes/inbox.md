# Deposits, the inbox and deliveries
> Summary: Why what must reach an actor from outside goes as a boxed inbox blob, and how the deposit path works and is bounded: deposits and their budget, invite requests (Tess: leaking an IP takes a click), clearing deposits under any label, stamping handled blobs, the accepted fill and push-out limits; why there is no placement order, the relay wait, why a copy is not judged by its author's chain. Rules: spec/sync-judgement.md §1, spec/sync-inbox.md §3 (deposit, Clearing deposits). Neighbours: notes/sync.md (index), notes/label-access.md (re-seal, claims), notes/permissions.md (grants, certificates).

## Why an inbox
- **Inbox blobs**: a non-holder cannot wrap anything under a label it does not read (a granter is not a reader of the grantee's home label), so everything that must reach an actor from outside goes boxed to its read KEM key: grants, grant changes and removals (so it erases), re-seals, each with the label IDs and hosts it needs. Not the target's `label.0` commit: a non-holder cannot wrap it, and it joins many commits anyway. Each carries a delivery expiry so an unconsumed one frees the budget by itself, and the inbox is a label the recipient's admins administer so they can delete consumed ones. Direct grants to a node go by pairing only: a node is listed by DH key and cannot enter a label cold.
- Rejected: plain (unboxed) grants under a shared label: every reader of that label would read them, against entitlement.
- **A reader's deposits** (its invite grant, self-removal, node card) sit deposited until a writer promotes them; co-readers read them meanwhile.
- **A reader's copy under a superseded stamp** (any author's, ephemeral or durable) is deposited where a host lacks it (relayed only when the head's write key verifies its stamp): after a write-key rotation, with the host's copy gone (an ephemeral entry at every host restart; a durable one on a new or wiped host) and every writer lacking it, it is the only way back. A promoting writer judges it by its author's rights. A reader deposits a body only while it judges it valid: a demoted writer's own writes refused under the rotated key are invalid at it too (kept for its user, spec/permissions.md §6); deposited, no writer would promote them and they would sit in the budget until an admin clears them (`sync_review::demoted_writers_refused_writes_take_no_deposit_budget_spec_sync_5`). It costs no new room: the one budget per label holds durable and ephemeral deposits alike, an ephemeral one also within the label's ephemeral cap, and any deposit-key holder could fill that budget already. Rejected: hosts taking old stamps on relays (a removed writer keeps the old write key and could fill the label's room).
- **The budget is host policy**, not a format constant: clients never compute it, they see refusals and the remaining budget in the reply. The recommended minimum (64 + 4 per padded claim slot, 32 KiB each) is sized per reader *actor*, while deposits come per *node*: a plain group under a team has a claim set of one, and thirty reader nodes' cards can approach it. Hence the client order (certificates, edge writes and revokes first); hosts with big read-only audiences raise the budget (`deposit_factor`). Deposits are unlinkable by design, so per-reader limits are impossible.
- Reasons behind spec rules: re-send ignores a recipient acting elsewhere because the blob may have been cleared (§5). A grant copy leaves the chain's `keys.0` out because a verifier judges by creation records and edge writes, and a fresh deposit seed is not for that actor's readers; the copy is what lets the actor's other and future nodes and its readers find the grant. Inbox blob order (§3): a label told unknown may be on its way. A client keeps sending its own deposit after losing read access because other nodes may hold parts of that commit from elsewhere and wait for the rest (§5). Relay-only deposits are refused: held by nobody, a reader's would be a write of its own, and readers write no ephemeral data.

## Invite requests (spec/sync-judgement.md §1; Tess: leaking an IP takes a user click)
- A decline binds inbox blobs only: the commit arriving under a synced label (a sibling's acceptance copied under home) applies, so the sibling's acceptance wins (`sibling_acceptance_wins_over_decline_*`); refusing it there would leave this node holding, under its home label, a valid grant it never applies while its siblings join. Judged per node: contacts are per node, and an accept on one node reaches the others only through the grant's copy (which waits for the joined label to apply).
- What an attacker can still do:
  - Fill: requests are inbox entries, bounded by `DEPOSITS_WAITING` per host (clearing, below) and gone with their blobs. A declined blob is stamped out of the budget; an unanswered one holds a slot until it expires or is cleared.
  - Spoof: a request never comes from a node known as connected (no forged signature passes); the API shows nothing about its sender or thing (the header and payload are the sender's word; a known name there would be a spoof or a node not seen yet: Attribution, notes/api.md). An app can show only "someone invites you" (left: a payload field pointing at the sender's card, verified once fetched).
  - Vouching: any holder of a role on a group this node syncs who may grant there can make any node connected by a valid grant. Accepted: they share a group already.
  - Hint hosts are unsigned: whoever holds a connected sender's genuine grant to the actor (a reader of the target's label) can re-deposit it naming its own hosts, and the node dials them. Accepted: that reader holds a role on the target.

## Clearing and stamping
- **One clearing rule, every label**, for all a stranger (inbox) or a reader (any label: it holds the deposit key) can put in the budget (junk that opens to nothing, requests, re-seals, commits it sealed and signed itself that open but are invalid, pending or parked commits, record blobs, far or no expiry): an admin node leaves at most `DEPOSITS_WAITING` (32) and 1 MiB of them per host and deletes the rest at each pass. A deposited commit stays out of the count only while the admin would promote it (a valid delta the label must hold and lacks stamped), so a skipped duplicate or a commit the label does not need counts. A valid deposit whose chain no writer can complete (none holds the chain's bodies) stays exempt for good: rare and hostile-only. No "this is junk" verdict: a blob sealed to a key this node lacks yet looks like junk and stays while under the cap, and what stays blocks nothing, half the budget being free.
  - Counted by size (`Entry::size`, recorded with the salt when bytes arrive, `keep_salts`): the admin drops the bytes of what it judged invalid and, outside an inbox, of what opens under none of its secrets. A deposit whose bytes never arrived is not counted yet.
  - Cost of judging: under a validity fork (permanent), an admin may clear what a writer on the other side would promote, only past the cap; an honest deposit pending at the admin past the cap goes the same way. Not a loss for an own role commit: its author's next pass finds no entry of it there and deposits a fresh encryption, so past the cap it cycles until judged. A reader's copy of another's body is not placed again by that reader (own role commits only).
  - Rejected: **host-side eviction** (a full budget takes a deposit by dropping the oldest unpromoted one): judgment-free, but a taken deposit is answered, and an inbox blob is not sent again for `INBOX_LIFETIME`, so any holder of the actor's `keys.0` (or honest traffic past the budget while the actor's admins are away) erases pending grants instead of delaying them; readers' re-deposits of evicted entries also evict each other at every pass. **Age-out** of unstamped entries: at 0 nothing waits for a writer, at infinity the hole stays.
  - Order: requests with an honest expiry last, earliest to expire first (junk that opens to nothing never displaces one, so requests are lost only past 32 of them); before them everything else, far or no expiry first, then earliest. Requests displaced only by junk would not do: anyone with `keys.0` can seal requests that open, and 72 of them would hold the budget for `INBOX_LIFETIME` again. Keeping the oldest instead lets one fill refuse every later invite. A host rule on deposit expiry was rejected: certificate copies and other non-inbox deposits carry no expiry, and hosts cannot tell them apart.
  - Inbox blobs whose commit is pending are exempt, so one who holds a commit pending at the actor, or a connected sender with commits that stay pending, can hold slots until they expire (at most `INBOX_LIFETIME` plus drift).
- **Promotion once**: a writer promotes a deposited commit only with a delta the label must hold that no stamped entry there (or own copy) holds: one stamped copy per delta, however many encryptions a reader deposits (any reader can seal any commit it holds again, and role commits and cards outside the closure are may-hold, `delete.rs` `judge`: a writer cannot tell which of them another placer's chain needs, so a duplicate stamped is kept for good). Must hold (valid parts only, not `judged_valid`: a link race loser's commit holds a void grant beside the valid removal of its link key, `link_race`): a delta on a closure thing (the deletion judgment takes it from there); a role commit's delta naming a closure thing as holder (a grant to the label's actor, its removal: `Engine::holders_named`; `tied::group_removal_deposit_promoted_without_new_keys`); a key actor's own card delta in its newest valid commit on that aspect. Nothing else outside the closure: chain certificates go in the author's chain of a commit promoted, which the promoter places as its own copies (`complete_chain`), so a reader's deposited chain certificates are duplicates left to clearing. Rejected: certificate-class deltas on anything upstream of the closure (`Held::upstream`): the reader's own account is upstream, so every edge it writes on its account and every thing it mints and grants there was stamped (`promotion::a_readers_own_role_commits_are_not_promoted_spec_sync_5`, `a_readers_fresh_things_are_not_promoted_spec_sync_5`). Co-writers' relay waits differ, so a second stamped copy needs a race (`promotion::a_readers_copies_are_promoted_once_spec_sync_5`). Only while the node writes the group by its own role (a demoted writer still serving a head whose write key it holds stamps under a key its peers refuse, and the chain records it places stay deposits: `sync_review::demoted_writers_refused_writes_take_no_deposit_budget_spec_sync_5`), and never an entry whose bytes it dropped (a purged or superseded part's: shredding cleared its stamp, and promoting it placed a destroyed thing's record a newcomer read back, `destruction::a_minting_of_a_thing_purged_past_its_window_is_dropped_everywhere_spec_commits_5`).
- Limits: a node's newest card is promoted because co-readers dial by it and a group label's writer cannot tie a node to its account, so a reader minting key actors gets each one's card stamped (issues/promotion-minted-key-actor-cards.md). A role commit naming a closure thing as holder is promoted at any thing, so a reader sharing things it mints with the group, or rewriting one such edge, gets each grant stamped (issues/promotion-readers-grants-to-the-group.md; sharing does not put the thing in the closure, so its content is not promoted). Chain churn (grant, revoke, grant on the chain of a commit the label must hold) is placed as copies when needed and kept as may-hold: bounded by the commits the label must hold.
- **Stamping** (after the same relay wait as a promotion): a handled blob leaves the deposit budget by a stamp in place, from any writer that holds the commit applied and valid or whose user declined it (an account otherwise took 72 grants per 90 days). A stamp goes only on the first blob of a commit under the label, of a valid commit about the actor with parts a copy would take or one declined, expiring within `INBOX_LIFETIME + 2 × MAX_DRIFT`: a stranger can seal any commit he holds in any number of blobs, so without both rules his re-seals of a held or once-declined commit, or of the actor's own card, would be stamped past the budget (`reseals_stay_in_the_budget_spec_sync_3`). A hostless served label has no entry budget (`serve.rs`: bytes only), so `local_promote` does not stamp there.
- **Accepted limit (Tess):** consumed inbox blobs are not deleted early (a MAY in spec/sync-judgement.md §1): later holders (a link's later joiners, an account's later nodes) open the named thing's label by the blob's hints while the copied `label.0` still waits for the granter's chain (deleting them failed every `link_redeem` sweep seed: a joiner never found the target's label), and an inbox at another group's label gets no copy at all. They expire after `INBOX_LIFETIME`; the cost is sealed storage at the host for 90 days per grant.

## What a stranger with `keys.0` can still do (accepted; spec/requirements.md, Data a node cannot read)
- Fill the budget while no admin node of the actor is online: grants wait at their senders (retried at the capped back-off) until one comes back, one pass later. A group's inbox is its home label, and waits for its admins alike. A group's readers can fill any of its labels the same way: readers' deposits (cards, self-removals, readers' copies) wait until an admin node passes (`inbox_retry::a_readers_invalid_fill_ends_with_an_admin_pass_spec_sync_5`).
- Flood without pause: up to the host's deposit rate (default 600 a minute; deletes 20 000) slots go as they free, so an honest deposit races him, and each junk blob costs the node its bytes (up to about 320 KiB/s at the default rate with 32 KiB blobs) until he stops. Tombstones: at that rate about 26 million in `TOMBSTONE_LIFETIME`, about 2.6 GB of the label's 16 GiB; the log trims (2^20 records, about 15 hours), so offline nodes reconcile.
- Push out unanswered requests with requests of his own (they open): more than 32 displace the earliest. No flood needed: 40 real invites from unconnected people while the user is away lose the first 8 at her next sync, before she sees them, and their senders are not told; each re-sends after `INBOX_LIFETIME`. Any constant has this shape (the budget itself holds 68-72); a larger one is Tess's call. A node that does not know a connected sender yet (fresh, or behind) sees its grant as a request too, so past 32 it can cost that blob at this host, though a sibling that opened it holds the commit.
- Past the cap, flood or not, what goes may be real: a reader's deposited linked blob (filler wrap, no expiry; only a very large chain reaches 32 entries or 1 MiB) or record blob (a promoter lacking it places its own), a commit still pending here, or a blob sealed to a key it holds only later (a team's, whose secrets come from another label). A node holding the head's read epoch holds its history, so readers' valid commits (cards, certificate copies) open and never count.
- A decline frees a slot; a new deposit seed needs a new `keys.0` and chain step.
- Any sender holding a role on a group the actor syncs is connected, and its fresh grants of its own things to the actor are valid, so they pass the budget (stamped), bounded only by the host's deposit rate, label caps (defaults 600 a minute, 2^20 entries, 16 GiB) and expiry: about 14 hours to fill the home label at a host, after which that label takes no uploads there (code 12) for up to 90 days, with no node path to delete, its blobs on each node too and each grant's copy under home for good (unstamped they would fill only the budget: 72 entries, about 2.3 MiB). Removing the sender stops new ones only.

## Delivery and promotion (reasons)
- **No placement order** (Tess's decision): projections of a node's own commit (certificate copies, inbox blobs) go at once. A receiver that holds any part of a role commit applies it and joins the rest on arrival (spec/commits.md §4; `apply::examine`), so nobody waits for a body that never reaches K's label (author's disk lost, K's host never taking it); copies waiting for the full commit would need a timeout, and receivers would still wait forever past it. Accepted cost: a K-syncer may see a grant before its boxes (an admin may re-seal needlessly).
- **Relay wait** (not in the spec; a choice for the rebuild): a writer uploads or relays another node's commit at a host lacking it, and promotes a deposit, only after a wait of `RELAY_MIN_MS` (5 s) to `SETTLE_MS` (30 s) since the commit arrived (the deposit was first seen; in memory), the point fixed per (node, blob) by a hash; its own commits (origin local, a held key actor's included) and `First` placements go at once. Without it writers online together each upload a fresh encryption of the same new commits and re-send every reader deposit; with it the author or the first co-writer is done and the others find the entry. A refused promotion (a step registered meanwhile) waits again.
- A copy is never judged by its author's chain: the chain moves after a copy (the granter's grant lowered, his node's key), so copies of a settled grant would be deleted for good, and a judge cannot tell a chain that will not come from one still uploading. Nothing needs it since promotion completes chains: a stamped partial upload by a writer rotated out stays pending at its readers while its entry is held (one row each, under the host's cap). Two writers (or a writer and the author) may place the same chain parts concurrently: the duplicates are may-hold.
