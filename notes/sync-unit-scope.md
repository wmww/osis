# What a sync unit covers
> Summary: A sync unit is a thing's readable closure, as built and specified (Tess); the model of one independent thing and its dependents per unit was designed, modelled and rejected. What the built model costs; the rejected model kept on purpose for when the question comes back (what it was, what it was meant to win, the six problems that sank it, the expiring-hold rescue and its verdict, the ways tried of hiding a closure's shape); what the work found that stands whatever the unit is.

Principle: **a sync unit is the part of an independent thing's closure that thing can itself read, and its sync ID carries that whole unit; so a thing is carried under the sync ID of every unit that references it and can read it** (spec/sync-set.md §2, spec/sync-judgement.md §1-§2). A thing behaves alike dependent or independent: promoting it changes nothing a user or app observes, since every sync ID that carried it keeps carrying it (spec/permissions.md §3 Own secrets add only). Tess: no behavioural cost to promoting a thing.

## What it costs
- An entry and stamp per enclosing unit on every commit: measured at about 1230 B on the wire, a sign and two stamp checks per commit per enclosing unit per reader pair, against a bare entry of about 153 B (issues/enclosing-unit-commit-cost.md).
- Work in the size of a thing when a reference to it changes (retag: its live blobs added under, or deleted from, the enclosing sync ID); attaching an existing thing measured at 83 to 146 KB (issues/attach-cost.md).
- Deposits into enclosing units by a nested thing's writers that cannot stamp there, and their promotion; per-sync-ID copies where a unit's readers cannot open every section of a shared blob. These paths are among the least settled code (issues/differential-full-size-classes.md and the sync leftovers, notes/priorities.md).
- What it buys is real only in part: a node keeping one root unread sees one bag of blobs and nothing of its inside, but only while it keeps none of the inner independent things under their own sync IDs (nor two overlapping roots); where it does, the same blobs under both show exactly which thing sits inside which. Bursts of entries when a thing is attached or detached show its extent to a node that watches.

## The rejected model, kept on purpose
Kept on purpose (Tess): a deliberate exception to notes holding no past states, for when the question comes back. Its design note, the expiring-hold study and its issues are folded in here; its model (held-model unit, with readings, counts and each finding's smallest world in notes/closure-model.md) is out of the tree: `git show 3e98c5b9a:core/test/held-model/src/unit.rs`.

### What it was
A sync unit is one independent thing H and the dependents of H reached from H by strongs inside the unit; a closure is a set of units synced by walking it; a commit is entered once, under the unit of each thing it writes. So that readers and nodes keeping units unread walk alike, each unit holds for its sync holders derived blobs, each a function of what every reader of the unit holds: a **handle** (`kdf("handle", tid)`, the same in every unit) for each thing its strongs target outside it, and a **key** for each unit its thing was granted sync or above on, naming that unit's sync ID, sealed so that only a holder of the target's handle opens it. A strong to a dependent of another unit carried a **home** (its independent's tid) as hidden delta metadata, so the strong could bring that unit. A unit was entered while an entered unit listed its handle and an entered unit held its key, by a secret of a thing its own step listed (steps listing every holder thing with an entry box). Writers judged the blobs at a store after a complete pass; under a dispute a blob stood while either side's view asked for it.

### What it was meant to win
- A commit entered once: about 1230 B, a sign and two stamp checks saved per commit per enclosing unit per reader pair [measured].
- No retag: an attach of 83 to 146 KB and 42 to 92 signs becoming one commit and one or two handle blobs of about 280 B; a detach of 17 to 80 entries becoming one.
- No nesting deposits, no per-sync-ID body copies, one walk rule for readers and nodes keeping units unread, evidence, removals and status stated once per unit, fewer states for the differential and the sim.
- The argument for it: the bag's privacy (above) is lost exactly where independent things are synced on their own, which is what they are for. That stays true; it does not outweigh what follows. Not won in any case: the cost of many sync IDs, which the change made central.

### Why it fails
One root: **a dependent thing has no address of its own.** Its tid says nothing of which unit holds it, so everything that touches it from outside its unit needs side-data, and each piece of side-data brought a corner where a dependent thing behaves differently from an independent one. Problem by problem (X a dependent of H; G a thing of another unit):
1. **A dependent thing its unit stops referencing is lost to an outside referrer.** G -> X; H removes H -> X. Everyone holding H's unit must agree on what it holds from the unit's own data, and G's strong is not in it. Either X leaves H's unit and G's holders lose it while G still references it strongly (an independent X would be kept), or a unit holds every dependent ever minted in it and nothing made in a unit is released (against spec/requirements.md Data model, release; Offline and network, nothing syncs for having been created with some admin; Scale, churn). **The rescue tried, expiring external holds**: an expiring strong on H to X, a commit of its own with `expires` (default `ROLE_KEEP`), under a source tag `kdf("hold", sync secret of G's unit, tid of X)`, placed and renewed (at under half its expiry left) by nodes reading G's unit that hold and may write H. Sound only with three more rules: renewal counted from each hold's source (else holds across two units keep a cycle nothing outside references, for good); reads gated by the same closure (else a node holding H alone reads X where an independent X reads not held); purges and destructions of a released dependent kept in H's unit as header-only blobs for good, about 300 B each (else a restore after a lapse brings purged content back). It also meant steady renewals (a small commit per maintained H per half-expiry, about 60 B more per outside reference: work while nothing changes), release up to the expiry late, the gap after every maintainer is away longer than the expiry, any writer of H able to end a hold, a fast clock harming others' entries at stores, and a hold aspect hidden from apps. And it **still loses X outright** where no holder of G's unit may write H (readers only read): no hold is placed, and X goes at once and for good when H drops it. The only ways out there were an external signal by which a reader asks for a hold, or carrying X under G's unit: the built model.
2. **Naming a referenced thing's unit took two rewrites.** A blob naming the target's unit, logged by a writer that could name it, failed in the model: the name is no function of the referring unit, so a node keeping a closure unread missed what only a reader of the root could name, the blob set depended on who had once known, and a forgery outlived every writer able to judge it. Key paths came next (3). What stood: three kinds of derived entry (handle blobs, key blobs, homes as hidden delta metadata), each with its own logging, deletion, judging and dispute rules. A home inside the reference value was rejected: two references to one thing would differ as values, set elements and map keys, so an app would observe how the thing was keyed.
3. **A role held through a group nothing references stops counting.** An account is in group W, W reads D, home -> D, nothing references W. D's key sat in W's unit, which the closure never enters, so D did not sync. In the built model D is in home's unit (home's keys open D through the chain, spec/sync-set.md §2), carried under home's sync ID, so it syncs to readers and to nodes keeping home unread alike. Key paths (a reference naming the holder units on its way, entered for keys only) were modelled and missed a chain the root holds and the referrer does not, and a read chain beside a direct sync grant; under forked sides over a grant on the path two writers logged and deleted the path without end; and they needed data only some writers could write or check. In the model's family the root's role over any chain exceeded its role through the closure's units in 16740 of 186624 worlds. Making every chain count would have a node open, for keys, every unit its things were granted anything on: work and storage in grants, not references.
4. **A node keeping data unread learns more.** A handle per thing each kept unit references, kept or not, the same in every unit (so which kept units point at a common thing) and testable by anyone who knows the tid; how many grants at sync or above each kept unit's thing took, and whether two kept units took one on the same thing; how many independent things a closure has, which refers to which, activity per independent thing, when references and grants come and go, and at a promotion which earlier entries were the promoted thing's. Unsealed key blobs would show the sync ID of every unit granted; handles made different per unit break the join that lets a node keeping a root unread hold what the root can sync through a folder that cannot.
5. **Promotion loses or strands content** (modelled). Z a dependent of X: after X's promotion Z is in X's unit only if X's unit reaches it. H -> X, H -> Z: Z is in no unit, lost to all. H -> Z -> X: H's holders lose X and Z. H -> Z, X -> Z with X unreached before: Z moves into a unit H's holders never enter. A promotion judged invalid: a writer holding the void deletes X's key and handle at H's unit and never syncs X's unit again, so a body written under X's unit alone is in no unit a keeper of H enters and stays at X's stores for good. Forked promotions: a node syncing X by the lesser sync ID never learns the greater and holds X frozen. And an invalid promotion below one that stood leaves a dependent holder, whose grant a node keeping the unit unread uses only until the target rotates (it knows no tid of the unit's dependents), while a reader keeps it. Each way out seen cost something: the promoter adding strongs from X into X's app data, or a dependent carried under each unit up its chain (per-unit carriage again).
6. **A reference written from a bare ID brought nothing**, and whether a re-added reference got its home depended on held history. A node that never held X, copying the reference from nowhere that knew, wrote no home. A home lived as long as its delta, not its strong: a concurrent bare-ID add outlived the homed delta's removal, and the strong stood with no home. A node re-adding a strong it once held removed wrote the home while it still held the removed delta and none once a cover dropped it: the same value, a different closure. The fix seen, a handle per dependent thing in every unit, costs about 400 B per dependent thing per unit and shows the count.

### Ways tried of hiding a closure's shape from a node keeping units unread
A flat list at the root (every inner unit carries its own list, so the tree can be put together anyway); peers telling the node which units to keep while it counts references (it knows the counts and not the edges, readers the edges and not the counts, so neither can release what a dropped unit referenced, and a cycle never reaches zero); the same per root computed by readers of the root (only they can update it, so a promotion by someone who reads only an inner unit waits for one); handles cut to what the referrer can itself sync (reads roles the unit does not carry, so readers split); handles in the clear or as store operations (Tess: no). Tess's two mitigation ideas (a closure synced as one encrypted bag; a closure of dependents only) are moot: the built model is the first already.
