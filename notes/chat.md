# Chat
> Summary: Chat (design E): `osis chat` and the app over the `osis` API, with crate `osis-chat` (libs/chat) holding only chat's shared rules (creation, edit as the settings say, reading by arrival): one actor per chat, its first admin the maker, participants hold write, a message is one write on the signal `chat:msg~0`, kept for good, the sender the verified actor; the chat's history is the chat. Why this shape, Tess's decisions, what is built, what is left out, costs, realms (servers and channels), the rejected designs. Behavior case by case: notes/chat-cases.md.

## Design E
- A chat C is one thing, actor and label, its maker's account its first admin (spec/permissions.md §1), synced as a group of itself with an empty member list; its label carries its own aspects, participants are its roster. No member actor, no member list, no per-message thing.
- A message is a fire of `chat:msg~0` (schema/chat.osis, with `chat:settings^.0`), a signal (spec/commits.md §5 Signals): no current value; author, time and retention come from the signed header; the fires are the chat. Retention `author_or_admin` by default, kept for good unless the chat disappears messages: a body is must-hold, compaction may not drop it, only purge rights decide, and a non-admin holds no delete key for it, so at honest nodes no non-admin can destroy or hide another's message.
- The chat's history setting (`retention^.0`, spec/commits.md §5 The setting) is written at creation, before anyone is granted: messages kept for good, the disappearing expiry and edits-off as writer defaults. No validity rule follows from it: a writer ignoring it can only give its own messages another expiry or purgeable-by.
- The sender is the header's `actor`, checked by validity (spec/permissions.md §5.2 Acting): nobody can speak as another.
- Why the message is the write, not a register holding a message thing's tid: the closure runs only through member lists (spec/data-model.md §8), so a message thing would need a member actor and a placement again.
- Cost of the shape: a demotion's frontier lists every surviving message of the removed author (about 34 B each, capping a kick near 1.9×10^6 messages of one participant) until frontier heads land (notes/frontier-study.md, unbuilt); no compaction; open is O(all commits) (issues/long-aspect-cost.md).

## Tess's decisions
- The maker as first admin is the default app's default, not a rule: a chat's first admin may be a realm or anyone. A chat keeps all messages, so it keeps its member history and needs no roster compaction. Recovery from a vandal writer needs only an admin able to demote.
- Accepted: a stolen unlocked device shows history; no deniability; deletion binds only honest clients; a node keeping a chat unread sees its metadata. Eventual revocation is a general osis limit.
- Member forward secrecy is probably undesirable (it would need erasable per-device KEM keys, MLS-sized; against spec/crypto.md §3).
- An edit is a whole new message replacing the old one (osis `replace`, honored only from the same verified sender), not ordinary osis editing of a value.
- Defaults: moderator delete; lasting tombstones; a removed participant keeps its copy (`retain`, erasing is a setting) and others keep its messages; block is local; disappearing timer per chat; add with history; edits purge the old text. Settings: add without history (core: `Reads::FromNow`, the newcomer reads the chat's settings and name as they stand and every later message, no past one nor a later edit of one (an edit carries its original's floor, spec/commits.md §5 Signals), spec/crypto.md §4 State at a cut; not yet offered by `osis chat`); admin-only delete (admins may always delete: no permanent chat, notes/engine-retention-history.md Tess's decisions, 2); edit history kept or editing off; private read position; participants may invite. Target realm size 10,000 members.

## As built
- **Create** (`create`): one commit of the record, `retention^.0` (the message entry: kept forever, the disappearing expiry, `final` when editing is off; `retain_copies`) and `chat:settings^.0` (name, edit mode), then `sync` (C's own secrets and label, a role commit). Two commits, not one: C's secrets and label are role-class, and a commit spans one kind (spec/commits.md §2). No reader sees the record without its settings, and a joiner reads C's record with no extra pending reads (`scale::joiner_reads_each_pending_commit_few_times`). A failure in `sync` leaves a whole chat on this node, granted to nobody and listed nowhere (no sync ID; the caller never learns its tid): `create` again. The maker's own nodes read it, so serve it with no setup; a node that cannot read it keeps it through its serving terms.
- **Participants**: adding is `grant`; a link is `make_link`, joining `redeem` (a link to a chat already reached returns it: notes/api.md Links; a kept copy is no role, so its link rejoins). A link joiner holds through K: a share view is `holders`' `Actor` entries with `through` empty or a single `Link`; a working link is a `Link` with a `Key` through it (notes/api.md). A direct participant learns a joiner at its first post; a joiner and the link's maker know all. Only the account that made a link can kick one joiner (`held_links`); any admin can kick K, ending everyone behind it, and kicking one joiner ends the link for those who have not redeemed it: make a new one. Moderator: `Role::Admin`; all admins equal. A kick is `set_role(.., None, ..)`, keep filled from the default, `Some(false)` erases; erasing what a participant sent is `purge(Purge::Author)`. `Osis::leave` removes the account's grant and its join of a link, not a team it holds through; the admin removal that audits backdated posts is not automated. A leave ends nothing: the link goes on working, and a leaver who kept the text can rejoin by it (notes/permissions.md Links).
- **Message**: a `fire` of the whole `message` struct (`schema::MSG`, a `Signal<Message>`). An id is the fire's `CommitId` (an edited message's original's, which listings show it under), so a reference is one `commit(id)`. Another writer's write that does not read as a message reads `Body::Held(None)` (the CLI's state `odd`, the app's `<unreadable>`); a reply id that does not parse is no reply (`schema::Message::reply`), one naming nothing here quotes the id. Purgeable-by comes from `chat:settings^.0` (`message_retention`), expiry from the setting's entry, filled by the node.
- **Name**: in the signed body, per message: the sender's own word; nobody can name another's messages; no registry. The tid beside it is what is verified. CLI: `--from` or `OSIS_CHAT_NAME`.
- **Read**: a page is a `fired` page: each message once, under its id, its newest text, `Part::edited` its newest version's place ("(edited)"); history on demand (`versions`). A quote is `commit(id)`, one commit however far back (the app's quote is a memo per message). `since(after)` goes by arrival at this node (`received`, then place) with a `Cursor` (text `RECEIVED/PLACE`), not by stamp: a post made offline, stamped before what the reader has seen, still comes after the cursor; an edit arrives as its message again. It reads the whole history each call (no arrival index), so it is for a reader that comes and goes; a timeline watches `chat:msg~0` (`on_fire`, which hears an edit once, as its message). A deletion moves no cursor (watch it). Missed: an entry arriving in the cursor's millisecond with a lesser place, or after the clock jumped back past the cursor. A cursor is this node's own. Others' invalid writes are not listed (a watch hears `commit: None` when one turns invalid, expires or is dropped); one's own show `valid: false` with the text.
- **Edit** (`osis_chat::edit`): `replace` of one's own message, keeping its name and reply; osis refuses another's message, a version and a chat with editing off (the entry's `final`, `RetentionEntry::refuse_replace`; a writer default, so another writer's edit is honored). Under `EditMode::Purge` the same commit purges the old text (`Purge::Commit` of every version held but the new one, under the fire, so it takes the disappearing expiry too); where the purge set does not let the author, the edit goes alone and the old text stays. Newest by `(ts, ID)` wins. The message keeps its id, place and time.
- **Delete**: `purge(Purge::Commit(id))`: the message and every version held here. `Body::Purged { actor }` is the request's verified actor. Refused locally without the right. Tombstones of permanent deltas reach new nodes; in a disappearing chat a tombstone goes at the message's expiry. Purge sets from `Options::purge`.
- **Disappearing**: the setting entry's expiry, filled by the writer (history still kept forever; the expiry destroys each message, kept copies too); changing it rewrites `retention^.0` and reaches later messages only.
- **Told, re-sent**: a write after its own kick (stamped past it) is `OwnWriteInvalid` (`on_diagnostic`). Re-sending fires its message again (`osis chat resend`); the old one stays, shown to its sender alone. An undelivered edit is not honored: the message shows its previous text.
- **Client with its own actor**: reads and watches over the port; with its own write grant it is its own sender, so it cannot edit what its account's nodes sent.

## The osis-chat crate
osis-chat holds only what every chat reader or writer must do alike for chat to be correct, where a mistake would show forged or wrong messages or leave a chat open to erasure. Everything else is a plain `osis` call at the caller (app `facets/log.rs`, CLI `chat.rs`) on the keys in `osis_chat::schema`.
- `schema` (keys of schema/chat.osis): the one generated copy every chat user goes through.
- `Options`, `create`: the creation rules are chat's security: `retention^.0` set before anyone is granted.
- `edit`: a writer skipping the settings' purge would leave old text a chat promised gone. The edit rule itself is osis's (notes/signal-edits-design.md): a forged edit is refused at its writer and honored nowhere.
- `since`/`cursor`, `Cursor`: reading by arrival, which no osis listing orders by (the CLI's `read --after`, `watch --after`). A cursor is its reader's own, so this is a helper, not a shared rule, kept beside its sim tests.
- `schema::Message::reply`: a reply id another writer chose is no reply unless it names a commit.
Not here, on purpose: wrappers over one osis call (grant, `set_role`, `make_link`, `fire`, `on_fire`, `on_diagnostic`, delete, purge by author, `commit(id)`, `versions`, the timer, resend), chat copies of osis types, a list of chats (the query API, issues/api-deferred.md). Ids stay `string` in the schema, parsed as `CommitId` (a schema type for a commit id is a core change for one use); the name stays in `chat:settings^.0`, not `name.0`, as only admins rename.

## Left out
- Rejected: segments, adding without history as a chain of chats (the core option does it with no chain); an admin creates C2, grants every current participant and the newcomer, copies the settings, and adds C2 to C1's `chat:next^.0` (admin-only set). Old participants stitch by time; the newcomer holds only C2 and learns nothing of C1. Any admin re-applies roster changes across segments on seeing `next`; links made on C1 die at each segment; a kick is one commit per segment; `next` is a set, so a fork shows both branches to old participants and one to each newcomer. About 15 KB per member per segment.
- Unbuilt core: stopping a fetch short of old messages (issues/partial-history-fetch.md; a sync ID fills newest first, so old messages reach a new node late, each past a cursor), kicks past the frontier cap (frontier heads), realms (below).
- Not done: reactions, read marker, typing, hidden set, segments, attachments, the leave audit.
- Known and small: an edit not held here when a delete is written still shows, as a new message could. Re-adding a participant revives posts it made while kicked (forced by design E; `flows::kicked_while_offline_*`). A kicked account's new node gets nothing, its own messages included.

## Costs
Counted (notes/test-scale.md Scale checks):
- A kick rebuilds no state for the messages (a signal has none): it re-judges which bodies count (osis-node `signals::signal_kick_rejudges_without_a_rebuild_*`).
- A post uploads 762 B after 10 and after 100 messages (chat `flows::post_cost_flat_in_history`); a post reads nothing per member and an add judges nothing per message (osis-sim `scale::post_reads_nothing_per_member`, `add_judges_nothing_per_message`); a write to a long kept aspect and its arrival are flat in its length, a writer's removal linear (osis-node `scale::long_aspect_writes_are_flat`). What still grows: issues/long-aspect-cost.md, issues/chat-add-cost.md; each participant stores about 52 KB at every device.
- Per message: about 0.5 KB on the wire, about 1.2 KB stored at every device for good; expired or purged about 33 B residue; a purged permanent delta keeps its 300 B header and its purge commit's. A hostile 10^7-message flood costs every participant about 5 GB until purged by author (about 11 purge commits, 750 MB, kept 90 days); purge by author never overrides author-only or `nobody` purge sets, so under those a flood stays.
- A read+write rotation costs 2.76 KB per remaining holder, linear in holders (osis-node `scale::roster_removal_is_linear`, budget 3000 B); downloads multiply by the holder count, only on removals. **Tess: accepted; keep the simple format.** Cheaper only by wire-format changes, both declined: the header once per commit (a second fetch per message); one KEM key per actor and one box per holder for all roles (done plainly gives read-only holders write and admin secrets; the safe variants make role separation classical-only, or delay reads after a lower-role grant). Removals and groups of hundreds are rare; simplicity and the post-quantum promise win.

## Links: why joiners stay on K
A joiner's own grant on C would need L to grant on C (K with `may_grant` on C, a spec change) and would not end shared fate: the joiner may keep the seed, so a kick that leaves the link lets it rejoin. A kick ends the link either way; a share view reads through K.

## Realms: servers, channels, roles
notes/realms.md (in discussion) applied to chat: a server or team is a realm R (members hold write on R, admins admin); a channel is a chat with an edge for R at write, or R its first admin (members post at the path minimum; read-only announcements: R holds read); a mod role is a realm M holding admin on R or on some channels (its members hold admin on M, since admin passes only through admin); realms nest by grants at the path minimum. A plain chat is no realm. A chat app's sidebar (categories, order, names of unsynced channels) is a layout aspect on R, admin-written. Chat must not preclude a realm's edge on a chat, a realm as a chat's first admin, or a role realm holding admin on a chat; nothing does.
- Removal: one commit protects new content everywhere; cutting the removed member's sync and upload keys at peers keeping a channel unread needs an admin step per channel per such peer, by whichever admin node next syncs that sync ID; until then it reads ciphertext and can upload.
- Fits about 1000 to a few thousand members. Core asks for big realms: default-off sync of a realm's things (a node syncs every label whose secret it holds, `SyncEngine::labels`); a settle rule for cascade rotations (any writer's next pass re-keys a child, eagerly, and online writers may fork); pure readers placing no chain (11.5 KB per account), or card (1.5 KB) under big labels (about 13 MB once per node at 1000 members); frontier heads by commit ID, so private channels' tids do not leak in kick certificates.
- Not traced: per-channel mute and timeouts (client-honored; rights cannot deny), delete-only mods (messages on a thing made under the chat, with an admin edge), unread counts (a latest-time register per channel on R), mentions in unsynced channels, the audit log (a realm's role history), read-only channels filling deposit budgets.

## Rejected designs
- **A** messages as things made under a member actor M in M's own group, C granted read on M: any member can edit C's member list (remove another's M, deleting their history at peers; kicking the vandal does not undo it), and a kicked member's writes on M stay valid, stopped only by the upload stamp.
- **B** as A with every message added to C's list: a one-commit send leaves a removed message's body at peers, pre-kick messages the kicker had not received vanish, any member can unlist any message, every message is a thing.
- **C** messages as map entries on M, an add-only member list (a core change): no admin erase of one message, a kicked member's late writes stopped only by stamps, a sender bound by M's admins. Better than E only on compaction and kick cost while frontiers list commits.
- **D** C plus an hourly content key chain for forward secrecy against a peer recording: refuted: the seed's box is in the recording and opens under keys the device keeps, so a thief rebuilds every hour key. Real member forward secrecy needs erasable per-device keys.
