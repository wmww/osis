# Chat
> Summary: Chat (design E): `osis chat` and the app over the `osis` API, with crate `osis-chat` (libs/chat) holding only chat's shared rules (creation, the edit model): one actor per chat, its first admin the maker, participants hold write, a message is one write on the signal `chat:msg~0`, kept for good, the sender the verified actor; the chat's history is the chat. Why this shape, Tess's decisions, what is built, what is left out, costs, realms (servers and channels), the rejected designs. Behavior case by case: notes/chat-cases.md.

## Design E
- A chat C is one thing, actor and label, its maker's account its first admin (spec/permissions.md §1), synced as a group of itself with an empty member list; its label carries its own aspects, participants are its roster. No member actor, no member list, no per-message thing.
- A message is a delta on `chat:msg~0` (schema/chat.osis, with `chat:settings^.0`), a signal (spec/commits.md §5 Signals): no current value, each write history from the moment it is written; author, time and retention come from the signed header. Its fires are the chat: reads are listings (`fired`: each commit with author, time, validity and its part). Retention `author_or_admin`, keep history forever (a permanent delta, unless the chat disappears messages): a body is must-hold for good, compaction may not drop it, only purge rights decide, and a non-admin holds no delete key for it, so at honest nodes no non-admin can destroy or hide another's message, by snapshot, purge, reset, `ts` or delete key.
- A required retention (`required^.0`, spec/commits.md §5) set at creation, before anyone is granted, refuses deltas with any other, so no participant can write under an earlier key.
- The sender is the header's `actor`, checked by validity (spec/permissions.md §5.2 Acting): nobody can speak as another.
- Why the message is the write, not a register holding a message thing's tid: the closure runs only through member lists (spec/data-model.md §8), so a message thing would need a member actor and a placement again.
- Cost of the shape: validity after a demotion lists every surviving message of the removed author in the frontier (about 34 B per message, a kick caps near 1.9×10^6 messages of one participant) until frontier heads land (notes/frontier-study.md, unbuilt); no compaction; open is O(all commits) (issues/long-aspect-cost.md).

## Tess's decisions
- The maker as first admin is the default app's default, not a rule: a chat's first admin may be a realm or anyone. A chat keeps all messages, so it keeps its member history too and needs no roster compaction. Recovery from a vandal writer needs only an admin able to demote.
- Accepted: a stolen unlocked device shows history; no deniability; deletion binds only honest clients; hosts see metadata. Eventual revocation is a general osis limit, not chat's.
- Member forward secrecy is probably undesirable (and would need erasable per-device KEM keys, MLS-sized; against spec/crypto.md §3).
- Adding without history should be a core op (issues/history-less-grant.md); segments (below) are the fallback.
- Edit is the one place chat does not use ordinary osis editing (an edit is a new message).
- A removed participant keeps its copy by default (`required^.0` `retain`, filled by a kick); erasing is a setting.
- Defaults: moderator delete; lasting tombstones; others keep a kicked participant's messages; block is local; disappearing timer per chat; add with history; edits purge the old text. Settings: add without history; permanent chat (`nobody`); admin-only or author-only delete; edit history kept or editing off; private read position; participants may invite. Target realm size 10,000 members.

## As built
- **Create** (`create`): `create()` alone (the record by a role commit), `sync`, then one commit of `required^.0` (the message rule on `chat:msg~0`; the plain retention as `default`, so `nobody` junk on another aspect is invalid; `retain_copies` true) and `chat:settings^.0` (name, edit mode). The record among the role commits is what a joiner reads first (spec/commits.md §2), so it re-reads each pending role commit about half as often as with the record in a content commit it waits on (`scale::joiner_reads_each_pending_commit_few_times`, bound 2m+1 at m members, the sim's chat made in this order). A crash between `sync` and the rules commit leaves a synced chat with no rules: `create` again. The maker's serving nodes take it by `admit own` (its account is the first admin); others by sync ID or `--admit open`.
- **Participants**: adding is `grant` (a chat is synced from `create`); a link is `make_link`, joining is `redeem`: a link to a chat this account already reaches at the link's role returns it, no link followed (its maker, a participant, a joiner pasting it again); a kept copy is no role, so its link rejoins. A link joiner holds through K, and `participants` reads through K to them, at the lesser role, and drops L; `links` lists the Ks with their key. An actor is read through when some holder of it has holders of its own (`grants` one level down: a link's K and a team are held by accounts, the first admin's record edge among them); an account's holders are its nodes and recovery key, key actors nobody holds. L is K's holder with `may_grant` that nobody holds. A direct participant learns a joiner at its first post (the joiner's chain comes with it) and lists K till it knows someone of it; a joiner and the link's maker know all. Only the node that made a link can kick one joiner (`MadeLink` is local); any admin can kick K, ending everyone behind it. Moderator: `Role::Admin`; all admins equal. A kick is `set_role(.., None, ..)`, keep filled from the default, `Some(false)` erases; erasing what a participant sent is `purge(Purge::Author)`. `leave` removes every grant the account holds the chat through (a link joiner's is on K); the admin's following removal that audits backdated posts is not automated. Kicking a link joiner ends the link for those who have not redeemed it (`set_role`): make a new one. A leave ends nothing: the link goes on working however many leave, and a leaver who kept the text can rejoin by it (notes/permissions.md Links).
- **Message**: a `fire` of the whole `message` struct schema/chat.osis declares (`schema::MSG` is a `Signal<Message>`: there is no state for it to equal or grow). An id is the commit's place (`CommitPos`) in text form `TS/COMMIT` (opaque to the app), so a reference needs no scan. Another writer's write that does not read as a message, or claims to edit what is no place, reads `Body::Held(None)` and is not shown; a reply to no place reads as no reply (`Message::reply_to`). Retention and expiry come from the rule, filled by the node.
- **Name**: in the signed body, per message: the sender's own word, nobody can name another's messages; no registry. Tid beside it is what is verified. CLI: `--from` or `OSIS_CHAT_NAME`.
- **Read**: `Timeline::older` goes from the newest through `fired` pages: it has then seen every edit of what it reaches, so a page back costs the distance from the newest, not a lookup per message. `since(after)` goes by arrival at this node (`received`, then `(ts, ID)`), not by stamp: a post made offline is stamped before what the reader has seen and still comes after the cursor. It reads the whole history each call (no arrival index), so it is for a reader that comes and goes: an app showing a timeline watches `chat:msg~0` (`on_fire`). An edit of an older message comes as that message again under its id; a deletion of an older message moves no cursor (watch it). Limits: an entry arriving in the cursor's millisecond with a lesser ID, or after the node's clock jumped back past the cursor, is missed. `cursor` is this node's: not to be carried to another. `find` reads back to the original. Others' invalid writes are not listed (a watch hears `commit: None` when one turns invalid, expires or is dropped); one's own show `valid: false` with the text.
- **Edit**: a new message naming the original and every earlier version; honored only if the original is here, is no edit itself and has the same verified sender, else it is a message of its own. Newest by `(ts, ID)` wins. Under `EditMode::Purge` a second commit purges the old (a commit with a request carries no expiry, so never the same commit). Named versions' tombstones are the message's, so an edited message shows once.
- **Delete**: `purge(Purge::Commit(id))` of every version known, one commit. `Body::Purged { actor }` is the request's verified actor. Refused locally without the right (a request without it drops nothing). Tombstones of permanent deltas reach new nodes and newcomers. Purge sets from `Options::purge`.
- **Disappearing**: per chat, the rule's expiry (history still kept forever: a signal keeps history, and the expiry ends each message); changing it writes `required^.0` whole. Leaves kept copies too.
- **Told, re-sent**: a write before the writer saw a `required^.0` change with a key after it, or its own kick, is `OwnWriteInvalid` (`on_diagnostic`). Re-sending fires its message again (`osis chat resend`); the old one stays, shown to its sender alone. An undelivered edit goes as a plain message. A window of one sync gap, or for good if a participant-held chat's setting changes later.
- **Client with its own actor**: reads and watches over the port; with its own write grant it is its own sender, so it cannot edit what its account's nodes sent.

## The osis-chat crate
Principle: osis-chat holds only what every chat reader or writer must do alike for chat to be correct, where a mistake would show forged or wrong messages or leave a chat open to erasure. Everything else is a plain `osis` call at the caller (app `facets/log.rs`, CLI `chat.rs`) on the keys in `osis_chat::schema`. What it holds, and why:
- `schema` (keys of schema/chat.osis): the one generated copy every chat user reads and writes through.
- `Options`, `create`: the creation rules are chat's security: `required^.0` set before anyone is granted.
- The edit model: `Message` (the original's place, the newest version's `osis::Body`), `Timeline`, `find`, `since`/`cursor`, `edit`, `delete`, `edit_of` (one commit's claim, checked, for a watch). Every reader folds alike or a forged edit replaces someone's message; `edit` and `delete` write what the fold reads. `since` folds what arrived by the same rules, so it lives here too.
- Not chat-specific, waiting on issues/share-view.md: `participants`, `links`, `leave`. osis cannot say which holders are people and which are links, so these read it from the roster's shape.
Not here, on purpose: wrappers over one osis call (grant, `set_role`, `make_link`, `fire`, `on_fire`, `on_diagnostic`, purge by author, writing `required^.0` for the timer, resending one's own invalid message), chat copies of osis types (`osis::Body`, `CommitPos`, `schema::Message`, `schema::EditMode`), a list of chats (the query API, issues/api-deferred.md). Joining by a link to a chat already reached is `redeem`'s own rule (notes/api.md Links). Ids stay `string` in the schema, parsed as `CommitPos` (a schema type for a commit's place is a core change for one use); the name stays in `chat:settings^.0`, not `name.0`, as only admins rename.

## Left out
- Segments (no core change, unbuilt): adding without history as a chain of chats; an admin creates C2, grants every current participant and the newcomer, copies the settings, and adds C2 to C1's `chat:next^.0` (admin-only set). Old participants stitch by time; the newcomer holds only C2 and learns nothing of C1. Any admin re-applies roster changes across segments on seeing `next`; links made on C1 die at each segment; a kick is one commit per segment; `next` is a set, so a fork shows both branches to old participants and one to each newcomer. About 15 KB per member per segment.
- Unbuilt core: adding without history (issues/history-less-grant.md), stopping a fetch short of old messages (issues/partial-history-fetch.md; a label does fill newest first, so old messages reach a new node late, each past a cursor), a kick past about 1.9×10^6 messages of one participant (frontier heads), realms (notes/realms.md: servers and channels; a channel is a chat a realm holds write on).
- Not done here: reactions, read marker, typing, hidden set, `chat:next^.0` segments, attachments, the leave audit.
- Known and small: an edited message deleted leaves a tombstone per version (the link was in the purged bodies). A watch hears an edit under `Purge` as an edit and then a deletion of the old version: a read tells them apart. A concurrent edit landing after a delete brings the text back on the versions it did not purge. Re-adding a participant revives posts it made while kicked (a design E forced answer; `kicked_while_offline` asserts it). A kicked account's new node gets nothing, its own messages included.

## Costs
- A kick rebuilds no state for the messages (a signal has none): it re-judges which bodies count (osis-node `signals::signal_kick_rejudges_without_a_rebuild_*`); what does grow per thing: issues/long-aspect-cost.md.
Counted (notes/testing.md Scale checks): a post uploads 762 B after 10 and after 100 messages (chat `flows::post_upload_flat_in_history`); a post reads nothing per member at any node and an add judges nothing per message (osis-sim `scale::post_reads_nothing_per_member`, `add_judges_nothing_per_message`); a write to a long kept aspect and its arrival are flat in its length, a writer's removal linear (osis-node `scale::long_aspect_writes_are_flat`). What a joiner and an add still cost per member and message: issues/chat-add-cost.md; each participant stores about 52 KB at every device.
- Per message: about 0.5 KB on the wire, about 1.2 KB stored at every device for good (body plus header row); expired or purged about 33 B residue; a purged permanent delta keeps its 300 B header and its purge commit's. A hostile 10^7-message flood costs every participant about 5 GB until purged by author (about 11 purge commits, 750 MB, kept 90 days); a purge by author never overrides author-only or `nobody` purge sets, so under those settings a flood stays.

## Rotation costs
- A read+write rotation costs 2.76 KB per remaining holder, linear in holders (osis-node `scale::roster_removal_is_linear`, budget 3000 B). Downloads multiply by the holder count, only on removals.
- **Tess's decision: accepted; keep the simple format.** Cheaper only by wire-format changes, both declined: the header once per commit (a second fetch per message); one KEM key per actor and one box per holder for all roles (done plainly gives read-only holders write and admin secrets; the safe variants make role separation classical-only, or delay reads after a lower-role grant). Removals are rare, groups of hundreds are rare, simplicity and the post-quantum promise win.

## Links: why joiners stay on K
A joiner's own grant on C would need L to grant on C: K with `may_grant` on C, a spec change. It would not end shared fate: the joiner may keep the seed, so a kick that leaves the link lets it rejoin. So `kick` ends the link either way. Kept; `participants` reads through K.

## Realms: servers, channels, roles
notes/realms.md (in discussion) applied to chat: a server or team is a realm R (members hold write on R, admins admin); a channel is a chat with an edge for R at write, or R its first admin (members post at the path minimum; read-only announcements: R holds read); a mod role is a realm M holding admin on R or on some channels (its members hold admin on M, since admin passes only through admin); realms nest by grants at the path minimum. A plain chat is no realm. The chat app's sidebar (categories, order, names of unsynced channels) is a layout aspect on R, admin-written. Chat must not preclude a realm's edge on a chat, a chat a realm is the first admin of, or a role realm holding admin on a chat; nothing does.
- Removal: one commit protects new content everywhere; cutting the removed member's sync and upload keys at hosts needs an admin step per channel per host, by whichever admin device next syncs that label; until then it reads ciphertext and can upload.
- Fits about 1000 to a few thousand members today. Core asks for big realms: default-off sync of a realm's things (today `compute_labels` syncs every label whose secret the node holds); a settle rule for cascade rotations (any writer's next pass re-keys a child, eagerly, and online writers may fork); pure readers placing no chain (11.5 KB per account), card (1.5 KB) or heartbeats under big labels (about 13 MB once and 2.4 MB a day per device at 1000 members); frontier heads by commit ID, so private channels' tids do not leak in kick certificates.
- Not traced to depth: per-channel mute and timeouts (client-honored; rights cannot deny), delete-only mods (messages on a thing made under the chat, with an admin edge), unread counts (a latest-time register per channel on R), mentions in unsynced channels, the audit log (a realm's role history), read-only channels filling deposit budgets.

## Rejected designs
- **A** messages as things made under a member actor M in M's own group, C granted read on M: any member can edit C's member list (remove another's M, deleting their history at hosts; kicking the vandal does not undo it), and a kicked member's writes on M stay valid, stopped only by the upload stamp.
- **B** as A with every message added to C's list: one-commit send leaves a removed message's body at hosts, pre-kick messages the kicker had not received vanish, any member can unlist any message, every message is a thing.
- **C** messages as map entries on M, an add-only member list (a core change): no admin erase of one message, a kicked member's late writes are stopped only by stamps, a sender bound by M's admins. Better than E only on compaction and kick cost while frontiers list commits.
- **D** C plus an hourly content key chain for forward secrecy against a host recording: refuted, the seed's box is in the recording and opens under keys the device keeps, so a thief rebuilds every hour key. Real member forward secrecy needs erasable per-device keys.
