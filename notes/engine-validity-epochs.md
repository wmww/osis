# Engine: the epoch rule
> Summary: How the engine keeps spec/permissions.md §5.4-§5.5 (`flows/account.rs`, `lists.rs`, `signing.rs`): the filter, name rows, sides (first revocation seen), `JudgedEpochs`, what a node writes.

## The filter
- Runs first in each re-judge pass (`refresh_epochs`). A role commit that does not count stays held but no timeline reads it (`Held::set_counted`, left out of `HeldSet::edge_commits`). Every other part of a commit that does not count is invalid in `Judge::part` (`created.0` aside), but a plain part a counting addendum saves (`Vouching::saves`).
- Commits held nowhere (a relay-only signal, an inbox request) are judged whole by `Core::counts_at_epoch`.
- Addenda: one least fixpoint per thing over every author (perm `Timeline::vouching`, fed each addendum's standing). Monotone: addenda save plain content only, which neither a timeline nor an epoch reads. Tests: perm `content::addenda_fixpoint_runs_through_closed_epochs_*`, `rotation::tests_account::closed_epoch_write_falls_and_a_witness_saves_it_*`.

## Names
- Thing lists (`Engine::hold_lists`; `Engine::ingest_with_lists` holds a batch's list blobs before its rotation applies) and `listing.0` deltas (`lists::note_listing`) become name rows (`tn/` commit‖thing‖source, `tb/` source‖commit‖thing). Certs stay for good (`tl/`), as role commits do.
- A name is judged once its source is held: a rotation's lists are kept before it applies (`ingest_with_lists`, own writes) and it may wait for a head not here, so `put_names` marks a named commit due only when its source is held (a listing is indexed before its names are noted); a rotation's arrival judges what it names (`refresh_epochs`, a rotation whose count moved), whatever arrived while it waited.
- Waiting (spec §5.5): at a node of the account, `refresh_epochs` passes `osis_perm::Awaited` (D, the deciding rotations: D list held in `ix.lists_on`, or header removals non-empty), so the account's commits on D at an epoch no closer decides on count. A removal-carrying rotation decides at once because a removed node gets neither the removal nor D's later lists from its siblings: the collapse is how it learns (osis-sync `duties::a_node_removed_from_its_account_stops_syncing_*`). Limit: a sibling taking a removal-carrying rotation before its D list still reads D's roster as P alone and leaves (issues/sibling-removal-rotation-before-list.md). Without it a sibling taking a rotation before its D list (a Prove's rotations, a header-only copy under another sync ID, the list blob fetched after the rotation's) read D's roster as P alone, was no node of P, dropped its home and could never fetch the list or the seed. A D list arriving marks the account's commits on D due (`lists::waited_list_held`, O(D's commits), once per rotation). Test: `rotation::tests_account::a_node_of_the_account_waits_for_d_s_list_*`; through sync: osis-sync `driver::a_sibling_rotation_proves_again_*`. The epochs model (roles-model `epochs`) lacks it.
- A list for a rotation not held is not kept: sync keeps the entry as `Kind::List { held: false }` until the rotation arrives.
- Inner and merge signatures are kept per role commit at an epoch, as facts of the commit whatever is known when it applies (`ix.role_sigs`, `note_role_sigs`); merge signatures are verified at chain build (`osis_commit::verify_merge`). Not persisted: an open reads them from the role commits' records, one record read per account role commit (issues/role-sigs-open-reads.md). Rejected: a `node/role sigs` table (one store page per role commit applied: `scale::role_history_join_is_linear` went past linear); noting only commits `devices_of` names as D's at apply (order-dependent: a rebuild from the records differed, `order::held_set_decides`).

## Sides (`osis_perm::chain`, `AccountView`)
- Principle (Tess): the first revocation seen holds, and nothing switches back by itself. A node places the held rotations in the order it came to hold each with what it needs (its line and what it takes: receipt ms of the latest, one ms's by ID; the user's chosen line first), ties ancestors first (by depth, never by ID alone: one ms's ID order would place a node's second removal before its first, and with what a revocation stands with read in order the node would disregard its own removal: `a_rotation_is_placed_after_its_line_whatever_its_id`), and decides each from what was placed before (spec/permissions.md §5.4). At a node of the account what the chosen line stands with (what a rotation on it takes, with its line) is placed with it, so a choice of a line standing with another revocation holds (`a_chosen_line_is_placed_with_what_it_takes`); outside, the line alone (a rotation it takes may be beside it, and outside `takes` decides only holding). Model: roles-model `forks` (README there); the engine's nodes, readers and honest policy are checked against it by `rotation::tests_forks` (module doc there: what is compared, what the mirror leaves to the engine).
- Outside the account every rotation beside a followed one ends it: a removed device's fork looks like any honest rotation, so a reader that held the removal first never counts the device's later forks. A node of the account reads `osis_perm::Devices` (who signed each rotation and each of the account's role commits on D, by inner signature; the removals a rotation carries, the removal edge writes on D by its minter signed at its `at`, with their frontiers; D's grants; the nodes D's creating commit grants) and takes revocations first-seen as a consistent set, following what they leave standing: its devices' rotations made at once all count, a removed device's plain fork never outweighs its removal, of two revocations hitting each other the one held first holds. No input of the side is D's roster, so a fight is not circular.
- A removal from D belongs to the rotation whose header names it (`RotationHeader::removals`, filled by `rotate_in` with its maker's removals at the epoch it rotates), and that rotation is placed no earlier than the removal arrived (`Devices::carried`). Read instead as every removal its minter signed at its epoch, a removed node writing a removal at an old epoch turned its earlier rotations into revocations, placed where those rotations were, ahead of the removal of it (`a_removal_written_later_turns_no_earlier_rotation_into_a_revocation_spec_permissions_5_4`); placed at the rotation's own receipt, a revocation whose removal is sent later is (`a_revocation_is_placed_when_its_removal_arrives_spec_permissions_5_4`). A header naming a removal its minter did not sign at the epoch it rotates carries nothing: anyone holding an old epoch's key mints a rotation there, and one naming an honest removal would stop every node naming it again (`a_header_naming_anothers_removal_carries_none_spec_permissions_5_4`). A removal carried by a rotation not counting here is no one's to name again (`rotations_due`), and a node whose taken revocations remove it signs nothing for the account.
- A device stands with every revocation it took (Tess's ruling applied: a device that took the other side's revocation is the other side, and the honest side treats its line exactly as it treats the thief's). Each rotation's header `takes` the revocations its maker took that are not on its line (`rotate_in`, from `AccountView::taken`); a node of the account reads a rotation as standing with the revocations on its line and those any rotation on its line takes, takes a revocation only if every other one it stands with is taken, and follows a rotation only if every one it stands with is taken. A rotation is held only once what it takes is held (placed after it), at every node: outside the account too, or a removed device's rotation taking a rotation no one holds would stay pending at the account's nodes, never answered, while readers outside it follow it unwarned (`a_rotation_taking_what_no_one_holds_is_held_nowhere_spec_permissions_5_4`). So a device that took the thief's revocation first, whose seed the honest side may not be able to tell apart (it signs at its own line, boxing the thief), is that side, whoever signs, for as long as its user stays, and once its user switches its old-side rotations stay disregarded at every node, in any order (`a_rotation_taking_a_revocation_not_taken_here_is_the_other_side`, `a_rotation_taking_the_thiefs_revocation_is_that_side_spec_permissions_5_4`). Outside the account `takes` decides nothing but when a rotation is held. Tradeoff: the header shows outsiders which rotations a device took as revocations (IDs only, not whom they remove); hiding it in the inner signature would let anyone holding the epoch's old inner secret rewrite it.
- Rejected: reading a device's side from what its rotations supersede (a line at odds with a taken revocation's: one disowns one on the other). A device that took the thief's revocation before it held the honest removal disowns nothing of the honest line, so its rotations (boxing the thief) were followed and merged by the honest side, round after round while its user stayed; at odds with any line followed before (not only a taken revocation's) splits honest nodes that placed a switching device's old-side rotation and the merge it disowned in different orders. Rejected too: reading which nodes a rotation's seed reaches (D's boxes at its `under` epoch): a node of P cannot open D's rotation commits of a device that dropped it.
- Who is a node (principle: a removed device's word is what its remover held of it, the role timeline's frontier rule): those a commit creating D grants it (while every taken removal's frontier lists that commit: a removed device holding the first epoch's key can make one again), and every holder of a grant of D by another node that no taken removal hits (least fixpoint). A removal hits its node's rotations off the line of the rotation carrying it (that line, merged forks included, was made before it) and its node's commits on D its frontier does not list, unless made after a re-add. So a removed device holding old epochs' keys gets nowhere by sealing inner signatures as a key actor it makes up, by backdating a grant, or by granting itself D at an unaware sibling's epoch: none of it is on its remover's frontier. A rotation whose minter is no node never stands; one is placed no earlier than its minter came to be known as a node (its grant chain held), so a forged revocation held before the grant naming its minter is placed after it. One whose minter is never known is held at its receipt, disregarded and answered (pending, it would go unanswered while readers outside follow it).
- The removal's frontier lists the removed node's counting commits on D and the commits creating D held (`Core::devices_word`, `demotion_frontier`). Its commits on D a frontier leaves out, and any commit on D signed by no node, do not count (`Chain::cut` → `JudgedEpochs::cut`, so the D timeline, the roster and `departures` never read them). One whose inner signature does not open here (its epoch's seed not held yet) counts once it opens: `rotations_due` takes seeds up to a fixpoint, as each seed opens the inner signatures that establish the D epoch the next sits under.
- Open: a node of the account that took the thief's revocation first holds no seed of the honest line (seeds come from `account.0` deltas, which count only where their rotation does), so it opens no inner signature there and its user's switch to it cannot take; one that took a revocation of itself leaves or signs nothing (issues/account-node-switch-back.md; the model lets it switch, so the differential's settle leaves those users on X's side).
- Limit: a device the removed node paired in plain sight (its grant on the remover's frontier) stays a node until it is removed too. A grant the remover had not received is lost, as any removed writer's unlisted word (spec/permissions.md §5.3).
- Rejected: first-seen grants (a removed node's grant hit only when held after its removal). A stale node that holds a backdated forged grant first keeps the made-up key actor as a node and boxes it every rotation (`forks-first-grants` control).
- `devices_read` reads the account's role commits on D from `ix.role_on` (`by_thing` for role commits alone) and their inner signatures from `ix.role_sigs`, without a blob. Read from `by_thing`, every filing into D (a `group.0` write) moved every account's chain and all that reads it (an inbox take re-judged per write: osis-sync `keep::a_taken_inbox_blob_is_taken_once_spec_sync_inbox_2`).
- A merge is pending until every fork it names is held: a line is whole when placed, so a fork arriving later never reopens a decision.
- Honest forks are owed a merge (`signing::mergeable`, `rotations_due`): readers outside follow one head only. A head whose seed the node lacks is left to one that has it (superseding it unnamed would reject a line it follows). Limit: merges two nodes make at once fork again, and nothing bounds the rounds while they keep merging within one sync of each other (the reason forks were once left standing; the first-seen rule cannot tell an honest fork from a stolen device's outside the account). No deterministic tiebreak ends it: any rule letting two nodes merge one fork without seeing each other's merge forks again, and one letting fewer merge designates a device (Tess: none). Treating merges of the same heads as one fails too: a removed node's fork at the removal's epoch has the removal's parents. A node deferring to a held merge of a superset of its heads (leaving that merge's maker to join them) stalls on that maker, which may be gone for good: rejected too. Lockstep is what a deterministic simulation with symmetric delivery gives (two siblings each handed the other's removal and rotation, then acting on every delivery at one ms): roles-model `forks` storms the same way when A and B act on the same receipts each round, so the rule, not the engine, is what does not settle. Measured (`rotation::tests_signing::merge_rounds_after_concurrent_rotations`, by hand; random interleaving of single deliveries and single nodes' passes): 2 nodes 1 merge in 29 of 40 runs, at most 13; 3 nodes median 8, at most 36; lockstep forks every round. While it lasts, outside readers see one node's writes at its merge only through the other's lists.
- Every rotation a node of the account disregards with no rotation on its line a counting rotation rejects, and that no rotation of its own supersedes, is superseded unnamed by its next rotation (`unanswered`), so every reader that followed it is told. Another node's superseding rotation answers nothing by itself: signed at the epoch the disregarded one is signed at, it rejects nothing of it (contested from below), and readers following it would never be told (`a_rotation_superseded_without_a_rejection_is_answered_spec_permissions_5_5`); once per node, as a node's own answer may be so contested too. One answer per line: a reader following the line holds that rejection already; answering every rotation has two sides answer each other's answers for as long as both stay online (roles-model `forks-every-answer`). The line test reads rejections, not supersedes: a removed node holding the answer's epoch key (an unaware sibling's head boxed to it) signs there accepting its answered fork, which puts that fork on a line through the answer's epoch and ends the rejection; its new rotation is then answered (`a_rotation_undoing_an_answer_is_answered_spec_permissions_5_5`). The answer cannot be undone again: its epoch is not boxed to the removed node.
- Warning: `Chain::contested` (disregarded tips in a rejection with a counting rotation) → `AccountView::doubt` → `Event::AccountDisputed` once on its rise, `Engine::account_disputes`, osis `Dispute::Account` (versions: the preferred head and those tips). Told at the account's own nodes too.
- The user's choice is kept in `sides::Sides::accounts` (`records::SIDES`), per node; the default is recomputed from the receipt order, so nothing is recorded for it. Not shared across the reader's devices: issues/account-side-shared.md.
- Rejected for the account's nodes (each failed in `forks`): judging as outsiders (two devices' rotations made at once split them); placing with a conflict relation (a plain fork held first outweighed its maker's removal and split the honest devices); revocations that drop earlier ones and revive (oscillates when a removed node's revocation hits its own line).
- Rejected: one followed head with a designated merger (the node with the lowest authorized head): listings, the warning and naming waited on one device that may be gone for good.
- A closer ends an epoch for the cutoff only if it descends from it or names (authorized D-list) the removal of a node the epoch's line names no removal of (`signing::ends`, `Departures`; at a witness every closer ends it). Without that, every write at a head made after its sibling arrived would be nameable only by its own writer, so lost with a gone writer (`the_lowest_heads_node_gone_for_good_leaves_nothing_waiting_spec_permissions_5_4`).
- The chain is rebuilt when `account_view`'s key moves (held rotations, choice, seeds, own D's roster, D's role commits).
- A rotation finds heads by `at` and accepted names, never by a supersedes list, which a removed node's fork may fill. It names only the heads D's roster authorizes: naming a removed node's fork would accept it and every later fork from its epoch.
- Authorized (`AccountView::authorized`): minted by a current node, and no rotation on its line beside a removal of its minter (`roster_epochs`: on the removal epoch's line, or descending from a later re-grant's epoch). It decides what this node writes (what it names, rejects, owes), never what counts. Only counting removals and grants are read: a removed node still holds its old epochs' keys and could forge a removal of any node there. Standing (`AccountView::stands`) drops the current-minter half: a removed node's word from before its removal.
- Nothing honest is lost by an unnamed head: the epoch rule names commits at void epochs too, so each node's listing answering the rotation that closes them saves its own writes there (`an_honest_write_at_a_removed_nodes_rotation_counts_spec_permissions_5_5`). Only nodes signing at a head write a counting listing, so a removed node gains nothing.

## JudgedEpochs
- What the rule says per account is kept in `ix.epochs` and persisted (`node/epochs`): a standing is a lookup, and an open builds no chain and reads no list. `named` holds only held commits (`unindex_commit`), so it is bounded by the store.
- The epoch rule reads D only at a node holding the account, so taking it up (`Engine::take_up`, pairing) or leaving it (`signing::leave`) judges its epochs again in that transaction (`signing::judge_account_again`); `Core::judged_from` covers only what moves while it is held. The take-up at open skips it: the persisted judgment was made with it held (`a_node_leaving_its_account_judges_its_chain_as_an_outsider_*`, `a_reader_paired_into_the_account_judges_its_chain_as_a_node_*`).
- `refresh_epochs` takes the accounts due (`ix.epochs_due`, from `note_rotation`; `ix.names_due`: a commit at a closed epoch, or a word naming a held commit, arrived) and judges by `osis_perm::Epochs` only the commits whose standing may have moved (at a moved epoch, named by a rotation whose count moved via `tb/`, due, and what listings among them name). So a rotation costs the commits at the epochs it moved, a list the commits it names, a write no chain build (`rotation_judges_its_epochs_once_and_writes_stay_flat`, `a_rotation_lists_its_epoch_only`, `a_listing_named_late_names_again_spec_permissions_5_5`).

## Writing (`flows/signing.rs`)
- Removing a node from D rotates the account in the same transaction (`rotate_in`; thing lists inline only).
- Every rotation applied is due an answer, a listing or addenda (`ix.rotations_due`, `rd/`), and again when it comes to count (`refresh_epochs`): a merge arriving before the seed its inner signature opens with is read as void (minter unknown) and answered by nothing; without the second answer the non-merger's writes at the heads it closes are never listed.
- An account's rotation goes, at D's sync IDs, under the D epoch that its seed's `under` supersedes (`Engine::account_rotation_wrap_epoch`, entry wrap only; sections stay under the newest). Sealing the account's own `keys.0` section under the superseded account epoch lets a sibling open it before the seed, but hands D's fresh deposit seed to the node that rotation removes (`a_removed_node_opens_no_keys_of_its_removal_rotation_*`); a sibling opens it once the seed is taken (osis-sync `driver::a_sibling_rotation_proves_again_*`). A sibling's removal R at an epoch the node closed before receiving it counts only by the sibling's rotation Q naming it, and R's D epoch is established only once R counts; Q under R's epoch would wait on R for good (`sibling_removals_apart_both_hold_spec_permissions_3`). Its list blobs at D's sync IDs go under that epoch's roles secret (`list_wrap_secret`). So the removed node, which holds that epoch, opens the D lists of every rotation whose seed sits under its removal's D epoch (until D rotates again): commit IDs and thing tids only, as the header it opens names its removal; it is served none of them, holding no later sync key of D. Narrowing to rotations carrying removals is wrong: a rotation naming another node's removal in its D list (spec/permissions.md §5.5, Honest nodes) needs the same wrap.
- What the chain owes (`rotations_due`, any node of the account, none designated) is decided at the end of a complete pass of D's sync IDs (`Engine::signing_due`; osis-sync does not call it yet: issues/sync-unbuilt.md), so a removal and its rotation arriving apart do not fork: a removed node's fork no authorized rotation supersedes yet (`unanswered`, once a rotation naming its maker's removal is held: rejecting earlier would reject the removal's own parent), a blocked head, or a removal no authorized rotation names that this node may name (its own, by the cutoff, or named by a standing rotation). A removal named only by a removed node's fork is not named again (`a_removed_nodes_removal_named_only_by_its_fork_is_not_named_again_spec_permissions_5_5`).
- Several nodes owing the same rotation each make one; their fork is owed a merge, by the first node asked (`concurrent_rotations_fork_and_every_node_supersedes_them_spec_crypto_9`). A removed node's every re-fork costs one rotation per node of the account online, never a listing (unanswered rotations are answered by the rotation superseding them).
- Pairing carries the rotations with their lists (`Package::lists`), so D's earlier commits count at the new node.
- D is sealed to its nodes only (`Core::seals_to`). A node off D's roster drops the account's seeds and what it read as the account (`signing::leave_if_removed`; lost access and retain read `Core::member`, not `me`).
