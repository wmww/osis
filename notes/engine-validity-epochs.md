# Accounts: the epoch rule, custody and sides
> Summary: An account P is several devices (nodes) whose roster is P's own signing chain: the principles (custody follows holding, the chain is the roster, a merge is a join) and why, how the engine keeps spec/permissions.md §5.4-§5.5 and spec/crypto.md §9 (`flows/account.rs`, `custody.rs`, `lists.rs`, `signing.rs`): revoked epochs, the filter, name rows, sides (first revocation seen), custody, merges, `JudgedEpochs`, what a node writes, costs, the model and differential, rejected designs.

To be rewritten once issues/actors-one-mechanism.md lands: this is the account signing chain it replaces.

Tess's rule (spec/permissions.md §5.4): every node, the account's and outside readers, follows the first device revocation it sees; a counter-revocation raises "account forked"; the user may switch sides and a switch sticks; nothing switches back by itself; D's roster is never an input of the side. The problem: removing a lost or stolen device X must stop X reading anything new and acting for P, with no server, devices offline at random, several devices changing things at once, and X fighting back with every old key it holds (forking the chain, revoking honest devices).

## Principles
- **Custody follows holding.** A node reads with every secret it can open from what it holds (a box to its own key, a seed derivable from ones it holds), whether or not the commit carrying it counts or its epoch is open or stale. Judgment decides only what counts and what the node uses (signs at, encrypts under, boxes to others). So judgment is a function of held commits alone, never a fixpoint with keys. Why: when a secret opened only once the judgment needing it counted (seeds under D epochs the epoch rule established, which read P's chain, whose side read inner signatures that open with seeds), a node on the thief's side never counted the honest line, so never opened its seeds, so its user's switch could not take; and a thing's listing wrapped under an epoch descending from the rotation it made count never opened.
- **The chain is the roster.** At a node of P, who is a node on a line is read from P's rotations alone: D has no role timeline or key schedule of its own. Why: with D a second roster source, a removal on D acted on D's roster and re-keyed D while its P rotation was not held, and the two disagreed.
- **A merge is a join, nothing more.** Its epoch and seed are pure functions of the tips it joins; anything else a node has to say goes in a plain rotation. Why: a merge carrying its maker's choices (a random seed, takes, answers) made two nodes merging the same heads mint two epochs, which forked again (the merge storm). Plain rotations are made only for carried content, at most once per node per event (removal, addition, taken revocation, answered line), merge epochs are subsets of them, and nodes holding the same plain rotations make the same merge, in lockstep or not: no timing, no designated merger, no wait on one device.

## Revoked
- A revocation ends the standing of what was signed at the epochs it closes; a rotation carrying no removal only re-keys (as a thing's key rotation ends no earlier write). An epoch is revoked once a counting closer carries a removal (`AccountView::carrying`: the header's `revokes`, read alike at every node; at a node of P a rotation whose sealed `devices` field belies it is left out, `Core::devices_read`). A removal exists only in the plain rotation carrying it (`Engine::remove_device` → `signing::rotate_with`), so nothing acts on D apart from its rotation. Commits at a closed unrevoked epoch count unnamed (`Epochs::unnamed` is `Counts`); names matter only at revoked epochs. One notion with sync-set §3: `AccountView::revoked` (`Engine::account_key_revoked`) is the signing keys of revoked epochs.
- Why: an outsider cannot tell a thing list not yet arrived from one never written; un-counting a granter's commits on a merge or plain rotation dropped the outsider's role, keys and sync ID before the list could come (`an_outsider_keeps_the_accounts_word_across_a_rotation_revoking_no_one_*`). With a removal it still does: sync's leave stops the sync ID after the pass in flight, waiting for no source to bring the lists; given back later, it is fetched again (notes/sync-leave.md).
- Writers still name everything (lists, listings, addenda) whatever the revocation state, since a later removal revokes earlier epochs: `nameable` skips only commits a held counting word names (`signing::named_by_a_word`), never ones counting unrevoked.
- A sibling holding a rotation header-only (a Prove) holds the account's `keys.0` in part: `deposit_seed_of` is None while a valid part of the owner's `keys.0` is missing, so no step is issued from the older seed (two siblings stepped past each other's deposit keys for good). Taking a seed up redelivers the account's own commits with a missing part on it (`custody::keep`).

## The filter
- Runs first in each re-judge pass (`refresh_epochs`). A role commit that does not count stays held but no timeline reads it (`Held::set_counted`, left out of `HeldSet::edge_commits`). Every other part of a commit that does not count is invalid in `Judge::part` (`created.0` aside), but a plain part a counting addendum saves (`Vouching::saves`).
- Commits held nowhere (a relay-only signal, an inbox request) are judged whole by `Core::counts_at_epoch`.
- Addenda: one least fixpoint per thing over every author (perm `Timeline::vouching`, fed each addendum's standing). Monotone: addenda save plain content only, which neither a timeline nor an epoch reads. Tests: perm `content::addenda_fixpoint_runs_through_closed_epochs_*`, `rotation::tests_account::closed_epoch_write_falls_and_a_witness_saves_it_*`.

## Names
- Thing lists (`Engine::hold_lists`; `Engine::ingest_with_lists` holds a batch's list blobs before its rotation applies, or as they come for one held already, e.g. header-only from a Prove) and `listing.0` deltas (`lists::note_listing`) become name rows (`tn/` commit‖thing‖source, `tb/` source‖commit‖thing). Certs stay for good (`tl/`), as role commits do.
- A name is judged once its source is held: a rotation's lists are kept before it applies (`ingest_with_lists`, own writes) and it may wait for a head not here, so `put_names` marks a named commit due only when its source is held (a listing is indexed before its names are noted); a rotation's arrival judges what it names (`refresh_epochs`, a rotation whose count moved), whatever arrived while it waited.
- D's roster is the chain's (`Core::devices_roster`; Principles, The chain is the roster): grants and removals on D are refused, and D re-keys only inside P's rotations (`signing::commit_rotation`). So no commit on D waits on a list: a removed node's later word as P is a rotation its removal hits.
- A list for a rotation not held is not kept: sync keeps the entry as `Kind::List { held: false }` until the rotation arrives.
- Inner and merge signatures are kept per role commit at an epoch, as facts of the commit whatever is known when it applies (`ix.role_sigs`, `note_role_sigs`); merge signatures are verified at chain build (`osis_commit::verify_merge`). Not persisted: an open decodes them from each record's signature items, which follow the header bytes it reads anyway (`Sigs::read_stored`), so it decodes no record (`scale::an_open_decodes_no_role_commit_record`). Rejected: a `node/role sigs` table (one store page per role commit applied: `scale::role_history_join_is_linear` went past linear); noting only commits `account_of_root` names as D's at apply (order-dependent: a rebuild from the records differed, `order::held_set_decides`).

## Sides (`osis_perm::chain`, `AccountView`)
- Principle (Tess): the first revocation seen holds, and nothing switches back by itself. A node places the held rotations in the order it came to hold each with what it needs (its line and what it takes: receipt ms of the latest, one ms's by ID; the user's chosen line first), ties ancestors first (by depth, never by ID alone: one ms's ID order would place a node's second removal before its first, and with what a revocation stands with read in order the node would disregard its own removal: `a_rotation_is_placed_after_its_line_whatever_its_id`), and decides each from what was placed before (spec/permissions.md §5.4). At a node of the account what the chosen line stands with (what a rotation on it takes, with its line) is placed with it, so a choice of a line standing with another revocation holds (`a_chosen_line_is_placed_with_what_it_takes`); outside, the line alone (a rotation it takes may be beside it, and outside `takes` decides only holding). Model: roles-model `custody` (README there); the engine's nodes, readers and honest policy are checked against it by `rotation::tests_custody` (module doc there: what is compared, what the mirror leaves to the engine).
- Outside the account every rotation beside a followed one ends it: a removed device's fork looks like any honest rotation, so a reader that held the removal first never counts the device's later forks. A node of the account reads `osis_perm::Devices` from the held rotations alone (`Core::devices_read`): each plain rotation's minter (its inner signature), the adds and removes its sealed `devices` field carries (opened with the seed of the epoch it is signed at), each merge's certifiers, and the creation device (the creation nonce, spec/crypto.md §3). It takes revocations first-seen as a consistent set, following what they leave standing: its devices' rotations made at once all count, a removed device's plain fork never outweighs its removal, of two revocations hitting each other the one held first holds. No input of the side is D's roster, so a fight is not circular.
- Who is a node on a line, what a removal hits and when a certificate stands: spec/permissions.md §5.4, spec/crypto.md §9; `osis_perm::Chain::roster`, `nodes_of`, `standing_certifiers`. An addition by a removed node is in a rotation its removal hits, so forged and backdated additions and a grant at an unaware sibling's epoch need no rule of their own. Every engine read of a role on D (`Core::root_holders`: role_now, rosters, key holders, own_node, card ends) is the chain's roster.
- A device stands with every revocation it took (Tess's ruling applied: a device that took the other side's revocation is the other side, and the honest side treats its line exactly as it treats the thief's). Each rotation's header `takes` the revocations its maker took that are not on its line (`rotate_in`, from `AccountView::taken`); a node of the account reads a rotation as standing with the revocations on its line and those any rotation on its line takes, takes a revocation only if every other one it stands with is taken, and follows a rotation only if every one it stands with is taken. A rotation is held only once what it takes is held (placed after it), at every node: outside the account too, or a removed device's rotation taking a rotation no one holds would stay pending at the account's nodes, never answered, while readers outside it follow it unwarned (`a_rotation_taking_what_no_one_holds_is_held_nowhere_spec_permissions_5_4`). So a device that took the thief's revocation first, whose seed the honest side may not be able to tell apart (it signs at its own line, boxing the thief), is that side, whoever signs, for as long as its user stays, and once its user switches its old-side rotations stay disregarded at every node, in any order (`a_rotation_taking_a_revocation_not_taken_here_is_the_other_side`, `a_rotation_taking_the_thiefs_revocation_is_that_side_spec_permissions_5_4`). Outside the account `takes` decides nothing but when a rotation is held. Tradeoff: the header shows outsiders which rotations a device took as revocations (IDs only, not whom they remove); hiding it in the inner signature would let anyone holding the epoch's old inner secret rewrite it.
- Rejected: reading a device's side from what its rotations supersede (a line at odds with a taken revocation's: one disowns one on the other). A device that took the thief's revocation before it held the honest removal disowns nothing of the honest line, so its rotations (boxing the thief) were followed and merged by the honest side, round after round while its user stayed; at odds with any line followed before (not only a taken revocation's) splits honest nodes that placed a switching device's old-side rotation and the merge it disowned in different orders. Rejected too: reading which nodes a rotation's seed reaches (D's boxes at its `under` epoch): a node of P cannot open D's rotation commits of a device that dropped it.
- A rotation finds heads by `at` and its line, never by a supersedes list, which a removed node's fork may fill.
- `AccountView::authorized_by`: the minter of a counting rotation (a merge: its lowest certifier). It decides what this node answers as its account's word, never what counts.
- The chain is rebuilt when `account_view`'s key moves (held rotations, choice, seeds held).
- Warning: `Chain::contested` (disregarded tips in a rejection with a counting rotation) → `AccountView::doubt` → `Event::AccountDisputed` once on its rise, `Engine::account_disputes`, osis `Dispute::Account` (versions: the preferred head and those tips). Told at the account's own nodes too.
- The user's choice is kept in `sides::Sides::accounts` (`records::SIDES`), per node; the default is recomputed from the receipt order, so nothing is recorded for it. Not shared across the reader's devices: issues/account-side-shared.md.
- Rejected for the account's nodes (each failed in a roles-model search of the first-seen rule): judging as outsiders (two devices' rotations made at once split them); placing with a conflict relation (a plain fork held first outweighed its maker's removal and split the honest devices); revocations that drop earlier ones and revive (oscillates when a removed node's revocation hits its own line).
- Rejected: one followed head with a designated merger (the node with the lowest authorized head): listings, the warning and naming waited on one device that may be gone for good.

## Custody
- Custody (`flows::custody`, spec/crypto.md §9): a rotation's header boxes its seed to each device on its maker's roster (`seed_boxes`; a pairing's also hands the new device every seed held). `take_boxes` opens a box to this device as the rotation applies, counting or not, header-only included, and `keep` records the seed (`records::SEEDS`, the custody truth: a rolled-back transaction, a Prove's trial among them, keeps no record) and takes it up. So inner signatures open with every held seed and the chain is a function of held commits alone: no seed fixpoint. A node signs as P only on D's roster and holding its open epoch's seed (`Core::writer`, `signing::signs_for`); a rotation boxes the roster of the line it makes (`Chain::roster_joining`), so it never boxes a node that line removed. At the end of a complete pass of D's sync IDs it re-seals each held head's seed to the roster devices no counting box is tagged for (`custody::reseal_seeds`, header field 16). Every held seed opens boxes to P, stale ones included, found by the signing key a box's epoch name carries, so a box opens whether or not the epoch's height (its name's n) is known here: a rotation answering a fork this node never holds still opens (`Core::account_keys`).
- A newcomer to a line (a device added, or one a joined fork added) gets the seeds its maker holds of the epochs on that line, none of a line beside it (else a node on X's side hands X's made-up device the honest line's seeds).
- Every box a node can open is opened for reading, stale and not-counting ones included; stale still means "do not encrypt under, re-seal or rotate", never "unread". Reading stale boxes reads nothing the node was not boxed. A role commit invalid here (a t rotation at an epoch beside the followed line, a demoted admin's rotation) has its `secrets.0` boxes opened reading-only, sourced from the commit, which keeps them while held (`apply::keys::read_invalid`, again as an opener arrives, `reread_invalid` from `reopen_dependents`; `a_write_under_an_invalid_rotations_epoch_reads_*`).
- Each secret of an epoch (its secret, its roles secret) carries its own pending sources (`EpochInfo::reading`, `roles_reading`): a roles secret from a checked history entry stays held when the pending commit its epoch's secret came from goes, as a rebuild that never saw that commit holds it.
- A thing getting secrets of its own again keeps only the derived epochs the kept record knows (`apply::keys::refresh_independent`): restarting from every current derived epoch left one derived from a P epoch newer than its own rotation current and unboxed, re-sealed by every node to every holder.
- **A thing's epochs follow the side.** A node encrypts a thing t boxed to P (with a holder outside P) only while each current epoch of t is boxed to P at an epoch that counts here and carries every taken revocation its head carries (spec/crypto.md §4 Encrypting under, §9); else it re-keys t at its head, and the history box carries the t epochs it holds established here. So a switch re-keys t and hands the side switched to the t epochs the node's own words there were under, while a node on the thief's side carries no t epoch of the other side, so hands X nothing of it. Rejected: history of every held t epoch (custody alone: a node on X's side holding the honest side's t epochs hands them to X, roles-model `custody` `secrecy`); re-sealing on a switch the seeds of the side left (a seed is per epoch, so it would hand the side switched to every word there, and a switch toward X would hand X the honest side's).

## Merges
- A merge is pending until every fork it names is held: a line is whole when placed, so a fork arriving later never reopens a decision.
- A merge's epoch ID is `BLAKE3("osis v1 merge epoch" || signing key || tips ascending)` (`osis_commit::merge_epoch`), a plain rotation's its commit ID. Rejected: naming every epoch by `[height, signing key]` in structures: the same identity for merges, but every `CommitId`-keyed structure would change type for nothing a merge ID does not give. A certificate boxes the merge seed only to roster devices not seen holding every tip's seed (`custody::derivers`). Who can derive a merge seed: exactly the holders of every tip's seed; a node aware of a removal merges only heads one of which carries it, so the removed node lacks that tip's seed and the merge's.
- Honest forks are owed a merge (`signing::merge_heads`, `rotations_due`): readers outside follow one head only. A merge carries only the join: its seed is `kdf("merge seed", tips' seeds)` (`custody::derive_merges` derives it wherever every tip's seed is held) and its epoch ID a function of its tips (`osis_commit::merge_epoch`), so nodes merging the same heads at once make certificates of one epoch, not another fork (`merges_made_at_once_settle_in_lockstep_*`). A node lacking a tip's seed leaves the merge to one holding it.

## Answers and the cutoff
- Every rotation a node of the account disregards with no rotation on its line a counting rotation rejects, and that no rotation of its own supersedes, is superseded unnamed by its next rotation (`unanswered`), so every reader that followed it is told. Another node's superseding rotation answers nothing by itself: signed at the epoch the disregarded one is signed at, it rejects nothing of it (contested from below), and readers following it would never be told (`a_rotation_superseded_without_a_rejection_is_answered_spec_permissions_5_5`); once per node, as a node's own answer may be so contested too. One answer per line: a reader following the line holds that rejection already; answering every rotation has two sides answer each other's answers for as long as both stay online (roles-model `forks-every-answer`). The line test reads rejections, not supersedes, of every rotation on the line, merges included (a merge is on the lines descending from it and its own, spec/crypto.md §9): a reader following a line through a rejected merge holds that rejection as it does a plain one's (`a_rejected_merge_is_answered_once_spec_permissions_5_5`): a removed node holding the answer's epoch key (an unaware sibling's head boxed to it) signs there accepting its answered fork, which puts that fork on a line through the answer's epoch and ends the rejection; its new rotation is then answered (`a_rotation_undoing_an_answer_is_answered_spec_permissions_5_5`). The answer cannot be undone again: its epoch is not boxed to the removed node.
- A closer ends an epoch for the cutoff only if it descends from it or carries (a counting rotation on its line) the removal of a node the epoch's line names no removal of (`signing::ends`, `Departures`; at a witness every closer ends it). Without that, every write at a head made after its sibling arrived would be nameable only by its own writer, so lost with a gone writer (`the_lowest_heads_node_gone_for_good_leaves_nothing_waiting_spec_permissions_5_4`).
- A rotation is answered (listing or addenda) as it comes to count here, never again as what the node holds changes: a commit of the account is received only once its epoch's chain is held (its key is known then, `apply::examine`), so what the cutoff names was held when the closer came to count. Rejected: answering again whenever what is held changes (the spec's target once): roles-model `epochs` answers once and holds; the every-tick policy's extra words came from the model dating a receipt before its epoch's chain.
- The cutoff compares receipts in exact order (`signing::Received`: receipt ms, then (`ts`, author, commit ID)), the closer's being its minting commit's (a merge's first certificate's), so a commit received in its closer's millisecond is named iff stamped before it (`a_write_received_in_its_closers_millisecond_is_named_by_its_order_spec_permissions_5_5`).
- Re-added nodes: a node off D's roster writes no listing, and its rotations stay due (`signing::listing` returns not answered) until it is on the roster again (its user's switch to a line keeping it, a re-add). A removed node leaves the account (outsider: it answers with addenda, the rotations then answered), so taking an account up (`Engine::hold_account`, from `paired`) notes each head due again (`signing::answer_again`): each head's rotation closes every closed epoch but those descending from it, so its listings name everything it owes, its own commits from before the re-add among them (they stay held when it leaves). Other nodes name nothing for it (the spec's earlier "every commit by a re-added node" in others' listings): its own word suffices, as for any node. Model: roles-model `epochs` (A re-adds T in the settle; control `NoReanswer`). Test: `a_re_added_nodes_write_before_its_re_add_counts_by_its_own_listing_spec_permissions_5_5`.
- Nothing honest is lost by an unnamed head: the epoch rule names commits at void epochs too, so each node's listing answering the rotation that closes them saves its own writes there (`an_honest_write_at_a_removed_nodes_rotation_counts_spec_permissions_5_5`). Only nodes signing at a head write a counting listing, so a removed node gains nothing.

## JudgedEpochs
- What the rule says per account is kept in `ix.epochs` and persisted (`node/epochs`): a standing is a lookup, and an open builds no chain and reads no list. The commits a counting word names are `ix.named`, a row each (`node/named`), so naming one more writes one row whatever the history (`an_accounts_judgment_row_holds_none_of_its_named_history_spec_permissions_5_5`); it holds only held commits (`unindex_commit`), so it is bounded by the store.
- The epoch rule reads the chain as D's roster only at a node holding the account, so taking it up (`Engine::take_up`, pairing) or leaving it (`signing::leave`) judges its epochs again in that transaction (`signing::judge_account_again`); `take_up` sets the account and its seeds together (`Core::member`) before judging and opening what is boxed to it (`flows::take_up_opener`), so what is boxed to the account opens by the roles held as it, as an open and a rebuild open it (`a_paired_node_opens_what_is_boxed_to_its_account_*`); `Core::judged_from` covers only what moves while it is held. The take-up at open skips it: the persisted judgment was made with it held (`a_node_leaving_its_account_judges_its_chain_as_an_outsider_*`, `a_reader_paired_into_the_account_judges_its_chain_as_a_node_*`).
- A standing is one fact per epoch (`EpochStanding::Counts` or `Closed`): which rotations close an epoch is the chain's (`Epochs::closes`, `Core::closes` over the account view), asked only by the addenda fixpoint (`Vouching::saves`). So the judgment and its row are linear in the epochs (`a_rotations_judgment_grows_with_the_rotations_spec_permissions_5_5`); per-epoch closer sets made both quadratic in the rotations.
- `refresh_epochs` takes the accounts due (`ix.epochs_due`, from `note_rotation`; `ix.names_due`: a commit at a closed epoch, or a word naming a held commit, arrived) and judges by `osis_perm::Epochs` only the commits whose standing may have moved (at an epoch whose standing moved; for a rotation whose count moved, what its lists name via `tb/` and what an addendum keyed by it lists, `ix.addenda_keyed`: it joined or left the closers of every epoch it closes, and closers decide nothing else; due, and what listings among them name). So a rotation costs the commits at the epochs it revokes or opens and what addenda keyed by it list, a list the commits it names, a write no chain build (`rotation_judges_its_epochs_once_and_writes_stay_flat`, `a_later_rotation_re_judges_no_revoked_epochs_history`, `a_rotation_lists_its_epoch_only`, `a_listing_named_late_names_again_spec_permissions_5_5`).

## Writing (`flows/signing.rs`)
- Removing or pairing a node is one plain rotation of P carrying the change (`Engine::remove_device`, `Engine::pair`), D re-keyed in it to the roster of the line it makes; a pairing of a node a taken removal removes is made only where the preferred head's line holds each such removal (`account::re_adds`: an addition beside it re-adds nothing), `pair` merging first in the same transaction and refusing (`Misuse`, nothing made) where it cannot merge (a tip's seed not held), as the model's re-adder waits (`a_pairing_waits_for_a_line_carrying_the_nodes_removal_spec_permissions_5_5`, roles-model `a_readd_waits_for_its_line_to_carry_the_removal`, control `ReaddBeside`); thing lists inline only, fit to the smaller of `CHUNK_THRESHOLD` and the list bound (`Core::list_bound`, `MAX_LIST_BYTES`); the plain content left out is the rotator's own listing's, which `signing::write_listing` cuts into as many listings as the list bound takes (`a_rotations_overflow_goes_into_listings_within_the_bound_spec_permissions_5_5`).
- Own cover (spec/permissions.md §5.5): before a plain rotation, `signing::own_covers` writes a snapshot marked as its own cover, signed at the open epoch, of each aspect where a live write of P stamped since the oldest epoch it closes opened stands only through a cover (`retention::snapshot::carried_only` with that bound), read only on aspects holding a snapshot or a forgotten commit stamped since then; its lists name them as own commits. So a forgotten commit needs no ID, and a later rotation writes none for epochs closed already (`a_rotation_names_its_own_cover_of_what_it_forgot_spec_permissions_5_5`; model: roles-model `epochs`, Own cover).
- Carrying (spec/permissions.md §5.5): a snapshot is a word carrying others' writes, so the cutoff applies to what it carries. `snapshot::may_snapshot` waits (`Later`) while a live held body on the aspect is an account commit at a head a counting removal closes (`AccountView::head_revoked`) that no counting word and no cutoff of this node names (`signing::lasts`, the listing `Cutoff`); `account::refresh_epochs` wakes the things of every commit whose standing it re-judges (`replace::standing_moved`). So a write counting only at an unaware sibling's open head shows through no snapshot or own cover once a merge closes the head (`a_snapshot_carries_no_write_only_an_open_head_counts_spec_permissions_5_5`; model: roles-model `epochs` Carrying, control `OpenCarried`). Rejected: judging carried writes at the reader (a snapshot's copies carry no epoch, and a forgotten write's commit is not held); naming an own snapshot only where the cutoff names what it carries (as for listings: its carried writes are mostly forgotten, and a snapshot at an epoch no removal revokes counts unnamed).
- A listing on a thing outside the node closure goes where the node closure holds the thing once written (`signing::listing`: the held things' listings first, the closure settled, then each left alone in a step undone if its thing stays out); the rotation stays due while one waits. The rotation may be what took the thing out: its keys and its holding strong on D were made at an epoch it closes, and count again only once named, so waiting for the thing first never ends (an honest node then never counts its own writes there and releases the thing): `a_listing_brings_back_the_thing_its_epoch_held_spec_permissions_5_5`.
- Every rotation applied is due an answer, a listing or addenda (`ix.rotations_due`, `rd/`), and again when it comes to count (`refresh_epochs`): a merge arriving before the seed its inner signature opens with is read as void (minter unknown) and answered by nothing; without the second answer the non-merger's writes at the heads it closes are never listed.
- An account's rotation wraps at D's sync IDs by the rule for a commit giving read epochs (spec/crypto.md §5; notes/crypto.md), no exception of its own.
- What the chain owes (`rotations_due`, any node of the account, none designated) is decided at the end of a complete pass of D's sync IDs (`Engine::signing_due`): a plain rotation where a disregarded rotation stands unanswered (`unanswered`) or a taken revocation no plain rotation of its own carries (`AccountView::carried_by`) is off the preferred line while no merge can be made; then the merge of every head; then seed re-seals (`custody::reseal_seeds`). A take is owed once per node, as an answer is: the preferred head may move to a line not carrying a revocation the node took (another node's merge beside its taking rotation), and a later rotation made there for another event takes it again in its header, but none is made for it (`a_taken_revocation_is_carried_once_whatever_the_preferred_head_*`). Rejected: keeping the preferred head on a line carrying the taken revocations (a condition on sides in a choice that decides nothing that counts; at first no such head may have a seed the node holds). A removed node's every re-fork costs one answering rotation per node of the account online, never a listing.
- A node names its own listing only where its cutoff names each commit the listing names (`signing::Cutoff`): a listing it wrote while following another side named the thief's later write by that side's closers, and naming it after a switch carried the write in whole. Rejected: never naming own words made on another side (a condition on sides; the claim check covers it and any other way a cutoff moves).
- Pairing carries the rotations with their lists (`Package::lists`), so D's earlier commits count at the new node, and the card and record of every node the chain names (`Core::chain_nodes`: minters, certifiers, devices added or removed, the creation device), which D's required set holds too (`own_piece`): the new node reads each rotation's minter by its card, so a paired node pairs another, its pairer removed or not.
- D's holders are its roster (`Core::root_holders`): a rotation re-keys D to the roster of the line it makes. Leaving (`signing::leave_if_removed`, `kept`): of the held rotations counting here or whose seed is held and whose line stands under its own removals (`AccountView::stands_alone`), no tip's line keeps this node a node (`AccountView::keeps`); D's roster decides nothing. So a node removed on the line it follows stays while another held line keeps it (its user may switch there), and a rotation minted by no node of its line removes no one. A node off D's roster rotates nothing (`signs_for`); a taken revocation of itself keeps it off unless its line re-adds it (`Chain::roster`). A pairing is a rotation (`Engine::pair`); it keeps D's deposit seed.

## Costs
- A pairing is a rotation of P (rare; readers outside see one more rotation, not whom it adds). A merge seed is held by everyone holding all its tips' seeds; for an unaware sibling's tips boxed to X that includes X: the existing unaware-sibling case (spec/permissions.md §5.5), no wider.
- The chain is linear in rotations and removals (`judging_the_roster_grows_with_the_rotations_*`, n and 10n rounds): rotations placed as they become held; each line a persistent set sharing its parents' (`osis_perm` `PSet`, a treap whose shape is a function of its keys, so a merge's union costs the branches' difference); who is a node judged along the lines in one pass (`Chain::standing`); `Chain::hits` (per rotation, the revocations hitting its line from beside) says where a check walks a line. Brute force left: a walk (`stands_walk`) is quadratic in its line, taken only where a hit is; rejection pairs are re-read per placed rotation outside the account (scales with forks, not rotations).
- Open: how storage stays attributable (Tess: release everything, zero; issues/storage-owners.md) for seeds and other accounts' chains.

## Model and differential
roles-model `custody` (core/test/roles-model/README.md, Custody): the thief holds every header and old key, forks, revokes, signs as a made-up key actor, adds, withholds, answers, certifies merges and re-seals; random, lockstep and exhaustive small worlds. Properties on top of `forks`'s: custody is monotone (no secret's custody waits on a judgment); a switch takes; one roster source; bounded rotations (at most one plain rotation per node per event, merges made at once of the same tips are one epoch); the listing-cycle shape opens; merge secrecy (no merge seed made by a node aware of X's removal derivable from X's seeds); certificates are interchangeable. Negative controls each fail: `custody-judged`, `roster-bare`, `merge-random`, `stale-shut`, `merge-carries`. The engine differential `rotation::tests_custody` plays real engines against the model per tick and per honest act, with controls planted in `Core::faults`; it compares t's re-key one way (the engine also re-keys where two of t's epochs are current, which the model's t never is), and checks that P's nodes and R1 hold every t epoch the model does, and once settled every honest t write's epoch. The model seals a t box to P at its maker's heads jointly (`custody::p_seal`), or leaves P out where a head's height is unknown there or a head misses a removal from D it knows (spec/crypto.md §4 Seal: no candidate, or blocked), P then owed a re-seal (`TReseal`); the engine may seal at fewer heads (Seal's anchors), so P's nodes may hold more than the model. The mirror tells a t rotation (its commit is a read keyset's first body) from a re-seal to P (box records naming P), and takes a t rotation's history from the engine's history box. Placing one millisecond's receipts by ID before depth: `an_outsider_places_one_milliseconds_rotations_by_id_before_depth_*` (depth first follows another honest merge than the engine). roles-model `epochs` judges layer 2 (lists, listings, addenda, cutoff, bound, sync IDs) over custody's chain, and osis-perm `epochs_model_agrees` checks `Chain` and `Epochs` against both at every observer and receipt prefix of every world.

## Rejected designs (account signing)
Removal and rotation as one commit (its header, under every sync ID, shows D's dot range and the device count); one account-wide list (shows every sync ID the IDs in others); lists as separate commits after the rotation (P's content reads invalid until they arrive); closing per sync ID (a removed node writes on where P's nodes have not synced); listing every commit (O(history)); later word as addenda only; rotations rejecting siblings at their own epoch (a removed node could void its removal); closing only what a rotation supersedes (a sibling's concurrent epoch boxed to the removed node stays open); merges named without the fork's signature (a removed node names its removal and escapes it); revealing node marks or group signatures; per-node subkeys certified to readers (per-node data in every sync ID). Flat or depth-padded list trees (both show how many things the rotator lists): `LIST_TREE_DEPTH` 32 with random empty subtrees.
