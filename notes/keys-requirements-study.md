# Keys requirements study: which requirement forces the rotation cost
> Summary: Read-only study (issues/keys-requirements-study.md, Tess's ask). Where the key-rotation cost went (about 290 commits, the largest machinery in the system: departures inside holder actors, names, bound seals, cycles, forks, re-seals, establishment, claim walks, keep), which requirement sentence forces each part, and six candidate relaxations with what each deletes, what a user loses, what stays hard and whether the threat model holds. Two recommended as questions for Tess (notes/questions-for-tess.md): unstamped removals (`until`, self-removal) stop being key departures (small, aligns keys with the requirement text); a group seals to a member actor's current keyset and never looks inside it (deletes the largest half of the machinery and three open issues for a stated limit on departures inside members). Not recommended: admin-only rotation, one rotator at a time, eager-only rotation, dropping keep.

Reads: spec/requirements.md, spec/permissions.md §1-§6, §8, spec/crypto.md §3-§5, spec/sync.md §2, §5, notes/core-triage.md §2.5, notes/crypto.md, notes/permissions.md (Keys, Revocation, role flows), notes/epoch-establishment-study.md, notes/roster-names.md, notes/sync.md Limits, the keys-tagged issues, core/test/tla/rotation (ASSUMPTIONS.md, AMBIGUITIES.md, the cfgs; TLC not run). No code or spec changed.

## 1. Requirement sentences and what each forces
| Sentence (spec/requirements.md) | Machinery it forces | Open issues it generates |
|---|---|---|
| Permissions: "Removing a holder never re-encrypts existing content. The holder stops reading new content of an actor once a writer or admin of that actor has written after the removal" | Lazy forced rotation: the covers rule and "encrypting under" (spec/crypto.md §4), the departure walk, history boxes, one commit for removal plus rotation, the cascade over everything the removed party could open (spec/permissions.md §3 Required rotation) | none by itself; it is the premise of the rest |
| Scale: "Direct rosters hold thousands of holders; larger ones nest"; Generality: "Share a set of things through one shared actor"; Decentralization: groupings "nest, may be cyclic" (role cycles allowed by Tess's call, notes/permissions.md) | Departures **inside** holder actors at any depth (`departures::collect`, `Via` chains, classes, cycles and the leave-out rule), names of every thing below the actor, keyset names, names on grant and re-seal boxes, bound seals and the edge `keyset` field, `keeps_out`/`presumed_boxes`, the §4 exception placing inside-holder revokes, `inside_holder_revokes`, `placed_with`, half of Rotation.tla (the group g, `GK`, `GOK`, `GNamesOK`, `Free`, `rk`, `GFork`, `Forge`) | keyset-names-placement-rosters (triage #5, a Confidentiality breach), keyset-name-later-admin-path, write-only-reseal-bound, rotation-chain-cost, most of epoch-establishment-redesign's open cases |
| Decentralization: "Nothing trusts a host's word"; "Every node that can read data verifies ... from what travels with the data"; Untrusted nodes: "knows no role holders" | Established epochs by names (a node uses an epoch only holding what its rotator held), reading-only secrets and their caps, claim sets and steps, the k-round-trip claim walk, stranded heartbeats, re-seal after a complete pass | epoch-establishment-redesign (review), fork-held-by-non-rotators, the deposit-seed residuals |
| Decentralization: "No central or trusted coordinator"; Offline: "Every operation completes with nobody else online" | Forks: boxes keyed per epoch, (n, nonce) order, history boxes over all forks, the re-seal invariant (`forked_reseals_settle`), first step wins at a host | fork-held-by-non-rotators, epoch-derived-same-secret (the 2^62 cap) |
| Permissions: "readers never rotate keys"; Threat model: "Authenticated malicious writers are bounded" (with the decided "write or above rotates", notes/permissions.md Keys) | Per-role epochs, keysets and boxes; per-record role rules on `secrets.0`/`keys.0`; lockout recovery by omission-on-purpose frontiers; the writer-rotated-but-no-step window | repudiation stated limit, write-only-reseal-bound |
| Permissions: "A removal nobody stamps (a timed grant ending, a self-removal, a link expiring) cuts off nothing by itself" (validity) against the spec's choice that `until` and self-removal are key departures (spec/crypto.md §4 Departure) | Expiry names and keys (`Key::expiry`, zero commit ID), clock-judged departures, the `MAX_DRIFT` re-seal guard, two encrypting-under exceptions (own `until`, own self-removal until placed), `expired_after_own_end`, the hear-out before dropping | none open; the clock dependence is where "times decide nothing" had to be written |
| Deletion: "A node that lost access ... yields nothing it can no longer read, unless its removal said to keep a read-only copy" | Lost-access drop and **Keep** (spec/permissions.md §3: ways, least fixpoint, per-copy holder records, late defaults, `until` 0 as "keep"; `rotation/lost.rs`) | none open (d36/d41 closed them) |
| Cost: "a holder that missed k key rotations may need k"; Permissions Limit: "Re-sealing is triggered, never guaranteed" | The claim walk, `ROLE_KEEP` set retention, stranded heartbeats and re-seal triggers | the refused-heartbeat residue (accepted) |

Reading: the sentence that costs most is not any one of these but their product: lazy rotation **x** holders that are actors **x** no trusted host. Lazy rotation means the node that must exclude a party is not the one that removed it, so knowledge of the removal has to travel with the keys (names, establishment); actor holders mean the removal may be inside a holder the node cannot read (names below, keyset names, bound seals, cycles); no trusted host means that knowledge must be verifiable from what travels, in any order, with any subset withheld (frozen coverage, reading-only secrets). Each factor alone is cheap; the inside-holder factor is the one the others multiply.

## 2. Candidate relaxations

### 2.1 A group never looks inside its member actors (nesting depth 0 for confidentiality)
**Rule.** A rotation, grant or re-seal of A seals to a holder actor M at M's newest valid keyset the node holds, for each role, with no judgement of departures inside M. A box to M of a current epoch of A is **stale** when the keyset it names is held invalid, or a held valid `keys.0` of M lists it as superseded (an unknown keyset is not stale). A node that may rotate A MUST NOT encrypt under an epoch of A with a stale box; it rotates first. A's history boxes name A's own departures only (frozen coverage at depth 0, unchanged). The remover still places the removal and every affected actor's new `keys.0` under every label the removed party could read (spec/permissions.md §3), and a holder's `keys.0` still goes under its targets' labels (§4); those certificates are now the only carrier. The remover MAY cascade eagerly through what it may rotate (today's `ensure_rotated`).

**Role cycles** (X and Y holders of each other) need one more rule, because a plain staleness trigger either ping-pongs or leaks (each rotation boxes the other at a keyset the departed party reached through the previous epoch; notes/permissions.md Role cycles): a departure rotation of X leaves out every holder actor Y whose holder closure contains X (graph reachability over the timeline, no epochs or boxes), and re-seals Y once Y's box to X in Y's `secrets.0` names X's post-rotation keyset (X's admin reads that box as a holder of Y). That is today's leave-out with the per-departure judgement replaced by reachability.

**Deletes.** spec/crypto.md §4: Names "and from each thing below A", keyset names, the keyset clause of Met, Inside/Concerns/Attributes for inside departures, Sealer's names for M, the whole "Sealing to a holder actor" block (Keeps out, Sealers' boxes, Free, Bound, `keyset` of an edge); spec/permissions.md §3: the stranded-until-intermediate-rotates sentence, Left out, Role cycles (replaced by the reachability rule), §4 the Exception placing inside-holder revokes, §2 the `keyset`-unchanged clause of self-removals; spec/data-model.md the edge `keyset` field and roster snapshots carrying it; spec/wire.md §7 `names` on boxes and keyset names in history boxes. Code: `rotation/departures.rs` all but `direct_departures` (about 600 lines), `osis_keys::covers` `Via`/`keeps_out`/`MemoryHeldActors`, `rotation/mod.rs` `seal_of`, `named_keysets`, `role_keeps_out`, `holder_keeps_out`, `left_out`, `grantee_left_out`, `fresh_box_epoch`, `epoch_keeps_out`, `left-out` re-seal skipping, `placed_with` of names below, `inside_holder_revokes` in labeling/certs.rs, `Dropped::names`, `Sealers`, the planner's chain walk; the group half of Rotation.tla and instances `MC_c1*`, `MC_c1f*`, `MC_c3*`, `MC_keyset*`, `MC_desc_*` (C2, forks, grants, drops stay). Issues: keyset-names-placement-rosters (nothing below A is named or placed: the Confidentiality sentence "never the entries of rosters they are not part of" holds again), keyset-name-later-admin-path, write-only-reseal-bound, rotation-chain-cost; epoch-establishment-redesign shrinks to the direct-holder model (C2), which Rotation.tla already covers. Roughly 2k lines deleted, a few hundred added (staleness, reachability leave-out, the cycle re-seal condition). It is a rework of the most-reviewed area, not a pure deletion; a TLA check of the two new rules before building is cheap (the direct-holder model plus one holder actor with a forkable keyset: ASSUMPTIONS.md's second-actor sketch, which is also what the full rule needs and never got).

**Loses.** Today, a node that knows of a departure inside M never seals A to a keyset of M made without that knowledge: an honest sibling's ignorant fork of M (study C1) or the departed party's forged keyset of M (C1') is judged as boxing the departed party and M is **left out** of A until M's next knowing rotation (the whole person or team locked out of A's new content meanwhile). Under 2.1 the same node seals to that keyset, and the departed party **reads** A's new content until M's next knowing rotation (the ignorant sibling learns the removal, must rotate M again by depth-0 frozen coverage; the forgery turns invalid wherever the removal arrives) and A's next writer rotates. So the trade is one liveness residual for one confidentiality residual, in the same rare shapes: a concurrent rotation of M by an admin that has not seen the removal (for a person: two devices removing two devices at once; for a team: an admin removing a person while another admin's device rotates the team for a member's device loss), or a withholding host between M's remover and A's writers plus a removed admin of M forging M's `keys.0`. Both heal at M's next knowing rotation; both already exist one level up today (notes/sync.md Limits "One level up", the compaction residue) and at every ignorant boxer ("What an epoch's names bind", notes/crypto.md). Grants of a holder actor after a rotation (C3) become the same residual.

**Stays hard.** Depth-0 frozen coverage and establishment (names of A's own departures, departs mark, reading-only secrets), forks and the re-seal invariant, the complete pass, claim sets and stranding, lockout recovery, the deposit-seed residuals, the cycle rule above. `keys.0` LWW across concurrent rotations of M (two keysets at one n) is picked as today by (n, nonce).

**Threat model.** Holds for direct holders exactly as today. For a party removed from inside a member actor it becomes: cut off once the member's keyset was rotated knowing the removal and the group's writers hold that keyset; an ignorant or forged keyset of the member extends it until the member's next knowing rotation. "Hostile admins are not defended against" already covers the forging party as a current admin of M wherever its removal has not arrived; the withholding host is "trusted for availability only" and here buys the attacker time, not permanence.

**Requirement sentences that change.** Permissions: after "The holder stops reading new content of an actor once a writer or admin of that actor has written after the removal" add "For content it reached through a holder actor M, once M's keys were rotated knowing the removal and a writer or admin of the actor has written holding them. Limit: a keyset of M written without knowledge of the removal (a concurrent rotation of M, or one the removed party forged where the removal has not arrived) keeps it reading until M's next rotation that knows." Confidentiality's roster sentence stops being breached. Cost's removal sentence is unchanged (the cascade is the same, driven by keysets instead of names).

**Depth limit instead of depth 0** (the brief's variant): deletes almost nothing. The cost is in the first level (names, keyset names, bound seals, grant names, the box judgement); depth only adds `Via` chains and the cycle fixpoint, which are a few hundred lines and the one issue rotation-chain-cost. Not worth a requirement change.

### 2.2 Only admins rotate (readers and writers never)
**Rule.** "Who rotates" becomes admin only; "write or above rotates its roles and below" (decided with Tess, notes/permissions.md Keys) is reversed. With it, epochs can be per actor instead of per role (one keyset, one history box, one fork space; boxes stay per (holder, role) since a reader of M must not open M's write box, notes/crypto.md).

**Deletes.** Lockout recovery (spec/permissions.md §3) and omission-on-purpose frontiers (§5.1), the repudiation stated limit, the per-record role rules on `secrets.0` and `keys.0` deltas (§3), the window where a writer rotated but no admin registered the step (notes/sync.md Limits first bullet), about a third of osis-keys' per-role bookkeeping if epochs merge, half the fork sources. write-only-reseal-bound closes (no write-only sealer); fork-held-by-non-rotators narrows to a demoted admin's fork.

**Loses.** A lost device keeps reading every actor its person only writes to (every chat the person is not an admin of) until an admin of each writes; the person can do nothing about it. That is exactly why Tess chose writer rotation. Under 2.1 the person's own device cascades through write; under 2.2 it cannot. Demotion write to read rotates all roles (3 boxes per holder instead of 1, cost only).

**Threat model.** Holds; it strengthens the bound on malicious writers (no writer can lock anyone out). But the honest primary scenario (stolen device, dogfood chat with one admin) gets worse, so not recommended. Requirement sentence: the "stops reading new content once a writer or admin ... has written" would become "once an admin has written".

### 2.3 Only the remover rotates (eager instead of lazy)
**Rule.** The remover rotates everything the removed party could open, at removal time; nobody else rotates for a departure.

**Deletes.** In principle the covers rule, names, establishment and the departure walk at every node but the remover. In practice nothing: the remover often cannot rotate what the removed party read (a device of M removed by M's admin, where M only reads A: no node of M may rotate A), so A's writers must act, and must learn why, which is the whole machinery again; and "Every operation completes with nobody else online" plus unreachable labels mean the remover's eager cascade is at best what `ensure_rotated` does today (a MAY). Lazy rotation is forced by "a holder of A rotates A" plus nesting; the remover-only rule is the special case where the remover holds every right, which 2.1 already gets by keyset staleness. Rejected.

### 2.4 One rotator per actor at a time; forks by a simple rule
**Rule.** A convention that one node rotates an actor at a time cannot be kept without a coordinator ("No central or trusted coordinator"; two offline admins each removing a holder must both succeed), so forks stay; the question is only how they are resolved. Today's rule is already the simple one: keep every fork's secret, the next rotation's history box carries all, (n, nonce) orders, first step wins at a host. What could go is the re-seal invariant ("every holder holds every current epoch any node holds", `forked_reseals_settle`, the re-seal scan over forks): replace with "content under a fork is readable to those lacking its secret once a node holding both rotates".

**Deletes.** The fork part of `reseal_scan` and its fixpoint tests, a few hundred lines, 20 of the 290 commits' worth of history. **Loses.** Content written under the losing fork stays unreadable to holders lacking it until a merging rotation; if no node holds both (fork-held-by-non-rotators), for good. A liveness loss for a small deletion; not recommended.

### 2.5 Timed ends (`until`, link expiry) are not key departures; a writer finalizes them
Tess agreed to the timed-share part. Self-removal stays as is: it is not clock-based.

**Self-removal today.** An explicit commit the leaver writes and stamps, with no rotation in it (spec/permissions.md §2, §3). Any node holding it knows the departure; the next node that may rotate before encrypting rotates (writers: read and write; admins: all three; readers never; spec/permissions.md §3 Who rotates). No clock in that path but two small things: exception 3 of Encrypting under (the leaver's own uploads not held back until its self-removal is placed; Placed is about hosts, not time), and the generic "roster at now" (`roles_now`, `lower_horizon`): a self-removal stamped ahead of the node's clock (at most `MAX_DRIFT`) counts once its `ts` passes, as every edge write does. So self-removal stays a normal departure; "unstamped" in requirements means only that it audits nothing, not that it is timed.

**Rule.** spec/crypto.md §4 Departure drops "M's edge passing its `until`". A **finalize** is a plain removal of an edge already past its `until` at the removal's `ts`:
- Any node with write on T may write it for an edge of role at most its own (a writer finalizes read and write edges; a timed admin edge needs an admin, who alone rotates admin). It carries the rotation of the roles the edge held, in one commit, like any removal.
- A writer that may rotate T and holds an edge on T past its `until` by its own (HLC-corrected) clock, with no finalize of it held, MUST write the finalize before its next commit encrypting under T (or in it).
- Validity, at every node from held data only: the author held write (or admin) at the `ts`, and the edge was past its `until` at the `ts` on the timeline (renewals ordered before included). Else the edge write does not apply; its rotation still does (a writer may always rotate), and the holder it left out is re-sealed as today.
- It is no explicit write: it audits nothing, carries no frontier, and says keep or erase as the expiry would (the default in force at the expiry key), so the guest's own timestamps still decide its writes (requirement unchanged) and the guest still hears out (§2).
- Keys judge the roster with every held applied write in effect and `until` not applied: an expired, unfinalized guest stays a holder for keys until a finalize. Links keep §8's sharer removal (it audits late invite grants as L); a writer's finalize of L's edge only rotates.

**Holes checked.** A writer removing an unexpired edge: the edge write is invalid (writer lacks admin, edge alive at its `ts`). A dishonest writer finalizing early: its `ts` must be at least `until`, and a commit more than `MAX_DRIFT` ahead of the receiver stays pending, so at most `MAX_DRIFT` early, as a fast clock does today; a writer can already rotate leaving anyone out (Lockout recovery), so no new power. A renewal raced by a finalize: whichever the timeline orders first decides; a guest left out by a void finalize is re-sealed. Concurrent finalizes fork, as concurrent expiry rotations do today.

**Deletes.** Expiry names and keys (`Key::expiry`, `osis_keys::EXPIRY`, "an expiry name is met wherever read"), the clock in the departure rule (`covers::expired`), the "roster at now" in departures (`roles_now`, `lower_horizon`, the departures cache's clock horizon), the `MAX_DRIFT` re-seal guard, exception 2 (own `until`: an expired node may not rotate, so the rule does not bind it) and `expired_after_own_end`, the clock-churn test shapes. Keys then read no clock: the clock decides only when a writer chooses to finalize, and the finalize is an ordinary stamped departure. Adds: the finalize authority in §5.1 step 2 and the MUST above. Stays: self-removal (exception 3 included), lost access by `until` (`arm_untils`), link §8.

**Loses.** Content written under keys the guest holds after `until` only by a writer whose clock is behind (up to its lag; the same as today) or one that does not follow the rule (it could leak anyway). Readers write no content, so a group with no writer online exposes nothing new. A guest's own writes past `until` are already invalid.

**Fallback** (Tess: fine if any-writer runs into trouble): only a node with admin on T finalizes, as links do; no new authority, but the guest reads new content until an admin device is online after `until`, while writers keep writing under the old keys. Recommended: any-writer (sound per the checks above; the new authority is one §5.1 case).

**Threat model.** Holds; timed guests and expired links are cooperative by construction (a stolen device is removed explicitly). Requirement sentence (Permissions, "A removal nobody stamps"): add "a timed grant's end is finalized by the next writer, whose removal rotates".

### 2.6 Drop keep (no read-only copy after removal)
**Rule.** Every removal erases; Deletion's "unless its removal said to keep a read-only copy" goes.
**Deletes.** spec/permissions.md §3 Keep (ways, least fixpoint, per-copy records, late defaults, `until` 0 as "keep", the `keep` default in `required.0^`, the link client writing it), `rotation/lost.rs`'s ways and keeps (about 400 of 721 lines), d36/d41's rounds. **Loses.** "You left the group but keep your history" has to be an app export before leaving. Tess asked for keep (chat design); it is self-contained and generates no open issue, so not recommended now; listed because it is a fifth of the rotation crate.

### 2.7 Not candidates
- **"Nothing trusts a host's word"**: the alternative (hosts told who may read) un-blinds hosts and breaks the Untrusted-nodes section whole. Claim sets, steps, the k-walk and establishment are its price and converged.
- **"Readers never rotate"**: already the relaxation; its residuals (a reader member keeps wrapping to a lost device until a writer writes; fork-held-by-non-rotators) are stated.
- **"Never re-encrypts existing content"**: re-encrypting on removal is O(content) against Cost's "proportional to the rosters"; and history boxes already give a removed party everything from before its removal (a stated Limit).
- **History-less grant** (issues/history-less-grant.md) is a feature, not a relaxation; it adds to 2.1 nothing and conflicts with nothing here.

## 3. Recommendation
Two questions for Tess, in notes/questions-for-tess.md, simplest first:
1. **2.5**: `until` stops being a key departure; any writer about to write after it finalizes the edge with a removal that rotates. Self-removal unchanged. Tess agreed to the direction; the refined rule is back with her. Small deletion, removes the clock from keys.
2. **2.1**: a group seals to a member actor's current keyset and never looks inside it; departures inside members are covered as well as the member's own keyset is, with the ignorant-fork and forged-keyset shapes as a stated limit (today they lock the member out instead). Deletes the largest half of the machinery and three open issues, closes the roster-privacy breach by construction, and keeps every direct-holder guarantee. Needs a short TLA check of the staleness trigger and the cycle leave-out before building.

Not recommended: 2.2 (Tess decided it the other way for the stolen-device case, and 2.1 depends on writers rotating), 2.3 (deletes nothing), 2.4 (a liveness loss for little), 2.6 (a feature Tess asked for, self-contained). If 2.1 is declined, the open work stays as notes/core-triage.md §2.5 says: the redesign's review and roster-names option 2.
