# Keys requirements study: relaxations considered
> Summary: Which requirement forces which part of the key-rotation machinery, and the relaxations weighed against them. Taken and now design: a timed end is finalized by a writer, not a key departure (notes/permissions.md); a group never looks inside its member actors, with no key cycles (notes/crypto.md, Holder actors at depth 0). Rejected, with why: admin-only rotation, remover-only rotation, one rotator at a time, dropping keep, a depth limit instead of depth 0. Also the cycle rules that failed in Depth0.tla before rule 4, so nobody tries them again.

## What forces the cost
The costly sentence is no single requirement but a product: lazy rotation (spec/requirements.md Permissions: removal never re-encrypts, the holder stops reading once a writer or admin has written) x holders that are actors (Scale, Generality: share through one actor; nesting and cycles allowed) x no trusted host (Decentralization: nothing trusts a host's word). Lazy rotation means the node that must exclude a party is not the one that removed it, so knowledge of the removal travels with the keys (names, establishment); actor holders mean the removal may be inside a holder the node cannot read; no trusted host means that knowledge must be verifiable from what travels, in any order, with any subset withheld. Each factor alone is cheap; the inside-holder factor is the one the others multiply, which is why depth 0 was the relaxation taken. Other sentences and their machinery: "no coordinator" and "works offline" force forks (boxes per epoch, (n, nonce) order, history boxes over all forks, the re-seal invariant); "readers never rotate" and bounded malicious writers force per-role epochs and the lockout case; keep after removal forces the lost-access ways (`rotation/lost.rs`); "a holder that missed k rotations may need k" forces the claim walk and stranded heartbeats.

## Rejected relaxations
- **Only admins rotate.** Would allow per-actor epochs and delete the lockout case, but a lost device keeps reading every actor its account only writes to (every chat it is not an admin of) until an admin of each writes, and the account can do nothing. That is why Tess chose "write or above rotates its roles and below" (notes/permissions.md Keys); depth 0 also relies on writers cascading.
- **Only the remover rotates (eager).** Deletes nothing in practice: the remover often cannot rotate what the removed party read (a device removed from M by M's admin, where M only reads A: no node of M may rotate A), so A's writers must act and learn why, which is the whole machinery again. The remover's eager cascade is already a MAY (`ensure_rotated`).
- **One rotator per actor at a time.** Cannot be kept without a coordinator; forks stay. Dropping only the re-seal invariant (every holder holds every current epoch any node holds) saves a few hundred lines but leaves content under a losing fork unreadable to holders lacking it until a merging rotation, for good if no node holds both.
- **Drop keep** (every removal erases). Deletes about 400 of `rotation/lost.rs`'s 721 lines, but Tess asked for "you left the group but keep your history" (chat), and it is self-contained.
- **A depth limit instead of depth 0.** Deletes almost nothing: the cost was in the first level (names below the actor, keyset names, bound seals, grant names, the box judgement); depth only added chains and a cycle fixpoint.
- Not candidates: telling hosts who may read (un-blinds hosts, breaks the untrusted-nodes requirements); re-encrypting on removal (O(content), against Cost; history boxes already give a removed party everything from before).

## Depth 0 trade, as weighed
Looking inside members locked M out of A while a departure inside M was known and M's keyset was an ignorant or forged fork. Depth 0 instead seals to that keyset, so the departed party reads A's new content until M's next knowing rotation (a confidentiality residual for a liveness one, in the same rare shapes: a concurrent rotation of M by an admin that has not seen the removal, or a withholding host plus a removed admin of M forging M's `keys.0`). Both heal at M's next knowing rotation, and the same residual already existed one level up. Stated in spec/requirements.md Permissions.

## Timed ends, as checked
A finalize is a plain removal of an edge already past its `until` at the removal's `ts`, by any node with write for an edge of role at most its own, carrying the rotation (design: notes/permissions.md, A timed end is finalized). Holes checked: a writer removing an unexpired edge (invalid: no admin, edge alive at its `ts`); a dishonest writer finalizing early (at most `MAX_DRIFT`, as a fast clock does; a writer can already rotate leaving anyone out, so no new power); a renewal raced by a finalize (the timeline order decides; a guest left out by a void finalize is re-sealed); concurrent finalizes fork, as concurrent rotations do. Self-removal stays a normal departure: it is a stamped commit, not clock-based; "unstamped" in the requirements means only that it audits nothing. Fallback Tess accepts if any-writer runs into trouble: only admins finalize (the guest then reads new content until an admin node is online after `until`).

## Cycle rules that failed (Depth0.tla, before rule 4)
In a cycle, X's new secret is safe in Y's keyset k only if no box from k back to X's old keysets is ever added, and re-seals are added by nodes that may not know X rotated. Every rule below that judged boxes failed:
- Leave the other out on a direct-departure rotation; re-seal once the other's keyset boxes back at the current keyset: staleness rotations ping-pong (`Settles`); a departure below a cycle leaks (a staleness rotation); concurrent departures on both sides leave each out of the other for good.
- Every rotation leaves the other out; re-seal when the keyset is unreachable from superseded ones over held boxes: leaks (`NoLeakDirect`), since a check on what is held now cannot see a later ignorant re-seal.
- **Fresh** (seal cycle member Y at k only when every box of k to X names a current keyset of X, and no superseded epoch of X boxes Y at k): passes every single-admin instance, fails with a second admin of Y (`D0_cyc_2adm_fresh`). Counting only boxes in k's own rotation commit closes that, but a left-out pair then rejoins only by rotation, and rejoin rotations ping-pong and chase round a 3-cycle.
- A rotation trigger for a keyset tainted by a superseded epoch, or judging only "dirty" epochs: ping-pongs or misses chains longer than 2.

Rule 4 (no key cycles: the earlier grant in an agreed order gets keys, the later none) passes every instance with every grant visible, including the second-admin one. Tess left the cycle rule to the agents, with model-checking first and, as fallback, accepting a leak in cycles until the next knowing re-key; nested shared tags can be one-way. A grant the sealer does not hold leaks (`D0_nc_hide`): plain rule 4 leaks until every admin of Y holds the grant and re-keys (a third party's sync, unbounded under a withholding host); with taint (4b) the leak stops once the writer holds the grants and the cycle's commits, at the cost of one extra rotation per race while a grant stays hidden (`D0_nc_hide_taint_lean` passes with one blind seal; unbounded blind seals churn until the grant arrives). Refining taint to fire only on superseded keysets would stop the churn but is the box-judging family that failed above.

Not modelled: grant removal (an earlier grant removed makes a keyless one keyed: a re-seal, as a new grant; the common case with first admins, spec/crypto.md §4 Key access); the agreed order itself (assumed total, known with the grants, consistent with causality); write and admin roles. If the agreed order is ever not causal (e.g. by claimed time), implicit first-admin edges need an explicit "always keyed" exception.
