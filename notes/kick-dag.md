# Kick frontiers as a hash DAG per aspect
> Summary: Tess's idea checked: every permanent write names the newest commits (any writer's) the writer held on that aspect; a kick names the demoter's newest ones per aspect; a write is covered iff reachable from them. It works and is the cleanest forgery argument of the three designs (covered = an ancestor of a commit the demoter held, so nothing made after the kick is ever covered, no counters, no contiguity, no `ts`), and its kick cost is one entry per aspect whatever the launch pattern, where per-session heads (notes/kick-cost.md C) degrade to one per message for a CLI that opens a session per call. Needed on top of the bare idea: writers extend only commits whose whole ancestry they hold (else a withheld pre-minted stash becomes valid after the kick), and the demoter lists by ID the received commits whose ancestry it held incomplete. Costs: about 35 B on every permanent write forever, a walk over the aspect's history once per kick per node with a stored result and resume points, and a node that applies the kick before holding its heads un-merges the member's history and merges it back when they arrive. Permanent aspects only, like C. Build about 1800 lines, half tests, risk medium-high; C is 900 and low-medium. Recommendation: build the DAG; it is what the requirement asks for, and C's build would be replaced by it.

## 1. Rule
- **Scope.** Permanent deltas only (spec/commits.md §5: keep-at-least forever, no keep-at-most, no expiry, no fixed retention), where every node keeps every header forever (purge leaves a lasting header; session-sources step 6 must keep permanent headers, F3's clause). Everything else stays by ID, left out when `droppable_here`. On compacting aspects a chain would pass through forgotten headers that new devices never fetch (frontier-study §6): the same partition as C.
- **Parents.** The header's `Aspect` entry gains `parents: [commit_id*]`, written on a permanent delta: the **heads** of what the writer holds on that `(tid, ak)` from any writer, where a held commit counts only when it is **complete**: it has a permanent delta on the aspect and each of its parents is held and complete (recursively; a root has no parents). Heads are the complete commits no held complete commit names. Usually one; several after concurrent writes nobody has merged. A writer's own previous commit is always an ancestor. Cap `MAX_PARENTS` (8): a resource bound (the walk is O(commits x parents); at 0 nothing links and every kick is by ID; at infinity a hostile header makes the walk quadratic); above it the writer names the newest 8 by `(ts, ID)` and the rest stay heads until someone merges them: an extra 34 B frontier entry each, no correctness change. A receiver checks nothing at decode; a parent is judged when held: a held parent without a permanent delta on the aspect is a dead end for good (final), an unheld one is unknown (not final).
- **Frontier.** `frontier` (spec/data-model.md §9) gains `1 heads: set<struct { 0 tid, 1 ak, 2 commits: set<blob> }>`; `commits` stays. For each aspect in question on which a demoted node has a permanent delta, A lists as heads the heads among the commits it had received by T that are complete within that set (`Meta::received`; a backdated revoke computes them within its cutoff). A received commit of X on the aspect that is not in that complete set (its ancestry was incomplete at A: in transit, or a dead end X planted) goes in `commits` by ID, as do receipts-only commits (`r/` keeps no parents), X's snapshots there (their retention is `writer` with relative bounds, never permanent, so they are not DAG nodes and may be forgotten), role commits, requests and everything on other aspects, as today. A MAY still leave anything out on purpose (repudiation, §5.1).
- **Covered.** A permanent delta of X on `(tid, ak)` is covered iff its commit's ID is in `commits`, or its commit is reachable (reflexively) from a head of that aspect through parents over held headers. A chain broken at an unheld parent leaves what is behind it **uncovered for now**: invalid, judged again when the parent arrives. Deterministic: a function of held headers and the frontier; the closure from the heads is fixed by their signed headers, so once every parent in it is held or a final dead end, the reached set is final and never grows.
- The demotion commit cannot carry the heads as its own parents (Tiller's guess): a role commit has no entry on content aspects and the heads are per aspect and as of T; the frontier field carries them, and the ledger carries the frontier as it does now.

## 2. Hostile writers
- **Nothing made after T is ever covered.** Every covered commit is an ancestor of a commit A held complete at T, and every ancestor of a complete commit was held by A. So the covered set is exactly a subset of what A received: the ID list, compressed. A re-signed commit on used counters is a new ID, unreachable: invalid everywhere at once, also at a new device that lacks the original (C's hiding-host limit, where the forgery reads until the original arrives, does not exist here). Equivocation (two commits with the same parents): both are DAG nodes; one reachable, or both, or neither, each by receipt; counter overlap voids as today, unrelated.
- **Fake parents** (an ID that is no commit, or a commit X never held): X's commit is incomplete at everyone forever. Honest writers never extend it (parents are complete heads), so it is never reached from a head; A lists it by ID if A received it. Its descendants from X are incomplete too: X's branch after the dead end is X's to lose. Naming a real commit X never held is harmless: it is a true commit, held or awaited like any.
- **Withheld commits.** Without the completeness rule the idea fails: X pre-mints f (or a chain f1 <- f2 <- ...), publishes c naming f, and after the kick releases the stash one at a time, each reading as a pre-kick write forever, on data nobody can purge. With the rule, c is incomplete at everyone, nobody builds on it, A lists c by ID (received) and f stays unreachable: invalid. So a write that existed before the kick but reached A late is **invalid**, as the requirement says (edits the revoker had not received are lost until re-grant), and a received commit that vouches for it does not help. Honest in-transit commits (p on a host A had not synced, c received) have the same shape: c by ID, p invalid, a few entries at most, and X's own sync re-uploads what a label lacks, so honest chains do not stay broken.
- **Side branches.** A private branch is unreachable until a received commit names it, and such a commit is incomplete until the branch arrives. Withholding never pays.
- **Receipt gaps are the same at both designs.** A hostile X can make every one of its commits incomplete (one fake parent each, 32 B) and force the ID fallback for its whole history, as under C it can leave a counter gap. The cap backstop of kick-cost §4 applies unchanged: past `MAX_LIST_BYTES`, leave out what was received last and report.
- **Hiding host.** A host that hides one header hides the coverage of that commit's ancestors (older writes, of any author, behind it) at nodes syncing only it, until another holder uploads it (permanent bodies are must-hold at every holder). Hiding-host class, already accepted; new is that a *held* older write can read invalid for a while, where today a hidden commit is only absent.

## 3. Cost
- **Header.** 35 B per permanent write with one parent (array head, 2 B CBOR head + 32 per parent), kept wherever the write is kept: about 12% on a 300 B message. Passes Tess's growth rule (growth.md: a write that adds permanent data anyway may carry more). Nothing on other writes; writers MAY set parents on non-permanent deltas (unused; harmless, they go with the body).
- **Frontier.** Per permanent aspect in question: one head (34 B), a few after unmerged concurrency, plus the in-transit tail by ID (honest: about 0). Independent of messages, sessions, launches and devices: a kick of 3 devices lists one head set per aspect. C lists one entry per (aspect, session that wrote): for a CLI opening a session per call, or a phone killed between messages, that is one per message, which fails the requirement (spec/requirements.md Cost: one entry per aspect, never to history) at exactly the pattern Tess expects.
- **Walk.** Reachability from the heads visits the aspect's whole history once per (kick, aspect, node): nearly every commit is an ancestor of the head. 10^6 header lookups, seconds, below the un-merge a kick already costs (chat-cases item 32: re-reading the year's aspect). It needs an index: per commit its parents (in meta); per aspect the writer's heads with completeness (a fixpoint on arrival: `waiting_on: parent -> children`, like pending parts); per (demotion, aspect) the stored result, compactly as "walked over commits received by W" plus the **unreached** set (small: in-transit and post-kick commits) plus resume points (unheld parents the walk stopped at; when one arrives the walk continues and X's deltas on the aspect are judged again). `covers` is then a set lookup. C needs none of this: `covers` reads the header's session and `hi`.
- **Order.** A node that applies the kick before it holds the heads (another host's log, a backdated revoke) can reach nothing: X's whole history on the aspect is uncovered for now, un-merged, and merged back as the heads arrive: two rebuilds of a long aspect instead of one. Treating it as valid until the heads arrive is not an option (a hiding host would keep it so). Usually the heads are older than the kick and arrive first.
- **Finality for `removed_author`** (gates.rs: a missing part of a cut author need not be waited for): "the frontier omits it" becomes "the closure from the heads is final and misses it"; a dead end X planted keeps its own commits with missing parts pending instead of applied-invalid, X's loss only.
- **Partial history.** On a permanent aspect a syncing node fetches every body (must-hold forever), so the closure completes after one complete pass; a fresh device writing mid-fetch names the heads of what it holds complete (old ones, or none): a side branch the next writer merges, nothing lost, one extra head meanwhile. Design E's per-year aspects are separate DAGs: a kicker that never synced an old year has no closure there and lists nothing, which invalidates that year's writes of X everywhere: today's rule and requirement (sync or receipts-read before revoking, §5.3), not new. Receipts-only reads give IDs unless `r/` also records parents (35 B per verified header of a chat the admin does not hold); then heads work there too, since a complete pass verified every header. A thin client writes through its node.
- **Snapshots** on a permanent aspect drop nothing (keep-at-least forever) and are not permanent themselves: not DAG nodes, not parents, listed by ID as today (few; their headers may be forgotten without breaking a chain, which is why they are kept out). A purged permanent delta's lasting header keeps the chain intact.

## 4. Fit
- **Whole commits** (notes/whole-commits.md, being written): parents sit in each `Aspect` entry, so a commit on several permanent aspects is a node in each DAG and is covered per aspect; links never cross things, so a node syncing one thing walks its DAG alone (frontier-study §6's per-session chain across aspects was unwalkable for that reason). Unchanged and worth a line there: a commit with a delta on a thing the demoter does not hold is still listed by ID whole (`part_missing`), under any design, so a message bundled with a write on something admins do not hold stays O(history) at a kick.
- **Sessions and counters** stay for dots, overlap voiding and snapshot `Frontiers`; `ix.ranges` stays. The DAG is a second structure, in headers, for demotion coverage only. C reuses counters and adds no structure, at the price of the contiguity rule.
- **Mixed aspects** need no fallback: each delta is judged by its own header; a non-permanent delta on the aspect is never a DAG node and stays by ID. C needs every delta of X on the aspect permanent.
- **Ephemeral** commits: never permanent, never nodes, all invalid at a demotion as today.
- **Imports**: a delta carrying permanent origin retention is permanent, so an import is a DAG node on the copy's aspect (its parents: the copy's heads, usually none) and may be a parent; origins on the moved thing are on another aspect and never parents. **Moves** change nothing else.
- **Deleted**: the ID list on permanent deltas, `droppable_here`'s part there (it already never drops them), C's per-session heads (unbuilt). **Non-permanent aspects and issues/frontier-clobbers-history.md**: no help; its open item is the permanent cost, which this closes as C would; the "also listed" tail (missing parts, expired writes) stays under both.

## 5. Against C (notes/kick-cost.md)
| | C: per-session counter heads | DAG |
|---|---|---|
| header | nothing | 35 B per permanent write, forever |
| kick entry | per (aspect, session that wrote): per message for a session-per-call CLI | per aspect, plus unmerged concurrency |
| `covers` | O(1) from the header | set lookup after an O(history) walk per kick, with index and resume |
| forgery argument | counters under a contiguous `ctr`, headers never forgotten, overlap voids; hiding-host re-sign limit | ancestors of a held commit; no limit |
| hostile fallback | everything above a gap by ID | received incomplete commits by ID |
| subtle rule | `ctr` is contiguous receipt, not the highest | writers extend only complete commits |
| order | judged at once | uncovered until heads and chain are held |
| build | about 900 lines, low-medium | about 1800 lines, medium-high |

## 6. Flaws, plainly
- The bare idea ("each commit names what it supersedes, a frontier names a commit") has the withheld-stash hole; the completeness rule closes it and is cheap, but it is a rule every writer must follow and a reviewer must look for (a writer that merges an incomplete commit re-opens the hole for everything behind it).
- The walk and its resume machinery are the real cost: a third coverage mechanism beside IDs and snapshots, with pending semantics in the gate. C has none.
- Transient invalidity on out-of-order delivery, expensive on long aspects.
- Receipts-only kicks need `r/` to grow parents, or they stay O(history).

## 7. Build
Prerequisites: F3's "never forget a permanent header" clause (moot until session-sources step 6). Steps, each gated alone: (1) wire and commit: `Aspect.parents`, validate, builder, vectors, spec wire.md §4, commits.md §1 (about 150); (2) engine writer: parents per commit in meta, completeness and heads per aspect, set on write (about 250); (3) schema and perm: `frontier.heads`, `Frontier { commits, heads }`, `covers(id, aspect)` through a coverage oracle the engine supplies, memo keyed by aspect (about 150); (4) engine frontier: heads as of T, incomplete tail by ID, cap backstop (about 120); (5) coverage walk: stored result, resume on arrival, re-judge, `removed_author` finality (about 300); (6) spec permissions.md §5.1-5.2, data-model.md §9, growth row, notes (about 80); tests about 750: forgery, withheld stash, fake parents, equivocation fork, concurrent heads and merge, kick-before-heads order, backdated cutoff, mixed aspect, whole-commit on two aspects, new device alike (differential: random DAG histories with kicks, a fresh node against a full one), `scale::long_aspect` kick at 10^5 measuring KB and walk time, roles-model `content.rs` with parents. About 1800 lines.

## 8. For Tess
Your idea works, and it is the cleanest of the three: a kick names the newest message(s) the kicker had, a message counts iff the chain of "I saw these" links from there reaches it, and nothing made after the kick can ever be reached. One rule has to be added: a writer only builds on messages whose whole past it holds, or a kicked member could publish a stash of pre-written messages afterwards; with it, late and withheld messages are invalid, as the requirements say. It costs about 35 bytes on every keep-forever message, and every device walks the chain once per kick (about what a kick already costs it) and needs bookkeeping to pick the walk up when links arrive out of order; a device that gets the kick before the messages it names treats the member's history as invalid until they arrive. The per-launch version costs nothing on messages and no walk, but lists one entry per app launch, which for a CLI is one per message, so it does not really meet the requirement. I would build the DAG; it is twice the per-launch build and would replace it anyway.
