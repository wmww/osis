# Engine: lifetime judgment
> Summary: How the engine judges thing lifetimes and releases (`retention/lifetimes.rs`; the writer's checks; design issues/thing-lifetimes.md, oracle held-model `lifetimes`): known/ended/unknown, the walk, the work list and `pending` as local rows, what queues, the release request as a purge of the creation, the keep rule and forgetting at the request's expiry, evidence under the labels of what needs it, the engine API and node-sync's trigger when heard out.

## Principle
A walk reads only the current held state, so a release never rests on a queue or a cache: a missed queue only leaks. The kept state is three local row sets, `lw/` (work: things a change touched), `lh/` (held: their walk read a thing not heard out) and `lp/` (pending: judged unreachable, not ended here: right not held, or its release request not applied yet).

## Reading
- Status (`lifetimes::status`): ended (`Core::ended`, or released earlier in the run), known (`exists`, a minting held, not a kept copy `forget::copy_of`), else unknown.
- List: `retainers^.0` read whole as `set<tid>`; absent or not a set of tids: standalone (kept).
- References: the reference index (notes/engine-refs.md): a durable row from R to X other than `retainers^.0` (`refs::references`); listers: X's `retainers^.0` rows (`refs::listers`).
- The walk (`walk`) as held-model `walk`: up the list through known retainers referencing what is below them; unknown or standalone: kept. A pending thing is walked through, never read as failed: `pending` is stale for a thing the same run made reachable (held-model `a_stale_pending_thing_is_walked_through`).

## Queueing
Only a known thing is queued; a thing turning ended or unknown loses its `lw/`, `lh/` and `lp/` rows, so a forgotten thing leaves none.
- `refs::note`: a durable reference row appearing or going queues its target; any join or rebuild of `retainers^.0` queues its thing (an empty list moves no row).
- `atomic::learn_record`, `end::end_ended` (ended or fallen), `forget::forget` (lost): the thing and its listers.
- `update_timelines` sets `Core::lifetimes_rights` (also set at open): the next judgment re-judges every pending thing (a right may have come). Brute force: O(pending) per role timeline change.
- Listers are read from `node/refs to` filtered by AK: O(rows naming the thing), not O(listers). Paid when a thing becomes known, ended or forgotten, and when its judgment flips (`below`).
- An index rebuild (`refs::write_all`) queues every referenced target and every list: rare, brute force.

## The release request
- A commit of `released.0` deltas alone (`timestamp`, fixed retention `author_or_admin`, no history), one per thing, no purge field, `expires = wall_ms(ts) + ROLE_KEEP` (`Header::validate`, `osis_commit::retention::release_expires`; `write_expires` gives it). Per-thing deltas, so an evidence copy names only its own thing.
- **One ending path**: `Header::requests()` reads it as a purge item on `created.0` of each such thing; every reader of a request (`purge.rs`: `names`, `note_requests`, `apply_request`, rejudging, `requests_resting_on`; `end::per_minting`; `View::purges`, fetch order, addenda's plain commits) goes through it. So validity is the purge right over each minting (`holds_right`, the ending's cut included), the minting goes value-less, `end_thing` ends the thing; a late minting or stale copy ends on arrival while the header is held.
- Differences from a purge, both erasure's alone: it names every minting whatever its `expires` (`names`: a purge in an expiring commit reaches only deltas expiring no later), and `purge_one` writes no lasting-header mark for it.
- Keep: an ending by request keeps the record, value-less creation, `roles.0` and the request parts (`end::judges_ending`: `created.0`, `roles.0`, `ENDING_REQUESTS`) while the request is held. Evidence for dependents: `ending_evidence` and node-sync's `ended_certificate` carry `ENDING_REQUESTS` parts, purge and release alike.

## Keep and forget (`end::keeps`, `end::forget_released`)
One rule for every ending kind (purge, expiry, release): an ended thing keeps its record, value-less mintings, `roles.0` and request parts while a request ending it is in force (a purge for good, a release until its `expires`) or a held thing **needs** it (`needs_evidence`: a dependent, or a durable reference-index row from another thing, `retainers^.0` included, `group.0` not: `needs_from`). Otherwise it keeps nothing; a thing ended by release alone (`release_state`: every minting dropped by a release, none by a purge or its own expiry) whose requests are all past `expires` is then forgotten whole in the same transaction:
- commits whose every part is on it or on a thing with no held record: `unindex_commit` (role commits and the requests too), pushed to `memory.dropped` as `MemoryDrop::Gone`, so node-sync judges their entries must-not-hold (deleted where it may) and forgets them once gone (`sweep_forgotten`); a commit with a part on a held thing keeps its header, its dropped parts on the thing `reopen`ed (missing, never received, not fetched);
- its per-aspect rows (FORGOTTEN, PURGES, PURGES_NAMED, LEFTOVER, ...) and `leaving` rows, and the FORGOTTEN rows unindexing a whole commit writes for a thing forgotten earlier whose header it kept.
- Re-judged (`end_ended`): when a `released.0` part goes value-less (the request's expiry, or a late request arriving past it), and `Core::ended_due` (a durable reference row to an ended thing went, `refs::note`). The ups cascade of `end_thing` covers dependents.
- `purge::lasting` reads no drop on a released thing as lasting (erasure's alone); `Engine::may_last_on` likewise for node-sync.
- `group.0` is no need: a group contains its members, readers show nothing for an unknown member, and a release never writes on the group, so a released member still listed is forgotten at its request's expiry. It still keeps a member alive for the judgment.
- node-sync keeps a forgotten commit's header in `memory.forgotten` while entries of it are known, persisted (`y/G`, `State::put_forgotten`, read back at start) so a restart before the deletes still deletes them at hosts; it goes with `sweep_forgotten` (past the deletes' tombstones).

## Evidence under labels (`Engine::ended_needed`, `ending_evidence`)
`ended_needed(g)`: `ended_upstream(g)` plus each kept ended thing (`Core::ended_kept`, a superset read from every record once after an open, kept by `end_thing`/`learn_record`) a live thing of `g`'s closure lists or references: O(kept ended things + their rows), bounded by live things. `certificates(g)` places for each its record chain, frozen edges and `ending_evidence`: mintings and requests as header-only copies (no prefix expiry: a release's copy outlives its blobs). node-sync's `ended_certificate` judges header-only copies of mintings and requests of ended things in the closure too (not one that may be lasting) and outside it, by `ended_needed`, waiting on the needers' rows.

## API (engine, through `internals` in osis tests)
`Engine::judge_lifetimes(heard, held)` judges the work list (with `held` the held things too; pending when rights moved). `heard` is asked of groups: a thing read is heard out when it and every group holding it (`ix.groups.of`) are; a thing whose walk read one that is not goes to `lh/`. The unreachable things the node's account may end (`purge::would_hold_right` over every held minting), with their cascade, go in one release request it writes in a second transaction (`Engine::release`); they stay pending until the request, applied, ends them (`queue_with_listers` clears the row), so a request refused or failing to be written is judged again at the next role-timeline change or open. Returns `Judged { release, waiting, judged, request }`. `Engine::release(xs)` writes a request for any things (`Permission` without the right; `w.release` builds on it). `lifetimes_due` (work due, things held: two store seeks), `lifetimes_pending`, `lifetime_status`.
- Writer checks (`Mutation::require_kept`, `require_reference`, `write::check_lifetimes` after `check_synced`, in the commit's transaction so a refusal applies nothing): `lifetimes::unreachable_now` is the walk on the state the commit left, heard out or not; `refs::references_in` reads one durable row (target, source, AK). Opt-in per mutation: osis's `Write` asks it for its own list ops; compaction, undo and the release request never do (a snapshot changes no read, and a pending thing's snapshot must not fail).

## Node-sync trigger (`sync/lifetimes.rs`, `SyncEngine::judge_lifetimes`)
Run in `handle` after the passes (`PASS_AGAIN`); a request written gets one more `Local` step so it goes up at once. Nothing due or held: two store seeks, nothing else. Else it reads `label_status()` (O(labels x connections), in memory): a label is heard out exactly when its status reads Synced (`LabelStatus::synced`, the one rule the API's `SyncState::Synced` uses: a source connected, a host by this node's pass or a co-reader by its stream and reports, each caught up, none opening); a group is heard out when each of its non-replaced labels is; a group with no label, and a thing under none, is heard out. A label with no source connected is not: offline, or under a label with no host while no co-reader is up, nothing under it is released (commits from a co-reader in the bad order would otherwise release a live thing). It judges when work is due, and passes `held` only when a label is heard out that was not at the last judgment (`lifetimes_heard`, `None` after start): so a step re-walks nothing while a label stays unheard, and the held things are walked again once a label is newly heard out.
- Cost: every held thing is walked again when any label is newly heard out, not only those waiting on it: a thing held for good under a label never heard out (a co-reader that never connects) is walked again each time another label comes back to Synced after a judgment ran while it was not (issues/thing-lifetimes.md, Left).
