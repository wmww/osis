# What a write states, and what a reader holds to judge it
> Summary: Design (not built, not in spec) for writes stating their path: what a path is and how each hop is checked, what judging along it alone does to the role timeline and osis-perm, where it sits in a commit and its bytes; evidence in the sync unit as the closure over the paths its writes state; the profile reference; things opened to strangers by one switch; the account as an ordinary independent thing with no account root; the last-admin rule; the model plan.

**Principle: a write states how it has its right, and is judged by that statement alone; what a reader needs to check the statement is in the unit it reads, as a function of the writes that unit holds.** Every rule below follows from it. Marks: *checked* (against the cited spec or code, as they stand), *argued* (a design claim to be confirmed by the model, below), *assumed* (from the membership design).

## Assumed of the membership design
- A signer is a key thing (its record holds its signing key) holding an ordinary grant on the account; no epochs, inner signatures, signing chain, custody or merges. The header's `epoch`, `rotation`, `reseal` and the inner signature go.
- Removing a node is the ordinary removal of its signer's grant on the account.
- Removal lists take a form whose per-thing part a reader of one thing gets alone (needed here: a frontier copied into a unit as evidence shows only that unit's part).
- The device list and the nodes' `reach.0` sit on the account thing (or its dependents).

## 1. Paths
### What a path is
- A commit has one signer S and one signature. For each thing T it writes it states a **path**: a list of things [A1, ..., Ak], k ≥ 0, no thing twice (a repeat is bad input: it gives nothing a shorter path does not, and bounds the work; argued).
- **Hops**: consecutive members of [S, A1, ..., Ak, T] are joined by **descent** when the first is a mint ancestor of the second (the record edges down the mint chain, each the edge (mint admin, thing) as the timeline holds it), else by their **direct edge** (the holder's entry in the second's `roles.0`). A descent of one step is the direct edge (the record edge is that entry's initial value, checked: spec/permissions.md §1 Record edge). Every hop is a fixed list of edges; the path's role on T is the minimum over all of them (spec/permissions.md §1 Effective role, restricted to one path).
- **The end**: the role a write needs is on T, except a self-removal, whose role is admin on the grant's holder X: its path ends at X instead of T. The path's end decides the class: a lowering whose path ends at the holder is a self-removal, one ending at the target an explicit write. This replaces the "only authority" test of spec/permissions.md §2 (argued: an author admin of both may choose; choosing self-removal lets X's members' writes through X be judged by their own timestamps, which an admin of X could bring about anyway).
- **What is listed**: only grant holders and the things they hold on, which are independent (a thing gets secrets of its own before it is granted anything, checked: spec/permissions.md §3; a dependent thing has no edge write, checked: §3 plain thing). Dependent things below the last listed thing are never listed: the descent covers them.
- **Why descent and not "the path ends at a holder on T's independent"**: a thing becoming independent would then change which paths are valid, so a concurrent write by a node unaware of the promotion would fall (argued; it breaks "sharing never changes behaviour", spec/requirements.md Permissions). Descent reads record edges, which promotion leaves standing (checked: spec/permissions.md §3 Own secrets add only).
- Consequence, stated: a mint ancestor two or more steps up reaches T by descent only; a direct grant it also holds on T is not usable by a path (rare; argued harmless).
- Typical paths: an account writing its own dependents [A]; a member of an organization writing its things [A, Org]; a holder of a grant on a shared independent doc D [A, D]; a link key's invite [] (L's direct edge on K).

### Judging (spec/permissions.md §5.1, §5.2 rewritten along one path)
- **Content** (argued): a delta on T with path P needing role r is valid iff every edge of P gives at least r just before the commit's key (grants' `until` at its `ts`) and no valid explicit write lowering an edge of P, taking P below r, is ordered after it; otherwise iff that lowering's frontier lists it, an addendum against it vouches for it, or P is **re-granted**: every edge of P gives r in the timeline's final state (every `until` counted removed), the write then valid from the first point P gave r.
- **Edge writes**: an edge write applies iff its path gives what it needs at that point of the walk: admin on the target; for an invite grant, the path's invite (the roles of every hop before the last, and the last direct edge's `invite`; a descent counts as admin); for a self-removal, admin on the holder; a finalize keeps its rule (write and a role at least the grant's, along the path). Effective role and effective invite (maxima over paths) leave validity entirely.
- **Attribution and demotion**: an applied write is attributed to its path. A write R lowering edge h **puts in question** every earlier applied write whose path contains h and whose path role falls through R (from its class before, with the edge as restored, to below). R drops those its frontier does not list; the walk restarts as now. The reachability search over "everything N reaches where X's class falls" goes: the set is read from an index hop → writes.
- **Removed rules** (argued): "losing a path while another keeps the class is no fall" (every path is audited alone); "R never demotes its own author" (R's frontier lists every commit through h its author received, its own included; forgotten ones by the own cover as now); the Acting check (§5.2: each hop is the check); the header's `actor`.
- **Kept unchanged**: the edge as restored; frontiers by commit ID with own covers; addenda (vouching for a delta whose path R cut); `until` and self-removal auditing nothing, the next explicit write on that edge auditing; invites; trust sets, with a trusted write's seeds read as the held edge writes through its edge that its frontier does not list (argued); role cycles (validity counts every grant, checked: §3 Who rotates).
- **Frontier scope**: for R lowering h, the aspects in question are those of the things the paths through h wrote (role-class and admin-only below admin, every aspect below write); listed are the commits through h on them that R's author received, the role commits of R's own path's evidence that pass through h, and the purge requests whose path passes through h.
- **Keys do not follow paths.** Who reads, whom a rotation boxes, what a departure forces to rotate, sync units: all stay on effective reach over every grant (spec/permissions.md §3, spec/sync-set.md §2). Only validity is path-judged (argued: reading is holding secrets, which travel by every route; a stated path cannot narrow it).

### What changes for users
- **Removing M from A** touches every write whose path passes through (M, A), on A and on everything A reaches, and nothing M wrote by another path. Its rotations are as now.
- **A write whose path is valid and another would also have been** is judged by its own path: it falls with that path's removal unless the remover received it, an addendum vouches for it or that path is re-granted; the other path does not save it (spec/requirements.md Permissions "judged along them alone" says so). Its writer's node is told (spec/permissions.md §6, own commits) and may write it again along a current path. Which path a node states is writer policy, invisible to validity; the engine should prefer the path whose first holder is nearest the signer (a direct grant over an organization's), so an organization's removal leaves direct writes alone.
- **Paths are not a narrowing of today's rule** (argued): today a write via P1 falls by the removal R2 that takes the author's last path, judged by R2's frontier; along paths it is judged by R1, the removal of its own path. Each write is judged by the removal of what it used, which is the correct one.
- **Adversarial checks** (argued; the model checks them): a writer cannot pick a path that survives a removal it should not: a removal audits every write through the edge it lowers, a write through no lowered edge stands only on a path that held at its key and was never explicitly cut, which is a right the writer holds; backdating through a second path is caught by that path's own removal or is a right still held. A demotion cannot be hidden: a lowering of any edge, descent edges included, audits every path through it, and a path naming an edge the reader holds no write for is unjudgeable, never valid (below). A forged path in a reader's re-encryption fails the header's hash (Bytes).
- **Purge rights**: the `author` class of spec/permissions.md §6 needs an author. Proposed: a delta's **purge author** is its path's first thing (A1, normally the account), else the signer; a request holds the author right when its own path for the thing begins with that thing and gives write. The level-2 delete key and a header list's thing blind follow the purge author (spec/crypto.md §7 names the header actor today). Question for Tess.
- Possible admins and writers (spec/commits.md §4, purge requests a node cannot fully judge) may read stated paths instead of every grant; not needed for correctness (argued).

### osis-perm
- Validity of a write is a point query per edge of its path (`Edges` with its checkpoints, checked: notes/perm-impl.md Queries) instead of `roles_on(thing)` (max over paths, memoized). The walk applies an edge write in O(hops); a demotion finds what it puts in question by the hop index, so `audit_fall` and the forward roles cache go. `first_held` becomes per path: the first point at which every edge of the path gives the role, a merge of those edges' histories.
- Components: things joined when an edge of one is on a path stated by a write on the other (finer than "joined by edges, authors and records"); incremental extend, append and shadow placement keep their shape (argued).
- `roles_on` and effective reach stay for keys, sync units and `holders`.
- Expected effect on issues/perm-eval-cost.md: evaluation walks each write's path once; the cost that came from a much-demoted holder over many leaves goes, since a leaf's writes name their path (argued; to count, notes/principles.md Check scaling).

### Where the path sits, and bytes
- Header: `signer` (the key thing's tid) replaces `author` and `actor`. A field `path`:
  - one path `[tid*]` when every thing the commit writes uses it;
  - else, per thing in the order of their first aspects, a 32-byte hash `BLAKE3(salt ‖ path)`; the thing's section carries `[salt, path]` (salt 16 random bytes; without it a reader of another thing could test guessed paths).
- A commit-wide path is used only when it is every thing's: a default overridden by some things would show readers of those things a path naming holders with no role on them (spec/requirements.md Confidentiality between readers). So the rule is one path for all or one per thing, never a default with exceptions (argued; it is the "only if simple" optimization).
- A section whose path does not hash to the header's entry is not that thing's part (as a delta hash mismatch, spec/commits.md §4 Judged alone). One path per thing per commit: a write needing a second path for the same thing is a second commit.
- Bytes (CBOR: a tid 16 B with its header, an array 1 B): common case 1 + 16k in the header, so [A] is 17 B against today's `actor` 16 B; [A, Org] 33 B. Per thing with paths of their own: 33 B in the header plus 17 + 1 + 16k in its section. Membership removes the epoch (33 B) and inner signature (95 B or more) from an account's commit (assumed).

## 2. Evidence in the sync unit
### What a unit carries
- **Evidence of a sync unit U** (argued): the least set holding, for every commit with a body under U's sync ID (role commits kept included): the records of every thing on its paths and descents, and every held edge write on every edge of those paths (grants, changes, lowerings with their frontiers); closed under the same for those edge writes' own paths; and the records of their signers. It is in U's required set beside its bodies, as copies of one role commit's parts on one thing each (with its header and that thing's path), logged by any node holding them and U (spec/sync-judgement.md §1-§2 Own store, unchanged).
- This is a function of the commits U holds: when a commit leaves (compacted, forgotten), the paths only it used leave the evidence, and their copies leave with no tombstone, per (commit, thing), so no copy outlives the parts it was sealed for (issues/certificate-copy-never-shrinks.md does not arise).
- **Not every actor that could write** (the direction in issues/actors-one-mechanism.md): the three points below rule it out at scale. Readers get exactly what verifies what was written, which is also what the requirements let them learn (the actors of each change, spec/requirements.md Confidentiality between readers) and what Tess asked readers to learn (only the devices that wrote the thing). Question for Tess.

### The three points
- **(a) Same blob for every reader.** The roles secret today is `kdf("roles", s)` of an epoch's secret; holders at read or above hold it, through history boxes back past their floor; it opens only role parts, their blobs' wraps and list blobs (checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Roles secrets, §5). Handing a holder's roles secret down to the readers of what it holds a role on fails: the set allowed to read it is the union of the readers of everything it reaches, so every removal from any of those would force it to rotate and hand the new secret down again, a cost in the size of everything above (argued). Instead evidence is a certificate copy: one encryption under a random blob key, one wrap per sync ID under that sync ID's own secret (checked: spec/sync-judgement.md §1 Certificate blobs, spec/wire.md §4 copy section). Readers open the same blob; a removal from U reaches its evidence by U's own rotation; no secret is handed down.
- **(b) Work per roster change.** For every possible actor, every roster change of a holder X (a device added included) owes an entry under every unit X reaches, transitively: a member of an organization holding roles on 10^5 things owes 10^5 entries (153 B each, checked: notes/thing-cost.md Per entry) per device change, and every reader downloads its share. That breaks spec/requirements.md Cost ("never in proportion to the size of what holds it"). Along stated paths: a grant costs nothing until its first use in a unit, where it rides with the writing commit (one entry); a lowering of edge h owes entries under the units whose evidence holds h, at most those the removed party could write in, which is the cost the requirements allow a removal. Rejected, the alternative: X's role data under its own sync ID that readers follow. Its readers would need a key held by everyone who reads anything X reaches (the problem of (a)), and a node keeping U unread cannot know which such sync IDs to keep, so a reader served by it alone could not judge (argued).
- **(c) Size.** Every actor: a thing shared with an organization of n members carries every member's devices. Along stated paths: the paths of its writers and their granters, independent of n.

### What follows
- **Pending**: "pending until the author's chain arrives" (spec/commits.md §4 Unjudgeable, spec/permissions.md §4) becomes: a part is unjudgeable while an edge of its path has no held edge write putting it (any validity) or a record on it is missing; it pends under Pending storage and is examined again as role inputs move, as now. Its evidence is in the same unit's required set, so a complete source has both; nothing waits on a separate logger. A path naming an edge that never exists pends within the bounds on unvouched input (checked: spec/commits.md §4 Pending storage). A commit judged on missing evidence is never judged invalid for it (a dropped invalid delta would be fetched again).
- **Nodes keeping U unread**: store and serve evidence entries as any entries; nothing changes (checked: spec/sync-judgement.md §6).
- **A removal up a path**: lowering edge h is an edge write in the evidence of every unit holding a write through h, so it is in those units' required sets; the remover owes it under every sync ID it tracks whose evidence holds h, any other node holding it and the unit logs it, and it applies before the deletes it causes (Role entries first, unchanged). As now, it reaches a unit only through a node holding both; a remover administering the edge's target reaches, by its path, every unit a write through h was in (argued).
- **Promotion of a deposit** waits for the commit's evidence under S (spec/sync-inbox.md §4, reworded).
- **What goes** of spec/permissions.md §4 and spec/sync-judgement.md §1-§2: chain certificates over any path from the author; "closed under authors"; each path thing's current `keys.0` commits as evidence (judging needs no KEM key; a signer's key is in its record); account rotations as header-only copies, thing lists and list blobs (assumed, with the signing chain); the duty to log one's account's chain on first logging under a sync ID. **What stays**: record blobs; a grant naming a holder travelling under the holder's sync ID with the target's boxes and `sync.0`, and the grantee's `keys.0` under the target's sync ID for sealing (keys, not judgement); destroyed things' frozen role data; Stranded holder keys.
- Readers see a holder's members only as far as paths show them, so `holders` shows less to non-members than today (observable; argued intended: those outside a holder see the holder act).

## 3. The profile reference
- **Where** (argued): a small role-class aspect of the thing (here `public.0`, name open) holding its public references: the profile, as a ticket (tid, sync ID, the profile's read secret at an epoch), and its inbox (moved from `keys.0`, checked: spec/crypto.md §3 `keys.0`, `Engine::inbox_thing` core/engine/src/flows/inbox.rs). Separate from `keys.0` so evidence carries it without four KEM keys (4864 B, checked: notes/thing-cost.md). Written by admins; judged as any role-class write. Single-valued: one profile per thing.
- **Who gets it**: the thing's holders read it as its data, and it is a strong reference, so holding the thing brings the profile in (Tess's rule). Readers who see the thing on a path get its current `public.0` commit in the evidence with its edges, so they hold the ticket of every face that wrote where they read: a face is seen by exactly those who see the thing act (plus those it was seen by before, who keep the ticket until the profile's read secret rotates).
- **Held or not**: proposed, the evidence's profile reference enters the profile like a strong from the unit and leaves with that evidence, so a missing face shows as a broken reference, never nothing. Cost: one sync ID per distinct face per reader. The alternative is the app holding the faces it shows. Question for Tess.
- The profile is independent (outsiders fetch it by its own sync ID) and minted with its thing as admin. A departure from that thing forces the profile's read rotation like any departure (spec/permissions.md §3 Required rotation); the next writer rewrites the reference, which reaches the units holding the thing's evidence as a removal does. Rare: device removals.
- **The dial list leaves the profile** (argued): a profile is now read by everyone who saw the account act and keeps being read by them after they lose access; addresses should reach those the account shares with, as today (spec/sync-set.md §1, spec/sync-inbox.md §5 Delivery of a grant, notes/sync-facade.md Dial peers). It moves to a thing of its own with today's profile readership (contacts, and those granted with, read-granted at delivery of a grant), named in `public.0` for holders and given by ticket to the others. Question for Tess.

## 4. Opened to strangers
### The checks (issues/open-signals-taken-by-default.md)
- **Routes by which a grant naming holder A reaches A**, its granter administering nothing of A (checked: spec/sync-inbox.md §2-§5, spec/sync-judgement.md §2, spec/sync-set.md §2):
  1. a box sealed to A's KEM key and a sealed `ticket@~0` fire on A's inbox (the decided route);
  2. a deposit of the role commit under A's sync ID: anyone holding A's current deposit seed (today in `keys.0`, which travels to readers of everything A holds a role on) deposits within the budget, and A's writers then log it as their own copy, since grants naming A are in A's required set;
  3. a ticket for the target in any value one of A's members reads (an out-of-band ticket): the member's node reads the target by the ticket's word.
- So the box and the ticket are **not** the only routes, and KEM keys do not close them for a thing that is already a holder elsewhere (it has KEM keys). Route 3 is only handing over a secret, which no rule can take back (spec/requirements.md Permissions); it makes A a holder nowhere.
- An unkept fire today: valid, joins listeners, and spends the deposit budget at nodes keeping N unread until stamped, deleted or expired (checked: spec/sync-inbox.md §3 Budget). Under the rule below an unnamed fire is invalid: it reaches no listener and is never promoted; it leaves the budget at its expiry or by the caps.

### The rule (ready for the spec; argued)
- **Setting**: a thing's admin-only `retention^.0` entries naming open signals (spec/commits.md §5 The setting). A thing is **open** at a key iff the setting of its inbox (the thing itself unless `public.0` names another) in force there names `ticket@~0`. One switch.
- **Open fires**: a fire of open signal `s` on N is valid iff N's setting in force at its key names `s`, with any keep duration (0: taken, out of history at once). Judged by N's readers; nothing else about the fire changes (spec/permissions.md §5.2 Open fires).
- **Public material follows the switch**: a thing publishes, to parties holding no role on it, KEM keys only while it is a holder or open (decided), and its deposit seed and sync ID only while open. The deposit seed and sync ID move out of `keys.0` into a part only the thing's holders read (`sync.0`); closing rotates the deposit seed (an admin may at any time, checked: spec/crypto.md §3).
- **What a node keeping N unread enforces**: deposits by signature and budget, as now (checked: spec/sync-inbox.md §3). With N closed no stranger holds a current deposit key, so a stranger's deposit fails its signature (answer 14), and a stale key stops at the next deposit-only step (checked: spec/permissions.md §3 Sync ID role keys). It judges nothing else.
- **What A takes**: a role commit with an edge write naming A on X is **taken** by A iff it also states a path to A giving write on A (a member's grant; the path is in the commit's header beside X's path), or A is open at its key and A's sync unit holds a strong to X (accepted, as an account's root accepts today, checked: spec/sync-judgement.md §2 role commits naming an actor). Only a taken commit is in A's sync IDs' required sets with X's boxes to A and X's `sync.0`; A's members' nodes take secrets through A, and count A's role on X, only by taken grants. An untaken grant stays in X's roster: X's readers judge X by X's timeline alone, so a member of A may still write X through A (argued harmless: it is the member's act, and the member could act as A anyway).
- **The granter's node** refuses (named by the setting, never by keys) an edge write naming A when it states no write path to A and holds no open public material of A.
- **A member of A** (holding write on A) needs no switch: it grants with its write path to A, boxes A symmetrically by A's secrets or by KEM, and logs the grant and boxes under A's sync ID as a writer, where every node of A's members takes them; no ticket fire is needed. A member holding only read may not (Readers only read; the requirements say "holding no role", which would let a reader make A's writers store its grants). Question for Tess.
- Ordinary signals: no switch (decided). How an app opens a thing, and the default per kind, stay open in the issue.

## 5. Account, account root, home
### Why the account root is a separate thing (checked)
1. An account is a key actor: it has no roster (no edge write targets it), no sync ID and no sync unit (spec/permissions.md §1, spec/sync-set.md §1). The root gives its nodes a thing with a roster, a sync ID and data (`holds.0`, `tickets.0`).
2. Its roster is the device list read from the signing chain, and only P's nodes read it, so node identities appear nowhere else (spec/permissions.md §1).
3. Its secrets rotate only inside P's rotations (spec/crypto.md §3).
4. Things minted with the account as admin cannot be dependents of it: an account is in no sync unit, so a dependent would be carried nowhere (spec/permissions.md §3, spec/sync-set.md §2). So profile and inbox are independent at birth and outside the root's sync unit; the root reaches them by tickets (spec/sync-set.md §1).
5. The root is never sourced away and holds the node's records (spec/sync-set.md §5).
### With the account an ordinary independent thing A (argued)
- 1, 3 and 4 go: A has a roster (its signers), a sync ID, data and dependents, and rotates as any thing. 2 is the membership design's (assumed). 5 moves to A. The node root's strong goes to A. So the account root goes: home is A (Tess: an account is a kind of realm; home is the user's, one per account), and the account's private things are A's dependents, carried in A's sync unit when held by strongs. Profile and inbox are minted with A as admin, so A's keys open them and no ticket is needed for A's nodes.
- **The inbox stays a thing of its own** for an account: strangers deposit into it, and helpers keeping it unread to store and forward (checked: spec/sync-inbox.md §1) should not keep A's whole unit. The rule is one: a thing's inbox is the one its `public.0` names, itself by default.
- **To write down**: a role on A reaches everything A holds a role on anywhere (spec/requirements.md Permissions: a role M holds on A reaches the parties holding roles on M). Today no one can hold a role on an account (a key actor) or its root (no edge write targets it, checked: spec/permissions.md §1); with A ordinary, granting anyone a role on home grants all of the account's access, so a space to share is another realm. An account holding a role on another account (a linked identity) is this same fact used on purpose.
### The two behaviours (issues/behaviour-depends-on-own-keys.md)
- **Removing a thing's last admin**: one rule for every thing, proposed: allowed. The roster is frozen and current holders keep their roles (spec/permissions.md §1, notes/realms.md "no admin left"). A dependent thing has no holder but its mint admin, so removing its record edge leaves it written by nobody and read, through derivation, by its mint admin's readers; no rotation is due until someone encrypts, and no one can. An adminless state arises anyway (a timed admin grant ending, an admin destroyed), so a writer-side refusal would be a nudge, not a rule; the API asks the app to say it means it. The alternative, refusing both locally, is as consistent. Question for Tess (she said either, but the same).
- **"A thing minted with an account as admin is independent at birth"** goes: its reason was that an account has no sync unit (checked: spec/permissions.md §3); a thing minted with A is a dependent of A like any. With it go `write::independent_first` for account-minted things and the record riding in the newborn's rotation for them (notes/thing-cost.md, The record in the rotation: only things that need their own secrets at birth, such as a link's K, keep it).

## 6. Model plan
- Adapt roles-model `joint` (several things, record edges, holder things, mutual roles, commit-ID ties) and `edges` (`until`, invites, self-removal, links): each write carries a path; validity, attribution, demotion, frontiers and re-grant by path; effective role leaves validity. Then `addenda` (vouching against a removal of a hop) and `trust` (seeds by edge).
- Properties: termination; input-order independence; nothing dropped with nobody offline (every write the remover received before its removal stands); **safety**: a write through a lowered edge stands only if the lowering's author or an addendum writer received it before the lowering, or its path is re-granted; **path choice**: a hostile writer choosing any path (valid, invalid, through things it holds nothing on, backdated) gains nothing over choosing the best honest one; **self-removal by path end** audits nothing; descent: a path's validity is unchanged by a thing on it becoming independent (the model has no keys, so it checks that nothing reads independence).
- Differential against osis-perm as the joint and edges models are today (notes/perm-impl.md Differentials).
- Evidence completeness is a sync property: in held-model, a reader holding only U (its required set computed from its own holdings) judges every write of U as a node holding everything does.
