# What a write states, and what a reader holds to judge it
> Summary: Design (not built, not in spec) for writes stating their path: what a path is and how each hop is checked, the purge author a write names, what judging along a path alone does to the role timeline and osis-perm, where it sits in a commit; evidence in the sync unit as the closure over the paths its held commits state; public things (the public actor, profiles as the schema's, the card that names a thing's inbox); external signals and who may grant to a thing; core without accounts (no account root, the last-admin rule, what leaves the `osis` crate); the built sync rules it rests on and what it needs of them; the model plan.

**Principle: a write states how it has its right, and is judged by that statement alone; what a reader needs to check the statement is in the unit it reads, as a function of the writes that unit holds.** Core knows signers, things, roles, a write's stated path and named purge author, and each session's configured defaults for those; it has no concept of an account, a profile or a home (Tess). Marks: *checked* (against the cited spec or code, as they stand), *argued* (a design claim for the model, §6), *decided* (Tess, issues/actors-one-mechanism.md Answers), *assumed* (of another design). Terms: notes/terms.md.

## Assumed of the membership design
(notes/account-membership-design.md)
- A signer is a key thing (its record holds its signing key) holding an ordinary grant on what its node acts through; no epochs, inner signatures, signing chain, custody or merges. The header's `epoch`, `rotation`, `reseal` and the inner signature go.
- Removing a node is the ordinary removal of its signer's grant. Its list has a leaf per thing and an entry per role commit; role writes it leaves out are dropped (issues/role-vouch-contested.md); no addendum counts for a commit of its own signer; a remover names nothing its removal would not apply with.

## The built sync rules it rests on
The sync unit is the built one: a thing's readable closure, so a thing may be carried under several sync IDs (spec/sync-set.md §2; notes/sync-unit-scope.md). Used here:
- A commit is one blob, wrapped under the sync ID of every unit that carries a thing it writes; a certificate copy logged under several sync IDs is one blob wrapped under each (checked: spec/sync-judgement.md §1 One blob per commit, Certificate blobs). Evidence is stated per sync ID.
- A grant naming a holder travels under the holder's sync ID with the target's boxes to it and the target's `sync.0`, so the holder's readers enter what it holds roles on, read by those boxes, where a strong in their closure reaches it, with no ticket per node (checked: spec/sync-set.md §2 Node closure, read; spec/permissions.md §4 Role changes travel).
- The public actor and a link key, key actors with no sync ID, are listed in a step's claim set by static entry with an entry box, so anyone acting as them opens the unit at readers and at nodes keeping it unread alike (checked: spec/sync-ids.md §2, §3 Claim set, Entry boxes).
- A node opens a sync ID only while it syncs it (checked: spec/sync-opens.md §4, the shared set); nothing is logged or fetched under a sync ID outside the node's sync set without holding it.
- Deposits work per sync ID (spec/sync-inbox.md §3).

## Needs of the built sync rules
What this design needs that the built rules do not give (for the coordinator; each lands in the stage named, notes/redesign-plan.md):
- **Evidence as the closure over stated paths**, a copy per (commit, thing) leaving with the commit that needed it, in place of chain certificates over any path (§2; A2). The placement rule stays the built one (a copy where the bodies do not carry the parts), re-derived at every change, retag included (modelled: notes/evidence-model-findings.md 2).
- **A removal kept for good** under every sync ID carrying a thing where the removed signer could act, judged required there by that sync ID's readers from the edge writes it comes with (§2; A3). Built: a removal travels for `ROLE_KEEP` after its `ts` (spec/permissions.md §4 Role changes travel).
- **Public things**: every thing's keys count as opening what the public actor reads (a clause of spec/sync-set.md §2 T's keys open; §3; A5).
- **Taken grants**: only a grant the holder took is in its sync ID's required set with the target's boxes and `sync.0` (§4; A5). Built: every grant naming a holder travels there.
- **A signer listed in A's steps by a claim under its own key** (notes/account-membership-design.md; A4). Built: a reader that is a key actor other than a node or an account is listed by static entry (spec/sync-ids.md §3); whether a signer per node per account counts as a node there is A4's spec brief.

## 1. Paths
### What a path is
- A commit has one signer S and one signature. For each thing T it writes it states a **path**: a list of things [A1, ..., Ak], k ≥ 0, no thing twice (a repeat is bad input; argued).
- **Hops**: consecutive members of [S, A1, ..., Ak, T] are joined by **descent** when the first is a mint ancestor of the second (the record edges down the mint chain), else by their **direct edge** (the holder's entry in the second's `roles.0`). A descent of one step is the direct edge (checked: spec/permissions.md §1 Record edge). The path's role on T is the minimum over its edges (spec/permissions.md §1 Effective role, restricted to one path).
- **The end**: the role a write needs is on T, except a self-removal, whose role is admin on the grant's holder X: its path ends at X. The path's end decides the class: a lowering whose path ends at the holder is a self-removal, one ending at the target an explicit write. This replaces the "only authority" test of spec/permissions.md §2 (argued).
- **What is listed**: only grant holders and the things they hold on, which are independent (checked: spec/permissions.md §3, a thing becomes independent before any grant on it). Dependent things below the last listed thing are covered by the descent.
- **Why descent**: were a path to end "at a holder on T's independent", a thing becoming independent would change which paths are valid, and a concurrent write by a node unaware of the promotion would fall (argued; spec/requirements.md Permissions: sharing never changes how a thing behaves). Descent reads record edges, which promotion leaves standing (checked: spec/permissions.md §3 Own secrets add only).
- A mint ancestor two or more steps up reaches T by descent only; a direct grant it also holds on T is not usable by a path, nor the path's remaining admin (§5; modelled: notes/path-model-findings.md 30).
- Typical paths: a node writing what its account administers [A]; a member of an organization writing its things [A, Org]; a holder of a grant on a shared document D [A, D]; a link key's invite [].

### The purge author
Who may purge an edit is fixed when it is written (spec/requirements.md Deletion); where the class includes its author, the write must say which of its actors that is. Core picks no position on the path (decided): "the first thing after the signer" would build in nodes with one account.
- **How a write names it** (modelled: roles-model `paths`, an index into [signer, path]): each stated path carries an index i into [S, A1, ..., Ak]: 0 the signer, j the thing Aj. One index per path, so per thing written, and one for the commit where it states one path. A write wanting two purge authors on one thing is two commits, as a write needing two paths is. Per aspect beside each purgeable-by class was the alternative: it lets one commit mix authors on one thing, which nothing needs, at a byte per delta.
- The index is always present and has no default on the wire. A session's default (what it was configured to act as) is local configuration, written by the layer that understands what the session acts through, never by core.
- **What a purger must hold**: a request holds the `author` right over a delta iff the request's own path for that thing contains the delta's purge author (as its signer or as a listed thing) and gives write on the thing, judged as a content write along that path at the request's key (modelled: `purges`, control `-purge-any-path`; the `author` class alone; today: the header actor while it holds write on the thing, and any author with write on that actor, checked: spec/permissions.md §6). So an edit authored as an organization is purged by any member writing through it, one authored as an account by any of its nodes, one authored as a signer by that node alone. So a signer removed from A and not from A2 still purges, by a request stamped at any `ts`, every edit authored as A2 on what A2 writes, until it is removed from A2 too; what it authored as A, or anything else authored as A, it purges only by a request A's remover received and listed (modelled: story moves 38-40).
- **"Purge everything by this author"** names a thing and matches, on the aspect, the deltas whose named purge author is exactly that thing: never every delta whose path contains it (modelled: control `-purge-contains`).
- **Today's forms**: the request by author (`Purge::Author`) keeps its shape, its argument read as a purge author; the header's `actor` is the indexed thing. For a node writing as its account the path is [A] with index 1, which gives today's behaviour.
- **Delete keys**: level 2 (spec/crypto.md §7) is the write secret of the purge author, for a signer its signing key; the writer holds it, since a content path gives write at every hop. Where no header is held, a snapshot's facts state the purge author in place of the actor (spec/commits.md §5 Purge, Without the header). A header list's thing blind (spec/crypto.md §7, today the header's actor) is keyed by the signer (argued).

### Judging (spec/permissions.md §5.1, §5.2 rewritten along one path)
- **Content** (argued): a delta on T with path P needing role r is valid iff every edge of P gives at least r just before the commit's key (grants' `until` at its `ts`) and no valid explicit write lowering an edge of P, taking P below r, is ordered after it; otherwise iff that lowering's list names it (its frontier), an addendum against it by another signer vouches for it (notes/account-membership-design.md §4), or P is **re-granted**: every edge of P gives r in the timeline's final state (every `until` counted removed), the write then valid from the first point P gave r.
- **Edge writes**: an edge write applies iff its path gives what it needs at that point of the walk: admin on the target; for an invite grant, the path's invite (the roles of every hop before the last, and the last direct edge's `invite`; a descent counts as admin); for a self-removal, admin on the holder; a finalize keeps its rule (write and a role at least the grant's, along the path) (modelled: invite grants and finalizes, notes/path-model-findings.md 23-25). Effective role and effective invite (maxima over paths) leave validity entirely. The last-admin rule: §5.
- **Attribution and demotion**: an applied write is attributed to its path and **rests on** each edge of it; a self-removal rests also on the edge it gives up, which only that edge's holder can do (modelled: `leave_in_question`; else a removed node's backdated leave, which no audit reaches, undoes the role writes it made after it). A write R lowering edge h **puts in question** every earlier held write resting on h whose path role falls through R, not yet dropped and not void: the same set a trusted R's seeds take (modelled: `audit_held`, notes/path-model-findings.md 18). Those its list does not name are R's **drops**. The reachability search over "everything N reaches where X's class falls" goes: the set is read from an index hop → writes.
- **A removal is judged in the world its list makes** (modelled: roles-model `paths`, `Rules::fixed`). The rule for how a lowering's own authority meets what it audits and lists:
  - **Nothing is exempt and nothing is read as absent.** R's list (its frontier) alone decides which of the writes resting on h are kept, in R's audit and in a trusted R's seeds alike. The writes R's own authority rests on get no special standing: where one of them rests on h (the removed party granted the remover, or the remover's granter), R keeps it by listing it, and its author holds it, since its own walk needs it.
  - **Each lowering is judged once** [modelled: roles-model `paths`, `Rules::fixed`; notes/path-model-findings.md 16, 17]. A **lowering** is an explicit write giving its edge h below admin at its `ts`. An **end** (an explicit write leaving h giving admin at its `ts` with an `until` earlier than h's own end: on a standing admin, or shortened) is no lowering but a grant: it applies where its path gives admin in the pass, carries no frontier and is no dispute candidate; the last-admin rule (§5) alone reads it. Taken as a lowering it fails a required property either way (modelled: notes/path-model-findings.md 38): judged at its key it drops nothing (every write before it is stamped before the end) and its verdict keeps it applying after its author's standing falls (a made-up key's end on the remover's admin stands at nodes trusting nothing); dropping forward the writes stamped past the end, its drops outlive it (an honest admin's later grant lost, a dispute in an honest history). The walk is a run of **passes**; each starts from the record edges and takes the held edge-class writes in key order (`ts`, signer, commit ID), skipping D. Two things carry from pass to pass: D, and a **verdict** (applied or void) per lowering that has one. In a pass:
    - A lowering with a verdict is not checked again: applied, it applies (whatever earlier writes now give its author, and auditing nothing) where it takes h as restored in this pass down or keeps it; where its value is above h as restored, in role or invite, it would raise h and applies only as any write does, by its path giving admin (modelled: notes/path-model-findings.md 27); void, it is skipped. Any other write applies iff its path gives what it needs there (Edge writes above; the last-admin rule, §5).
    - A lowering with no verdict that applies there and takes h down, as restored, in role or in invite is judged (modelled: `Rules::any_fall`, notes/path-model-findings.md 23: an invite grant rests on a writer-level edge). Its **drops** are the held writes ordered before it resting on h, not in D and not void, whose need at h (admin for a write applied by admin; for an invite grant its level as a role on the edges before the last and as an invite on the last) the edge as restored met and its new value does not, and that its list does not name. It is **void** iff they would take its authority: `keeps(L)` fails, where `keeps(W)` holds iff every edge e of W's path keeps admin: of the writes this pass applied on e before W's key, latest first, pass over each of L's drops (and of its commit's lowerings applied before it: a commit of several edge writes takes effect one write at a time in key order, the write on an edge of its own stated path last, each judged with the earlier ones in effect, and no write's drops reach its own commit, notes/path-model-findings.md 28, 37) and each grant (a write giving admin at its `ts`, not a self-removal) for which `keeps` fails (read the same way, with L's drops); the first one left decides (e keeps admin iff it gives admin at W's `ts`), and with none left e keeps admin iff it is a record edge. `keeps(L)` also needs the remaining admin edge L relies on (§5) to stay standing, read the same way (29). Each step reads only writes ordered before the one it checks, so the reading ends; it reads the pass's state and nothing later, and walks nothing again. Void, L does not apply and drops nothing.
    - Otherwise it is **applied**: it applies; if it has drops they join D and the pass ends there. The next pass replays the writes before the earliest drop unchanged (an implementation resumes from a checkpoint there) and walks on from it.
    - A lowering that applies without taking h down (h at or below its new value, as restored, in this pass) audits nothing and gets no verdict; one that does not apply gets none. A later pass reaching it applying and taking h down judges it then: a drop can give back the right, or the edge (where the thief's backdated removal of N5 is dropped, X's honest earlier removal of N5 takes N5's edge, and drops the grant N5's key backdated, `a_lowering_taking_nothing_is_judged_when_a_restart_gives_it_the_edge`). A lowering goes at most once from no verdict to one.
    - The walk ends at a pass that adds nothing to D; every other pass adds to D, so there are at most as many passes as held writes, plus one. Nothing is undropped. The reading is a function of the held writes and the trust set: passes, order, drops and verdicts are all read from them.
    - The check does not see what L's drops free: a drop can let an earlier lowering take an edge again in a later pass, and that lowering's drops can take L's authority; L keeps its verdict (`a_lowerings_check_does_not_read_what_its_drops_free`: A left with no admin at a node trusting nothing, shown as a dispute). Reading it would need a walk per check.
    - **Trusted writes**: a trusted R's seeds (the unlisted held writes before it resting on h whose least need there R's value does not meet: a trusted R's drops, made before the first pass; modelled, notes/path-model-findings.md 32) start D. A trusted write the settled walk does not apply is void and loses its seeds, all such at once, and the walk is redone from scratch without them (spec/permissions.md §5.1 Trust set): the one redo in the rule, bounded by the trust set's size.
  - **What judging once gives** (modelled, notes/path-model-findings.md 16, 33 to 36): a lowering applied at its key keeps its effect for good: where a later lowering drops it, or a grant its author's standing came through, it stays applied and its drops stay made (a **stray** drop: dropped by a write that does not apply in the end). A trusted R's seeds act before the walk, so at a node trusting R nothing of the removed party's that R drops is ever judged; at a node not trusting R the same writes are judged at their keys and dropped only when R is reached, and what they did meanwhile may stay: that difference is a dispute (below).
    - **What holds where** (modelled at full size, gated): at nodes trusting the removal, safety (a thief holding a stolen signer keeps or regains no role, cannot void its removal, cannot bring back a party removed earlier), liveness, honest history kept, the last standing admin, alike; at every node, one reading (a function of the held set and the trust set, any input order, relabelling), no gain by a party that never legitimately held the right, and no dispute in an honest history where nobody lowers anybody concurrently (`quiet`).
    - **What an idle write changes** (counted, not required; notes/path-model-findings.md 34 to 36): a held commit the reading does not apply (void, refused, never given a verdict) mostly changes nothing users observe (roles over time, invites, cuts, valid, copied and purged content; which writes show as applied may differ, nobody sees that: 33), or the reading shows a dispute. It can decide a fight: present in the pass that first reaches a later lowering with its right, it gives that lowering its verdict (the right itself, or exemption from the last-admin rule by making a standing admin timed), and the lowering's drops then undo it; or a lowering's drops let an earlier write take its authority, so it applies nowhere and keeps its drops. A verdict once given stands even where the write that let the pass reach it applies nowhere in the end. Every such case is a fight among parties each holding admin at their write in some reading, with honest writers too; none makes a commit of a made-up key or a removed node's key show, at any node, and at a node trusting the removal none is the thief's write and the only thief's writes it makes show are by a path the thief still holds. Most are at nodes trusting nothing, where the thief's idle write decides for the remover. Counts: notes/path-model-findings.md 35. The non-monotone fixpoint that would undo a verdict breaks safety (16). Open whether every such fight must be shown (issues/admin-fights-not-always-shown.md). An end date taken as a lowering does not close it: the smallest honest case reads the same with a removal in place of the end date (a re-grant by the lowerer's own grantee lets the lowered admin's counter-lowering apply, and that counter-lowering's drops take the re-grant), and both readings of an end as a lowering fail a required property (38).
  - **Disputes** [modelled: `disputes`]: a held lowering of another holder's edge, not trusted and not dropped by a trusted write's seeds, whose trust would change the node's reading (what applies, what is valid, the roles). A node computes it from what it holds: its own walk, and its walk with that write added to its trust set. It holds every lowering spec/permissions.md §5.1 calls a dispute (the walk does not apply it and does once it is trusted: its trust changes what applies), and also a removal that applies but whose reach-back the walk cannot undo, and one that is a no-op where trusted (notes/path-model-findings.md 21): one rule. **Candidates** are exactly the held explicit lowerings of another holder's grant (no end: Each lowering is judged once): a grant at write or read included (a grant and a demotion to one value are one write); never a self-removal or an invite grant, which carry no frontier and are never trusted (modelled: an honest holder thing's leave, as a candidate, is shown in an honest history; `dispute` loses nothing: notes/path-model-findings.md 31). A trusted grant's seeds drop its holder's earlier writes resting on the edge that it does not list, so an honest re-grant lists what rests on its edge as received, as every explicit write carries its frontier (spec/permissions.md §5.1 step 4; notes/path-model-findings.md 25). Honest histories (no move of the thief) raise none (modelled: `quiet`, 95,542 readings at nodes trusting nothing in 400,000 story worlds; with honest re-grants listing nothing 15% of such readings show one).
  - **The list is its writer's.** A remover names what it had received in the order it received it, leaving out a role write with which R, added to its own trust set, would be void in its own walk (modelled, `Sim::applies`) (notes/account-membership-design.md §4). Readers check none of it: a list that keeps what unmakes its removal leaves that removal unapplied, and void where it is trusted, for every node alike.
  - It follows from the principle: a write is judged by the statement it carries, and a removal's statement is its path and its list; it cannot hold where that statement leaves it no authority, and nothing outside the statement (which edges its authority happens to pass over, who benefits) is read.
  - **What it gives**, each a hand world (core/test/roles-model/src/paths/tests.rs):
    - a removal listing nothing, by a party its victim granted, is void, raises no dispute and drops nothing (`a_removal_whose_drops_take_its_own_standing_is_void`);
    - the removed party's backdated grant to the remover, timed or not, and its grant to a made-up key that re-grants the remover, are unlisted writes resting on h (or made under one) and fall like any (`nothing_through_the_removed_edge_is_exempt`); a party removed earlier that writes a grant to the remover holds no authority, and nothing reads a write that does not apply;
    - a list naming the removed party's removal of the remover, or of the remover's granter where that drops the remover's grant, voids its removal, so its writer leaves it out; an honest earlier removal of the granter that listed the remover's grant is kept, and stays made (`a_list_keeping_what_unmakes_its_removal_voids_it`);
    - a removal made under a grant a later removal drops keeps its verdict at a node not trusting the later one, and is never applied where it is trusted (its seeds take the grant first): the later removal is shown there as a dispute (`a_removal_whose_author_falls_later_keeps_its_verdict`).
    - a removal whose drops take the grant its author's granter holds is void: the check reads the chain back (`a_lowering_dropping_its_granters_grant_is_void`).
  - **Cost** (modelled: notes/path-model-findings.md 17; counted in A1, notes/redesign-plan.md): a lowering that drops nothing costs its place in the walk; a void one costs its check (the setters along its own authority); an applied one that drops costs its drops and one restart from the earliest of them, once. Honest histories pay a restart per removal that drops, as today; a party holding admin pays a write per restart it forces, and each restart's drops are its own. A void lowering's check grows with the length of its author's grant chain: k void lowerings by the last of a chain of k grants cost k² setters read (k = 100: 20,000), all forced by one party's own 2k role writes. An implementation keeps a verdict per judged lowering and a checkpoint at each restart point; the chain reads can be memoized across checks of lowerings with the same drops on the chain, which this attack has, but not in general (open, A1 brief 7).
  - **Rejected**:
    - *Exempting the writes R's path rests on from its audit.* Read from the held writes, it spares the thief's own: a backdated grant to a made-up key that re-grants the remover, a backdated timed grant to the remover (`paths-whole-exempt` fails safety). Read from the walk, it is circular: what R rests on is decided by what R drops.
    - *Reading as absent a list entry that lowers an edge R rests on.* By edge it is either too narrow (the remover's granter) or reads held writes that do not apply (a removed node's junk grant to the remover strikes the listing of its own removal), and it undoes an honest earlier removal of the granter. By effect at the reader it has no single answer: keeping some of the removed party's writes makes R's authority and keeping others unmakes it, so there is no greatest sublist; the writer, who knows the order it received them in, has one.
    - *Applying a lowering whose own drops take its authority* (`paths-no-void`): a thing is left with no admin, and the thief's removal of the remover that lists nothing removes.
    - *Making a void write have no effect by redoing the walk* (a lowering that made drops and does not apply in the settled walk is void, its drops unmade, the walk redone): the set of void writes is a fixpoint that is not monotone. Chosen by rounds (earliest, latest, all at once) a void write keeps an effect or the thief brings back a party removed earlier; as a consistent set it is not unique and may not exist; in key order with overrules re-resolved a void write still has an effect; each costs a walk of the component per self-voiding write.
- **Removed rules** (argued): "losing a path while another keeps the class is no fall" (every path is audited alone); the Acting check (each hop is the check); the header's `actor`.
- **Kept unchanged**: the edge as restored; lists by commit ID with own covers (modelled: a list naming a cover names what it carries); covers by copies: a valid snapshot's copies show what it carries, so one made by a writer not yet holding a removal carries the removed party's late write for good, until the writer holds the removal (modelled, the accepted window: notes/path-model-findings.md 26; judging carried writes by their facts instead would make every snapshot state each carried commit's signer, `ts` and path); addenda; `until` and self-removal auditing nothing; invites; trust sets, with a trusted write's seeds read as the held edge writes resting on its edge that its list does not name and whose need its value does not meet (modelled); role cycles (validity counts every grant, checked: spec/permissions.md §3 Who rotates).
- **List scope**: for R lowering h, the aspects in question are those of the things the paths through h wrote (role-class and admin-only below admin, every aspect below write); named are the commits through h on them that R's author received (content in a leaf per thing, each role commit by an entry of its own: notes/account-membership-design.md §4, The list), the role commits of R's own path's evidence that pass through h, and the purge requests whose path passes through h; of the role commits, only those R applies with (above, The list is its writer's).
- **Keys do not follow paths.** Who reads, whom a rotation boxes, what a departure forces to rotate: all stay on effective reach over every grant (spec/permissions.md §3). Only validity is path-judged (argued: reading is holding secrets, which travel by every route).

### What changes for users
- **Removing M from A** touches every write whose path passes through (M, A), on A and on everything A reaches, and nothing M wrote by another path.
- **A write whose path is valid and another would also have been** is judged by its own path: it falls with that path's removal unless named, vouched for or re-granted; the other path does not save it (spec/requirements.md Permissions, judged along them alone). Its writer's node is told (spec/permissions.md §6, own commits) and may write it again along a current path. Which path a node states is writer policy; the default is the session's configured path, and the engine prefers the path whose first holder is nearest the signer.
- **Adversarial checks** (argued; the model checks them): a writer cannot pick a path that survives a removal it should not: a removal audits every write through the edge it lowers, and a write through no lowered edge stands only on a path that held at its key and was never explicitly cut, a right the writer holds. A lowering of any edge, descent edges included, audits every path through it, and a path naming an edge the reader holds no write for is unjudgeable, never valid (§2). A path is header data: a re-encryption carries only the thing's path key, which opens the signed entry (notes/path-form.md).
- Possible admins and writers (spec/commits.md §4, purge requests a node cannot fully judge) may read stated paths instead of every grant; not needed for correctness (argued).

### osis-perm
- Validity of a write is a point query per edge of its path (`Edges` with its checkpoints, checked: notes/perm-impl.md Queries) instead of `roles_on` (max over paths, memoized). The walk applies an edge write in O(hops); a demotion finds what it puts in question by the hop index, so `audit_fall` and the forward roles cache go. `first_held` becomes per path.
- Components: things joined when an edge of one is on a path stated by a write on the other; incremental extend, append and shadow placement keep their shape (argued). A component holding a trusted write is walked from scratch (notes/disputes.md, Cost).
- `roles_on` and effective reach stay for keys, sync units and `holders`.
- Expected effect on issues/perm-eval-cost.md: evaluation walks each write's path once (argued; to count, notes/principles.md Check scaling).

### Where the path sits, and bytes
- Header: `signer` (the key thing's tid) replaces `author` and `actor`. A field `path`:
  - one path, its purge-author index then its tids, where every thing's own path is read off it and it tells no reader a roster fact it could not read;
  - else the split form: the purge author in the clear and each thing's path sealed in the header under that thing's path key, which every encryption of its parts carries. Built: notes/path-form.md (rules, what readers learn, bytes).

## 2. Evidence in the sync unit
### What a unit carries
- **Evidence of a sync unit U** (argued; decided as the direction): the least set holding, for every commit with a body under U's sync ID (role commits kept included): the records of every thing on its paths and descents; every held edge write on every edge of those paths (grants, changes, lowerings with their leaves for U's things and their entries, present or absent, for each role commit U holds as evidence); for a lowering of an admin edge, the same for the remaining admin edge it relies on (§5, The last admin); closed under the same for those writes' own paths; and the records of their signers. It is in U's required set beside its bodies, as copies of one role commit's parts on one thing each (with its header and that thing's path; a lowering's copy with its frontier leaves for U's things and for the thing it is on), logged by any node holding them and U (spec/sync-judgement.md §1-§2 Own store, unchanged). Each node computes it from what it holds now, at every change of its holdings or of U's things, never once at logging: when a thing leaves U, what U's remaining bodies still need of that thing's role commits becomes copies, logged by any holder whose view requires them (modelled: notes/evidence-model-findings.md 2, 4).
- **A commit carried under several sync IDs** (a thing in a root's unit and under its own, or in two roots' units) has its evidence under each: the same copies, one blob per (commit, thing) wrapped under each sync ID whose unit needs it, placed where that unit's bodies do not carry the parts (checked for today's certificate copies: spec/sync-judgement.md §1 Certificate blobs).
- It is a function of the commits U holds: when a commit leaves (compacted, forgotten), the paths only it used leave the evidence, and their copies leave with no tombstone, per (commit, thing), so no copy outlives the parts it was sealed for (issues/certificate-copy-never-shrinks.md does not arise).
- **A removal's copy** (decided, Tess: no eager delivery, notes/handoff.md): a removal is logged where it is evidence, under each sync ID whose bodies' paths use its edge (a node holding both it and a write it judges logs it there), and as Role changes travel says (under the removed holder's own sync ID and the sync IDs whose keys open it). A far thing gets it with its next key rotation by a writer holding it (the keyset carries the demotion: spec/crypto.md §4 Carried demotions). Nothing logs a removal for its own sake (notes/a3-spec/README.md, Far delivery).
- **Not every actor that could write**: that costs an entry under every unit a holder reaches per roster change of that holder (a node added included), against spec/requirements.md Cost, and a key handed down to everyone above, which every removal would force to rotate (argued). Additions travel as their writes need them; only removals travel at once.

### The three points
- **(a) Same blob for every reader.** The roles secret today is `kdf("roles", s)` of an epoch's secret; holders at read or above hold it (checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Roles secrets, §5). Handing a holder's roles secret down to the readers of what it holds a role on fails: the set allowed to read it is the union of the readers of everything it reaches, so every removal from any of those would force it to rotate and hand the new secret down again (argued). Instead evidence is a certificate copy: one encryption under a random blob key, one wrap per sync ID under that sync ID's own secret (checked: spec/sync-judgement.md §1 Certificate blobs, spec/wire.md §4 copy section). Readers open the same blob; a removal from U reaches its evidence by U's own rotation; no secret is handed down.
- **(b) Work per roster change.** For every possible actor, every roster change of a holder X (a node added included) would owe an entry under every unit X reaches, transitively: a member of an organization holding roles on 10^5 things owes 10^5 entries (about 153 B each: blob ID, wrap and stamp, notes/thing-cost.md Per entry) per node change. Along stated paths a grant costs nothing until its first use in a unit, where it rides with the writing commit; a removal costs an entry per sync ID carrying a thing the removed signer could write (decided), which the requirements allow a removal and nothing else. Rejected: X's role data under X's own sync ID that readers follow; its readers would need a key held by everyone who reads anything X reaches (the problem of (a)), and a node keeping U unread cannot know which such sync IDs to keep, so a reader served by it alone could not judge (argued).
- **(c) Size.** The paths of a unit's writers and their granters, independent of the size of any organization that reaches it.

### What follows
- **Pending**: a part is unjudgeable while an edge of its path has no held edge write putting it or a record on it is missing; it pends under Pending storage and is examined again as role inputs move (checked for today's form: spec/commits.md §4 Unjudgeable, Pending storage). Its evidence is in the same sync ID's required set, so a complete source has both. A commit judged on missing evidence is never judged invalid for it. A reader holding part of a path's evidence (an earlier lowering not yet arrived) finds a setter on every edge and judges: no rule over the held set alone tells partial from complete evidence, so a verdict is final for a sync ID only after a complete pass of it (spec/sync-status.md §1), as must-not-hold already is; what is shown as settled and a deposit's promotion wait for it (modelled: notes/evidence-model-findings.md 5).
- **Nodes keeping U unread** store and serve evidence entries as any entries (checked: spec/sync-judgement.md §6).
- **Promotion of a deposit** waits for the commit's evidence under the sync ID (spec/sync-inbox.md §4, reworded).
- **What goes** of spec/permissions.md §4 and spec/sync-judgement.md §1-§2: chain certificates over any path from the author; "closed under authors"; each path thing's current `keys.0` commits as evidence (judging needs no KEM key; a signer's key is in its record); account rotations as header-only copies, thing lists and list blobs; the duty to log one's account's chain on first logging under a sync ID. **What stays**: record blobs; a grant naming a holder travelling under the holder's sync ID with the target's boxes and `sync.0`, and a holder's public keys under the target's sync ID for sealing (§3, keys, not judgement); destroyed things' frozen role data; Stranded holder keys.
- Readers see a holder's members only as far as paths show them, so `holders` shows less to non-members than today (argued intended: those outside a holder see the holder act).

### Readers hidden from each other
issues/readers-see-readers.md wants an option under which reader-level grants open only for holders at write or above. Checked only for conflicts with this note and notes/account-membership-design.md (argued; not designed):
- **No conflict with validity or evidence**: content needs write at every hop, so every edge a content path names is a writer-level grant, which every reader still opens. An edge write is sealed for writers only when the value it sets is at or below read and the entry it ends was; a raise above read or a lowering from above read stays open to readers, so a reader's walk sees every write that sets or ends an edge it needs. The last-admin rule reads admin edges only.
- **No conflict with the public actor**: its grant is a reader-level grant like any; whether a unit is public can be tested by anyone holding its sync ID, hidden roster or not (§3).
- **Two points to settle in that design**: (1) an act made at read through a holder (a history add by a member whose role on the holder is read) shows the target's readers that member's reader-level edge on the holder as evidence, so such acts are left to writers or reveal their maker; (2) a removal is logged only where the removed signer could write, invite or make an enabled reader-level act (decided), so it names the account to no reader of a unit the account only reads; where a reader-level act brings a copy, hidden readers want it sealed for writers.

## 3. Public things
### The public actor
A well-known key thing **public**: its seed is a constant of the protocol, so every node derives its signing key, KEM key, role secrets and static entry (as for any key actor, checked: spec/crypto.md §3 Key actor). **A thing is public by granting public read** (Tess). One edge on the thing however many read it, and nothing on it showing where its owner writes. **Every thing's keys count as opening what public reads** (Tess; a clause of spec/sync-set.md §2 T's keys open), so a strong reference brings a public thing into the referrer's sync unit like any thing the referrer can read: carried under the referrer's sync ID as well as its own, held by a node keeping the referrer unread too. It is a special case and may be one (Tess); every difference from an ordinary key thing is listed here and nowhere else, so that the whole set can be weighed, regretted or replaced together if public readers are ever redone. A new difference found in building is added to this list before it is built.

Special cases (argued), each judged by every node alike from the write or commit alone:
1. **A signature by the public key counts for nothing**: a commit signed by it is invalid whole and is not taken (stored, promoted, relayed as a deposit's content). A key everyone holds says nothing about who signed. This one rule closes every act a key thing makes for itself: leaving (a self-removal would un-publish anything), lowering its own grant, writing its own `keys.0`, a redemption, an anonymous external fire, a history add signed as public, a connection proof used for anything but entering public units.
2. **An edge write naming public sets at most read**: one setting write or admin, or an invite, is bad input. Those roles' secrets are delete keys and the unit's stamping key, which would let anyone delete or store at a node keeping the unit unread (checked: spec/crypto.md §7 Levels; spec/requirements.md, Data a node cannot read).
3. **Public takes every grant**: no write path to it, no external signal, no inbox, no delivery (it is externally grantable by definition; §4's refusal never applies).
4. **Constants**: its record, tid and keys are built in: no evidence copy of its record, no `keys.0`, and a box to it, the entry box of a step included, takes the symmetric form under a key derived from its role secret (argued: every node holds the recipient's secret, so a KEM ciphertext buys nothing). It has no sync ID (a key actor, checked: spec/sync-ids.md §2): a grant it takes travels under no sync ID for it, and whoever acts as public enters the target by public's static entry in the target's own steps.
5. **A departure at or below read forces no rotation while public reads**: the departed party still opens the role as public, so the required rotation would stop nothing (derived from the rule's purpose, spec/permissions.md §3 Required rotation; a rule-text exception all the same). For the same reason no step of the unit's sync ID is owed for such a departure: the departed party still opens it by public's entry. Departures above read rotate their roles as always; removing public rotates read, boxing the named holders, and is due a step.

Every act open to a reader or a key thing, for a party whose only role is through public:

| act | may it | by what rule | what a node keeping the unit unread checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| read | yes | holds public's read secret, the box to public | nothing |
| enter the unit | yes | public's static entry is in the claim set as any key reader's (checked: spec/sync-ids.md §3 Claim set); no rule of its own | the claim, as any; it can test for the public entry, so it knows the unit is public, as anyone holding the sync ID does |
| leave, lower the grant | no | special case 1 | nothing (it stores no commit it cannot be given by a writer or the deposit budget) |
| be removed, by the thing's admins | yes | an ordinary explicit removal, with its rotation of read | nothing |
| deposit | within the budget, like any reader | the deposit key derives from a seed readers hold (checked: spec/sync-inbox.md §3); what is deposited is valid only if it would be from a stranger: an enabled external fire | signature and budget, as today |
| history add | no | it is a commit; the party's own signer holds no role, and public's signature counts for nothing | nothing |
| external fire | as any outsider, under its own signer, where the signal is enabled (§4) | unchanged | deposit signature and budget |
| be granted write, admin or an invite | no | special case 2 | nothing |
| rotation of the read epoch | boxed like any holder, symmetric form | ordinary; special case 5 removes rotations that serve nothing | nothing |
| holder in boxes and claim sets | ordinary, with a fixed keyset (no stale boxes: a key actor's keyset never changes, checked: spec/crypto.md §3) | | |
| stranded (left out by a rotator that did not hold its grant) | asks any connected writer, naming public as the holder to re-seal | the receiver checks the holder itself (checked: spec/permissions.md §4 Stranded) | nothing |
| serve the unit to others, answer offers | yes | it holds the unit like any reader | |

- **The deposit budget of a public thing is open to everyone**: no special case; it is the reader budget the requirements already call best effort (checked: spec/requirements.md, Data a node cannot read). What waits when it is full: named readers' self-removals and history adds reaching nodes that keep the unit unread (they still reach reading peers directly), and external fires where enabled, which are exposed the same way on any thing that enables one. Tess would rather public could not deposit, but only if that were a check in one place; it is not (the seed lies beside the keysets, which every reader opens, so keeping it from public means moving it), so it stays open.
- **What follows from ordinary rules, to say in the API**: a public thing's dependents are public; a thing that grants a public thing read is read by everyone through it; an inviter at read may publish (it may already invite any number).
- **Paths never pass through public**: nothing holds a role on a key thing, and its signature counts for nothing, so no valid path names it.

### Profiles and contact-only information (the schema's)
- A **profile** is a schema concept: a thing with a name and a picture, public, with a sync ID of its own. A writer puts a strong reference to its own profile, as ordinary data, in the thing it writes in (an aspect the schema names) (decided). The write placing it states its actors, which is the actor naming its profile, checkable by any reader without reading the actor.
- It is reached by the reference: every thing's keys open it (public reads it), so the strong brings it into the referring thing's sync unit, carried under that sync ID too (one blob, a wrap per sync ID), and a reader served only by a node keeping the referring unit unread still fetches it. Nothing about profiles is in evidence and no rule pulls things in. Cost: each commit on the profile is an entry and stamp under every unit referencing it; profiles change rarely.
- Rejected: granting the referring thing read on the profile (its roster would list every thing its owner writes in); a ticket or a link's secret in the reference; proving whose profile it is from the profile's side (by "minted with the actor", which leans on things being minted empty and on the mint admin differing from other admins; by "the actor is its admin", since anyone can mint a thing, fill it and hand its admin over).
- **Contact-only information** (decided): the dial list is in a thing of its own, read by those its owner shares with; it is the schema's where it sits and who reads it. Sync still needs addresses: the dial aspect stays core's, and a node dials what it reads in that aspect on any thing it holds; which units a connection then shares is found by the connection, as today (checked: spec/sync-opens.md §4) (argued).
- One profile blob under every unit its owner writes in links those units at a node keeping them unread: issues/sync-units-linked-at-rest.md.

### The card: where a thing's inbox is named
To grant to a thing A it holds no role on, a party needs: A's tid; A's current KEM public key for each role granted (to seal the boxes); and, for delivery, A's inbox I: its tid and record (the dedup secret a sealed fire needs), its read KEM key, its sync ID and its deposit seed (checked: spec/sync-inbox.md §2-§3). Today A's `keys.0` holds all of it and names the inbox, and travels to the readers of everything A holds a role on (checked: spec/crypto.md §3; `Engine::inbox_thing`).
- **The card** (argued; name open, here `card.0`): a core aspect holding exactly that material for one thing A, written on any thing A's members choose. **It counts as A's only where the write placing it states A as an actor** (the last thing of its path is A, or the thing it is on is A itself): the same check as a profile reference. So a stranger's thing claiming to be A's card, with the stranger's keys, is no card of A: its write was not made through A.
- **Who can grant to A is who reads a card of A**: put on a public thing (the profile, by the first-party schema) anyone can; put on a contact-only thing, the contacts can. A's members need no card.
- **An admin-only aspect of A names its inbox and its cards** (replacing the inbox field of `keys.0`): A itself by default, another thing A administers where storing and forwarding A's messages should not mean keeping A's whole unit, as for an account. A node rotating A's read role, or I's deposit seed, writes the cards it can; a card it cannot write is stale until a member that can does, and a box sealed to a superseded keyset is stale as any (checked: spec/crypto.md §4 Stale).
- **What an outsider holds, and how it gets it**: a reference to a thing carrying A's card (from A's profile, a link, a contact). It enters that thing, checks the card's write, and has everything; nothing waits on A.
- **A holder's keys under the target** (kept from today, spec/permissions.md §4): the grant commit carries the card's key material into the target's role data, so every node that rotates the target can seal to A. That is the only place a thing that is not externally grantable publishes KEM keys: where its own members made it a holder (decided).

## 4. External signals and grants from outside
### The routes (checked: spec/sync-inbox.md §2-§5, spec/sync-judgement.md §2)
A grant naming holder A reaches A, its granter holding nothing on A, by: (1) a box sealed to A's KEM key and a sealed `ticket@~0` fire on A's inbox; (2) a deposit of the role commit under A's own sync ID, by anyone holding A's deposit seed, which A's writers then log as their own copy; (3) a ticket for the target in any value one of A's members reads. Route 3 is only handing over a secret, which no rule can take back, and makes A a holder nowhere. Routes 1 and 2 are closed by the rule below. An unkept fire today is valid, joins listeners and spends the deposit budget (checked: spec/sync-inbox.md §3 Budget).

### The rule (ready for the spec; argued)
- **The setting**: a thing's admin-only `retention^.0` entries naming external signals (spec/commits.md §5 The setting). A thing's admins **enable** an external signal by an entry of its own naming it, with any keep duration (none: taken, out of history at once); the default entry never enables. None is taken otherwise.
- **External fires** (specified: spec/permissions.md §5.2 External fires; modelled: held-model `ExternalFires::Enabled`): a fire of external signal `s` on N valid by its author's authority (a writer's, frontiers, addenda and re-grant included) is valid whatever the setting; any other is valid iff N's **current setting** (spec/commits.md §5 In force: the newest held valid one by stamp) enables `s`. Judged by N's readers; nothing else about the fire changes. An unenabled fire is invalid: it reaches no listener and is never promoted, and leaves the budget at its expiry or by clearing. Re-judged whenever the current setting changes, as any delta whose ground changes.
  - **Current, not "in force at the fire's key"**: a fire's `ts` is its writer's word, so judging by the setting at its key lets anyone stamp a fire back into an enabled window after a disabling (valid, promoted; a deposit key rotation is not built yet to stop delivery), and a fire between two settings turns invalid once the older body drops as out of history (the setting's own retention); both are negative controls in held-model. With the current setting the keep entry is the switch exactly (Tess: one rule per signal): disabling withdraws earlier strangers' fires, which no setting kept anyway (no entry, no history); enabling admits earlier ones still held.
  - Forks: which settings are valid is the role timeline's under the trust set, so nodes holding the same commits under the same trust set judge every fire alike, in any arrival order.
  - A node keeping N unread judges nothing new: it takes a fire's deposit by signature and budget (spec/sync-inbox.md §2); a writer never promotes an unenabled one, so it goes by clearing or expiry.
- **Externally grantable**: A is externally grantable at a key iff its inbox (§3) enables `ticket@~0` there. One switch with taking tickets (decided).
- **What enabling publishes**: the cards of A (§3): KEM keys, and the inbox's sync ID and deposit seed. The deposit seed and sync ID appear nowhere else outside the thing's own role data, so a stranger holds them only through a card. Disabling writes the cards empty and rotates the deposit seed (an admin may at any time, checked: spec/crypto.md §3), with the deposit-only step that follows (checked: spec/permissions.md §3 Sync ID role keys).
- **What a node keeping the thing unread enforces**: deposits by signature and budget, as now (checked: spec/sync-inbox.md §3). With no signal enabled no stranger holds a current deposit key, so a stranger's deposit fails its signature; a stale key stops at the next deposit-only step. It judges nothing else and learns nothing of the setting.
- **What A takes**: a role commit with an edge write naming A on X is **taken** by A iff it states a path to A giving write on A (beside X's path, in the same header), or A is externally grantable at its key and one of A's writers accepted it (a reference to X in A's unit: a strong where every node holding A should hold X, checked for today's accept: spec/sync-judgement.md §2 role commits naming an actor; a weak where each node holds X by a strong of its own, argued). Only a taken commit is in A's unit's required set with X's boxes to A and X's sync ID; A's members take secrets through A, and count A's role on X, only by taken grants. An untaken grant stays in X's roster: X's readers judge X by X's timeline alone, so a member of A may still write X through A (argued harmless: the member could act as A anyway).
- **Without the switch: write on A gives the right to grant to it** (decided), for a new thing and an existing one alike: the granter states its write path to A, boxes A by A's own secrets (symmetric) or its KEM keys, and logs the grant under A's unit as a writer, where A's members take it. No ticket fire is needed. A reader of A may not: readers only read (spec/requirements.md Permissions). Cost, stated: a writer can attach outside things, adding to what members read and to the keys that change when a member is removed.
- **The granter's node, refused**: an edge write naming A is refused locally, before signing, when the node states no write path to A and holds no card of A whose inbox enables tickets. The error names the setting ("this thing does not take grants from outside it"), never keys. Nothing is written on X.
- **No grant waits on the grantee's side**: a grant that is written is valid on X at once and complete (its boxes sealed from the card or A's secrets); only delivery of the ticket is pending, shown as such (checked: spec/sync-inbox.md §2 delivery).
- Ordinary signals have no switch (decided). How an app enables a signal, and the default per kind of thing, are the schema library's.

## 5. Core without accounts
### No account root
The account root exists because an account is a key actor: it has no roster, no sync ID and no data; its roster is read from the signing chain; its secrets rotate only inside the account's rotations; and things minted with it cannot be its dependents, since it is in no sync unit (checked: spec/permissions.md §1, §3; spec/sync-set.md §1). With the account an ordinary independent thing A, each reason goes: A has a roster (its signers), a unit, data and dependents, and rotates as any thing. So home is A, and the account's private things are A's dependents (argued).
- **To write down**: a role on A reaches everything A holds a role on anywhere (spec/requirements.md Permissions). Granting anyone a role on home grants all of the account's access, so a space to share is another realm. An account holding a role on another account (a linked identity) is this fact used on purpose.
- **"A thing minted with an account as admin is independent at birth" goes** (checked: spec/permissions.md §3; issues/behaviour-depends-on-own-keys.md): a thing minted with A is a dependent of A like any.

### The last admin
Removing a thing's last admin is refused for every thing unless the write says the thing may go adminless (decided), as a rule every node judges alike.
- **The rule** (modelled: roles-model `paths`, `LastAdmin::Named`): an unmarked edge write lowering or removing a standing admin edge of T is applied only where the **remaining admin** it relies on is, as the pass stands before the write's commit, a standing admin edge of T other than the one it lowers. The remaining admin is the last edge of the write's own path (its final hop where that joins its last member to T in one edge) where that is such an edge (an admin removing another: nothing is named); otherwise the write names the holder of one (a leave, a lowering of one's own edge, a removal by a timed admin, a path reaching T by a descent of two steps or more). A lowering judged in the walk is also void where its drops, or an earlier write of its commit, leave that remaining admin standing no more (§1, `keeps`; modelled: notes/path-model-findings.md 29). A write carrying the mark relies on none. A **standing admin edge** is a direct admin edge on T, the record edge included, with no `until`, whoever its holder.
- **Local to a reader's evidence**: the rule reads two edges, the lowered and the relied-on, and the edge writes on the second are evidence of the lowering wherever it travels (§2). So a reader holding one unit's evidence judges it as T's members do. Reading every admin edge of T is not local: a reader of a unit where the other admin never wrote holds the leave and not that admin's grant, judges the leave invalid, and shows the leaver's later writes (notes/path-model-findings.md, 8). It follows from the principle: the write states what its validity rests on, the admin that remains included, and what checks the statement is in the unit. Cost: a lowering relying on an admin that turns out removed by an earlier-stamped write is not applied though a third admin stands; its author is told and writes it again.
- **Why no `until`**: a timed admin grant is a removal already written. Counting it would let a thing go adminless on a date with no marked write; not counting it means the last standing admin can be removed, or given an `until`, only by a marked write, so a timed grant ending never strands a thing that was not knowingly put in that position. A thing whose only admins are timed was put there by a marked write.
- **Why direct edges only**: "someone can still act as admin" would make a removal on a holder M invalid by what M administers, a cost and a dependence in the size of what M reaches (spec/requirements.md Cost), and nothing in osis knows whether a key is lost. Each thing's own rule covers one level. Not prevented, stated: an admin edge held by a destroyed thing, by a thing whose only admins are each other in a cycle with no signer among them, or by a signer whose key is lost.
- **A function of the held writes** (modelled: `the_last_admin_rule_is_a_function_of_the_held_writes`, and the search's alike check): it reads the walk's state at the write, like authority. It flips when an earlier grant arrives, as validity may; under a dispute it is read in each node's own walk, per trust set; a write it stops is not applied, so it audits nothing and, trusted, is void (§1, A removal is judged in the world its list makes). So a removed party's backdated timed grant to the remover, kept, would stop the removal: it is an unlisted write resting on the removed edge and falls with the rest.
- **Two admins removing each other at once** (established from spec/permissions.md §5.1 steps 2-4 and Trust set, and notes/role-timeline.md, mutual removal resolves to the earlier timestamp): in the plain walk the earlier-stamped removal applies, leaving its author; the later one finds its author without authority and applies nothing. It is raised as a dispute; on the side trusting it, its seeds drop the earlier removal and its own author remains. Neither side is ever adminless and the rule is never met. Two admins each *leaving* at once (self-removals, each naming the other as the admin that remains): the earlier-stamped applies; the later relies on an edge that stands no more and is not applied, so its author is still admin and is told, as for any write that turns out invalid.
- **A dependent thing** becomes independent first, as for any role change on it (checked: spec/permissions.md §3).
- **As apps see it** (Tess): the API returns an error where the node can tell; a removal made on an incomplete view goes out, and once nodes hold the writes that make it the last admin's it is judged not applied and the admin stands again, with the role's listeners told.
- **A thing that is adminless anyway** (marked, or by the cases not prevented): admin edges and admin-only aspects change no more, so its settings, its enabled external signals and its cards are frozen; nobody issues a step of its sync ID (checked: spec/permissions.md §3 Sync ID role keys, only an admin issues a step), so a holder that left keeps opening it at nodes keeping it unread; holders keep reading and writing as granted (checked: spec/permissions.md §1). Not frozen: writers still rotate the roles at or below write, self-removals and finalizes below admin still apply, and a holder with an invite still invites (checked: spec/permissions.md §2, §3 Who rotates). spec/permissions.md §1 says "edges, secrets and keys change no more", which is stronger than those rules give. Nothing recovers it.

## 6. Model plan
- Adapt roles-model `joint` (several things, record edges, holder things, mutual roles, commit-ID ties) and `edges` (`until`, invites, self-removal, links): each write carries a path; validity, attribution, demotion, lists and re-grant by path; effective role leaves validity. Then `addenda` and `trust` (seeds by edge).
- **Path properties**: termination; input-order independence; nothing dropped with nobody offline; safety (a write through a lowered edge stands only if the lowering's author or an addendum writer holding the needed right names it, or its path is re-granted); path choice (a hostile writer choosing any path gains nothing over the best honest one); self-removal by path end audits nothing; a path's validity is unchanged by a thing on it becoming independent.
- **Purge author**: a request holds the author right iff its path contains the named thing and gives write; a request by author matches only deltas naming exactly that thing; a signer removed from A loses the right over edits authored as A; no index gives a right the request's path lacks.
- **A removal judged in the world its list makes**: each lowering judged once at its key; a removal whose own drops take its author's authority is void under every trust set and raises no dispute; the removed party regains nothing by any write aimed at the remover or at an edge above it; at a node trusting nothing every reading unlike the trusting side's is shown as a dispute. Negative controls: a self-voiding lowering applying (`paths-no-void`), the remover listing all it received (`-listed`), the held chain exempt (`-exempt`), the remover keeping in key order (`-key-order`), the audit over the applied writes (`-audit-applied`). Open: notes/path-model-findings.md, Open.
- **Last admin**: a reader holding one unit's evidence judges every lowering of an admin edge as T's members do (negative control: the rule reading every admin edge of T); with the rule, no schedule of unmarked writes leaves a thing that had a standing admin without one (mutual explicit removals, mutual leaves, a lowering to a timed grant, a removal concurrent with the only other admin's removal by a third); a marked write does; the rule's verdict is the same in any input order and differs across trust sets only as the walks do; termination. Negative control: counting timed grants as standing (a thing goes adminless by a date with no marked write).
- **Public**: a commit signed by the public key changes nothing on any thing, whatever it carries (a self-removal, a grant, a fire); an edge write giving public more than read applies nothing; removing public by an admin un-publishes.
- **External grants**: a grant by a party with no write path to A and no enabled inbox is taken nowhere; one with either is; disabling stops deposits by strangers at the next step; a card whose write does not state A is no card of A.
- Differential against osis-perm as the joint and edges models are today (notes/perm-impl.md Differentials).
- Evidence completeness (modelled: held-model `evidence`, notes/evidence-model-findings.md): a reader holding only U (its required set computed from its own holdings) judges every write of U as a node holding everything does, a thing carried under several sync IDs included; a removal's copy with its reach is judged required by a reader holding only U; a removal logged under a unit its signer never wrote stops that signer's first write there; evidence leaves with the commits that needed it.
