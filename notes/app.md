# The first-party app
> Summary: Design direction for the first-party osis app over osis and schema/ (decided with the owner, pre-prototype): data-oriented UX (things with facet views, lenses, tags as filters), the look (color from the user's tags, flatish), state kept in osis including view state, the layer split (osis state, toolkit-free actions crate, Masonry driver and widgets), the kit/app split (osis-kit toolkit and osis-app in their own Cargo workspace), the toolkit decision (Masonry without Xilem) with the alternatives weighed, the platform work accepted, the prototype order, and the prototype as built (kit module map, the app's data model and screens, patches, what it showed).

## Direction (owner)
- Fresh and timeless, not a bundle of generic apps over osis types. Bright colors, flatish but not distinctly flat, its own look rather than the native aesthetic.
- Oriented around data, not functionality: no "tab for notes, tab for calendar". Views show whatever combination of things they can meaningfully place.
- Responsive and lightweight on every device; webview is out. Native first, web later.
- Build first in something light, simple and running everywhere; if the UX suffers, rebuild differently. Taking on platform work (Rust embedding, possibly an iOS shell of our own) is accepted for the sake of the right foundations in text, accessibility and low-level control.
- Convergent design, not a mobile/desktop split: one layout keyed on width and input kind, phone layout designed first, desktop adds columns. Touch on desktop supported.

## Model
- **The thing is the unit.** No `kind` aspect exists (notes/tags.md); a thing is whatever its aspects make it, and may be several kinds (a meeting note is note and event).
- **Facet views.** Kind-specific views exist because aspects do not map 1:1 onto UI. Opening a thing picks the best-matching view for the aspects present (event aspects plus `/body` → the calendar view, which includes the freeform text area; `/body` alone → the notes view). When several views apply and none covers every aspect, the thing offers them side by side (tabs or a scroll list; not decided, not important).
- **Schema fallback view**, behind a context menu: shows and edits every aspect of a thing from its schema-declared types alone. Covers things whose schema the app has but no view for.
- **Create** offers a kind selector (note, event, contact, ...) plus "empty thing".
- **Lenses** are few and fixed: a filter plus an arrangement over all things, each admitting only what it can place. Time (event times; faint creation and edit ticks for the rest, from commit timestamps), People (contacts and profiles plus what links to them), Tags (the graph as navigation, with color), Search as the primary way in, and a home lens mixing today, recently touched things and people. User structure lives in tags and links, not in lenses.
- **Tags filter.** Tags also narrow a lens or find things within a collection.
- **Every thing shows** its tags, what links to it (`db.backlinks`), who can see it (roster) and its history (undo). Placing into a shared tag confirms the reach (notes/tags.md guideline).
- Backlinks from markdown (`/body` has no tid reference syntax): the owner is taking that to the library side. Not an app concern until then.

## Look
Themes (notes/theming.md): one main colour from the content drives accents, selection and a subtle background; the rest is a theme's parameters. Superseded the earlier "chrome calm, no decorative gradients" draft.

## State in osis
- All app state lives in osis; widgets hold only what is too hot to be data (drag positions, caret blink, animation).
- Durable view state (open things, lens, filters, layout, window management if it grows) as things in a person-private group: a view can be moved between devices, and between apps or toolkits, and saved and restored. Not specified or built on the first pass, but the architecture assumes it.
- Ephemeral view state (selection, expanded panels, scroll anchor) as ephemeral things and aspects (notes/ephemeral.md): in memory, local unless placed in a synced group; hot live state across devices is synced ephemeral data, not commits.
- Needed from the API (asks for the library side): in-memory things must support watches, queries and backlinks exactly like stored things; and a mode where the app pumps the watch dispatcher on its own (UI) thread, removing the cross-thread hop and ordering questions.

## Layers
- **State**: osis (above).
- **Actions** (in `app/`): a toolkit-free Rust crate. Widgets emit intents (open, tag, share, edit); the crate turns them into osis writes and holds all behavior and policy, including the share confirmation's rule.
- **Driver** (in `kit/`): Masonry-specific glue. Watches fire, the driver edits the retained widget tree through Masonry's mutation API. Two pieces written once: marshaling watch callbacks onto the UI thread (until the dispatcher-pump mode exists), and a keyed list reconciler turning a changed query result into insert/move/remove on a list widget.
- **Widgets** (in `kit/`, product views in `app/`): our own look as Masonry widgets.
- A toolkit swap rewrites driver and widgets only; state and behavior survive. The earlier idea of a separate "view model" crate dissolves into the view-state schema plus the actions crate.

## Web mockup
`app/mockup/` (plain HTML/CSS/JS, no deps, no osis; serve the directory, e.g. `python3 -m http.server`) explores look and layout before building in kit. Owner brief: things and scopes (tags, views) are fungible tiles in one list or grid; an ordered favorites list; one search over names, content and scopes; with a view open and room enough, a left bar holds search and favorites. The look should feel like what 1996, 2006, 2016 and 2026 thought 2026 would be: colored gradient backgrounds or abstract shapes, a little Apple liquid glass (never at a perf cost), almost no Material. Layout in style.css; themes are parameters (themes.js, notes/theming.md) that engine.css turns into its custom properties.

Owner direction so far:
- Themes: wanted; every theme has light and dark (mode is separate from the theme: Light/Dark/Auto).
- One main colour in the UI at any time, from the content (the open thing's colour, else the first among what it holds or links; the theme's default on home). Accent, selection and background derive from it.
- The background follows theme and content: the theme owns the engine (what is drawn), the content picks the colour and the arrangement (seeded from the tid, varied slightly by kind, e.g. calendar vs note).
- Backgrounds subtle: shading, highlights and variations of the one colour, not big splotches of several colours.
- Less UI: fewer bordered elements, simpler backgrounds.
- Themes (owner-approved set): Frost, Glass (the earlier Aero with Dusk merged in), Workstation, Memphis, Slate (square, flat, modern), Cel (flat, white or dark grey, anime-ish). Older looks are in git history.
- Motion: almost none. Only unobtrusive fades, or motion that adds clarity. The mockup fades the main colour and nothing else.

## Themes
A theme is a set of parameters, not code: notes/theming.md (the system, its goals, the parameter list). The mockup's engine.css and themes.js are its reference implementation.

## kit and app (owner)
- An app's own per-person data (favorites, view state, themes) lives in an internal group found by scanning home's subgroups for the app's marker aspect, reading the union and writing to the smallest tid (owner: a well-known per-app thing is incoherent, since a tid depends on its owner's key; the scan is the convention).
Two top-level projects, `kit/` (package `osis-kit`) and `app/` (`osis-app`).
- **osis-kit**: the first-party UI toolkit over Masonry (or another base if Masonry fails us). Holds windowing and platform shells (the iOS shell too), the osis-Masonry driver, the look (theme tokens, tag tinting, card styling), common widgets (markdown editor, text, lists), generic osis widgets needing only the osis API (schema fallback view, who-can-see display, share confirmation dialog), and the patches we carry to low-level crates. A small special-purpose app that looks and feels like the main one depends on kit alone.
- kit does not abstract over the osis API. Over Masonry: no wrapper for now; kit re-exports it (`kit::masonry`, one pinned version) and apps use its types directly. Design an abstraction only if we actually change base, knowing both sides.
- **osis-app**: facet views, lenses, home, create flow, the actions crate, the view-state schema.
- **Own Cargo workspace** for kit and app, depending on core by path: keeps the GUI stack (wgpu, Vello, winit) out of core's builds, gate and feature unification, and puts `[patch]` entries for forked crates at the GUI workspace root. Costs a second `target/` per worktree. Machine-load limits (notes/coordination.md) still count GUI agents.
- Gate split by changed paths (notes/build.md, Components pay for themselves).

## Toolkit: Masonry without Xilem
Masonry is a retained widget tree designed to sit under other frontends; Xilem's job is to own app state and diff it into Masonry. With osis owning the state and delivering fine-grained changes (watches per aspect, live queries over a visible range), Xilem solves a problem we do not have. What we get: Vello (2D, GPU compute; vello_hybrid for low-end GPUs and WebGL, vello_cpu as a no-GPU fallback), Parley (the best Rust text stack: rich layout, IME), AccessKit designed in, a renderer abstraction, and a funded multi-contributor org.

Costs: pre-1.0 with breaking releases (the layout system changed in Q1 2026): pin versions, upgrade deliberately, keep the driver small so churn lands in one place. Thinner docs than Slint, so agents read source. Small stock widget set: we build our own anyway.

Alternatives weighed:
- **Slint**: the most mature on all six targets today (1.15 in Feb 2026 brought Android/iOS safe areas and virtual keyboard; viewers in both app stores; mobile is Rust-only), excellent tooling and docs, tiny runtime, software renderer, royalty-free license for apps (verify terms). Against: UI in a separate DSL, drawing limited to paths, plain-text editing only, less low-level control. Would have been the pick under "light and simple now" alone.
- **Makepad**: the only Rust toolkit shipping web plus all five native targets from one codebase (1.0 ~April 2025; Robrix, Moly), shader-level custom drawing by default. Against: thin docs, small team, unusual DSL, no accessibility, needs a GPU, no 2026 release notes found.
- **Xilem** on top of Masonry: duplicates osis's reactivity.
- **egui**: lightest and everywhere, but the look is hard to escape and mobile text input is weak. **iced**, **GPUI**: desktop only. **Dioxus native**: immature, mobile is webview. **Compose Multiplatform**: Kotlin, JVM desktop. Flutter and webviews excluded by the owner.

## Platform work accepted
- Desktop (Linux, Windows, macOS): winit, works today. Start here; test with the gui-testing skill.
- Android: winit plus android-activity; IME is the rough spot and under active Linebender work (IME moving onto ui-events); AccessKit has an Android backend.
- iOS: winit's backend is thinly exercised, IME weak, no AccessKit backend. Expect a UIKit shell of our own hosting the render surface and feeding touch, keyboard and VoiceOver into Masonry.
- Web: later; needs WebGPU for Vello proper, vello_hybrid/vello_cpu for a canvas fallback.

## Text
Parley's plain editor exists; rich editing does not. The markdown editor is ours: an editor over Parley layouts with styled spans, first as a styled-source editor (headings larger, links colored, markup visible), not WYSIWYG. Also the one place a plain-text fallback is acceptable early.

## Next: the app prototype (handoff from the UX/planning session)
- **Owner, during the build**: do not copy the mockup closely; it was mainly a test bed for the look and the theme spec. The app leans simpler and less distracting and may choose its own structure and UX: make it work and be sensible, with at least a basic theming implementation (a subset of parameters to start; the six defaults plus light/dark/auto ideally). The owner iterates on details later.
Owner: a new coordinator runs the buildout. Goal: something the owner can play with and iterate on; it is a major test of both kit and core.
- **Scope (suggested v1)**, Linux desktop, the mockup as visual reference (not pixel-perfect; the app will diverge): home (search, ordered favorites, new); with room, a rail (search, favorites) beside the open thing, else home and a back button; things and scopes as one tile kind; views by aspects for notes, events (schema/event.osis), contacts (schema/contact.osis) and tags (items, subtags), plus the calendar-week, people, tags and recent views; each thing shows tags, who can see it and when it changed; search over names, bodies and scopes by scanning; the main colour from the content; the theme engine (notes/theming.md) with the six defaults and light/dark/auto. A demo-data option so there is something to play with.
- **Owner decisions**: UX gets figured out along the way (iterate once there is something to play with); the builder updates and refactors kit as needed (Masonry upgrade allowed); core is never edited, gaps become `core-ask` issues; start without blur (panelBlur unused for now); the app's own data (favorites, theme, view state) lives in its internal group found by scanning home; TextSession caret and resurrection gaps are core's, later; per-screen view state can be ephemeral now that it has landed.
- Open toolkit checks: backgrounds in Vello (gradients, sweep gradients, masks; beams uses a blur), bundling the OFL theme fonts through Parley's font collection.

## Prototype order
1. Linux desktop: a Thing view with the facet-selection logic, the Tags lens, view state in osis from day one.
2. An Android build within the first few iterations so the convergent layout stays honest.
3. Time and People lenses, search, the schema fallback view.
4. iOS shell, web, durable view-state spec.

## Prototype (as built)
`osis-app DIR [--server ADDR]... [--demo] [--theme ID] [--light|--dark]` (kit/ workspace; Masonry `=0.4.0`, masonry_winit `=0.4.0`: wgpu 26, Vello 0.6, Parley 0.6, winit 0.30). Milestones 1-3 of the handoff scope: home, rail, tiles, demo data, the theme engine, facet views with tags, reach and history (`--theme ID`, `--auto|--light|--dark` set the same choice as the picker).

**app**:
- `data` (actions, toolkit-free): the app group (marker `/osis-app/app`, an item and subgroup of home, smallest tid wins, made with the four view things on first use) holds every thing the app makes, the favorites (`/osis-app/favorites: list<tid>`, edited with `edit`) and per-device navigation (`/osis-app/open: map<node, tid> [ephemeral]`, gone on restart: the app starts at home). Tags are the group's subgroups too, so their items sync. `Kind` by aspects (view, `/tag`, any `/event/`, any `/contact/`, else note); `Tile` (name, sub line, colour); colour = `/tag.color`, else `/osis-app/color`, else the first tag's; `main_color` per notes/theming.md; `things()` scans the app groups and, transitively, tags; `search` scans names, bodies, places and contact fields (scopes, things, mentions with snippets, things in matching scopes); views (calendar, people, tags, recent) are things with `/osis-app/view`, so they are tiles and favorites like tags. `tags_of` takes direct placements only (`groups_of` is transitive through subgroups). Event times are floating wall times shown as written (no zone handling yet). Two devices racing (review, app/tests/it/sync): `things()` keeps one view per kind (smallest tid) and `favorites()` maps the other group's views onto those; `toggle_favorite` appends to the main group's list and removes from every group's (no copying: copying the other group's entries into the main one brought back a favorite removed concurrently, test `unfavorite_survives_a_concurrent_first_write`); a reorder (`set_favorites`) moves every entry to the main group, so a concurrent unfavorite of an entry held elsewhere can still come back; a device's open entry is found on any group and moved to the main one. Reads go through `Data::get`, where a value of another shape (synced data can hold anything, or a newer enum case) reads as absent; a screen that still fails to build goes home. `delete` also drops subgroup edges (`groups_of` follows membership, not subgroup edges; a deleted tag stayed as an empty note). `opened()` is `None` for a thing gone from `things()` (deleted elsewhere; `knows` stays true after a delete). `--demo` keeps existing favorites.
- The look is per person on the app group (`/osis-app/theme`: a built-in id, `/osis-app/mode`: auto, light, dark), chosen at home's foot (six theme rows, three mode rows). Auto reads the window's system theme once and follows `on_theme_changed`.
- Things (milestone 3): `create(NewKind)` makes a note, an event (next full hour, one hour), a person (an empty `/contact/names`) or a tag (a group with the next `TAG_COLORS` colour, a subgroup of the app group); "New" offers the four as chips. `add_tag`/`remove_tag` place and unplace; `reach(t)` walks the owner and the rosters (through shared-tag actors, until-less edges only) down to people, me and my devices being "you", names from profiles or a contact naming the profile; placing into a tag whose reach holds anyone else asks first ("Tag and share"). `undo(t)` is `db.undo(Scope::Thing)`; `edited(t)` the newest change in that scope (a tag placement is written on the tag, so it does not count). `delete` skips subgroup edges on groups I cannot write.
- `demo`: `--demo` adds tags, people, notes and this week's events once (`/osis-app/demo`).
- `edit`: `splice`, typing into a `TextSession` against the text the widget showed.
- `ui` (mod: state, refresh, input; screens: building home and a thing; content: slot models and their widgets): a `Root` widget (kit) holds the screen; the width class is read from the render root's size (logical) on each refresh and `on_resized` refreshes when it changes; under 760 px an open thing fills the window with a back button, else a 264 px rail (home row, search, favorites or results, new) sits beside it. A screen is built whole when the open thing, width class or look changes; its slots (home body, rail list, rail new, pane extras, meta) are models (`Content`: lines, labels, tile runs, chip runs) rebuilt only when they differ, so editors keep their caret. `ViewState` (UI memory, reset per screen: the new-kind chooser, the tag picker, a pending shared-tag confirmation). One `watch_thing` per shown thing, home and the app groups posts `Changed`; the UI re-reads (no queries yet). The pane: kind label ("Event · Note" with a body), favorite star, delete; the accent's title mark; text fields over text sessions (title; an event's place; body for notes, events, people); extras per kind (event time, contact fields, the tags as removable chips plus "+ Tag" and its picker, a tag's subtags and items, a view's items, Recent as rows with edit times); meta chips (who can see it, when it changed, undo). A thing just made gets the title focused.
- Slots, the status line and the star are found by widget tags (freed with their widget since the vendored masonry_core fix); a tag cannot come with properties, so the styled status label sits in a tagged `SizedBox`.

**kit modules**: `theme` (the parameters, the six defaults, `Mode`, `Look::new(theme, dark, main)` resolving colours: background tinted by bgTint, panel at panelOpacity, card fill by fill composited over the background, white text past fill 0.55, accent from the main colour, shadows stronger in dark), `backdrop` (all ten backgrounds as fills, gradients and paths in shades of the background colour, placed by four seeds hashed from the open thing and its kind; no blur or layers), `fonts` (every theme font and JetBrains Mono bundled in kit/assets/fonts with their OFL texts, registered in `on_start`), `icons` (line icons as SVG path data), `text` (parley layouts in the theme font family list), `widgets` (`Chip` (dot or icon, removable ×, flat actions), `Wrap` (flow), `Root` (also fades the background to a new colour over 600 ms: the one motion), `Panel` (panel, inset, field), `Tile` (card, row, new; slant, shadow, gloss, icon roundness), `TileGrid` (auto-fill columns), `IconButton`, `Limit` (centred max width), `style` (labels and borderless text inputs per text role, accent weights and caps, mono section labels for bar, the title mark: rule, bar, slash underline); tiles carry bar's stripe and slash's corner flag), `list` (reconciler, unused by the app now), `marshal`, `window`.

**Patches** (kit/patches/, `[patch.crates-io]` in kit/Cargo.toml; each with a `.diff` against upstream beside it): `masonry_winit` 0.4.0: its clipboard was copypasta's X11 context, `unwrap`ped at startup, so the app panicked on a Wayland-only session (no `DISPLAY`); now the Wayland clipboard over the event loop's own connection (dropped in `exiting`, before the loop drops the display), else X11, else a no-op. Upstream candidate. `masonry_winit` also: `AppDriver::on_resized(window_id, ctx, PhysicalSize<u32>)` (after the render root has the new size) and `on_theme_changed(window_id, ctx, winit::window::Theme)`, default no-ops (`masonry_winit-hooks.diff`; upstream has no equivalent as of late 2026; candidate). `masonry_core` 0.4.0 (`masonry_core-tags.diff`): `remove_child` never freed a removed widget's tag, so re-adding a tag debug-panicked and lookups found a dead id; the tag is now kept in `WidgetState` and freed on removal (still unfixed upstream; candidate; test `tags::`). Its Cargo.toml allows `unused_imports` and `unfulfilled_lint_expectations` (upstream noise under this rustc). Upgrading Masonry means re-vendoring and re-applying.

**Tests**: app/tests/it (`data` over an in-memory database: app group, favorites, open, delete, demo kinds and search, the look choice; `edit`: splice), kit/tests/it (`list`, `marshal`, `theme`: every default readable in both modes). The window was checked by hand with the gui-testing skill (home, rail, search, typing, narrow, every theme in light or dark).

**What the prototype showed** (asks of core: issues tagged `core-ask`):
- The watch-driven model works with very little driver code; the cost is in re-reading (no queries) and one subscription per visible note.
- `TextSession::on_remote` gives only the merged text, so a remote edit moves the caret to the end; a session dropped after `delete` commits its pending text and resurrects the thing (the app closes the editor before deleting).
- No well-known per-app root: the app finds its group by scanning home and tolerates duplicates.
- Masonry 0.4: `TextInput`/`TextArea` do not fill a flex slot by themselves (`SizedBox::expand`); `Portal` content needs `content_must_fill`; `reset_text` drops the selection; user events can only reach the driver as `Action`s; widget tags leaked when their widget was removed and the driver had no resize or system-theme hook (both fixed in kit/patches, Patches above); `NewWidget` takes a tag or properties, not both.
