# Keys: epochs, boxes and rotation
> Summary: Why the key machinery (spec/crypto.md §3-§4) is shaped as it is: a KEM key, epochs and one box per role, `keys.0` current only, key actors and link keys, self-verifying epochs, rotating needing only the right to rotate (history added by whoever holds it), the keyed right to rotate, the three promises, grants and re-seals while blocked, forks standing, untrusted epoch numbers, supersession from the rotation commit, pass-end rotations, lockouts, which requirements force all this and the relaxations rejected. Map of the other key notes at the top.

Principle: **rotating needs only the right to rotate** (Tess), never a prior secret; every other key rule is one of the three promises below.

Other key notes: who rotates and when, re-seals: notes/keys-rotation.md; history boxes, establishment, withdraws, passing on content: notes/keys-establishment.md; what an epoch covers (the pin): notes/keys-coverage.md; holder actors at depth 0: notes/holder-actors.md; key cycles: notes/key-access.md; timed ends: notes/timed-ends.md; the osis-keys crate: notes/keys-impl.md; grants without history: notes/history-less-grant.md; primitives and costs: notes/crypto.md.

## Structure
- **A KEM key per role** for things other than key actors: effective role is the minimum along the chain, so a box sealed to A's write key must open only for A's writers. Organization A holds admin on doc T and has read-only holders: T's admin box to A must stay closed to them, else they get T's admin secret, hence its admin delete key and sync ID keys ("readers only read", spec/requirements.md). One KEM key per thing would be held by A's readers, and binding role and epoch only in the AD is replay-proof, not confidential. One box per holder actor carrying all its roles, sealed at its top role, would lock out its lower-role holders. Cheaper variants (one ML-KEM key per thing with an X25519 key per role; roles sharing a key while their holders coincide) were declined by Tess (notes/chat.md Costs).
- **Epochs per role**: a writer may rotate read and write but not admin, so each role is an independent chain of secrets, boxes and history boxes, and `keys.0` an independent keyset per role that its rotator writes. A per-thing epoch would have to say which roles carry over and where their boxes live.
- **One box per (holder, role, epoch)**, key actors included (Tess's decision): a rotation of one role touches boxes of that role only, and the covers rule and re-seal trigger look at exact keys. Rejected: a key actor's box carrying every role, keyed at the top one: it needed a guess at what such a box carried (it could not be opened to check), a forced rotation of a carried role, a refusal to rotate when a carried secret was not held, per-fork exception boxes, and still stranded holders.
- **`keys.0` holds the current keyset only**: nobody seals to an old epoch on purpose, and a granter with a stale key still succeeds because holders keep old secrets. The deposit seed lives beside the keysets (notes/sync-reasons.md); refreshing it is a MAY against an ex-holder filling the budget, which a stranded holder never needs (notes/sync-access.md).
- **A keyset's `demotes` names the commit its write is** (spec/crypto.md §4 Carried demotions): `KeysBody::of` adds the `Commit` source of the keyset field's writes to its `demotions` (a delta's own commit, a snapshot copy's stamp), and `Core::keyset` needs every listed commit's edge writes. Not via `epoch_intro`: only a role commit's own `keys.0` part gives that, and the part goes once any role rotates again (its write of that role's keyset died: replaced, so covered past `ROLE_KEEP`) while its other keysets stay current; a node joining after the drop must still hold them (`own_demotion_keyset_held_from_a_snapshot_after_read_rotated_spec_crypto_4`). The superseded keyset is in no snapshot (current only), so a newcomer never holds it.
- **Key actors have seed-derived role secrets** so a thing whose mint admin is a key actor (a node's local thing) is a dependent thing like any other; they never rotate and are never shared. Their KEM key never rotates either: the seed is the node, a compromise is a node removal, and an epoch for key actors would enter every box and claim-set rule for a case nothing uses (nodes and link keys are replaced by add-and-remove; a suite migration is a new key actor).
- **Link keys are full key actors** (signing and KEM keys from the seed). Rejected: a sign-only link key from whose seed K's secrets derived (it handed every read-link holder K's admin secret and sync ID delete key), and a symmetric link box only the sharer could write (a writer's rotation of K could not refresh it) (notes/links.md). Cost: 1.2 KB per box to L per rotation of K, and one hybrid entry box per step of K's sync ID.
- **Symmetric box form** where the writer holds the recipient's role secret (a dependent thing's secrets to the mint admin it derives from): 49 bytes plus the epoch of the recipient's secret, which the covers rule needs (without it a mint admin's rotation after a lost node would not make the thing's epoch stale).
- **Self-verifying epochs** (spec/crypto.md §4 Epochs): an epoch's nonce hashes its secret, so each source of a secret is checked alone, in any order. It removes "which copy do we trust" state, which was order-dependent: recording the first keyset seen per epoch let a writer re-publishing an epoch's keyset with junk keys make late joiners refuse the genuine box for good. Hash the secret, not the keyset: holders check either at the same strength, a non-holder gains nothing (a writer can name a fresh epoch matching junk keys), and checking the keyset costs an ML-KEM keygen. 32 bytes: 16 would let a rotator equivocate at 2^64 work. Establishment still decides planted epochs, huge n, false supersession by a history box opened with a genuine secret (bounded: such writers could rotate anyway), and junk keysets seen by non-holders (healed by re-seal).
- **Big rosters**: a rotation's boxes page like any map, and `secrets.0` compacts by ordinary paged snapshots, so no roster size makes rotation impossible.

## Rotation
- **Rotating needs only the right to rotate**: a rotation lists and supersedes every current epoch, held or not; a stale box or a keyless box at an epoch whose secret the node lacks makes the rotation due as at a held one, and so does a known departure the epoch is ordered before (its rotator held nothing after it). One ordered after is the business of the rotators boxed at it, who judge it by its history (notes/keys-establishment.md, Uncovered forks rotate). History is a separate box any holder of the old secret adds (`rotation::add_history`, at the end of a complete pass, with the re-seals), at the roles it holds now (a history-only put needs the role its keys name: a holder lowered below r still holds r's current epoch until r rotates, so it must add nothing at r), delivered under the thing's sync IDs (a non-writer by deposit). A current fork's secret goes by box and by the rotation listing it only: an add listing a current fork would supersede it at the epoch's holders and nowhere else, breaking the re-seal invariant. Grants and re-seals write the same superset history entry, except at an epoch blocked at the writer, where they copy only what is listed (a history entry is encrypted under its epoch, promise 3). A box the rotation cannot open stands, and its holder adds the secret once the keyset arrives (`flows::reopen_dependents`). Only the commit's wrap needs a secret (the newest established read secret held, current or not); a node with no read secret at all is stranded and waits for a re-seal. So nothing waits for a secret: every lockout, a fork only non-rotators hold, a vandal that left and a removal whose remover may not rotate recover by the ordinary rules (a removal or finalize whose rotation its author may not make goes alone).
  - Rejected: rotating only while holding every current secret: each lacked fork then needed its own way out (a recovery predicate on who made the epoch, removals going alone, finalizes holding back writes), and some shapes stayed stuck.
  - Cost: a node left out of a knowing rotation rotates over it when it knows a departure instead of waiting for a re-seal; content under an epoch reaches holders not boxed at it only once a holder of its secret adds it, so content whose only holders are gone or hostile is lost to the others. A hostile writer gains nothing: superseding an epoch takes no box or secret from its holders, and the writer could always box only itself (Lockouts).
  - Model: core/test/tla/rotation Depth0 `D0_via_late`, `D0_stall`, Lockout.tla, Cover.tla. Tests: `rotation::tests::forks_unboxed_with_departure_rotate_lacking_secrets_spec_crypto_4`, `tests_members::rotator_lacking_*`, `vandal_that_left_recovered_by_due_rotation_spec_crypto_4`.
- **The right to rotate is keyed** (`rotation::may_rotate`, spec/permissions.md §3 Who rotates): write or above over grants with key access only, read by every key decision. A role through a keyless grant never gets the secret of the holder actor it runs through, so it holds none of the epochs it would judge a rotation by. Its rotation is valid all the same, and authority over writes counts keyless grants: a keyless grant is a role.
- **The three promises** (each key rule is one of them; a rule that is none of them is suspect):
  1. A secret once shared cannot be un-shared (spec/requirements.md Permissions): a rotation keeps a departed party from new content and recovers nothing from anyone; whoever holds an old secret may hand it to any holder of the current epoch, so superseding an epoch unread loses nothing a live holder has.
  2. Every current holder of r is entitled to every current epoch of r and to the history its holders have: a node holding a current secret that sees a holder without its box gives it, whether or not that epoch is blocked at it, because the box enlarges only an entitled party's reach; a node holding an earlier secret the history lacks adds it. The departed party is no current holder, nor is a holder whose grant the node may finalize (its departure is due there: `rotation::ended_holders`).
  3. Encrypting under e means using e's secret as a key (sync ID keys, stamps, steps, deletes, boxes and history entries under it); a node knowing a departure e does not cover does none of it, and one that may rotate rotates first. A box carrying e's secret is encrypted under its recipient's key and judged by the recipient's rules (a holder actor's keyset not blocked at the node; a device's KEM key, nothing). Counting the carried epoch as encrypted under would make grants and re-seals refuse while blocked, violating 2.
- **Grants and re-seals go while blocked** (blocking applies only where steps, entry boxes or content go under the epochs): a node that may rotate rotates first (`ensure_rotated`), so a blocked grant or re-seal arises only where it may not rotate the blocked role. An ended holder is never re-sealed. Model: Depth0 `CarryFree`. Test: `rotation::tests::grant_rotates_first_boxes_grantee_only_spec_crypto_4`.
- **Forks stand** (spec/crypto.md §4): nothing rotates a role for having several current epochs. Holders are re-sealed at every current epoch, each rotation boxes every current holder, and the next rotation for any reason lists every current epoch; a fork costs re-seals and boxes, never a rotation, so no rotation answers a fork and none can fork another (churn guard: `rotation::tests::partial_views_rotate_nothing_for_forks_spec_crypto_4`). The one case: a fork beside a cut whose floor bars a holder cannot re-seal it, so the rotation is its re-seal, settling at once (notes/history-less-grant.md).
  - Rejected: merging forks proactively. By every node that may rotate, merges forked each other (a sim churn run: 204 commits); by one merger read from rosters (109 commits), any rotation needing the merge waited on one specific node, which may be gone for good: a designated party breaks "every operation completes with nobody else online" (Tess: if it is fine for nobody to do X, why can anyone). Joint sealing (notes/holder-actors.md, Seal) removed the one reason a fork had to be merged. Also rejected: boxes merged per (holder, role) (holders end up with different secrets after concurrent rotations and cannot read each other's new wraps); rotating a forked role because a box to M names a fork other than M's newest (A's admin, holding no secret of M, and M's admin each judged the other's box to M uncovered and rotated A back and forth); rotating every forked role (a used link with its sharer offline hung).
  - An account's signing chain does not follow this rule: readers follow the first revocation they see (spec/permissions.md §5.4, notes/engine-validity-epochs.md Sides), so a fork of the account's devices is owed a merge, and boxes to an account are sealed at the heads its reader follows (`Keysets::heads`).
- **Epoch numbers others name are never trusted**: the next n counts from the rotator's own current and unsuperseded epochs, a pending commit records no epoch beyond its bounded reading-only secrets, and an epoch above 2^62 establishes only next to an established one at n-1 (a planted n = i64::MAX would overflow n+1 and block every later rotation, removals too).
- **Supersession is read from the rotation commit** (spec/crypto.md §4 Epochs), the same at every node in any arrival order. Its keyset's and history box's lists differ on derived epochs and where the rotator superseded an epoch through one the node finds invalid (n1, then n2 by a rotator demoted unaware, then n3 listing only n2: n3's history box still carries n1's secret, so n1 stays current only at nodes holding no secret of the role, where it is moot). Rejected: reading supersession from box removals (a re-grant hid the chain and a departed granter steered it to a forged claim; an epoch whose rotation boxed nobody stayed current at nodes that could not open the history box); taking it also from the `keys.0` keyset the applier held before (order-dependent). Tests: osis-keys `rotation::supersession_independent_of_arrival_order`, the re-seal invariant in `rotation::tests_property`.
- **Pass-end rotations**: withdraws (notes/keys-establishment.md) hold back no encrypting, so they are made at the end of a complete pass of the thing's sync IDs (`pass_end_rotations`, with the holder actors below), as re-seals and history adds are, never by `ensure_rotated` (which makes only what encrypting waits for; any rotation lists every current and unsuperseded epoch, so it withdraws in passing).

## Lockouts
A writer W that rotates A boxing only itself (or garbage to the others) leaves every other node without a current secret; no rule stops it (a malicious writer is bounded, not prevented). Nothing special recovers it: W's epoch is valid and current everywhere, and any rotation supersedes it unread. The admin removes W like any holder, its rotation listing W's epoch; or, once any departure A's epochs cannot be seen to cover is known, the admin's due rotation does it at its next write. Two concurrent vandals: removing either rotates over both. W's content under its epoch was received by nobody, so the removal's frontier does not list it and it is invalid by the ordinary rule. A rotation by W that boxed others but missed the admin stays valid; a holder adds its secret to the history. With no admin left nothing recovers. Not a dispute: every view agrees W's rotation and the removal are valid. A hostile admin is a dispute (notes/disputes.md); open there: an admin that rotated to itself can register a step at a node keeping S unread, and a child step needs the head's delete key, derived from the hostile epoch (notes/disputes.md Storage under a fork).
- Rejected: frontier omission (leaving W's rotations out of the removal's frontier so they turn invalid): a recovery resting on a protocol side effect (Tess); an admin wanting a write gone reverts it. The admin forking from the epochs it holds (W's epoch stays current beside it). A history add under an epoch missing a removal the adder knows (an ignorant fork boxing W gets the recovery epoch). Model: core/test/tla/rotation/Lockout.tla.

## What forces the key machinery
No single requirement but a product: lazy rotation (spec/requirements.md Permissions: removal never re-encrypts) x holders that have holders of their own (Generality: share through one thing that holds the role, nest rosters; Permissions: roles in a cycle) x no trusted peer (Decentralization). Lazy rotation means the node that must exclude a party is not the one that removed it, so knowledge of the removal travels with the keys (names, establishment); such holders mean the removal may be inside a holder the node cannot read; no trusted server means that knowledge must be verifiable from what travels, in any order, with any subset withheld. The inside-holder factor is the one the others multiply, hence depth 0. Also: "no coordinator" and "works offline" force forks; "readers never rotate" and bounded malicious writers force per-role epochs and the lockout case; keep after removal forces the lost-access ways (`rotation/lost.rs`); "a holder that missed key changes may need a round trip per change" (Cost) and may need "a current holder to act for it" force the claim walk and stranded reports.
Relaxations rejected:
- **Only admins rotate**: per-thing epochs and no lockout case, but a lost node keeps reading every thing its account only writes to until an admin of each writes. Tess chose "write or above rotates its roles and below" (notes/keys-rotation.md); depth 0 also relies on writers cascading.
- **Only the remover rotates (eager)**: the remover often cannot rotate what the removed party read (a device removed from M by M's admin, where M only reads A), so A's writers must act and learn why: the whole machinery again. The remover's eager cascade is a MAY.
- **One rotator per thing at a time**: cannot be kept without a coordinator. Dropping only the re-seal invariant leaves content under a losing fork unreadable to holders lacking it, for good if no node holds both.
- **Drop keep** (every removal erases): deletes about 400 lines of `rotation/lost.rs`, but Tess asked for "you left the group but keep your history" (chat), and it is self-contained.
- Not candidates: telling a node that cannot read L who may read it (un-blinds it); re-encrypting on removal (O(content), against Cost).
