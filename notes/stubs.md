# Stubs: grants with the granted thing, things found by references
> Summary: The principle behind stubs, grant signals, locators and entries (Tess), where each rule lives in the spec, why the rules have their shape, and the alternatives rejected. Owed work and open questions: notes/stub-rewrite.md.

**Principle (Tess):** a grant is the granted thing's own data, never its holder's. A node syncs what its references reach, and its role on a thing is that thing's grants as it holds them: signal → reference → sync → permissions. Telling a holder is optional; a grant nobody is told of is a valid state and holds the holder nothing. Nothing is kept with a holder, or sent to it, for having been granted something; nobody can make another track, remember or sync what they granted. Signals carry values only. A grant's box to its holder is also the holder's way in: the commit boxing a holder carries its **entry** (spec/sync-ids.md §3), so grant and entry are one write and never diverge, and the holder gets in at any node holding the sync ID, with no reader online. Stores learn a padded count of the holders each role commit boxes, nothing of who.

## Why the rules have this shape
- **What a sync ID carries is what some node logged** (notes/terms.md, Log). A rule saying a unit carries X's stub describes what a node reading the unit and holding X's role entries logs; with no such node there is no stub, and the reference is a bare one (`NotCarried`).
- **Carrying X whole includes its stub**, so whether U's keys open X is always judged from what U carries of X. That one rule covers public things (the public's open boxes are in every cut) and a unit's own grants.
- **A reference at sync brings a stub** (U's thing holding sync or above on X, its keys not opening X): with "keys open" alone a thing held at sync through a holder is never entered, while the same role granted directly is (held-model `closure::entry`).
- **The holders on a unit's chains are stubs in it; the holders a roster names are not.** A role-class aspect's weaks bring no stub: counted, a unit carrying X would hold the stub of every holder in X's roster its keys open, which is grant-driven entry (held-model control `Chains::Up`; `stubs::a_holder_on_no_chain_is_no_stub_*`). Chain stubs are the one way up from a thing; the roster's holders stay known as backlinks.
- **A chain holder the unit carries whole is no stub**: otherwise a chain of local dependents made independent gave every holder again at each rotation (`scale::a_deep_dependent_made_independent_costs_its_depth_per_rotation`).
- **A sync holder's entry is the evidence of its role**: it sees no roster, so an entry giving holder H a sync secret of X counts as a sync-only cut of X under H (`entry_syncs`, rows `se/`, kept until X is forgotten: the secret is never taken back, the removal shows only as the step past its keys). With it a strong in H's unit (or the local part, H an own thing) reaches X at sync and keeps it unread, and H is a claimant of X's sync ID; without a strong X is a stub. A stub raised to kept unread pulls S anew (its sources showed it role entries alone), and a stub's heard head takes the entries bound to it (they arrive before the step that binds them).
- **A held signal's thing is held as a stub by the fire's weak** (held history, the inbox's setting holding its targets) while the signal is followed, before entry too: a stub is a vertex whatever secrets of its thing are held (spec/sync-set.md §2 First entry, Holding a stub), so the signal has no retention root or holding rule of its own.
- **Who follows a signal is a node reading its holder**: only a node reading the holder computes the holder's claim and opens its entry (`flows::tests_signals::a_signal_is_followed_where_its_holder_is_read_*`).
- **The public's role has one source, its grant**: a store-visible listing of the public at the head would be a second, judged apart from the roster and able to disagree with it. The public's entry is in the grant commit and each rotation boxing it, like any holder's; a redeemer reaches a public thing once it holds the public's grant (spec/sync-set.md §4).
- **A KEM key stays where the keyset it supersedes had one**: a rotator judging "the thing holds a role somewhere" needs to learn of every grant to it, which nothing guarantees (KemKeys.tla `KK_untold` fails; `KK_sticky_*` pass).
- **An entry is in the commit, never apart from it**: a claim signed apart from the grant is a copy that lags and needs repair; with grant and entry one write, a store holding the commit serves the holder's way in, and a re-seal or rotation boxing a holder carries a fresh one.
- **Claims bind to the sync epoch, not to a step**: a step without a rotation (a deposit key, a read key in force) then changes no claim, and a grant concurrent with it is not stranded. The claim key mixes a read roles secret into the sync secret so a store, holding only sync secrets, cannot test a known KEM key against the claims it shows.
- **One open rule**: a credential of the head opens S whole, an older step's sync proof opens S up to that step's end. A removed holder learns its removal (its rotation is wrapped under an epoch it holds) and nothing written after; a reader behind is served the rotations it lacks; no node judges who a peer was (no departed peers, no notices, no proof by role).
- **A removal on a chain hop reaches the units reading through it by the hop's stub**; where nothing honest carries the hop's target beside R, R's readers take the stolen key's writes as valid until the removal reaches them (held-model `spec_reach::reach_a_chain_stub_carries_a_removal_to_the_units_reading_through_it`). That is the requirement's "removal binds honest nodes that receive them".
- **An entry is judged on the commit it opens**: a node holding nothing of X takes the boxes its entry reaches for reading before the commit applies (its evidence is under X's sync ID, under the epochs those boxes give), and judges the grant once it applies; a removed admin can still hand a holder its old grant, valid until the removal reaches it. The same holds for a sealed fire; not a new hole.

## Rejected
- **A grant list kept with the holder** (every grant naming a holder copied under the holder's sync ID, searched down from the account): it needs a judgement of which grants count that only the holder's readers can make, fires kept as proof, and a standing read of every holder reached. Asking the granted thing dissolves it. Gone with it: taken or accepted grants, a path a role commit states for its holders, any rule whose job is to make sure a holder hears of a grant.
- **A per-grant entry box in X's steps** (the signal carrying an entry credential): every store keeping X unread sees each box, so counts sync-only holders, standing signals and stranded requests (Entry.tla `EN_sig_bare` fails `StepsBare`). A stranded reader's box in a later step is also never found (`EN_left_next`).
- **Claims signed apart from the grant**: a copy that lags and needs repair.
- **Claim-only child steps** (a step listing new readers): concurrent ones are siblings needing a fix-up; a claim set per step strands a grantee at a step without a rotation.
- **Claim key from the sync secret alone**: stores hold it and could link a holder's card to the thing.
- **One stable claim key per thing**: a removed reader poses as any holder forever.
- **Store-visible boxes found by trial decryption**: a trial per box, and unpadded counts.
- **An entry carrying the earlier epoch's secret**: breaks floors for a grant without history.
- **W from the claim's X25519 alone**: loses post-quantum protection.
- **Notices** (a role commit's copy sealed per connection by a reader to a holder that cannot open it by its wrap): a holder then gets in only by meeting a reader, so a store alone never serves it; entries put the same way in into the commit, served by any node.
- **Entry by role** (opening S toward a peer whose proven things hold a role, ByRole) and **judging departed peers**: a second open rule beside credentials, needing a reader online and the roster at the judge; the older step's sync proof served to its end does the departed peer's job.
- **The blob key sealed to each holder with no claim** (the form Entry.tla checks, `NT_*`): a holder finds its box by trial over every blob or by a flag that counts told commits. Entries add the claim, so a holder finds its entry by index.
- **A signal carrying commits or boxes** (the holder's boxes sealed into the fire): it makes delivery a duty of the granter and a second entry path; values plus entries are one mechanism with locators and pairing.
- **Delivering the public's grant to each sync holder**: notes/public-and-cards-brief.md.

## Models
held-model `closure::entry` (properties, controls and hand worlds: core/test/held-model/README.md; notes/closure-model.md) is the sync differential's oracle (notes/test-properties.md, Reference-graph differential). TLA `Entry.tla`, `KemKeys.tla` and Depth0's chain-stub removals: core/test/tla/rotation/README.md. Entries: a design-study explicit-state model (grant, a rotation removing a sync-only holder, re-seal, a step without rotation, two stores, store-to-store sync, public or not, stores alone serving), not kept in the tree; its controls (no entry in the grant, claims bound to the step, no public entry) fail as they should. Not modelled: bridges, rotation and re-seals in held-model, the Dependent-entry oracle gap, entries with forks, holder actors, floors, compaction or padding.
