# Acting through holders without their content
> Summary: The decided direction (Tess, stage A2) for a role on C through holders A and B used without downloading A and B: notifications (tickets) are a bootstrap offer only; roles never feed what syncs; your roles are your account's data (every grant A takes under A's sync ID); no accepting, a grant to A from outside A's writers being an external signal A's admins enable; a node syncing C holds for keys (roster, role commits, own key boxes; no content) every holder on every path giving its account a role on C, found by following taken grants down through holders' key parts; the availability gap accepted. With the measured baseline, the candidates costed on the same scenarios and why (b) was chosen, the weak spots, why the other path searches were rejected, what goes at A2, and the model (held-model `closure::keys` over taken grants: properties i to v, controls, counts, hand worlds) and the joint property with held-model `evidence` (one sync ID plus its paths' key parts judges and acts as everything). Nothing here is built or in spec.

**Principle: roles never feed what syncs.** What a node syncs comes from its strongs alone. A role decides only what the node may do with what it syncs, and which holders' key parts it fetches to do it. Choosing to sync C is a choice of its own: a grant matters to a node only where it is on a path from an own actor to something the node syncs, and the admins of each thing on that path (or the holder's own admins, for a grant from outside) arranged it.

## The direction
1. **Notifications are a bootstrap only** (Tess). A ticket fire on an inbox ("you've been offered X", a link redeemed) is an offer: it tells a node a thing exists, with its sync ID and a first key. It can be disabled without breaking roles, keys or syncing, and no role, key or sync step after bootstrap depends on it. Key changes reach holders through the holder's key part (2, 5), never by a key-change ticket on the holder's inbox; nothing fetches on a ticket's word; the account's tickets (D's, telling the account's other nodes what and how to sync) duplicate strongs and key parts and are not kept.
2. **Your roles are your account's data** (Tess). Every grant naming A that A takes sits under A's own sync ID (A2's "every grant naming a holder under the holder's sync ID"), so A's nodes, syncing A, know A's roles with no notification. The same holds for every holder: its taken grants are under its own sync ID, in its key part.
3. **No accepting** (Tess). A grant to A counts whether or not anyone accepted it; syncing X is a separate choice (a strong). A grant to A from A's writers is ordinary data under A. A grant to A from anyone else is an **external signal**: taken only if A's admins enable it (A is **externally grantable**), and then governed like any external signal: disabled, its history capped so such grants do not accumulate, or purged (notes/write-path-design.md §4, The setting). A grant not taken gives A nothing. A node's role on a thing it syncs is its account's effective role over that thing's real holder chains of taken grants (spec/permissions.md §1): a function of what it chose to sync and the world, never of what happens to be held, so the convergence class of roles counted over held role commits (Convergence below) cannot arise.
4. **Held for keys.** Syncing C pulls the **key part** of every holder on **every** path from an own actor to C that gives a role (roster, role commits and certificates, the key boxes the node opens; no content, no blobs), from each holder's own sync ID, by role entries alone (spec/sync-ids.md §6). Every path, not only the one a write picks (core picks per write: the shortest path giving the needed role, `engine/write.rs` `role_path`/`path_with`), so a revoked path never leaves C unwritable while another is fetched.
5. **Paths found by following key parts down** (Tess). From A's grants, which the node holds by syncing A (2), it walks into each holder's key part (its grants and the boxes it opens), opening each holder's sync ID by that holder's box to the one above. Only holders on real paths to synced things are fetched, key parts only. The walk is bounded because only taken grants are followed (3): what it reads was written by a holder's writers or let in by its admins. Why the other searches were rejected: Finding the paths.
6. **Own boxes only.** A node fetches only the boxes it opens, never a holder's whole box set (issues/holder-key-churn.md). Any node holding a holder's key part can serve it.
7. **Availability gap accepted.** A node whose only reachable sources keep C unread and hold no key part of A or B gets no key change of A there. A store meant to serve C to people acting through A keeps A's and B's key parts too: the user's or app's configuration (a store keeping C unread cannot read C's roster, so it is told), no new mechanism. Revisit if the gap bites.
8. **Lands with A2** (evidence per sync unit) as one design, before A4.

## What a path needs
For each edge "H holds a role on X" there is one **edge piece**: the edge write (`roles.0` on X), X's boxes to H, X's sync ID. To act on X through own actor n and holders H1..Hd a node needs the d+1 edge pieces of its path, each holder's current keyset commit, and nothing else of any holder. That is about 11 KB per holder (computed: `keys.0` 6.3 KB, the node's own boxes 2.4 to 4.3 KB, an edge piece 2.1 KB), whatever the holders' sizes and rosters. Every path held, not one: the union of the holders on them, 11 KB each.

Where edge pieces travel today: only under the holder's own sync ID, with the holder's whole sync unit (spec/sync-judgement.md §2, a grant naming a holder travels under the holder's sync ID), so a path through a 30 GB A costs 30 GB. X's own sync ID cannot give a first box: its role commits are wrapped under X's secrets.

## Baseline: what already churns
Measured in the engine (the commits of `grant`, `demote` and `ensure_rotated` packaged as `thing_cost::what_a_reader_costs` packages its grant: holder H with m writer members, H write on X, X's roster its admin and H; sync entries and stamps not counted), with notes/thing-cost.md and notes/a2-spec/README.md For Tess 6:

| event | bytes | who downloads it |
|---|---|---|
| a member granted on H (read) | one commit, 4.0 KB | every reader of H; nothing below H |
| H granted write on X (granter holds H's secret: symmetric boxes) | one commit, 2.1 KB (`secrets.0` 1.15 KB) | X's syncers; copied under H's sync ID for every reader of H |
| a member removed from H | H's rotation: 2.7 KB + 4.9 KB per remaining writer (12.5 KB at 2 holders, 32.2 KB at 6; a reader's boxes are 2.7 KB) | every reader of H, every box of it (a commit applies only with all its pages, spec/crdt.md §8) |
| then each independent X that H holds a role on | X's rotation when next written, 11.7 KB + boxes of X's other holders (about 21 KB seen by a document's reader, notes/a2-spec) | X's syncers; and its whole `secrets.0` part (5.0 KB here, every box of X) plus `sync.0` is copied under H's sync ID for every reader of H (engine `holder_piece`) |
| H's content changes | the change | every syncer of H's sync ID, path users included |

So with m members and k independent things below H, one removal costs, by design and before any change here:
- **H's rotation, in m**: 52 KB at 10 members, 4.9 MB at 1,000 (2.7 MB if they only read), downloaded by each member: m times that across the network. 100,000 direct members cannot exist (`MAX_CLAIMS` 65,536); nested three deep at fan-out 47 a removal costs every member the top rotation (about 230 KB), and the members of the two groups below it theirs.
- **A rotation of every thing below H, in k**: lazy (stale boxes, spec/crypto.md §4), 11.7 KB or more each, required for confidentiality (spec/requirements.md Cost: "in proportion to the rosters of what it could read"). Fundamental; candidates are judged against it.
- **A copy of each of those rotations' boxes to every reader of H, in k times m**: about 5.5 KB per thing below, to every member, whether or not it syncs the thing. Not fundamental: a reader of X needs its box to H from X's own rotation commit, which it opens under the epoch before; only a node entering X for the first time needs the copy, and only the boxes to H (direction 6).

A grant added costs nothing below H, and that must stay (notes/write-path-design.md §2, Not every actor that could write).

## Candidates
**(a) Key parts carried down.** Each unit carries, beside its bodies, the key part of every holder above it, as shared blobs listed under the unit's sync ID and stored once per node. A node syncing X opens only X's sync ID.

**(b) Key parts pulled** (chosen). Nothing more is listed under X. A node syncing X also syncs, for each holder on its paths, that holder's sync ID restricted to role entries: the holder is **held for keys**.

**(c) A key unit per thing.** Every independent thing has two sync IDs, one for its key part and one for its sync unit; paths pull the first.

(a) and (b) are one mechanism with two placements: the same blobs (A2's copy per (commit, thing), one blob wrapped under each sync ID that needs it, notes/write-path-design.md §2 E3), stored once per node either way. They differ only in **which sync ID's log lists the blob**: that decides who writes an entry when a holder changes (a: a writer of every unit below, k entries per change; b: nobody, the holder's own log has it) and who can serve it (a: whoever serves X; b: whoever holds H's key part). (c) is (b) with a second sync ID per thing: stores already tell role entries apart by stamp domain, so the split hides nothing more and doubles the cost per sync ID that stage S1 cuts (issues/many-sync-ids-cost.md).

The half of (a) that judging needs is built or planned and stays: X's sync ID carries the edge writes its held commits' paths use (A2 evidence) and the keyset commits its boxes name (spec/crypto.md §4 Carried), both functions of what X holds. What (a) would add is the boxes.

### Privacy
- (a): every reader of X receives the rosters and box lists of every holder above X. Within spec/requirements.md (readers may learn who holds roles on it, directly or through other things), but far more than a judgement needs. A node keeping X unread sees one shared blob listed under every unit below a holder when that holder's roster changes, and its size: which of its units share a holder, when it changes and how big it is.
- (b): a node holding H for keys learns what a reader of H learns today less its content (H's roster, which members see anyway; the things H holds roles on). A node keeping X unread learns nothing of H. A store of H sees which peers pull role entries only (network metadata). With own boxes, a member no longer receives the box list of every thing H holds.

### Hostile input
- A stranger's grant naming the account, or naming a holder the account acts through, is an external signal of the named thing: not taken unless that thing's admins enabled it, and then capped or purged by their setting (direction 3). Taken, it changes no fetch and no role unless its target is on a path to something the node syncs; there it is a grant like any other.
- (a): the node opens only sync IDs its strongs chose. A writer of X can list junk as ancestors' parts, bounded as any writer.
- (b): the node also opens the sync IDs of holders it did not name one by one. Each is on a chain of edges from an own actor to a thing the node syncs, so each was put there by the admins of the next thing down. A writer of H attaching things costs a path user nothing unless a strong reaches the thing (own boxes), beyond one list entry per grant the search reads (Finding the paths).

### Convergence
The convergence class (issues/roles-apart-from-syncing.md, Why roles counting over every held role commit fails) came from an edge counting because its role commit happened to be held. Under the direction the role is defined without reference to holdings: the node's role on a thing it syncs is the account's effective role over that thing's edges in the world. A node computes it from the key parts of the holders on its paths, which it fetches for exactly that reason; an edge in a certificate that arrived for another reason either is on such a path (and counts anyway) or is not (and gives no role, since no path from an own actor runs through it). What is synced is the least fixpoint of: the node root; the targets of strongs of things the node reads; read-ness by that role. The held-for-keys set is the holders on every path from an own actor to a thing in it. Dropping the last strong below a holder shrinks both: the holder is no longer held for keys, its secrets go, and the role goes by the one lost-access rule. spec/requirements.md "Referencing a thing grants no rights, and removing a reference revokes none" holds as written: the reference chooses what is held, the grant gives the role.

### The first box
(a) fails here alone: a node that never held X cannot open X's sync ID at a store (no credential) nor the copies wrapped under it. Tagging entries so that a holder's key opens them, or listing every holder thing in X's claim set with an entry box (spec/sync-ids.md §3), makes X's stores serve key material by holder, and only an admin issues steps while any writer rotates. (b) has no such gap: the node reads H by H's own box to it, and H's key part holds X's boxes to H.

### Rejected
- (a) carried down: an entry under every unit below a holder per change of the holder (the cost notes/write-path-design.md §2 refused for rosters), roster-sized rotations at every store of every small thing below, no first box. Its availability advantage is direction 7's gap.
- (c) a key unit per thing: a second sync ID per thing for a split stores already make by stamp domain.
- A pointer to each holder's key part in every unit below (to close the gap): one entry per unit per holder change, and stores learn which units share a holder.
- The strong stays the choice, with boxes carried down the keys-open chain (the issue's option 1): a path still costs A's 30 GB.
- Roles decide what is fetched from every grant naming the account (option 3): a stranger's grant makes the node fetch.
- Accepting (a grant counting once the account makes a strong, or once one of a holder's writers references it): a second consent beside choosing to sync, and the source of rules that shift a role by what is reached (notes/node-closure.md clauses 3 and 7). The holder's admins' external setting gates strangers instead (direction 3).
- Key changes and grants delivered by tickets the roles depend on: a disabled or lost inbox would then break roles and keys (direction 1).
- Key parts of the path a write would pick only: a revoked path leaves C unwritable until another path's key parts arrive.

## Weak spots
1. **Own boxes touch "a commit applies with all its pages"** for role parts: fetching only the pages a node opens makes unfetched pages a may-hold state (already one, spec/sync-judgement.md §2); a node that may rotate H still needs them all, and what a re-sealer and the stale-box rules read of the other boxes decides how far it goes (issues/holder-key-churn.md).
2. **What the path search reads**: one list entry per taken grant of every holder an own actor reaches by taken grants (Finding the paths). A writer of a holder, or an outsider its admins let grant, can grow that: the stated cost of write giving the right to grant (notes/write-path-design.md §4, Without the switch).
3. **Availability** (direction 7): a member through H served only by a node keeping X unread gets X's rotations there but not H's. X already carries H's keyset commits (Carried); carrying H's boxes too is (a)'s cost again.
4. **A new way to hold a thing** in the closure walk (beside read and kept unread), in the least settled code: lost access, release, what a held thing needs, sync status per class (a node holding H for keys is no source of H's content and its complete pass is of role entries). Modelled (The model).
5. **Role entries are wider than keys**: the class also holds commits carrying purge requests and blobs they link (spec/sync-ids.md §6). Either the filter takes them (small) or the class is narrowed.

## Finding the paths
Decided (Tess): **(p1), down from the account's grants, over taken grants only** (direction 5). C is opaque until its first box arrives, and an edge "B holds a role on C" travels under B's sync ID, so a search runs from the node's own actors down or from C's roster up.

The searches compared, by what each reads (an earlier cut of held-model `closure::keys` modelled each; the model keeps (p1) alone):
- **(p1) by lists** (kept): the list of taken grants (target and role per grant, tens of bytes each) of every holder an own actor reaches by taken grants; key parts only for holders on a path.
- **(p3) the reference names its holders**: whoever makes the strong states the holders it found C through. The node makes keyed lookups ("does h hold a role on x") among its own actors, the holders named and the target.
- **(p2) bounded by C**: keyed lookups from what the node has opened to the holders named by the rosters of the things it reads.
- **(p3) then (p2)** (the lean), and that also reading the roster of every holder on a path found (deep).
- **Backward by rosters**: the roster of every thing on a chain of grants ending at a thing the strongs reach.

**(p1) is bounded by taking.** A grant from outside its holder that the holder's admins did not enable is not taken: no list holds it, so it changes nothing a node reads or does (hand world 3: nothing read at n = 5 and 50; following every grant naming the account reads 6 and 51 entries, with no bound in what strangers build). What (p1) reads, at every hop, was written by that holder's writers or let in by its admins; one entry per such grant is the stated cost of write giving the right to grant (Weak spots 2; hand world 3 with the grant enabled: 6 and 51). Its other cost: A holding roles on k things, k entries at first sync (hand world 6).

**Rejected:**
- **(p3), (p2) and the two together miss honest paths.** A keyed lookup needs both ends named, and the middle of a path is named nowhere the node can read: not by a strong made before the path existed or by someone who found C another way, and not by C's roster, which names the last holder only. The smallest worlds: (1) the account read on H and admin on G, G admin on H, H admin on C, the strong naming H: the world gives admin on C, the lean shows read (G is in no roster the node reads); (2) the account read on C and on X, X on Y, Y on Z, Z on C: with the direct grant revoked the lean and deep lose C while the world gives read; (3) two paths to C, the strong naming one, that one revoked: the node that was there holds the other, a node arriving later finds no path, so a role is no function of strongs and chains (property (i)).
- **Backward by rosters**: reads the roster of every holder above C, on a path of the account or not (hand world 6: 12 and 102 entries at m = 10 and 100) and an entry per member granted after, against "a grant added costs nothing below H" (Baseline); every reader of C learns who holds every thing above C; and a roster sealed under its thing's secrets would need new design to be readable down an edge.
- **(p1) with a picked bound on list reads**: past the bound a real path is missed, so (i) fails at the bound (notes/arbitrary-values.md).
- **A role only through holders somebody named**: gives up paths nobody named (worlds 1 and 2) and is still no function of strongs and chains (world 3).

## What goes at A2
notes/node-closure.md clauses 3 and 7 (Roles taken up) and accepting throughout: spec/sync-set.md §2 Taken up, spec/sync-inbox.md §5 "Nothing before the strong", "only accepting takes a grant up" and grants taken from a ticket fire's carried commit, spec/permissions.md §3's "a grant no longer taken up" and the account's own grant counting only while taken up, A2's taken-grants brief; the account root counting for keys-open travel only on things it reaches (spec/sync-judgement.md §2); tickets beyond the offer (direction 1): key-change tickets on holders' inboxes (spec/sync-inbox.md §5 Key changes), fetching on a ticket's word (spec/sync-set.md §2 What a held ticket does and On a ticket's word; notes/node-closure.md clause 8 and Tickets), since roles are under the account's sync ID and keys are pulled from holders, and the account's tickets (spec/sync-set.md §1 The account's tickets), since strongs decide what syncs and key parts give keys; the holder piece as a copy of X's whole `secrets.0` part (direction 6). What A takes from outside (direction 3) is written to notes/write-path-design.md §4 and built at A5 with the existing external-signal setting. It closes gap 2 of issues/roles-apart-from-syncing.md. A4's "members enter what A holds roles on by its boxes under A's sync ID" is this rule at the node's first hop: a node may hold its account for keys only, so pairing no longer means syncing the account's whole unit.

## With A2
One design, two halves, one blob format (the copy per (commit, thing)):
- what a reader needs to **judge** a held write rides down in the unit that holds the write (A2 evidence, a function of holdings);
- what a node needs to **act** through a holder is pulled from the holder (its key part, own boxes), a function of strongs and the holders' real chains.
The edge piece is A2's "a holder's grants under the holder's sync ID" (every grant naming it; notes/a2-spec/README.md Open 1), cut to the boxes to that holder.

## The model
held-model `closure::keys` (core/test/held-model/src/closure/keys.rs, tests/it/spec_holders.rs; core/test/held-model/README.md, Holder access), and the joint property with held-model `evidence` (`World::joint`, tests/it/spec_joint.rs). It precedes any spec or code of this half of A2.

**What it holds.**
- **Taken grants** (direction 2, 3): every grant written is in its target's roster; its holder takes it iff a writer of the holder wrote it, or it came from outside and the holder's admins enable grants from outside (the current setting, `Keys::grantable`). Only taken grants give roles, lie on paths, are boxed for paths and are listed in a holder's key part. Turning the setting off rotates nothing at the targets: their rosters are unchanged.
- `oracle`: the direction's definition as a function of the world: roles as the own actors' effective roles over the world's taken grants; what is synced as the walk from the root strongs reading by those roles; `Closure::for_keys`, the holders on every path of taken grants to a synced thing less what is held whole.
- `Keys`: per thing its grants, a keyset per epoch and a box per holder per epoch. A revocation rotates the target at once, boxed to the holders left at their current epochs, and leaves everything below it stale until next written.
- `Node`: the search down from the account's taken grants (p1), a source (the sync IDs and key parts it can reach in one sync) and a ledger counting once each edge write, keyset, box, list entry and content version it fetches.
- Abstracted: every thing independent; holding an epoch gives the earlier ones; a rotation is readable under the epoch before, so a thing's own sync ID gives every box but the first; a holder's part is readable with the holder's current epoch; a keyless grant (over the taken grants) is on no path. Validity and which path a write states are roles-model `paths`, joined in the joint property.

**Properties** (`check`: per world and rule, by name), over the small family and random histories:
- (i) Roles, and what is read and held, are the oracle's; the same with a certificate of every grant not standing held too; under relabelling; and a node that was there through a revocation shows what one arriving after it shows.
- (ii) A grant from outside its holder that the holder's admins did not enable is as if never written: no role, no fetch, no list entry (but its roster entry, for a reader of its target); enabled, it is a grant like a writer's. Each grant of a world is checked both ways. A taken grant naming the account or a holder, on a thing on no path to a synced thing, changes no role and no key part (its list entries are the stated cost).
- (iii) No content of a thing not synced is fetched.
- (iv) After a revocation, heard from the synced things and the key parts already held: what the world leaves writable is writable, no holder is newly opened, and a thing with no path left loses its secrets. Then, synced with everything, the node shows the oracle's.
- (v) A content change of a thing not synced fetches nothing; the counts of hand world 6.
- The fixpoint's own: a sync settles and a second fetches nothing; with every strong dropped nothing is held for keys.

**The small family**: 914,126 worlds. Three things with every grant among them and the account absent, read or admin, and with up to four grants one of them write; four things with up to six admin grants, and with up to three one of them read; root strongs to one or two things; no strong inside a thing or one; every grant its holder's writer's, each flipped to come from outside by (ii). The design fails none (`holder_access_small_family_plain` at `OSIS_HOLDERS_STRIDE=1`, about 320 s of the heavy lock).

**Controls**, each on the design over every fourth world, each failing its property: roles from every held edge, (i) certificates (47,050 worlds; hand world 10); following every grant, taken or not (the rejected "roles from every grant naming the account"), (ii) untaken roles and fetch (183,269 and 214,475; hand world 3); key parts of the chosen write path only, (iv) a holder newly opened (24,103; hand world 2); whole box sets, (ii) key parts (87,805) and (v) in hand world 6 (boxes fetched at a member's removal 10 and 100 by m, at the rotations below 1 and 10 by k).

**(v) counts** (hand world 6: A with m members holding admin on k things, the node syncing one through A; (m, k) = (10, 1), (100, 1), (10, 10), (100, 10)): first sync 2 keysets and 2 boxes; A's content changing, nothing; a member removed, A's keyset and the node's own box; C's rotation, its keyset and the node's own box. What the search reads at first beyond the path's two edge pieces: 2, 2, 11, 11 (A's list: k entries).

**Random histories** (grants, a quarter of them from outside, revocations, rotations owed, content changes, strongs added and dropped, a stranger's thing granted from outside to the account or a holder, holders' admins turning grants from outside on and off; three nodes with the same strongs syncing at random moments from random sources; two more that after half the steps reach only a store keeping what they sync unread, with every key part there or none), 600 histories of 40 steps (`holder_access_histories_plain`). Under the design every node ends as the oracle and alike whatever the order, the node behind the store with key parts included; behind the store without them the node is left behind in 274 (the availability gap, direction 7). Following every grant ends unlike the oracle in 94; the chosen path in 29.

**Hand worlds**, each a default test (`hw1` to `hw12`): 1 A to B to C, C alone synced (iii, v); 2 two paths, one revoked, then the other (iv); 3 a stranger's X holding n things granted from outside, not enabled and enabled (ii, list reads); 4 X granted on C from outside X: not taken, then taken once X's admins enable it, then revoked; 5 the strong dropped; 6 churn at m and k (v); 7 a read hop gives read; 8 a cycle of holders, with and without the keyless rule; 9 A also synced whole; 10 the old class (a certificate off every path; a revoked grant's certificate); 11 the availability gap, without and with A's key part at the store; 12 the account's only path from outside, the setting turned off (the role and secrets go, nothing rotates) and on again.

**The joint property** (`World::joint`, over held-model `evidence` worlds under the design's delivery, `Stage::A2`): a node acting as a signer and holding one sync ID S plus its paths' key parts (each holder's on a path from the signer to a thing of S's unit: that holder's sync ID's role entries as logged; the signer's own: every edge write naming it or on it, with their evidence) judges and acts as the node holding everything:
- `J-judge`: each body S should carry that it holds, it judges as S's reference or as the node holding everything;
- `J-act`: a content write it would make on a thing of S's unit, along any path the world's roles or its own reading run, it judges valid iff the node holding everything does;
- `J-logged`: one it judges valid, logged under S with what its view requires there, S's reader judges as the node holding everything.

Over 3,164 worlds (every 13th family world, 400 random, 400 wide; `joint_one_sync_id_and_key_parts_act_as_everything_plain` at `OSIS_JOINT_STEP=13 OSIS_JOINT_RANDOM=400`, about 70 s wall) the design fails none. Controls: the sync ID alone fails `J-act` (2,514 worlds: a write pending for want of a hop's grant, or valid by a removal it lacks) and `J-logged` (191: its reader shows a removed member's write, the gap); logging its write with what S's entries alone require fails `J-logged` (2,462: the path's grants missing); key parts without their removals fail `J-act` (2,254: it acts along a removed hop; default test `joint_a_removed_hop_reaches_the_actor_through_the_key_part`). `J-judge` has no control: no generated world holds a key part that moves a verdict on S's bodies off both S's reference and the node holding everything. Found on the way: the key part carries what lazy delivery leaves out of S for the actor's own writes (the signer's removal is in its own part, direction 2), so a node that acts closes the gap for what it writes, as D1 says.
