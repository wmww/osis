> Summary: Stage A1's spec text drafted before any code brief (notes/redesign-plan.md, A1): per draft section the spec section it replaces, the roles-model `paths` check that is its oracle, and its status (SETTLED, OPEN); the open questions with options and costs; where the model overrode the design notes; what A1 removes from spec and every heading or anchor that breaks; the order in which code briefs move the text into spec/; the walk checked clause by clause against core/test/roles-model/src/paths.rs; why MAX_PATH_THINGS is 64.

Each holds the words that will stand in spec/ for every section A1 changes; a bullet marked *Stands* keeps today's words. Sources: notes/write-path-design.md §1-§6, notes/account-membership-design.md §1-§4 and §8, notes/path-model-findings.md, core/test/roles-model/README.md (Paths). **Statuses.** SETTLED: the rule is held by roles-model `paths` under `Rules::fixed` at full size, its oracle named. OPEN: a decision the design has not made (below; where the draft needed one, it takes the option marked *taken*), or a sentence no model checks, marked OPEN in the draft with its question.

## Where the model overrode the design notes
Each is written as the model has it.
1. **Dispute candidates** (`disputes`, `Candidates::Explicit`): held explicit lowerings of a grant whose holder is not their signer (never a self-removal, none the walk trusting it applies as an invite grant), not trusted and not dropped by a trusted write, by its seeds or by its drops. notes/write-path-design.md §1 took every edge write of another holder's edge below admin but finalizes: an honest holder thing's leave was then shown as a dispute (notes/path-model-findings.md 31).
2. **The remaining admin's own edge** (`remains`, `Rules::last_hop`): the path's last edge only where its final hop joins Ak to T in one edge; a descent of two steps or more has none and names its remaining admin, as the design says (notes/path-model-findings.md 30).
3. **The last-admin rule is read only by a part without a verdict**, against the pass before the part's commit (`remains` is skipped for `Verdict::Applied`), and a judged lowering is void where its drops, or an earlier write of its commit, take the remaining admin it relied on (`Rules::remains_kept`, notes/path-model-findings.md 29). The design leaves the first two unsaid.
4. **A vouch is an edge-class part of the walk** (`edge_class`, `order`): it applies iff its path gives admin on its thing at its key, is ordered by key, and is put in question and dropped by lowerings of its path's edges like any part. Today's spec/permissions.md §5.1 Vouches orders a vouch by its position and asks "W could write c" at each later lowering of W; the walk gives both.
5. **Re-grant from each edge's first chained write** (`Regrant::FirstChained`, `first_chained` along stated paths, strictly after its key). notes/account-membership-design.md §4 says so; today's spec/permissions.md §5.2 reads the first point the walk gave the role.
6. **Edge as restored is a role and an invite** (`Pass::restored`, `Pass::rinv`): the highest of each since the last explicit write, the record edge admin in both from the start; `until` is disregarded in the role. A lowering is judged where it falls in either (`Rules::any_fall`, notes/path-model-findings.md 23); a lowering applied by its verdict raises its edge only by admin along its path (27).
7. **Seeds and drops reach a self-removal of the lowered edge** (`leave_in_question`): today's spec/permissions.md §5.1 Seeds exception ("except a write lowering its author's own edge") goes.
8. **A lowering drops the parts whose need it no longer meets** (`need`): a fall from admin drops the parts applied by admin; a fall from write, or of the invite, drops the invite grants it no longer gives; content falls by cuts (`content`). A trusted write's seeds read the same needs against its value (`Rules::seeds_by_need`, notes/path-model-findings.md 32).
9. **The writers' list** (`Sim::stand`): where R, trusted, applies with everything received it lists everything; else the content, then the role writes one at a time in receipt order (ties by commit ID), each kept iff R still applies. The design says "leaving out a role write with which R ... would be void".
10. **Self-removal by path end**: a signer leaving by itself states the empty path and no edge (`World::push`: `leave`).

## Open
Each is a question with its options; *taken* marks what the draft writes meanwhile, **settles** what closes it. The model runs named are roles-model `paths` changes (one rule or world each, `Rules::fixed` held at the gate's size, with a control failing without it).
1. **What a removal's result names** (notes/path-model-findings.md 19): the members a kept lowering of the remover by X dropped, beside those X lowered. (a) Only X's own lowerings (*taken*: no spec text; it is the `osis` result): a co-admin's loss is not shown. (b) Also every part a lowering the list keeps dropped: the remover's walk computes it, one diff of two readings. (c) The writers' rule leaves out a lowering of the remover even where R applies with it: reopens the key-order class (control `-key-order`). The spec is unaffected in (a) and (b). **Settles**: the coordinator, at brief 5 (the `osis` result); (a) is safe to build on, (b) adds to it later without a spec change.
2. **The void check's cost** (notes/path-model-findings.md 17): k void lowerings at the end of a chain of k grants cost k² setters read, all forced by one party's own 2k role writes. (a) Cap nothing and state the cost (*taken*: the spec caps nothing; brief 7 counts it). (b) Memoize chain reads across checks with the same drops on the chain: linear for this attack, not in general; an implementation choice, no spec text. (c) A cap on the depth `keeps` reads: the verdict would depend on the cap, which notes/arbitrary-values.md rejects. **Settled** as (a), (b) left to the implementation. Within one check the walk (and the model) rescans an edge's history for each setter chased through it: m grants on an edge, each chased through an edge of m dropped grants, cost m² setters in one check (`counts::a_void_check_rescans_an_edge_per_setter_through_it`), so k such lowerings cost k·m² from one party's own 2m + k writes. A skip pointer per edge per check (past what that check passed over) makes one check linear in the setters it visits: an implementation choice, step 7.
3. **Detecting disputes at a node that trusts nothing** (notes/path-model-findings.md Open 0): the rule is a walk with each candidate trusted, so k held candidates cost k walks of their component per change. (a) The spec defines the dispute and leaves when it is computed to the node (*taken*). (b) Narrow the candidates to lowerings the walk leaves without a verdict or applies with drops that dropped a lowering or a grant a lowering applied under (the classes of findings 16 and 21). (c) Compute only on demand, when a thing's roles are shown: a fight on a thing nobody views is never reported, against spec/requirements.md Permissions ("reports the dispute"). Never part of validity in any option. **Settles**: a model run for (b) (`disputes` narrowed, property `dispute` at full size: every reach-back still shown; control: the unnarrowed rule giving the same set); until then the engine computes (a) per change for candidates on things in its node closure, counted in brief 7.
4. **The adminless reading at untrusting nodes** (`a_lowerings_check_does_not_read_what_its_drops_free`, one `tiny` world in 1,000,000): a lowering's check does not see what its drops free, so A can be left with no admin where nothing is trusted, shown as a dispute whose trust restores an admin. (a) Accept, stated (*taken*: nothing in the draft; a fight of admins, which spec/requirements.md Permissions leaves to trust sets). (b) Replay from the earliest drop before giving the verdict: a walk per check. (c) Read the last-admin rule again after the drops: circular with the verdict. **Settled** as (a).
5. **A remaining admin taken by the lowering's own drops**: settled as (b). `keeps(L)` also needs the remaining admin edge L relies on (its last edge where that stood before L's commit, else the named holder's) to stay standing past L's drops, else L is void; one more edge read per check (notes/path-model-findings.md 29).
6. **Path bytes**: settled. A commit states ONE `Path` in the clear when every thing it writes is written along the same path (a thing created under its parent, an edit touching siblings, every commit with one acting thing); only where the things' paths differ does it state a salted hash per thing (`kdf("path", salt, Path)`, a 16-byte random salt), each thing's `Salted` inside its sections. The writer MUST use the one-path form when all paths are equal; a decoder accepts it for any number of things (it cannot check equality without opening every section) and rejects only malformed forms (a hashed form for one thing, a hash count other than the things written). What a reader of one thing learns from the one-path form is that the commit's other things were written along the same path as its own: accepted (the signer is public anyway; a path differs only in the hashed form). The salt keeps a reader of one thing from testing guesses at another's path; a node keeping the unit unread opens no blob and learns nothing of either. The hashes are always inline, outside Inline or link: a reader needs them before opening any section, and they are bounded by the things the commit writes (34 B each, about 220 KB at the 6 500 things a blob holds; spec/wire.md §12). Rejected: the form following the count of things written (two things along one path paid 34 B a thing and a `Salted` in every section, about 130 B a commit on every creation, for nothing a reader could not already tell).
7. **Where the remaining admin goes**: *taken* and settled: (a) header field `remains` at 17, `flags` to 18: positional, one presence rule, one byte (`[]`) on every commit. (b) after `flags` costs the same byte (flags no longer optional) and one more case; (c) in the `roles.0` value: a removal has none; (d) in `Path`: it is per target, not per thing written.
8. **Header-list chunks' delete key**: *taken* and settled: (b) an empty group. Today's spec already has the case (a commit listing no level-2 key, spec/sync-ids.md §6 Delete: such a blob goes by the delete key or at its expiry); a header list lives and dies with its header, which no purge removes. (a) the signer's level-2 key is not in the header where the signer is no purge author, so a decoder could not check it. **Owed**: brief 4 checks that nothing deletes a forgotten commit's header-list chunks by level 2 (spec/commits.md §5 Forgetting, spec/sync-ids.md §6).
9. **The remaining admin by descent**: settled as the draft took it: the path's last edge is its final hop where that is one edge from Ak to T (Ak's direct grant, or T's record edge where Ak is its mint admin); a descent of two or more steps has none, and the write names its remaining admin. Reading Ak's direct grant off the path split a reader of the write's evidence from members (notes/path-model-findings.md 30).
10. **A commit with several lowerings** (spec/commits.md §2): settled as the draft took it: each edge write applies or not by the pass before the commit; its lowerings are judged in key order with the earlier ones in effect, their drops in D and passed over by `keeps`; the pass ends once the whole commit has taken effect; none sets an edge of the path the commit states (else the commit is bad input). A later lowering whose remaining admin an earlier one took is void by 5 (notes/path-model-findings.md 28).
11. **"Its actor's secrets"** in spec/commits.md §4 Examined again and Hostile input: settled. The engine awaits no such input (core/engine `waits::Wake`: `Moved` for a reach or an epoch, `Epoch` for an account's chain); the draft drops the words.
12. **Dispute candidates**: settled as the draft took it: explicit lowerings only. A self-removal and an invite grant carry no frontier and are never trusted; as candidates an honest holder thing's leave is shown in an honest history. `dispute` loses nothing at full size (notes/path-model-findings.md 31).
13. **An idle write deciding a fight, unshown** (notes/path-model-findings.md 34 to 36, 38, Open 4): a verdict given when a pass first reaches a lowering stands for good, even where a write that let the pass reach it applies nowhere in the end, so such a write can decide a fight among admins and no dispute is shown. Settled for the walk as (a), accepted: the required properties are gated at full size (at nodes trusting the removal safety, liveness, honest history kept, the last standing admin, alike; at every node one reading, no gain by a party that never legitimately held the right, `quiet`) and idle writes' effects are counted (roles-model README.md, Paths; `tiny`: 47 of 1,000,000, 2 of invites, 9 with honest writers, each a fight among parties holding their right at their write). The non-monotone fixpoint is rejected (finding 16). An end date on admin (a write giving admin at its `ts` with an `until` earlier than the edge's own end) is no lowering: a grant, no frontier, no candidate, read by the last-admin rule alone (permissions.md §5.1 Lowering). Taken as a lowering it fails a required property both ways it fits the walk (finding 38: judged at its key it drops nothing and its verdict lets a made-up key's end on the remover stand at nodes trusting nothing; dropping forward what is stamped past the end, its drops outlive it and an honest history shows a dispute), and the smallest honest case needs no end date (36). Whether every fight must be shown: issues/admin-fights-not-always-shown.md (Tess). Does not block brief 3a.
14. **Several edge writes in one commit**: settled (notes/path-model-findings.md 37). A receiver judges each edge write of a commit on its own, as spec/commits.md §2 keeps: the parts take effect one at a time at the commit's key, in holder order but the part on an edge of the commit's stated path for the target last (a self-removal of the path's last member, as in a single-use link redemption, or the record edge a descent ends with; with both, in holder order), each judged with the earlier ones in effect; no part's drops or seeds reach a part of its own commit (no list can name its own commit). Two edge writes on one holder's grant cannot be encoded (the `roles.0` delta's map keys repeat: deterministic CBOR) and are bad input. The redemption applies; redeemed twice concurrently the first in key order grants (as today); a thief with the link key after it keeps nothing stamped later, and a grant it backdates before the redemption stands until the sharer's removal of L, or for good where the sharer received it first (as today, spec/permissions.md §7 Finalized). Control `-refuse-shapes` (the draft's earlier "bad input"): the redemption applies nowhere.
15. **A removal saying retain in the edge as restored** (spec/permissions.md §2: the grant written anew with `until` 0, role read, "a removal all the same"). §5.1 reads every value's role with its `until` disregarded, so after such a removal h is restored at read, where after a plain removal it is restored at none: a second, concurrent explicit removal of the same holder then falls (read to none) and drops the parts resting on h its list leaves out and the first removal kept, which after a plain first removal it does not. Retain or erase then changes what other writers' writes survive. (a) Read an `until` at or before its write's `ts` as no role in the edge as restored (a value gone at its own `ts` gives nothing): the walk's `an_ended_value_restores_its_role` pins the opposite for a timed grant already ended when written, so (a) needs checking against finding 23's worlds. (b) Read only `until` 0 so. (c) Accept and state. Not modelled (the model has no retain); the walk follows the draft's words. **Settles**: a model run of (a) with a retain removal in the `tiny` generator.

15. **A write acting for the thing it writes, or for a thing holding no role on it** (step 2): `act_as(X)` names X as the write's purge author and the actor readers see; a path's index names the signer or a listed thing, so a write on X itself acting as X (`[S, X, X]` repeats X) or acting as an X that holds no role on the thing has no real path naming X. (a) Such a write names the signer (its purge author changes: users see it). (b) The purge author may be the thing written (an index k+1). (c) Refuse it: an app acts for X only through X. *Taken* meanwhile: the Interim form `[X]` (step 3 judges it strictly: invalid). **Settles**: the coordinator before step 3.

## Why MAX_PATH_THINGS is 64
A resource bound against hostile input (class R, notes/arbitrary-values.md): past it a writer refuses the path and a decoder rejects the header, alike everywhere. At 0 no write could go through a holder, so the bound must be at least the deepest nesting of holders anyone builds: a few levels (account, organization, team). Unbounded, one commit's path costs every reader a point query per hop (each up to a descent of `MAX_CHAIN_DEPTH` record edges) and, while unjudgeable, a wait on a record per listed thing: a junk path of n made-up things parks a commit on n records, n set by the writer alone. 64 keeps a path's hops, and so a part's waits and point queries, at most 64 (each hop's descent bounded on its own by `MAX_CHAIN_DEPTH`) and a `Path` at most about 1 KiB; it hurts only a holder nested 65 deep, which no design here builds. Any value well above real nesting serves; 64 matches `MAX_CHAIN_DEPTH`, the like bound on a record chain.

## Bytes per commit
A tid is 15 bytes, 16 encoded. Today's `author` and `actor` are 32 bytes. A1's `signer`, `path` and `remains` for a commit whose things are all written along one path (whatever their number): signer 16, `path` `[i, A1..Ak]` 2 + 16k, `remains` `[]` 1: 19 bytes with the empty path (every commit at A1, accounts unchanged: 13 fewer than today), 35 with one listed thing (an account, from A4: 3 more), 51 with two (19 more). A commit whose n ≥ 2 things' paths differ pays 1 + 34n header bytes of hashes and, in each of its sections, `Salted` of 20 + 16k bytes inside the ciphertext; every section's `Contents` adds 2 bytes of heads in either form.
