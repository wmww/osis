# Engine: history
> Summary: The thing's `retention^.0` setting in the engine (`retention/history.rs`, `leaving.rs`; spec/commits.md §5 The setting, §7): history judged by the current setting alone, purge on replace's one-way floor, Tess's five decisions, the one door every reader asks, must-hold by the setting, the security argument, leaving history as per-aspect events, signals' bound, and undo's keep check and pre-state.

## The rule
History is judged by the **current setting** alone: the newest held valid `retention^.0` body (by the write it carries: a delta by its own key, a snapshot by its newest write's stamp; `validity::note_settings`, `setting_now`). A replaced body is in history while that setting keeps it, counted from when it was replaced. No earlier setting counts, so no node keeps any: `retention^.0` is an ordinary admin-only aspect, `admin` purgeable-by by default, snapshotted, compacted (`compact::compactable`), purged and expiring like any other, and governed by its own setting's history.

Purge on replace is the one thing that needs the past, and the current setting carries it: an entry's `purge_before` F puts every replaced body holding a dead write stamped before F under purge on replace, whatever its `history`. The writer of a setting fills F from the setting it holds (`osis_commit::retention::carry_floors`, in `write.rs`'s setting rules): its own stamp where the entry governing those AKs purged on replace, else the F there; floors of AKs that lose their own entry go to `default`. Receivers read F as written (Tess's decision 5: a writer default).
- Why the floor and not the alternatives (held-model `thing_history::Rule` keeps two as negative controls): a flag in the replacing commit's header is any writer's word (it could end others' history) and is lost when the flagged write is compacted away before a node holding the replaced body sees it; a floor compared with the dated replacement fails at a node holding fewer bodies (it dates the replacement after the floor: the old counterexample); covering at once alone fails whenever a keep setting reaches a node before the cover does. The current setting is the one thing every node judging the body holds.
- Why "written before F", not "replaced before F": a write's stamp is fixed; when a body was replaced depends on what a node holds. A snapshot is judged by the oldest of its dating dead writes (`bounds::written_of`, read only where a floor is set), so its copy of purged content is purged too.
- What it costs: turning purge on replace off promises history only for what is written from then on (content written before F and replaced later is under purge on replace too). A setting written by an admin that did not hold the purging one (concurrent admins) carries no floor: the writer's word, as for any default.
- Lost-access `retain` for an ended timed grant also reads the current setting (`validity::retain_default`): no earlier default is kept.

## The setting (`retention/history.rs`)
- `ix.retention` holds each thing's held valid setting bodies by the write each carries; `settings` filters to valid ones with values, `setting_now` takes the last. A setting's arrival, flip or drop marks its thing (`Core::settings_due`); `retention::settings_changed` judges each aspect again at the transaction's end: bodies leaving or coming back (Leaving history), and purge-on-replace covers a setting may now owe (`none_due`).
- **One door**: every reader asks `history::of(core, tid, ak) -> Kept` (the current entry's option and floor; `ROLE_KEEP` after replaced for role-class and `addenda.0`; nothing for `purge.0`). `leaves_at(replaced, written)`, `live_until(written)`, `purge_on_replace(written)` and `forever(written)` take the body's oldest dead write stamp for the floor. `Engine::part_keeps` (a memo) reads the setting it judged by. `View` stores nothing that depends on a setting (`index.rs` debug-asserts the meta row equals `View::of(header)`).
- The wire carries no history: a part's retention is its purgeable-by alone; a snapshot's is `writer` (or the aspect's fixed one). The writer fills a write's expiry from the current setting (`settle`), refuses a replace under a `final` entry, and refuses a fire no setting keeps.
- A thing has history only where a setting is written, usually at creation: chat's `create` writes its own, kit writes `keep_a_day` (kit test `undo_works_on_a_kit_created_thing`), a plain `osis create` promises none. Without one no write is undoable; undoing the app's delete (which removes the strongs to a thing) restores them on each holder, so a holder needs a keeping setting too.
- Every judgment reads only the current setting, the bodies held and the clock, nothing dated by arrival, so nodes holding the same show the same history. A newer setting moves bodies only where still held, and no resend loop arises: a body out of history is neither must-hold nor fetched, one in history at a node that dropped it is not fetched (dropped stays dropped).
- **In every store the setting is the promise**: a body in history is must-hold whoever's snapshot covers it (spec/sync-judgement.md §2: a delta kept, whatever snapshot covers it; the facade reads `part_keeps`), so a new sync ID and a node that lost its copy get it again; out of history a covered body is may-hold. No loop: a cover never drops a kept body, and deletes wait for the bound plus `MAX_DRIFT`.
- Cost: the bodies that move plus O(log) per aspect, no body read (`scale::setting_change_and_window_passing_are_flat_spec_commits_5`), nothing re-judged for validity. An aspect turning dated has every held part dated once. Turning purge on replace on costs one snapshot per governed aspect holding replaced content, at each writer (notes/growth.md).

## Tess's decisions
1. Dropped stays dropped: the current setting reaches what a node still holds (a longer one may re-show a body out of history there, a shorter one ends it at once); nothing a node dropped is fetched again; no per-node marks.
2. Admins may always purge: purgeable-by is `admin` (the default), `author_or_admin` or `writer`, the setting's own included; no per-write keep-forever. Shortening needs only the admin right and reaches every body.
3. Purge on replace is one way: content replaced while it was set never becomes history, through the floor the setting carries (model-checked: with no floor, or one read against the dated replacement, purged content shows).
4. Out-of-history bodies drop under any valid writer's cover: data nobody promised is not kept for good.
5. Who-may-purge, edits off (`final`), the expiry default and the purge floor are writer defaults only, no validity rule: a commit is valid with what it carries, and a change reaches later writes only.

## Security
Keep is promised only by nodes that hold the setting. Beyond purge an admin can only shorten windows; it cannot touch live values or force a prompt deletion, and a setting re-shows only bodies readers still hold. Purge on replace keeps its property (replaced content is never history, however late a node dates the replacement) through the floor; an admin writing a setting without it (or a concurrent admin that never held the purging setting) can re-show purged content only at nodes still holding it, as it could by keeping a copy. A writer ignoring the setting can only give its own writes another expiry or purgeable-by.

## Model
held-model `thing_history.rs` (`tests/it/spec_thing_history.rs`): the greenfield judgment and its negative controls (`Rule`), checked over every small world and random larger ones against the oracle `purged` (replaced while the setting in force by stamp purged), plus the eager-dropping node showing a subset in every arrival order; then through `evaluate` (`Outcome::current`, `oldest_dead`). Not modelled: covers and rights, validity flips of settings, what peers hold.

## Leaving history (`retention/leaving.rs`)
- Bodies of one aspect leave in replaced order, so per (thing, AK) the engine keeps places in replaced order, a cursor (every place at or before it is out) and one timer at the first place past it (`Indexes::leave_from`, `hf/`). Places: a signal's fires by `ts`; a value aspect's rows (`Indexes::leaving`, `hr/`), one per held valid part replaced on a dated aspect (and per snapshot a kill there may have replaced), in history or out.
- A place leaves at the first time at or after its replacement that the current setting does not keep it (`leaving::leaves`; a body under the floor is scheduled as one above it: told late, never early), so places leave in replaced order whatever the clock (`setting::a_late_row_leaves_no_earlier_than_replaced_spec_commits_5`). Leaving: a value part is told to listeners and marked for sync; a held fire drops (or at its commit's purge floor, `T_KEPT`).
- A tick walks the due aspects' places; a setting change walks those it moves out, and back from the cursor over those it brings back: O(the bodies that move), no body read. It walks after `date_due` (a superseder turning invalid in the same step moves its victims' rows later: `order::a_superseder_turning_invalid_dates_its_victim_before_the_timeline_walks_spec_commits_5`).
- **Rows are a schedule, never early, not an exact date**: they only time events; every read and every drop dates from the held set when asked (notes/engine-retention-dating.md), the compaction trigger reading them only to pick which bodies to weigh (`compact::droppable`). `date_due` sets them: a kill sets the row of each dying write's commit and of each snapshot of the aspect to the latest stamp winning, after each join of the apply, at its reach (`Killed::at`; every join counts, so pieces date as the whole join: `tests_paged::leaving_told_alike_*`, never early: `killed_fill_leaves_an_hour_after_its_overwrite_*`), reading no body (`scale::kept_overwrite_reads_no_body_spec_commits_5`). A snapshot carrying none of the dying writes gets a row that leaves with nothing to tell (`setting::a_snapshot_is_told_leaving_history_spec_commits_5`). A rebuild and an aspect turning dated date whole (`dated_replaced_at`). Late, never early: the killing writes may be stamped after the first superseder, and an earlier superseder of a dead write may arrive with no kill; either leaves an event late, never before the body leaves. Rejected: exact dating at each kill (an overwrite of a kept register read every held body of the aspect, per edit). A debug check in `left` confirms a leaving row's part is out of history or not replaced (a snapshot's row from a kill of writes it does not carry; a text creation an undelete revived, issues/seq-retention-dating.md).
- Rows exist whoever listens, so a later listener needs no arming. Rejected: a timer per body (a setting change re-armed and read every body).
- A purge request's header keeps its commit's parts to its purge floor, which no setting moves: a per-part `T_KEPT` timer like an expiry, armed when the part arrives, live or not, on any aspect (`listing::purge_floor_part_told_at_its_floor_spec_commits_5`). Setting-dated events are the cursor's, header-dated ones per-part timers; a purged fire's tombstone is a per-part timer.

## Signals (spec/commits.md §5 Signals)
A signal's body is in no state, so nothing covers it: it is replaced when written (`bounds::part_replaced_at` returns its own `ts`) and kept to its keep bound under its AK's entry (keeping history means how long a write is exposed, never a deletion promise). Past the bound this engine drops it, whole or as its leftover, at apply, at its place leaving, or by `T_KEPT`: data nobody promised to keep is not kept for good, and needs no compaction. sync never counts a signal part established uncovered, so it is must-hold only while kept (spec/sync-judgement.md §2). Convergence with a node that keeps it: reads judge by the bound from the header (`listing::past_due`); no copy past the bound is taken (`takes_late`, so no resend loop: `signals::signal_dropped_past_its_bound_not_taken_again_*`); nothing makes it missing again (`past_signal_bound` in `valid_again` and `purge::rejudge_purges`, as expiry: no refetch, no suspect flag nothing could clear). No join, rebuild, snapshot or cover touches it.

## Undo (`undo/`; spec/commits.md §7)
- **Kept by the setting**: each part an undo reverts (`undone_parts`, except a signal's, the creation record and `retention^.0`) passes `bounds::setting_keeps` at the undo's `ts`, by the current setting alone, so no node's compaction or timing changes the answer (Tess).
- **Pre-state** (`kept_before`): the held valid parts before the target that are live or within their keep bound, joined, only where the undo reads: parts writing the undone positions (`osis_crdt::touches`), the composites above, every snapshot, and inside a composite left absent kept parts newest first until it is present. Parts per position come from the undo index (`undo::touch`, a per-aspect cache on `Core` dropped past `TOUCHES_MAX`), so a check is flat in history (`undo::tests::check_cost_scales`). Debug builds compare with the whole join (`undo::compared`).
- **Replaced content kept** (`replaced_kept`, asked when the inverse needs it, `osis_crdt::restores`): every valid part before the target is in kept history, or its retention would keep it counted from the target's `ts`, or a kept part supersedes it: a later root reset or a snapshot whose `Frontiers`/`Absorbed` reach it (`retention::reaches`; tables cannot tell a part whose writes all died from one never applied, and an expiring part is never reached). A snapshot part is excused only by a later reset. An unkept part the target did not replace blocks too, since judging its body would depend on whether it is still held: one untyped or CLI write with no history on a map, set or list blocks restoring undos there until a kept snapshot or whole write follows (the limit spec/commits.md §7 states; lifting it needs writes to record what they overwrote, a wire change). Where the target's retention keeps what it replaces (`keeps_now`), only a part not fetched yet or a forgotten commit can block.
- **Forgotten commits** leave their `ts` in `Indexes::forgotten` per aspect their part was valid on (durable as `f/` rows; an invalid part replaced nothing), blocking unless a kept reset is stamped after. So forgetting may narrow what undo allows, never widens it; it is the undoing node's own refusal, never a receiver's rule (spec/crdt.md §4.6). Keeping more to excuse the part would be a lasting trace (notes/deletion.md).
- Memory: the trigger weighs no body a keep bound holds, and the memory cover drop waits for `part_keep` (notes/ephemeral.md Undo).
