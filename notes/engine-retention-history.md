# Engine: history
> Summary: The thing's `retention^.0` setting in the engine (`retention/history.rs`, `leaving.rs`; spec/commits.md §5 The setting, §7): Tess's five decisions, the one door every reader asks, hosts' must-hold, the security argument, leaving history as per-aspect events, signals' bound, and undo's keep check and pre-state.

## The setting (`retention/history.rs`)
- `retention^.0` is admin-only, fixed retention (`nobody`, never out of history, never snapshotted or undone), written whole. `ix.retention` holds each thing's held valid settings. A setting's arrival, flip or drop marks its thing (`Core::settings_due`); `retention::settings_changed` judges each aspect again at the transaction's end: bodies leaving or coming back (Leaving history), and purge-on-replace covers a setting may now owe (`none_due`).
- The pure rules are osis-commit's `retention::Timeline`, matching held-model's `thing_history`.
- **One door**: every reader asks `history::of(core, tid, ak) -> Kept`, fixed retention included (`ROLE_KEEP` after replaced for role-class and `addenda.0`; never out for `created.0`/`retention^.0`; nothing for `purge.0`/`released.0`). `Engine::part_keeps` (a memo) reads the settings it judged by. `View` stores nothing that depends on a setting (`index.rs` debug-asserts the meta row equals `View::of(header)`).
- The wire carries no history: a part's retention is its purgeable-by alone; a snapshot's is `writer` (or the aspect's fixed one). The writer fills a write's expiry from the newest valid setting it holds (`settle`), refuses a replace under a `final` entry, and refuses a fire no setting keeps.
- A thing has history only where a setting is written, usually at creation: chat's `create` writes its own, kit writes `keep_a_day` (kit test `undo_works_on_a_kit_created_thing`), a plain `osis create` promises none. Without one no write is undoable; undoing a delete restores memberships on the holding group, so that group needs a keeping setting too.
- Every judgment reads only the settings and bodies held and the clock, nothing dated by arrival, so nodes holding the same show the same history. A late or fallen setting moves bodies only where still held, and no resend loop arises: a body out of history is neither must-hold nor fetched, one in history at a node that dropped it is not fetched, settings are must-hold for good. Under a dispute each side's timeline is the settings valid under its trust set.
- **At hosts the setting is the promise**: a body in history is must-hold whoever's snapshot covers it (sync `must_hold` reads `part_kept` before any cover), so a new label and a host that lost its copy get it again; out of history a covered body is may-hold. No loop: a cover never drops a kept body, deletes wait for the bound plus `MAX_DRIFT`, and a holder judging later does not place it again while the label's snapshot carries its writes (`delete_stands`). Test `deletion::second_label_gets_kept_bodies_spec_sync_6`.
- Cost: the bodies that move plus O(log) per aspect, no body read (`scale::setting_change_and_window_passing_are_flat_spec_commits_5`), nothing re-judged for validity. An aspect turning dated has every held part dated once. Turning purge on replace on costs one snapshot per governed aspect holding replaced content, at each writer (notes/growth.md).

## Tess's decisions
1. "Never comes back" is a property of the timeline: a setting arriving late may re-show a body a node still holds; no per-node marks.
2. Admins may always purge: purgeable-by is `admin` (the default), `author_or_admin` or `writer` (`nobody` only for `created.0` and fixed retention); no per-write keep-forever. Shortening needs only the admin right and reaches every body; bodies leave an aspect in replaced order (one threshold per thing and AK).
3. Purge on replace is one way: where an earlier setting purges an AK on replace, a later `keep` there reads as no history (model-checked: without it late dating lets purged content show).
4. Out-of-history bodies drop under any valid writer's cover: data nobody promised is not kept for good.
5. Who-may-purge, edits off (`final`) and the expiry default are writer defaults only, no validity rule: a commit is valid with what it carries, and a change reaches later writes only.

## Security
Keep is promised only by nodes that hold the setting. Beyond purge an admin can only shorten windows; it cannot touch live values or force a prompt deletion, and a backdated setting ends no more than one stamped now and re-shows only bodies readers still hold. Purge on replace keeps its property (replaced content is never history, however late a node dates the replacement) only through the one-way rule. A writer ignoring the setting can only give its own writes another expiry or purgeable-by.

## Model
held-model `thing_history.rs` (`tests/it/spec_thing_history.rs`) checks the decisions above and that an eager-dropping node shows what a keep-everything node shows when nothing is late, and a subset in every arrival order. Not modelled: covers and rights, validity flips of settings, host placement.

## Leaving history (`retention/leaving.rs`)
- Bodies of one aspect leave in replaced order, so per (thing, AK) the engine keeps places in replaced order, a cursor (every place at or before it is out) and one timer at the first place past it (`Indexes::leave_from`, `hf/`). Places: a signal's fires by `ts`; a value aspect's rows (`Indexes::leaving`, `hr/`), one per held valid part replaced on a dated aspect (and per snapshot a kill there may have replaced), in history or out.
- A place leaves at the first time at or after its replacement that no setting keeps it (`leaving::leaves`), so places leave in replaced order whatever the clock (`setting::a_late_row_under_no_history_leaves_no_earlier_than_replaced_spec_commits_5`). Leaving: a value part is told to listeners and marked for sync; a held fire drops (or at its commit's purge floor, `T_KEPT`).
- A tick walks the due aspects' places; a setting change walks those it moves out, and back from the cursor over those it brings back: O(the bodies that move), no body read. It walks after `date_due` (a superseder turning invalid in the same step moves its victims' rows later: `order::a_superseder_turning_invalid_dates_its_victim_before_the_timeline_walks_spec_commits_5`).
- **Rows are a schedule, never early, not an exact date**: they only time events; every read dates from the held set when asked (notes/engine-retention-dating.md). `date_due` sets them: a kill sets the row of each dying write's commit and of each snapshot of the aspect to the latest stamp winning where a write died (`Killed::at`), reading no body (`scale::kept_overwrite_reads_no_body_spec_commits_5`). A snapshot carrying none of the dying writes gets a row that leaves with nothing to tell (`setting::a_snapshot_is_told_leaving_history_spec_commits_5`). A rebuild and an aspect turning dated date whole (`dated_replaced_at`). Late, never early: the killing writes may be stamped after the first superseder, and an earlier superseder of a dead write may arrive with no kill; either leaves an event late, never before the body leaves. Rejected: exact dating at each kill (an overwrite of a kept register read every held body of the aspect, per edit). A debug check in `left` confirms a leaving row's part is out of history or not replaced (a snapshot's row from a kill of writes it does not carry; a text creation an undelete revived, issues/text-creation-undelete-dating.md).
- Rows exist whoever listens, so a later listener needs no arming. Rejected: a timer per body (a setting change re-armed and read every body).
- A purge request's header keeps its commit's parts to its purge floor, which no setting moves: a per-part `T_KEPT` timer like an expiry, armed when the part arrives, live or not, on any aspect (`listing::purge_floor_part_told_at_its_floor_spec_commits_5`). Timeline-dated events are the cursor's, header-dated ones per-part timers; a purged fire's tombstone is a per-part timer.

## Signals (spec/commits.md §5 Signals)
A signal's body is in no state, so nothing covers it: it is replaced when written (`bounds::part_replaced_at` returns its own `ts`) and kept to its keep bound under its AK's entry (keeping history means how long a write is exposed, never a deletion promise). Past the bound this engine drops it, whole or as its leftover, at apply, at its place leaving, or by `T_KEPT`: data nobody promised to keep is not kept for good, and needs no compaction. sync never counts a signal part established uncovered, so it is must-hold only while kept (`must_hold`). Convergence with a node that keeps it: reads judge by the bound from the header (`listing::past_due`); no copy past the bound is taken (`takes_late`, so no resend loop: `signals::signal_dropped_past_its_bound_not_taken_again_*`); nothing makes it missing again (`past_signal_bound` in `valid_again` and `purge::rejudge_purges`, as expiry: no refetch, no suspect flag nothing could clear). No join, rebuild, snapshot or cover touches it.

## Undo (`undo/`; spec/commits.md §7)
- **Kept by the setting**: each part an undo reverts (`undone_parts`, except a signal's, the creation record, `retention^.0`, parts on things it created, and a `group.0` part that only lists, `lists_only`) passes `bounds::setting_keeps` at the undo's `ts`, by the settings alone, so no node's compaction or timing changes the answer (Tess).
- **Pre-state** (`kept_before`): the held valid parts before the target that are live or within their keep bound, joined, only where the undo reads: parts writing the undone positions (`osis_crdt::touches`), the composites above, every snapshot, and inside a composite left absent kept parts newest first until it is present. Parts per position come from the undo index (`undo::touch`, a per-aspect cache on `Core` dropped past `TOUCHES_MAX`), so a check is flat in history (`undo::tests::check_cost_scales`). Debug builds compare with the whole join (`undo::compared`).
- **Replaced content kept** (`replaced_kept`, asked when the inverse needs it, `osis_crdt::restores`): every valid part before the target is in kept history, or its retention would keep it counted from the target's `ts`, or a kept part supersedes it: a later root reset or a snapshot whose `Frontiers`/`Absorbed` reach it (`retention::reaches`; tables cannot tell a part whose writes all died from one never applied, and an expiring part is never reached). A snapshot part is excused only by a later reset. An unkept part the target did not replace blocks too, since judging its body would depend on whether it is still held (issues/undo-unkept-part-blocks.md). Where the target's retention keeps what it replaces (`keeps_now`), only a part not fetched yet or a forgotten commit can block.
- **Forgotten commits** leave their `ts` in `Indexes::forgotten` per aspect their part was valid on (durable as `f/` rows; an invalid part replaced nothing, `forgotten_invalid_commit_blocks_no_undo_spec_crdt_4_6`), blocking unless a kept reset is stamped after. So forgetting may narrow what undo allows, never widens it; it is the undoing node's own refusal, never a receiver's rule (spec/crdt.md §4.6). Keeping more to excuse the part would be a lasting trace (notes/deletion.md).
- Memory: the trigger weighs no body a keep bound holds, and the memory cover drop waits for `part_keep` (notes/ephemeral.md Undo).
