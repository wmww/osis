# Priorities
> Summary: The ordered list of big osis-core projects the primary workstream draws from (major design change/refactor first, then complex bugs, then polish), each with its issues and notes; how coordinators use and maintain the list. Core only for now.

## How to use and maintain
- The primary workstream (notes/coordination.md, Job mix) takes the top unfinished item. Coordinators may reorder locally when convenient (an item blocked, two items that collide, a cheap item that unblocks a bigger one) and run non-colliding lower items in parallel, but when in doubt, follow this list, especially while Tess is away.
- Keep it big-picture: an item is a project or a cluster of related issues, never one small bug. Order by what makes osis's architecture right (design changes before the bugs they would reshape), not by what was discussed last. Don't promote an item just because it is on your mind.
- Update it when an item finishes (delete it), when a finding reshapes the order (say why in the commit), or when Tess sets direction. Keep each item to a few lines with pointers; detail lives in its issues and notes.
- Cleanup and workflow jobs (Job mix) run beside this list, not from it.

1. **Accounts on the ordinary rules, by the staged plan: notes/redesign-plan.md.** A1 and A2 (the sync rewrite) are built; next is A4, accounts as ordinary things, on main with no branch, then what A2 still owes (notes/sync-rewrite-state.md), then A3, A5, A6 (the route and its order: notes/handoff.md). An account becomes an ordinary independent thing whose members are things holding roles on it, a key that signs holds an ordinary grant on the thing it signs as, a commit states its actors and is judged along them, evidence travels per sync unit, profiles are public, core has no concept of an account (issues/actors-one-mechanism.md; notes/account-membership-design.md, notes/write-path-design.md). Stated for the built sync unit (notes/sync-unit-scope.md). The plan orders it and the work around it in stages, and says what every other item here does meanwhile. Realms are designed with it. Issues: actors-one-mechanism, behaviour-depends-on-own-keys, realms-design, external-signals-taken-by-default, readers-see-readers, sync-units-linked-at-rest. By stage: S3 role-vouch-contested (the notice), s3-addenda-build; A1 admin-fights-not-always-shown, dispute-scan-cost, perm-eval-cost; A2 engine-evidence-trails-model, cert-blob-hostile-delete; A3 removed-key-writes-show-far; parked: recovery-design.
2. **Whole commits tail.** The strip per blob and the sealed-sections rule are built (notes/whole-commits.md), and readers' own stores keep one record or list blob per certificate set. Left, each needing a design step: drop-rejoin's holder-logs-again and brute-force refetch. Issues: drop-rejoin-robust, zero-growth (storage growing with writes or churn; a removal's list outliving the history it served; work per write). Notes: whole-commits.
3. **Key passing after a removal, and grants without history.** history-less-grant carries on (next: records of dependent things under another's sync ID, its reproducer ignored). Two leaks to a removed party, removed-device-reads-later-grants and fresh-deposit-seed-readable-by-departed, wait for the account redesign, which change what a thing publishes and how evidence travels; each was proposed as a stated limit and is to be judged again as a bug to fix or a weaker property (notes/principles.md, Requirements state concepts and properties). Notes: keys, history-less-grant.
4. **Bounded memory tail.** Decode and read are bounded (notes/decode-bounds.md). Left: the snapshot writer loads and clones a whole state (needs an encoder pulling pages in wire order), and writing a whole collection over an existing one loads the old state whole. Issues: durable-decode-bound.
5. **Account signing tail: paused.** Everything left is in machinery the account redesign removes (a re-added node's ordering of revocations, own cover's scan, commit growth at rotations; account-side-shared). Take it up again only if the model sends the redesign back. Issues: account-signing, account-side-shared, rotation-names-grow, maker-unknown-from-snapshot, linked-thing-lists, prove-chain-cap.
6. **Slimmer things.** Tess: fixed overhead per thing, aspect, reference and commit (wire, disk, memory, CPU) is paid on everything forever, so cut whatever need not be there; no threshold, and nothing is assumed sensible as built. Surveyed (notes/thing-cost.md, Survey: every byte placed, ranked candidates, floors): a dependent thing holding one small value is 168 B of data, about 1.4 KB on the wire to an account's second node and 3.3 to 3.6 KB stored per node, the sync store counted; first candidates are storing each commit once (every node holds the sealed blob and an opened copy), one opened-under record per commit, and not echoing an entry to its sender. An independent thing is 14.9 KB in the engine's rows alone, mostly four KEM keys most of them will stop publishing (issues/external-signals-taken-by-default.md). Tess: millions of dependent things must be reasonable; independent things are rarer and their cost less urgent, but consolidate their keys where that does not blow up code complexity. General perf work: it runs after the bug tails above, and its format changes land before stability. Notes: thing-cost. Issues: behaviour-depends-on-own-keys, many-things-cost, many-sync-ids-cost, enclosing-unit-commit-cost, perm-eval-cost, derived-state-recomputed, chat-add-cost, rotation-names-grow.
