# Priorities
> Summary: The ordered list of big osis-core projects the primary workstream draws from (major design change/refactor first, then complex bugs, then polish), each with its issues and notes; how coordinators use and maintain the list. Core only for now.

## How to use and maintain
- The primary workstream (notes/coordination.md, Job mix) takes the top unfinished item. Coordinators may reorder locally when convenient (an item blocked, two items that collide, a cheap item that unblocks a bigger one) and run non-colliding lower items in parallel, but when in doubt, follow this list, especially while Tess is away.
- Keep it big-picture: an item is a project or a cluster of related issues, never one small bug. Order by what makes osis's architecture right (design changes before the bugs they would reshape), not by what was discussed last. Don't promote an item just because it is on your mind.
- Update it when an item finishes (delete it), when a finding reshapes the order (say why in the commit), or when Tess sets direction. Keep each item to a few lines with pointers; detail lives in its issues and notes.
- Cleanup and workflow jobs (Job mix) run beside this list, not from it.

## Core, in order
1. **Accounts on the ordinary rules.** Tess: an account becomes an ordinary thing whose members are signing keys, a key per device (visible) or one shared (hidden), with device removal the ordinary removal and a device fight the ordinary admin dispute, replacing the signing chain, epochs, custody, merges, sides and lists. Studied (notes/account-membership-design.md, notes/write-path-design.md: argued from the spec, with questions for Tess in the issue). Next: the model of path-judged validity with signers as members, against the stolen-node moves; then the gaps the studies found in the ordinary rules (removal lists per thing with no size limit, rotations under a dispute, re-grants, pairing), then visible mode. Until the study reports, take from the two account tails below only what would survive it. Realms are designed with it, since home and the account become one thing. Issues: actors-one-mechanism, behaviour-depends-on-own-keys, realms-design, open-signals-taken-by-default.
2. **Whole commits tail.** The strip per blob and the sealed-sections rule are built (notes/whole-commits.md), and readers' own stores keep one record or list blob per certificate set. Left, each needing a design step: invalid bodies kept at stores (a Tess call: re-grants restoring from stores, or deleting), drop-rejoin's holder-logs-again and brute-force refetch. Issues: invalid-junk-kept, drop-rejoin-robust, zero-growth (storage growing with writes or churn; work per write). Notes: whole-commits.
3. **Account forks and keys tail.** Remaining custody disagreements and key-passing bugs. Issues: removed-device-reads-later-grants, fresh-deposit-seed-readable-by-departed (both wait on Tess), history-less-grant (next: records of dependent things under another's sync ID, its reproducer ignored). Notes: engine-validity-epochs, keys.
4. **Bounded memory tail.** Decode and read are bounded (notes/decode-bounds.md). Left: the snapshot writer loads and clones a whole state (needs an encoder pulling pages in wire order), and writing a whole collection over an existing one loads the old state whole. Issues: durable-decode-bound.
5. **Account signing tail.** Re-adds, judgment cost and cutoffs are built; left: a re-added node taking an earlier revocation it could not judge ahead of one it took (roles-model custody large fails a world; order a revocation by when it came to stand), own cover's scan, commit growth. Issues: account-signing, account-side-shared (waits on settings).
6. **Slimmer things.** Tess: fixed overhead per thing, aspect, reference and commit (wire, disk, memory, CPU) is paid on everything forever, so cut whatever need not be there; no threshold, and nothing is assumed sensible as built. A dependent thing holding one small value is 168 B of data, 703 B on the wire and 1.6 KB stored (over half of it local index rows); an independent one 14.9 KB, mostly four KEM keys. Tess: millions of dependent things must be reasonable; independent things are rarer and their cost less urgent, but consolidate their keys where that does not blow up code complexity. General perf work: it runs after the bug tails above, and its format changes land before stability. Notes: thing-cost. Issues: behaviour-depends-on-own-keys, many-things-cost, many-sync-ids-cost, perm-eval-cost, derived-state-recomputed, chat-add-cost, rotation-names-grow.
7. **Sync leftovers.** The full-size differential's open classes, storage attribution, stubs, owed tests, timers. Issues: differential-full-size-classes, redeem-leaves-a-blob (a revoked link's seed thing is held forever), storage-owners, stranger-fire-bounds, writes-on-unheld-things-tests, forged-ticket-sync-id-entries, refetch-ticket-test, cover-before-commit, sync-unbuilt, sync-tests-owed, sync-pending-leftovers, sync-parks-check, sync-models-lag, sync-link-first-contact, dial-candidates, opens-hit-index-cost, timed-loss-wait.
8. **Long tail of core bugs and costs.** The remaining `normal`/`low` core issues (grep `> Tags:` for engine, sync, crdt, store, perm, keys); take them when they fit beside other work or block a higher item (settled-writers-dispute-script, index-check-rebuild-cost among them).
