# Co-reader peers as built (osis-node `sync/peers.rs`, `sync/serve.rs`)
> Summary: How a node syncs directly with other readers of its groups: `reach.0` publishing and rotation, which co-readers are dialed and how they prove themselves, LAN announcements (sealed signature, binding, routes and switching), co-reader serving over the node's own entries with its budgets and limits, local passes placing own writes in its store (and hosting labels synced with nobody), complete passes waiting on co-readers, and the driver's listeners and LAN loop. Design and transports: notes/networking.md; the protocol engines: notes/sync-protocol.md; node sync in general: notes/node-sync.md.

## `reach.0`
- `dialable_secret()` (random, local record `y/r`, shredded); `rotate_reach()` (osis `rotate_peer_address`: new dialable and lan secrets, `reach.0` written at once with the new secret's Ed25519 key and the old items, `Action::Reach` to the driver); `publish_reach(dialable key, items)` writes `reach.0` on the node thing when changed (the listener's key, every transport's key being the Ed25519 key of its secret; items: `p2p::reach_items`, global addresses then relays, notes/networking.md Reach items).
- The node card, `reach.0` included, goes under every synced label (IP consent: any positive action is consent, spec/sync.md §10; notes/networking.md). `#[must_use]` on `rotate_reach`.
- A reader removed from every shared group keeps the dialable key and lan secret until a rotation (by hand, or a direct link ending).

## Dialing co-readers
- Co-readers: node things other than this one with a held `reach.0` whose stated `account` (a dial hint, never proof) `may_read` a synced label's group, at most `max_peers_per_label` (8) per label and `max_peers` (64) in all, by node tid. Dialed (`SyncConfig.peers`, default on) at a LAN candidate, else a proven LAN address, else `peer://` + their `reach.0` items (global addresses, relays); `Conn.host` is the dialable key (presence, cursors), while the session pins the address key (a LAN address presents its per-network key). Redial pause `peer_retry_ms` (60 s); a connection that was up is redialed at once, at most once per `peer_retry_ms` per co-reader (`redialed`: a P2P switch to direct ends the relayed connection and the redial takes the parked direct one; the bound keeps a peer that closes every session, e.g. at `max_served`, from making a dial loop).
- Every dial uses an ephemeral transport key; what a connection shares is its shared set (issues/net-shared-set.md Built, `sync/shared.rs`): proofs, Resume, then filters and Offers on the same connection, whichever side dialed. Nothing else runs between readers: a peer connection carries no `LConn`, so no Open, pass, upload, delete or registration (`add_new_labels` starts hosts' labels only); both sides pull (The stream).
- **One connection per pair**: two connections bound to one record (`bind_record`) close down to one, whoever dialed each, at both ends: a direct one before a relayed one, then the smaller `connection_order`. Both ends tell a relayed connection alike: the transport's `Connection::relayed` (`P2pConn::Relayed` at dialer and target), passed by the driver as `set_relayed` before the connection's first event (the sim's `World` does the same for its relay routes). So a LAN dial crossing the peer's relayed one keeps the LAN one at both ends. A node whose dialed connection lost to one the peer dialed does not dial it while that session lasts (`stand_in`).
- The listener binds a Resume's record after the session's answer is out (`serve_with`), so the shared set's first Beacons never reach a dialer that has not begun its set (which would answer Error 14: an Error to a generation is a refusal of it (`shared_message`), so the connection settles; a whole filter goes again after `resync_ms` (`shared_retry`), as a refused Pull does).
- Connections to co-readers no longer wanted are closed (`drop_unwanted_peers`). `Config::direct_sync` off, and a driver without a `peer://` transport, turn `SyncConfig.peers` off (`set_peers`). Co-readers and the `reach.0` holders are cached until the engine generation changes (`Core::generation`, bumped by every changing transaction) or a minute passes (grants expire).
- A followed direct link's sharer node (`y/l`: K, the target, `peer://` addresses) is dialed for K and the target only; what opens there is the shared set: the link key's static entry on K, then the target's read secret once K's grant gives it (notes/networking.md, First contact).

## Connection proofs (`sync/proofs.rs`)
`SyncEngine::proven(conn)` is the one reader of what a connection proved: `Prove` then `Proved` (spec/wire.md §11.2), the node key, key actors, and the account by its epoch key, counted only at an epoch open by the rotations this node holds (`Engine::account_key_open`). A removed node keeps the seed of the epoch its removal's rotation closes: a verifier that does not hold that rotation still counts its proof, so a proven account must not give access; J counts key actors alone.

## LAN
- **Binding the announcement**: it carries an Ed25519 signature by the dialable key over BLAKE3(tag || key || be16 port) (domain `"osis v1 lan announce"`), XORed with a 64-byte keystream `kdf_xof("lan sig", lan secret, le64(window) || digest)` (bound to the digest, so another key or port in the window never reuses it; osis-crypto `seal_lan_sig`/`open_lan_sig`), so checking it needs both the lan secret and the dialable key. A plain signature would let every holder of the dialable key (relays the dialable listener registers at, co-readers) recognise the node's announcements across networks and windows. Chosen over a MAC under the lan secret (any co-reader of any label shared with the node knows that secret and could pose as the node on the LAN) and over a signature inside the first stream message (a protocol change, known only after connecting). Cost: dialable and LAN keys must be Ed25519 (every transport here). Vector: `core/test/vectors/lan/announce.vec` (provisional).
- `lan_tag(secret, ms)`; `lan_secret()` (a fresh transport secret per join); `lan_announcement(key, port)` (None until `reach.0` holds the dialable secret's key).
- `lan_seen(&Announce, from socket address)`: ignores non-local sources; looks the tag up in a per-window cache of the co-readers' tags (current and adjacent windows); opens and verifies the sealed signature under the matched lan secret, window and dialable key; binds each verified announcement of the newest window to its first source (`LanRoute::bound`, at most `LAN_BOUND` 64; a repeat is not verified again). A copy from another source, or an earlier window's, is a replay and ignored: else a spoofed replay draws one dial per source, showing a LAN attacker who reads the announcer. Binding the source IP into the signature was rejected for multi-homed machines; a network change rejoins with a fresh key, so a moved node is a new announcement. Residual: an announcement the receiver missed, replayed from a spoofed source, binds there and its identical 30 s repeats are ignored for the rest of its window, at most 10 min; a clock jump back on the announcer likewise ignores it until its window catches up.
- Then it counts the source among up to 4 verified addresses per peer (`heard`) and sets the address as the co-reader's candidate (`LanRoute`) at most once per `LAN_CHANGE_MS` (5 s); a candidate that fails falls back to the most heard other address; a repeat only refreshes it, and `handle` runs only when a dial can follow (no retry pause and no connection to the peer, or only one through a relay).
- **Routes**: the proven route first, else the candidate, else `peer://` + relays. An announced address is a candidate until a connection through it is up, never displacing a running connection before that: `connect` dials the LAN route beside a single running non-LAN connection (`lan_probe`; two `Conn`s share the peer key meanwhile), and when it comes up and is the proven route (`lan_up` true) the others close (`lan_switched`: cleaned up as lost, the retry entry dropped; labels resume on the LAN connection from their cursors; queued deposits in flight record their connection, `Outgoing::sent`, so only the closed one's go again). `lan_up` also proves a verified source (`heard`) the candidate moved away from while its dial ran. A LAN dial that fails before coming up backs off that address (`lan_failed`), and the next route is dialed at once (a relay connection, if any, just stays).
- A new candidate ends the peer's retry pause at most once per `peer_retry_ms` (`LanRoute.cut`): without it a node whose relay dial failed would wait the full pause; unbounded, replays from many sources would loop dials. `peer_routes()` lists co-reader connections (key, address, up).

## Co-reader serving (`sync/serve.rs`)
- Between readers a served session serves its shared set by the stream alone. `accept(local key, peer key)` starts a `ServingSession` (static key = the listener's key, which open signatures bind) over `PeerStore`, a `LabelStore` over the sync state's entries and the engine's blobs. Served labels are the synced ones at the deepest recorded step (plus at most 64 steps per label peers registered, in memory), cached until a `label.0` or role commit, a roster change or a peer step. An entry is live while this node holds its blob. Accepted uploads and deposits (from a node that is no reader: a peer that cannot read, a hand-driven dialer) go through `log_events_from` like downloads. Entries this node dropped (host deletion, own delete, must-not-hold drop) are tombstoned (`y/x`, corrected wall ms) and refused with code 6 for `TOMBSTONE_LIFETIME`; a peer's Delete is answered 0 and removes nothing (a deletion reaches other readers by the deleter's own stream), an upload's replacements likewise. `PeerStore` keeps no per-label log (`LabelStore::serves_log` false): Sync, Subscribe and Reconcile get Error 11. Registrations of new labels are refused. Signals from a peer fire locally and go to other peer sessions.

## The stream (`sync/stream.rs`; spec/sync.md §10 Stream)
What a peer reads of this node goes by one Pull stream per connection over the node log (notes/local-store.md, Node log), filtered by the connection's shared set. The module comment states the invariant (no write dropped, a source judged caught up holds nothing the requester lacks) and its argument; `stream_differential_no_write_dropped_spec_sync_10` attacks it.
- **Serving** (either side of any connection): `served_labels` and a state flush, then the log read in its own transaction from the lowest start among the stream's cursor and the joins', each member's events only past its own start, at most `SCAN_ROWS` (2^16) rows a reply; Added only where entry and bytes are held, inline within the requester's `inline_limit` unless the bytes came over this connection (`crossed`, at most 2^14). A reply with `more` false and `follow` starts pushes (`stream_push`, at the end of each step, one page per connection per step). Memory entries are the log's memory part (`MemLog` in `State`, one latest event per memory entry, deletions kept `MAX_DRIFT_MS`), which no cursor names: listed on a connection's first Pull and at every join, then in counter order, paged by the same budget. Pushes go at the end of each step after the shared set's re-judging, so a member a step ends gets nothing of it.
- **Requesting** (both sides, whoever dialed): a member entering S here joins (`shared_open` → `stream_join`, from its record lag, else the start; the side answering an Offer sends its answer first, so a join never reaches a server that has not opened L); leaving S leaves the stream at its position (`shared_close` → `stream_leave`, `Stop`: this side's position, a `Lost`'s cursor when nothing of the stream is unsettled, or none after a digest difference), and the record re-marks it pending in that transaction. One Pull in flight, joins batched into the next. A reply with `more` false and nothing waiting, or a push applied to a stream read to its end, is a pass end at the source for every member (`source_passed`, keyed by `source_key`: the dialed key, or the proven node's `reach.0` key). The record holds the cursor and S (each member with its class and beacon and `At`: at the cursor, or joining from its own position), written when a member opens (`stream_join`, taking its stopped lag) and moved as positions settle (`record_member_at`, which never reopens a member closed since). A Pull with any cursor that names no position (the stream's or a join's) is answered from the start (`whole`).
- **Applying** (`reader_events`, `Src::Reader`): a stamp counts only under the head this node serves; a `Deleted` does nothing (only a right holder's drop counts); a Head nothing (heads come by `label.0`); a relay-only entry goes on to the other readers sharing L (`stream_relay`: a Stream with no cursor).
- **Settling** (`Pulling::unsettled`): a position covers a body announced without bytes only once its bytes are held. Each reply or push is an `Unsettled` position with the bodies it owes (`wants`: bytes not held, not tombstoned here, not refused by the ephemeral cap); positions settle in order (`stream_settle`, on arrival and at the end of each step) once their bodies are held, no longer wanted, deleted by that source, or answered by its Fetch (bytes not kept, a relay-only blob or one past a budget, are owed no more: else fetched every step). A reader's announcement without bytes makes no entry row (`log_events_from`): the debt lives in the stream's memory alone and the row comes with the bytes, so the local pass keeps nothing for a stream and drops any byteless row; a lost connection or restart forgets the debt and the stream re-announces from its last stored (settled) cursor. Only a settled position is stored (cursor, merged members, page lags), reported (Applied) and a pass end; `stream_idle` and `stream_caught_up` want none unsettled. A member leaving keeps the last settled cursor as its lag, none if its merge or page is unsettled (read again from its last stored lag, else the start). Each step a body a stream owes that is held nowhere and asked nowhere (its fetch lost with a connection) is fetched from that stream's source (`stream_settle_all`). Without settling a crash or a lost connection between an announcement and its fetch loses the write for good: a stored cursor past the Added (sim `no_host`).
- **Bound** (`MAX_WAITING`, `Pulling::full`): a stream waiting on 1024 positions or owed bodies sends no Pull and drops pushes; after a dropped push it takes none until its next PullReply (pushes follow on from each other), and a settle asks again from the cursor. Flow control, not loss: what a peer can make this node keep per connection is that plus one reply and one push. A cursorless push (relay-only) applies only adds with bytes, so it owes and fetches nothing. Rejected: merging positions past a cap as `MAX_UNREPORTED` does (owed bodies still grow), a fetch deadline (a picked timeout).
- **Skipped requests** (`Pulling::sent`, `stream_skipped`): a peer answers a connection's Pulls and stream Fetches in order (spec/wire.md §11.2, Pull; this node answers both at once on arrival), so at any answer every earlier stream request still out was skipped: a Fetch is a failure (next source asked), a Pull a refusal. Stream requests go through the session's request window (notes/flow-control.md, The client window), which sends in the order made, so a request made earlier than an answered one was sent: one the window holds back is never counted skipped (proto `loopback::held_back_node_requests_keep_their_order_spec_sync_6`). A Fetch answered with nothing under `REPLY_BUDGET` is a failure (node `stream::a_source_answering_fetches_with_nothing_is_failed_spec_sync_10`). A peer that answers nothing at all is closed at the stall time whichever side dialed, one pushing or asking once its bytes pass `MAX_AHEAD` (spec/sync.md §6 Stall; notes/flow-control.md): its stream goes with the connection, so its fetches are asked nowhere and `stream_settle_all` asks another stream owing the body at the end of that step. A skipped request is never answered, so it stays the window's oldest and the stall closes a skipping peer too (once its other bytes pass `MAX_AHEAD`). A peer that keeps its connection (trickling its oldest reply, or pushing within `MAX_AHEAD`) while answering nothing holds its fetches only for the stall time (Bodies). Rejected: a probe whose in-order answer shows the Fetch skipped (a silent peer answers no probe either).
- **Bodies**: `inline_limit` is 0 while any tracked member has another source (`sources_of`: hosts with it open, readers with it in S); a member gaining a source makes every other stream tracking it Pull again if its last `inline_limit` differs (`stream_source_gained`, `Pulling::inline`), so a mesh body crosses once. Every body announced without bytes is fetched (`stream_fetch`, ids in the node range, `Fetch`/`FetchReply` handled by the node, not the session; `FIRST_REPLY` per ID, what did not fit asked again under `REPLY_BUDGET`, as any Fetch), those that open now first, one source at a time per blob; a failure asks the next connection sharing L, none left waits for another announcement. One rule for "asked" across sources, hosts and readers alike (`asked`): a fetch counts while it is younger than `STALL_TIMEOUT_MS` (`Fetching::at`, a host's in `Requests::fetching`); past it the next source is asked too, a stream owing the body (`stream_settle_all`, or its announcement) or a host that announced it (`fetch_on`, fill, `refetch`), each source at most once while its fetch is out (`may_ask`; the rest of a partly answered Fetch keeps its first ask time, so answering one body at a time holds the others no longer). Whatever skipped the body for a fresh fetch gets a pass at its stale time (`asked_for`: `settle_by`, so the next step's `stream_settle_all` runs too). Whichever answers first holds it, the later answer is a re-delivery (no row, no charge: `!has_blob`), and positions owing it settle. A trickling or silent source of either kind thus holds nothing beyond its own connection: passes wait on a fill's batch only while it counts as asked (notes/node-sync.md, First sync) (node `stream::a_partial_answer_does_not_restart_the_stall_time_spec_sync_10`, `stream::a_trickling_co_reader_holds_a_body_for_the_stall_time_only_spec_sync_10`, `stream::a_silent_co_reader_holds_a_body_for_the_stall_time_only_spec_sync_6`, `stream::both_sources_answering_store_once_and_settle_spec_sync_10`, `stream::a_trickling_source_of_either_kind_holds_a_body_for_the_stall_time_only_spec_sync_10`, `stream::a_host_and_a_co_reader_both_answering_store_once_and_settle_spec_sync_10`, `fill::a_trickled_order_0_batch_holds_the_label_for_the_stall_time_only_spec_sync_6`, `fill::a_body_skipped_for_a_trickled_fetch_comes_from_a_slow_host_by_the_stall_time_spec_sync_10`). A body crosses twice only when its first source was slower than the stall time. Rejected: asking a second source whenever a fetch elsewhere is older than the second stream's announcement (each body crosses twice in a mesh); a rate floor (a picked number). 
- **Reports and status** (`Serving`: `pulled`, `at_end`, `unreported`): every reply and push with a cursor and events asks `report`; the requester answers once the position settles; its Applied pops the reports up to that cursor (at most `MAX_UNREPORTED` kept apart, past it the oldest two merge). A reader source is caught up on L (`stream_idle`) when this node applied its stream of L to the end with no fetch out, and the reader joined L on this connection, was served to the log's end and reported every message carrying L: a fresh own write reads `Syncing` until the reader reported it. A reader connection still finding its shared set (dialed, or served with its set begun) counts as `opening`, which keeps the label from `Synced` (osis `label_state`).
- **Reconnect** (`shared_begin` with a record; spec/sync.md §10 Agreement): S, `acked_pos`, the own filter's generations and the peer's stored filter (`p` rows, one per generation since its last whole one) come from the record; the stream resumes the record's members where they are (`stream_bind`), the server counting them as joined (`pulled`). `Digest` goes first, then a re-judging of every member (a `Lost` carries the position last served on this connection, or the member's `until` where lower (`shared_stopped`); its receiver keeps it only with nothing unsettled and no push dropped), then the first generation: additions past `acked_pos` with `base` the last acknowledged, empty when nothing changed, whole when nothing was acknowledged, the last generation sent was not (its width and counts are not what the peer holds), or the session proves other actors. Offers wait for the peer's digest (`digest_read`), Pulls for agreement (`shared_agreed`); a difference lists S (`Members`) and closes what one side alone holds with no lag (`Stop::Listing`, `record_drop`). An idle reconnect: proofs, Resume, a Digest and an empty Beacons each way, one empty Pull each way, about 1.5 KB at any number of sync IDs (`reconnect::an_idle_reconnect_sends_no_per_sync_id_message_spec_sync_10`).
- **Ending at a rotation** (`until`; spec/sync.md §10 Lost): `shared_rejudge` gives a member opened by a credential no longer admissible, J neither holder nor departed, `until` = the node log's end after a flush (`log_end_now`): that step applied the rotation, and its pushes go after. Records not on a connection are judged in the same step for the sync IDs whose `changed_at` moved (`records_end`, `changed_moved` from `stamp_changes`), so a rotation while apart ends at the rotation, not the reconnect. Kept in the record (`record_member_until`). An ended member is scanned only to `until` (`scan`'s bound), gets no memory events, a Fetch only of entries whose latest event is at or below it (`log_entry_pos`). Its requester's reported position (`Serving::reported`, this connection only) comes from a Pull (its join's position, else the cursor for a tracked member, `stream_pull_reports`) or an Applied (the `ends` of the reports it pops); at or past `until` it closes with `Lost` naming `until` (`shared_until_due`, on every Pull, Applied and re-judging). A reply or push bringing it to `until` with no report there awaited asks one, a push then going even with no events (`Serving::until_due`). Holder or admissible again: `until` cleared. Departed meanwhile: `Lost` at once, at `until` or what was served, the lower (`shared_stopped`). Tests: `until::` (hand dialer: J unknown with real nodes is not constructible in `Net`, so the stream differential does not reach it).
- Not built: backpressure on pushes beyond one page per step.
- **Budget**: bytes new here that a reader's stream or fetches bring for entries unstamped under the served head (`charge_reader`), and bytes uploaded or deposited by a session that is no reader, count against the node the session proved when it is known here, else the label (`charge_key`: dialer keys are fresh per connection, so strangers with a link share K's budget instead of filling the node's total; `peer_bytes` 64 MiB each), and against all peers (`peer_total_bytes` 1 GiB), while the entry is held, unopened and at no host or peer; over it a reader's entry is not kept, uploads get code 12, deposits code 5; the reply budget reports what is left. Costs: blob sizes cached in memory (`blob_sizes`, pruned at ticks); charges keep running sums per charge key and in all, pruned at each tick and, at most once a second, after a budget refusal.
- **Sessions**: at most `max_served` (64): a new one evicts the oldest that serves nothing (no open, no member of its shared set), else is closed (a dialer chooses its key, so a per-key cap would bind nothing; silent LAN hosts cannot starve co-readers); in the driver as many connections (`link::serve`). A session silent for `serve_idle_ms` (120 s) is closed, but one sharing a sync ID (`kept_while_up`: a followed stream is silent while nothing changes; the transport ends a dead connection) or an authenticated client; one that serves nothing within `serve_open_ms` (30 s) is closed.

## Local passes
- Every synced label gets a local pass (`local_passes`, `pass/`) against this node's own store, on every change that marks labels dirty and every `resync_ms`, only while a reader can pull the node (`serves_readers`: `reach.0` published, or a connection sharing a sync ID; the first member shared starts one): a node's own writes reach readers only through its own log, so they are placed there whatever hosts are connected; one label's error is a diagnostic. Its work sets: notes/node-sync-work-sets.md.
- Every local pass places (`body_sends`, certificates, stamps), then runs `local_deletions`: what this node's store must not hold goes, whoever else holds the label. Under a label with hosts a host pass then finds a placed entry (`entry_for`) and uploads what its host lacks; steps, promotion and re-seals stay the host pass's, and an entry a host holds goes by that host's deletion (`absent_at`). An upload in flight keeps nothing: one dropped here before a host's answer (mid-upload, or the answer lost with the connection) is tombstoned with what it was, and that host reporting it later (an OK answer, an announcement) records it again as present there (`host_holds`, notes/node-sync.md), so the host pass judges and deletes it as any present entry, whatever its kind (`local_serve::covered_own_entry_*`, `purged_chunks_*`, `certificate_copy_whose_answers_were_lost_*`: mid-upload, across a restart, past the tombstone where the announcement is fetched and judged as any, chunks by a writer's delete key, a certificate copy with its linked blobs). Without local deletions there, an own entry placed while no host is connected and covered before any host took it would stay for good (`local_serve::own_entries_covered_while_the_host_is_away_leave_spec_sync_5`). A label with no non-`peer://` host in its `label.0` has this node as its host: its local pass also does the host's part below.
- The host's part shares the host pass's pieces: `step_due` (then `issue_step` from a head recorded in `label.0`, and the pass goes on under the new step, as `reregister` would at a host); `body_sends` (must-hold bodies and their linked blobs, placed; inbox copies, certificates; what it placed is stamped when it writes); `deletable` (judge, carrier, `may_delete`, delete item; the host pass and `verdicts` use it too).
- `local_deletions`, over entries no host or peer holds: commit entries go as `deletable` says, even blobless ones (a dropped body with live writes waits for its carrier, a snapshot asked for), expired ones outright; other entries go when their bytes are gone or their prefix expiry passed (read once, `local_expiry`); chunks of dropped deltas go; bytes no other entry names are deleted. Snapshots under the label (its own included) drop the bodies they cover, as in the host pass (`drop_label_covered`).
- A writer promotes deposits locally as at a host (`local_promote`: past the relay wait, the author's missing chain parts placed as its own copies, then the stamp; `local_serve::deposit_promoted_on_local_label_spec_sync_5`, which fails without it when the writer cannot dial the depositor back; `deposit_chain_completed_on_local_label_spec_sync_5`: the reader's `keys.0` deposit lost, the writer places its own copy). Not done locally: orphans (another writer's stamped copy with an incomplete chain stays; issues/node-sync-gaps.md). Deposits to such a label (inbox blobs, own edge writes) are logged locally (`local_inbox`, through `log_events` like a peer's deposit).
- Chosen over a loopback session (a `Conn` to its own served store): that needs presence and cursors against itself for nothing a peer sees. Without local passes a sharer with no saved peer or other node would hand a direct-link redeemer an empty K label (osis `redeem_direct_no_peer_in_process_spec_permissions_7`).

## Complete passes and co-readers (spec/permissions.md §3)
`complete_pass` also waits for each co-reader X the node received the label's entries from by a reader connection (`peer_received`, by `source_key`: the dialed key, or the proven node's `reach.0` key) while X is a co-reader of that label and a connection to X shares it (`pass_peers`, computed at the end of each `handle`), for a pass end there after the role change (The stream, Requesting), and at most `peer_pass_wait_ms` (5 min) after the actor's last remote role commit (`role_change`; from the engine's start, `started`, when none applied in this run). A label with neither hosts nor such peers passes once that wait is over; a role change drops X from labels it no longer reads. So a removed co-reader, or one that stalls on purpose, cannot hold re-seals back.

## Driver (`sync/driver.rs`)
- Dials ephemerally. Listeners come with `run_serving`; `Control::Reach { urls, reply }` listens with the dialable secret at the first of `urls` that listens (for `P2p` the socket address to bind and the relays to register at; osis gives `[::]:p2p::PORT`, then `[::]:0`, each with `Config::relays`) and calls `publish_reach` with the listener's key and `reach_items` of its address (the routes read when its bound IP is unspecified) (the URLs remembered).
- `Action::Reach` (a rotation): after the current event the driver drops the old dialable listener (awaited: an in-process URL is free only once dropped; the sessions a listener accepted, `served_by`, close with it, as a P2P listener's socket ends its connections), listens again at the remembered URL with the new secret (a failure is logged and retried every `RELISTEN_RETRY`, 5 s: `reach.0` already names the new key), and rejoins the LAN at once.
- `Control::Lan(LanConfig { port, to, listen, watch, announce, join_repeats, join_gap })`: takes its announcement socket from the transport (`Transport::lan`; a failed bind is logged, the node runs on with no LAN loop). Sockets share the port (`SO_REUSEADDR`), so nodes on one Linux machine all bind it and all hear broadcasts; a unicast target reaches only one of them. A join announces at once and `join_repeats` more times `join_gap` apart and starts a `NetWatch` task (unless `watch` is off, in tests); its changes and `Control::LanRejoin` (an app's OS callback) make it rejoin at most every `LanConfig.rejoin_min` (`LAN_REJOIN_MIN` 30 s; a timer runs a deferred one), rotations at once. A rejoin drops the old LAN listener (awaited), listens at `listen` (default `peer://0.0.0.0:0 [::]:0`, IPv4 only if that fails) with `lan_secret()` and announces at once, then every `announce` (`LAN_ANNOUNCE`, 30 s) to each of `to` (default broadcast and ff02::1 at 47380), each family's announcement with the listener's port on that family (one dual-stack `[::]` socket: the same port on both); datagrams heard (a bounded queue, a flood is dropped) go to `lan_seen`.
- The osis handle's driver (`Net::spawn_driver`) runs on `Multi` (always `unix` for the client port; a test's in-process transport as `mem`, else QUIC and `P2p` over QUIC, relaying for co-readers, fed by `Control::PeerKeys` from `peers()` at ticks and local changes), sends `Reach` (P2P: `peer://[::]:<p2p::PORT>`, then `peer://[::]:0`, each with `Config::relays` as relay items; in process: `mem://<endpoint>-peer`) when `Config::direct_sync` (default on), and `Lan` (`LanConfig::default()` plus `Lan::On`'s targets) unless `Config::lan` is `Off`.
- Tests: by hand (notes/testing.md By hand, networking): node driver tests over P2P with the LAN loop on localhost, IPv4 and IPv6, through a relaying node, and relay plus LAN loop ending on the LAN route. Default tests: the LAN loop over the nat.rs model's segments (osis-node tests/it/driver.rs `driver_lan_*`); (osis tests/it/runtime.rs) first contact by a direct link with the host partitioned from the redeemer; a rotation listened on without a restart.
