# Co-reader peers and served connections as built (osis-node `sync/peers.rs`, `sync/serve.rs`)
> Summary: How a node syncs directly with other readers of its groups: `reach.0` publishing and rotation, which co-readers are dialed and how they prove themselves, LAN announcements (sealed signature, binding, routes and switching), served connections (every one a hub session, node sync above it) with their budgets and the one rule bounding them, local passes placing own writes in its store (and hosting labels synced with nobody), complete passes waiting on co-readers, and the driver's listeners and LAN loop. Design and transports: notes/networking.md; the protocol engines: notes/sync-protocol.md; node sync in general: notes/node-sync.md.

## `reach.0`
- `dialable_secret()` (random, local record `y/r`, shredded); `rotate_reach()` (osis `rotate_peer_address`: new dialable and lan secrets, `reach.0` written at once with the new secret's Ed25519 key and the old items, `Action::Reach` to the driver); `publish_reach(dialable key, items)` writes `reach.0` on the node thing when changed (the listener's key, every transport's key being the Ed25519 key of its secret; items: `p2p::reach_items`, global addresses then relays, notes/networking.md Reach items).
- The node card, `reach.0` included, goes under every synced label (IP consent: any positive action is consent, spec/sync.md §10; notes/networking.md). `#[must_use]` on `rotate_reach`.
- The dialable key is the node's one key: every listener of it but the LAN's and the unix socket's listens under it (spec/sync.md §10, One key; `Osis::listen`'s URLs too), so a client, a saved peer and a group's `label.0` hosts name it.
- **No automatic rotation**: the dialable key and lan secret change only by `rotate_peer_address`; ending a direct link, or removing a reader, rotates nothing, so an ex-reader keeps both and a link holder the dialable key. Why: an old key gives its holder only a way to dial the node, and dialing opens nothing (the shared set and J decide what a connection reaches); what it still learns is the node's address, that it is online, and, while LAN announcements carry a lan-secret tag, the node on a LAN (issues/sync-rewrite.md drops the tag; issues/privacy.md). Rotating would strand every node that knew only the old key and hears the new `reach.0` from nobody (a joiner away behind a NAT), break a key named in `Config.relays`, and change the key own data lists. `rotate_peer_address` drops the old listeners at once (their connections end with them) and listens again at the same URLs under the new key, returning only once the driver has done so or failed (a failed listen is logged; the `Control::Reach` one retries after `RELISTEN_RETRY`, and a rotation during that wait returns before the retry; `Osis::listen`'s URLs stay down, notes/sync-rewrite-inputs.md) (`Net::caught_up`: a marker `Control::Call` after the rotation's `Apply`, answered after the driver's `after_rotation`, which runs at the end of every loop turn): a dial right after it reaches the new key, never a closed old QUIC endpoint still draining on the port (it answers CONNECTION_REFUSED until its socket goes).

## Dialing
- **Whom** (`wanted_nodes`, by dialable key): the configured nodes, those the synced labels' and hints' `label.0` name, a queued deposit's, and (`SyncConfig.peers`, default on) co-readers and followed links' sharer nodes. A dial is a dial: nothing about it but its key and address (`Conn::dial`) is kept, and per-label state starts on it for each label naming that key (`add_new_labels`). A dialed node no longer wanted is closed (`drop_unwanted`).
- Co-readers: node things other than this one with a held `reach.0` whose stated `account` (a dial hint, never proof) `may_read` a synced label's group, at most `max_peers_per_label` (8) per label and `max_peers` (64) in all, by node tid. Dialed at a LAN candidate, else a proven LAN address, else `peer://` + their `reach.0` items (global addresses, relays); the dial is by the dialable key, while the session pins the address key (a LAN address presents its per-network key).
- **Pace** (`lost`, one rule for every node dialed): a relayed connection that was up is redialed at once, at most once per `peer_retry_ms` (60 s) per node (`redialed`: a P2P switch to direct ends the relayed connection and the redial takes the parked direct one; the bound keeps a peer that closes every session from making a dial loop); another that was up waits `retry_ms` (5 s); a dial that never came up doubles the pause, from `retry_ms` to `peer_retry_ms` (`Dial::pause`), so a node that stays away costs a dial a minute and one back after a blip is dialed within seconds. Test: node `peers::a_relayed_connection_is_redialed_at_once_spec_sync_10`.
- Every dial, a host's included, proves this node's actors (`Event::Up`; spec/sync.md §10, Connection proofs) and runs Resume, else NoRecord (no known actor proved back, or the Prove or Resume refused: `no_record`), so a listener's unread entries reach any dialer.
- Every dial uses an ephemeral transport key; what a connection shares is its shared set (issues/sync-rewrite.md, `sync/shared.rs`): proofs, Resume, then filters and Offers on the same connection, whichever side dialed. Nothing else runs between readers: a label a co-reader reads holds no `LabelAt` on its connection, so no pass, delete or registration there; both sides pull (The stream).
- **One connection per pair**: two connections bound to one record (`bind_record`) close down to one, whoever dialed each, at both ends: a direct one before a relayed one, then the smaller `connection_order`. Both ends tell a relayed connection alike: the transport's `Connection::relayed` (`P2pConn::Relayed` at dialer and target), passed by the driver as `set_relayed` before the connection's first event (the sim's `World` does the same for its relay routes). So a LAN dial crossing the peer's relayed one keeps the LAN one at both ends. A node whose dialed connection lost to one the peer dialed does not dial it while that session lasts (`stand_in`).
- A dialer's Prove and Resume are node sync's, not the session's (`answer_prove`, `answer_resume` in `serve.rs`, from the hub's `Up::Message`, notes/serving.md): it checks each at most once, Prove after the dialer's Hello and Resume after Prove (else Error 2), verifies the proofs over its own challenge and key, and answers Resume Error 1 when no proved actor is known here; an engine error in either is logged and answered Error 1, never a failed step (a peer cannot make the node's step fail). A Prove or Resume arriving on a dialed connection is Error 11. The listener binds a Resume's record after the session's answer went down (`hub_up`), so the shared set's first Beacons never reach a dialer that has not begun its set (which would answer Error 14: an Error to a generation is a refusal of it (`shared_message`), so the connection settles; a whole filter goes again after `resync_ms` (`shared_retry`), as a refused Pull does).
- `Config::direct_sync` off, and a driver without a `peer://` transport, turn `SyncConfig.peers` off (`set_peers`). Co-readers and the `reach.0` holders are cached until the engine generation changes (`Core::generation`, bumped by every changing transaction) or a minute passes (grants expire).
- A followed direct link's sharer node (`y/l`: K, the target, `peer://` addresses) is dialed for K and the target only, and only while one of their labels is synced here (notes/networking.md, First contact); what opens there is the shared set: the link key's static entry on K, then the target's read secret once K's grant gives it (notes/networking.md, First contact).

## Connection proofs (`sync/proofs.rs`)
`SyncEngine::proven(conn)` is the one reader of what a connection proved: `Prove` then `Proved` (spec/wire.md §11.2), key actors, the account by its epoch key, counted only at an epoch open by the rotations this node holds (`Engine::account_key_open`), and the node key only between nodes of one account. J, known actors, records and credentials count every proven actor alike (`Proven::all`): a node of another account is the account it proves.
- **Proved once, at the connection's start**: a connection where this node proved its actors and lacks one it proves now (a link key held since, the account's seed) is closed (`reprove`, looked at when the key actors or the role generation moved): a dialed one is dialed again at once, a served one (`Conn::answered`: the dialer proved a known actor, so this node answered with its own) by its dialer. No Prove goes twice, and a peer judges a connection only by what it proved, so a redeemer connected before it held the link key was a stranger to a reader of K and never saw K in its filter; served too, as a record's two connections keep the one of smaller rank, which may be the peer's dial. A dialer it answered with none (unknown then) is not closed when it becomes known. Cost: each new actor closes every connection once (rare: a link followed, an account joined).
- **Never its own peer**: no address naming this node's own key (its account's saved peer, a label's host, a hint's) is dialed (`wanted_nodes`, `own_key`): such a dial took the record of another connection and was closed as its duplicate, then dialed again at once, about every 5 ms.
- **Node key only to own nodes** (`provers(engine, own)`): a dialer proves it when the key it dials is, by the `reach.0` held, a node on its account's D roster (`dials_own_node`, `Core::own_node`); a listener when the dialer proved its account at an open epoch or its node as such a node (`own_peer`). `Proven::node` is the first proof only when it is such a node. A node of another account that sends its node key anyway gets nothing for it: it holds roles only on its own D (private to its account), so it is neither known nor a holder here.
- **Source** (`Proven::source`): the node for an own node, else the first proved account known here (`Judge::known`; an account known by nothing can be made per connection, so it bounds nothing and the label is charged instead). Budgets (`charge_key`) and suspect-repair dedupe (`repair_state`, `suspect_message`) are per source, so all nodes of another account share one budget and one repair per state.
- **Departure of a node** is its removal from D plus the rotation of P in one transaction (`rotate_in`). Limit: a verifier not yet holding the rotation (outside P only the rotation shows the removal) still counts the removed node's account proof, as it keeps that epoch's seed; its node key stops counting at P's nodes with the removal (off D's roster).

## LAN
- **Binding the announcement**: it carries an Ed25519 signature by the dialable key over BLAKE3(tag || key || be16 port) (domain `"osis v1 lan announce"`), XORed with a 64-byte keystream `kdf_xof("lan sig", lan secret, le64(window) || digest)` (bound to the digest, so another key or port in the window never reuses it; osis-crypto `seal_lan_sig`/`open_lan_sig`), so checking it needs both the lan secret and the dialable key. A plain signature would let every holder of the dialable key (relays the dialable listener registers at, co-readers) recognise the node's announcements across networks and windows. Chosen over a MAC under the lan secret (any co-reader of any label shared with the node knows that secret and could pose as the node on the LAN) and over a signature inside the first stream message (a protocol change, known only after connecting). Cost: dialable and LAN keys must be Ed25519 (every transport here). Vector: `core/test/vectors/lan/announce.vec` (provisional).
- `lan_tag(secret, ms)`; `lan_secret()` (a fresh transport secret per join); `lan_announcement(key, port)` (None until `reach.0` holds the dialable secret's key).
- `lan_seen(&Announce, from socket address)`: ignores non-local sources; looks the tag up in a per-window cache of the co-readers' tags (current and adjacent windows); opens and verifies the sealed signature under the matched lan secret, window and dialable key; binds each verified announcement of the newest window to its first source (`LanRoute::bound`, at most `LAN_BOUND` 64; a repeat is not verified again). A copy from another source, or an earlier window's, is a replay and ignored: else a spoofed replay draws one dial per source, showing a LAN attacker who reads the announcer. Binding the source IP into the signature was rejected for multi-homed machines; a network change rejoins with a fresh key, so a moved node is a new announcement. Residual: an announcement the receiver missed, replayed from a spoofed source, binds there and its identical 30 s repeats are ignored for the rest of its window, at most 10 min; a clock jump back on the announcer likewise ignores it until its window catches up.
- Then it counts the source among up to 4 verified addresses per peer (`heard`) and sets the address as the co-reader's candidate (`LanRoute`) at most once per `LAN_CHANGE_MS` (5 s); a candidate that fails falls back to the most heard other address; a repeat only refreshes it, and `handle` runs only when a dial can follow (no retry pause and no connection to the peer, or only one through a relay).
- **Routes**: the proven route first, else the candidate, else `peer://` + relays. An announced address is a candidate until a connection through it is up, never displacing a running connection before that: `connect` dials the LAN route beside a single running non-LAN connection (`lan_probe`; two `Conn`s share the peer key meanwhile), and when it comes up and is the proven route (`lan_up` true) the others close (`lan_switched`: cleaned up as lost, the retry entry dropped; labels resume on the LAN connection from their cursors; queued deposits in flight record their connection, `Outgoing::sent`, so only the closed one's go again). `lan_up` also proves a verified source (`heard`) the candidate moved away from while its dial ran. A LAN dial that fails before coming up backs off that address (`lan_failed`), and the next route is dialed at once (a relay connection, if any, just stays).
- A new candidate ends the peer's retry pause at most once per `peer_retry_ms` (`LanRoute.cut`): without it a node whose relay dial failed would wait the full pause; unbounded, replays from many sources would loop dials. `peer_routes()` lists co-reader connections (key, address, up).

## Connections at the hub (`sync/serve.rs`, `sync/conn.rs`)
- Every connection, dialed or one a peer opens on a listener of this node, is a session of its hub (notes/serving.md, Hub), started at its Hello under the listener's key (which open signatures and connection proofs bind); node sync keeps one record of each (`Conn`, by the hub's connection ID in `hub_conns`, `hub_up`) and is its upper layer: proofs, records, the shared set, the stream, push room and the client port, alike whoever dialed. Which side dialed is read by the dial logic alone (`Conn::dial`: the key and address dialed, redial, LAN routes, pings) and by the proof exchange's order (the dialer proves first).
- **A peer that dialed this node** holds per-label state here only for what it proved (`add_new_labels` starts labels only on a connection this node dialed to a node the label names; a client's session holds none: `port_tiers::a_client_session_holds_no_label_state`). Where it cannot read L it opens L only by proving L's sync secret (spec/sync.md §5 Opens): this node's filter and Hit table to a known dialer carry the proof credential (`PROOF`: `kdf("sync proof", s)`, `s` the seed of the sync key K of the newest step `label.0` records, `open::sync_seed`) of each label it reads whose sync secret it holds (`Which::accepts`; `stamp_changes` counts K, so a step under a new one sends additions; a reader lacking that secret shows none; an older step's proof is never shown, and a removed sync holder, J departed, sees none: `unread::only_the_newest_steps_proof_is_in_a_readers_filter_spec_sync_10`; each whole filter derives one seed and its Ed25519 key per label, more only where the newest step's key is not the newest held sync secret's), the dialer Hits the proof of its head's sync key where it holds the secret (`shared_proof_keys` yields seeds; `Which::proves`, at each filter arrival), and that Hit opens L. The Hit carries no signature: only a holder of the sync secret computes the proof's match value, so its Hit and tag prove the secret as a read secret's do, and a node holding only L's public values finds no proof in a reader's filter (`unread::a_node_holding_only_the_public_keys_finds_no_proof_in_a_readers_filter_spec_sync_10`; NetOpen `ON_proofpub`). The proof is its own credential, not the raw sync key: a filter showing raw sync keys of labels a node reads made a co-reader's walk (it Hits raw sync keys it finds shown) open L between two readers by sync key, as at a peer that cannot read (osis `runtime::a_paired_peer_serves_what_it_reads_in_process_spec_data_model_9` stalled) (`unread::a_peer_that_cannot_read_pulls_from_a_reader_it_dialed_spec_sync_10`). This node sends no Hit or Behind item signed by a sync key or claim there (it walks nothing there, and `behind_from` sends none on a served connection), so the public key alone gets nothing (`unread::a_dialer_holding_only_the_public_sync_key_pulls_nothing_spec_sync_10`). Every open signature (Hit, Static, Behind) binds its signer's side (`open_digest`, as `proof_digest`): signed as this side (`Shared::by_listener`), verified as the peer's, so a signature this node sent, reflected through a dial whose Hello challenge copies this node's from another connection, is no signature of the peer's (`shared::a_static_item_reflected_from_a_second_dial_opens_nothing_spec_sync_10`; NetOpen `ON_side`). The open is a `PROOF` member (`by_key`, as `SYNC`): re-judging closes it once the newest recorded step bears another sync key (one run per proof: `unread::a_dialers_proof_ends_at_the_next_sync_key_spec_sync_5`). J judges a holder of Sync alone unknown, not departed, so its creds stand. This proof is the start of 6c's entitlement check (issues/sync-rewrite.md 6c builds on it). The open gives L a `LabelAt` on that connection, open from the start at the head kept for the peer or the newest recorded step (`member_proved_there`), moved by its stream's Head events, and gone when L closes there: the dialer serves L as a store and is a source of the pass as one this node dialed (presence, deletes, Deposit items, registration under the same sync key: `unread::a_reader_deletes_at_a_peer_that_dialed_it_spec_sync_10`), kept by their record (Presence, notes/node-sync.md). Its own writes go to it as Deposit items directly while it lacks them, not through the outbox (there is no address to dial it again by). Left: this node's static-entry Hit (a key actor it holds) still goes to such a dialer, which opens L here by answering with tag and grant from claim sets it keeps (issues/sync-rewrite.md). Between readers a session serves its shared set by the stream alone; the hub's store answers the store's kinds (data this node hosts; nothing while it hosts none), so what a peer uploads, deposits or registers there is the hub's, never node sync's. Between readers an inbox blob goes as a shared set's Deposit item (notes/inbox.md, Deposit items).
- **Tier** from the transport type alone (`Remote::peer`): a unix peer of this process's uid is main, everything else keyed (notes/client-port.md §0). A relayed connection (`Remote::relayed`) is marked before its first message (`set_relayed`), as a dialed one is.
- **Push room**: a session's room (`Down::WantRoom`, `Up::Room`) serves its two pushers, the client port's Notifies and the stream, one grant at a time in the order they asked (`Room`, `want_room`, `release_room`): the holder gives it back when its push went or it had none (the stream at the end of its push step, `stream_room_unused`; the port by its `Event::Notify`, which the worker always sends after an `Action::Room`, empty when it had nothing), and the next in line asks again. Granting both at once could put two pushes past `REPLY_BUDGET` behind the backlog. A stream that waits for room is not at its log's end (`at_end` false), so a write waiting there reads `Syncing`.
- **Sessions**: no count limit (spec/sync.md §10 Served connections): every connection is served until it closes or its peer stalls, a client's at its `until` (`serve_timers`); none is closed for sharing nothing, so a slow first contact is never cut and its redials are not wasted. A dialer chooses its key, so a per-key count would bind nothing anyway. Memory: notes/serving.md, Connection memory.
- **Budget**: an entry unstamped under the served head, held, giving nothing held (`gives_nothing`: unopened, bad, parked, awaiting, its commit pending, or known only for a part dropped while invalid: notes/node-sync.md, Input giving nothing) and at no host (`counts`) costs its size (`Entry::size`, as it came) against the budget of the peer that brought it and against all peers (`peer_total_bytes` 1 GiB). The peer is the session's source (`Proven::source`; for a Deposit item its node, when known here) when known here, else the label (`charge_key`: dialer keys are fresh per connection, so strangers with a link share K's budget instead of filling the node's total; `peer_bytes` 64 MiB each). Such entries come only from a reader's stream or fetches (`charge_reader`) and Deposit items (`charge_deposit`); an entry of a host goes once no host has it (`absent_at`). The source is stored with the entry (`Entry::source`, 15 bytes at the row's end, only while it counts: `keep_source` when charged, `clear_source` when `prune_charges` finds it stamped, judged, at a host, or gone), so at open `charge_held` charges every entry that counts to its stored source, and a restart gives no peer a fresh budget. Cost: 15 bytes per charged row, nothing on others; charged entries are bounded by the budgets. Over a budget a reader's entry is not kept (nor its pending commit), a Deposit item is refused, and the app hears `InputCapped` once until a charge is freed. In memory: blob sizes (`blob_sizes`, pruned at ticks); running sums per charge key and in all, pruned at each tick and, at most once a second, after a budget refusal. Rejected: budgets per label (fully derived, no stored source): one hostile reader in 16 groups fills the 1 GiB total and holds back every label's unjudged input, where per source its share is 64 MiB. Tests: `peers_review::peer_budget_holds_across_restart_spec_sync_5`, `a_fresh_account_splits_no_budget_spec_sync_5`, `a_charged_source_costs_its_row_until_judged_spec_sync_5`.

## Sync IDs kept unread (`sync/unread.rs`; issues/sync-rewrite.md)
Principle: what a node keeps and cannot read names no actor of its own, so its head's credentials go to every connection and a credential's proof alone opens it.
- **Heads** come from the hub's store (`HostStore::unread_labels`, `head_step`), cached until `heads_generation` moves (any head, a label dropped, the read set); a move also re-stamps `changed_at` (`unread_moved`), so additions and re-judging follow as for read sync IDs. `credentials` gives such a label `head_credentials`.
- **Filter**: every connection's shared set holds them (a NoRecord starts the set whoever the dialer proved); a node's read credentials only while the peer proved a known actor (`Shared::read_out`: a change of that sends a whole filter). Classes `SYNC` and `DEPOSIT` beside `READ`, `STATIC`. The node tests and offers none of them (`TESTED`), nor ByRole on them, but the proof of its head's sync key as a dialer where it holds the secret (`shared_proof_keys`).
- **Hits** (`shared_hit_proved`): the Hit table holds what the filter shows (`shared_table`: read credentials only for a known actor, so a stranger's blind Hit on a read secret opens nothing); a sync key's needs its open signature (the dialer's side, over the receiver's Hello challenge and the listener key); a static entry's a key actor proved here (`Proven::signing`, known or not), answered with the hub's claim-set box and path (`unread_grant`); a deposit key's never opens. A Deposit item for an unread inbox goes through the hub store's Deposit rules (`unread_deposit`: `osis_proto::session::deposit`, then `Down::Changed` so host subscribers hear).
- **Serving**: a member is never pulled on this side (`stream_bind`'s pulled set, no `stream_join`; the record keeps it `At::Stream`); the stream serves its entries and head from the hub's store (`stream_added`, Head events, Fetch fallbacks). Its ephemeral entries: the hub keeps the events of the streamed set (`set_streamed`, `take_memory`), taken into `MemLog` (a newly streamed one's live entries listed first, `unread_restream`; an expiry is `MemLog::gone`). Relay-only uploads to it pass the hub's relay gate and come up as `Up::Relay`, forwarded to the other members (`stream_relay`).
- **Ending**: a member whose credential is no longer the head's gets `until` = its head's event (`unread_until`, `log_head_at`), on connections and in records not on one (`records_end`); served through that event, Lost once reported past it. The hub logs a head before node sync sees it, so a moved head is judged at the start of the next step, before its event (a Pull) is answered (`Unread::moved`).
- **The reading side**: a reader tests the credential of the newest step of L it knows at every peer that cannot read L it dialed (a node L lists) at each arrival of its filter (`Shared::sync_due`), Hits it with the open signature, and pulls that peer's stream as a store's log (notes/node-sync.md, Heads at a peer that cannot read L). A peer that cannot read L and dialed a reader proves the sync secret instead, and is read as a store alike (Connections at the hub).
- Not here (issues/sync-rewrite.md 3i2): the hub gating Delete on these opens.

## The stream (`sync/stream.rs`; spec/sync.md §10 Stream)
What a peer reads of this node goes by one Pull stream per connection over the node log (notes/local-store.md, Node log), filtered by the connection's shared set. The module comment states the invariant (no write dropped, a source judged caught up holds nothing the requester lacks) and its argument; `stream_differential_no_write_dropped_spec_sync_10` attacks it.
- **Serving** (either side of any connection): `served_labels` and a state flush, then the log read in its own transaction from the lowest start among the stream's cursor and the joins', each member's events only past its own start, at most `SCAN_ROWS` (2^16) rows a reply; Added only where entry and bytes are held, inline within the requester's `inline_limit` unless the bytes came over this connection (`crossed`, at most 2^14). A reply with `more` false and `follow` starts pushes (`stream_push`, at the end of each step, one page per connection per step). Memory entries are the log's memory part (`MemLog` in `State`, one latest event per memory entry, deletions kept `MAX_DRIFT_MS`), which no cursor names: listed on a connection's first Pull and at every join, then in counter order, paged by the same budget. Pushes go at the end of each step after the shared set's re-judging, so a member a step ends gets nothing of it.
- **Requesting** (both sides, whoever dialed): a member entering S here joins (`shared_open` → `stream_join`, from its record lag, else the start; the side answering an Offer sends its answer first, so a join never reaches a server that has not opened L); leaving S leaves the stream at its position (`shared_close` → `stream_leave`, `Stop`: this side's last settled cursor, none if its merge or page is unsettled; a `Lost`'s cursor, always, since it names a position this side stored; none after a digest difference), and the record re-marks it pending in that transaction. One Pull in flight, joins batched into the next. A reply with `more` false and nothing waiting, or a push applied to a stream read to its end, is a pass end at the source for every member (`source_passed`, keyed by `pass_source`, Complete passes). The record holds the cursor and S (each member with its class and beacon and `At`: at the cursor, or joining from its own position), written when a member opens (`stream_join`, taking its stopped lag) and moved as positions settle (`record_member_at`, which never reopens a member closed since). A Pull with any cursor that names no position (the stream's or a join's) is answered from the start (`whole`).
- **Applying** (`reader_events`, `Src::Reader`): a stamp counts only under the head this node serves; a `Deleted` does nothing (only a right holder's drop counts); a Head nothing (heads come by `label.0`); a relay-only entry goes on to the other readers sharing L (`stream_relay`: a Stream with no cursor).
- **Settling** (`Pulling::unsettled`): a position covers a body announced without bytes only once its bytes are held. Each reply or push is an `Unsettled` position with the bodies it owes (`wants`: bytes not held, not tombstoned here, not refused by the ephemeral cap); positions settle in order (`stream_settle`, on arrival and at the end of each step) once their bodies are held, no longer wanted, deleted by that source, or answered by its Fetch (bytes not kept, a relay-only blob or one past a budget, are owed no more: else fetched every step). A reader's announcement without bytes makes no entry row (`log_events_from`): the debt lives in the stream's memory alone and the row comes with the bytes, so the local pass keeps nothing for a stream and drops any byteless row; a lost connection or restart forgets the debt and the stream re-announces from its last stored (settled) cursor. Only a settled position is stored (cursor, merged members, page lags), reported (Applied) and a pass end; `stream_idle` and `stream_caught_up` want none unsettled. A member this side closes keeps the last settled cursor as its lag, none if its merge or page is unsettled (read again from its last stored lag, else the start). Each step a body a stream owes that is held nowhere and asked nowhere (its fetch lost with a connection) is fetched from that stream's source (`stream_settle_all`). Without settling a crash or a lost connection between an announcement and its fetch loses the write for good: a stored cursor past the Added (sim `no_host`).
- **Bound** (`MAX_WAITING`, `Pulling::full`): a stream waiting on 1024 positions or owed bodies sends no Pull and drops pushes; after a dropped push it takes none until its next PullReply (pushes follow on from each other), and a settle asks again from the cursor. Flow control, not loss: what a peer can make this node keep per connection is that plus one reply and one push. A cursorless push (relay-only) applies only adds with bytes, so it owes and fetches nothing. Rejected: merging positions past a cap as `MAX_UNREPORTED` does (owed bodies still grow), a fetch deadline (a picked timeout).
- **Pushes in order** (`Stream::seq`, `Serving::pushes`, `Pulling::pushes`): a push with a cursor carries how many such pushes the server sent since its last PullReply, and is taken only as the next; one out of order is dropped as the bound drops one. Without it a lost push is invisible: a dishonest host dropping the announcement of a co-writer's covering snapshot leaves one writer judging a body established uncovered and placing it again, fresh, each time the co-writer (seeing the snapshot) deletes it, a storm until a reconnect (sim `evil_host_drops` 455770488995/455770489005, and 941804269790/941804269794 "not quiescent"; `stream::a_push_lost_on_the_way_is_read_again_spec_sync_10`). A host that lies in its log itself (never lists the cover to one node), or announces deletes it never had, gets one turn of that loop per body: the holder places it again once, and a delete of that placement stands for its tombstone's life while the label's head stays (notes/node-sync.md, Placed again once; `hostile_host::a_host_hiding_a_cover_from_one_writer_costs_one_placement_again_spec_sync_6`). Unbounded, each turn left a fresh blob record and two tombstones at the holder and two tombstones at the deleter (about 800 B and 560 B per turn), kept 30 days. A duplicated push (sim `dup_pct`; no real transport duplicates) costs one PullReply; an honest stream needs none (`stream::in_order_pushes_need_no_reply_spec_sync_10`). Rejected: the previous cursor in each push (72 bytes against a counter's one or two). Test harnesses number hand-made pushes as the peer's next (node `Net::in_order`, sim `host_says`).
- **Skipped requests** (`Pulling::sent`, `stream_skipped`): a peer answers a connection's Pulls and stream Fetches in order (spec/wire.md §11.2, Pull; this node answers both at once on arrival), so at any answer every earlier stream request still out was skipped: a Fetch is a failure (next source asked), a Pull a refusal. Stream requests go through the session's request window (notes/flow-control.md, The request window), which sends in the order made, so a request made earlier than an answered one was sent: one the window holds back is never counted skipped (proto `loopback::held_back_node_requests_keep_their_order_spec_sync_6`). A Fetch answered with nothing under `REPLY_BUDGET` is a failure (node `stream::a_source_answering_fetches_with_nothing_is_failed_spec_sync_10`). A peer that answers nothing at all is closed at the stall time whichever side dialed, one pushing or asking once its bytes pass `MAX_AHEAD` (spec/sync.md §6 Stall; notes/flow-control.md): its stream goes with the connection, so its fetches are asked nowhere and `stream_settle_all` asks another stream owing the body at the end of that step. A skipped request is never answered, so it stays the window's oldest and the stall closes a skipping peer too (once its other bytes pass `MAX_AHEAD`). A peer that keeps its connection (trickling its oldest reply, or pushing within `MAX_AHEAD`) while answering nothing holds its fetches only for the stall time (Bodies). Rejected: a probe whose in-order answer shows the Fetch skipped (a silent peer answers no probe either).
- **Bodies**: `inline_limit` is 0 while any tracked member has another source (`sources_of`: hosts with it open, readers with it in S); a member gaining a source makes every other stream tracking it Pull again if its last `inline_limit` differs (`stream_source_gained`, `Pulling::inline`), so a mesh body crosses once. Every body announced without bytes is fetched (`stream_fetch`, ids in the node range, `Fetch`/`FetchReply` handled by the node, not the session; `FIRST_REPLY` per ID, what did not fit asked again under `REPLY_BUDGET`, as any Fetch), those that open now first, one source at a time per blob; a failure asks the next connection sharing L, none left waits for another announcement. One rule for "asked" across sources, hosts and readers alike (`asked`): a fetch counts while it is younger than `STALL_TIMEOUT_MS` (`Fetching::at`, a host's in `Requests::fetching`); past it the next source is asked too, a stream owing the body (`stream_settle_all`, or its announcement) or a host that announced it (`fetch_on`, fill, `refetch`), each source at most once while its fetch is out (`may_ask`; the rest of a partly answered Fetch keeps its first ask time, so answering one body at a time holds the others no longer). Whatever skipped the body for a fresh fetch gets a pass at its stale time (`asked_for`: `settle_by`, so the next step's `stream_settle_all` runs too). Whichever answers first holds it, the later answer is a re-delivery (no row, no charge: `!has_blob`), and positions owing it settle. A trickling or silent source of either kind thus holds nothing beyond its own connection: passes wait on a fill's batch only while it counts as asked (notes/node-sync.md) (node `stream::a_partial_answer_does_not_restart_the_stall_time_spec_sync_10`, `stream::a_trickling_co_reader_holds_a_body_for_the_stall_time_only_spec_sync_10`, `stream::a_silent_co_reader_holds_a_body_for_the_stall_time_only_spec_sync_6`, `stream::both_sources_answering_store_once_and_settle_spec_sync_10`, `stream::a_trickling_source_of_either_kind_holds_a_body_for_the_stall_time_only_spec_sync_10`, `stream::a_host_and_a_co_reader_both_answering_store_once_and_settle_spec_sync_10`, `fill::a_trickled_order_0_batch_holds_the_label_for_the_stall_time_only_spec_sync_6`, `fill::a_body_skipped_for_a_trickled_fetch_comes_from_a_slow_host_by_the_stall_time_spec_sync_10`). A body crosses twice only when its first source was slower than the stall time. Rejected: asking a second source whenever a fetch elsewhere is older than the second stream's announcement (each body crosses twice in a mesh); a rate floor (a picked number). 
- **Reports and status** (`Serving`: `pulled`, `at_end`, `unreported`): every reply and push with a cursor and events asks `report`; the requester answers once the position settles; its Applied pops the reports up to that cursor (at most `MAX_UNREPORTED` kept apart, past it the oldest two merge).
- **Reported position** (`Serving::position`, this connection only; spec/sync.md §10 Lost): where the requester stored each member, what every `Lost` names (or `until` if lower), and what places this node's own commits with no host to take them (`note_applied`, notes/engine-lost-access.md Placed). The stream's (`Serving::reported`): the first Pull's cursor (the record's, stored before this connection), then each Applied's message position. A join's own (`Serving::own`): its position in the first Pull naming it (a record lag, stored), then a page's as its message is reported, until the message that merged it (`Own::merged`, counted by `Serving::seq`) is reported. A later Pull's cursor and page positions report nothing: the requester sends them before they settle, so a lag kept at one would skip bodies it still owed or entries not flushed. So the receiver keeps a `Lost`'s cursor always, and a push it dropped (`MAX_WAITING`) needs no rule (`reconnect::a_lost_after_a_dropped_push_names_a_reported_position_spec_sync_10`). Cost: an Applied crossing the `Lost` makes the rejoin read a little again. O(1) per message (no per-member positions per report); O(joins) state. A reader source is caught up on L (`stream_idle`) when this node applied its stream of L to the end with no fetch out, and the reader joined L on this connection, was served to the log's end and reported every message carrying L: a fresh own write reads `Syncing` until the reader reported it. A reader connection still finding its shared set (dialed, or served with its set begun) counts as `opening`, which keeps the label from `Synced` (osis `label_state`).
- **Crossing Losts** (`stream_lost_closed`, `shared_lost`): a side that closes a member itself keeps its stream position as the lag, but a peer that had ended the member (`until`) skipped its events past `until` while that position moved on, so a rejoin from it misses writes. The peer's Lost, when it comes for a member already closed here, replaces the lag (by the peer's key or in the record); a node receiving a Lost for a member it was ending answers with its own Lost naming where the member stopped.
- **Reconnect** (`shared_begin` with a record; spec/sync.md §10 Agreement): S, `acked_pos`, the own filter's generations and the peer's stored filter (`p` rows, one per generation since its last whole one) come from the record; the stream resumes the record's members where they are (`stream_bind`), the server counting them as joined (`pulled`). `Digest` goes first, then a re-judging of every member (a `Lost` carries the reported position, or the member's `until` where lower (`shared_stopped`)), then the first generation: additions past `acked_pos` with `base` the last acknowledged, empty when nothing changed, whole when nothing was acknowledged, the last generation sent was not (its width and counts are not what the peer holds), or the session proves other actors. Offers wait for the peer's digest (`digest_read`), Pulls for agreement (`shared_agreed`); a difference lists S (`Members`) and closes what one side alone holds with no lag (`Stop::Listing`, `record_drop`). An idle reconnect: proofs, Resume, a Digest and an empty Beacons each way, one empty Pull each way, about 1.5 KB at any number of sync IDs (`reconnect::an_idle_reconnect_sends_no_per_sync_id_message_spec_sync_10`).
- **Ending at a rotation** (`until`; spec/sync.md §10 Lost): `shared_rejudge` gives a member opened by a credential no longer admissible, J neither holder nor departed, `until` = where L's last rotation superseding the credential's epoch was logged (`shared_until_at`, after a flush: of the established epochs superseding the credential's, found by its beacon among the held secrets, every rotation commit's (`EpochInfo::rotations`, `snapshots`) entries in L, the highest first-logged position), else the log's end (a static-entry credential, an epoch derived from the base actor's, no entry of the rotation in L). That step applied the rotation and its pushes go after; what was logged after the rotation in the same step (another writer's write in the rotation's batch, an app write placed by the same pass, the label's own step) is not served (`until::a_write_after_the_rotation_in_its_batch_is_not_served_spec_sync_10`, `until::a_write_logged_after_the_rotation_in_its_step_is_not_served_spec_sync_10`). Every rotation superseding it is served, not only the first: a holder J judges unknown needs each to reach the current epoch (`until::every_rotation_superseding_the_credential_is_served_spec_sync_10`); what lies between two of them is under a newer epoch than the credential's. What one flush logs before the rotation is served: the log's order within a flush is not the commits' order. No re-judging before a step's event is needed: an own write or a batch is logged only by the pass or the event that applies it, and the step's end re-judges before anything later is served. Records not on a connection are judged in the same step for the sync IDs whose `changed_at` moved (`records_end`, `changed_moved` from `stamp_changes`), so a rotation while apart ends at the rotation, not the reconnect. Kept in the record (`record_member_until`). An ended member is scanned to `until` and, past it, only for events of entries first logged at or below it (`scan`, `log_entry_first`: a stamp coming back from a host re-logs the rotation past `until`, which must still reach a holder J judges unknown; such an event carries no new content: a blob ID is its bytes' hash under a fresh key, so only a wrap of the same key, a stamp or a deletion is new), gets no memory events and no relay-only entries (`Shared::live`), a Fetch only of entries first logged at or below it. Its requester's reported position (above) at or past `until` closes it with `Lost` naming `until` (`shared_until_due`, on every Pull, Applied and re-judging). A reply or push bringing it to `until` with no report there awaited asks one, a push then going even with no events (`Serving::until_due`). Holder or admissible again: `until` cleared. Departed meanwhile: `Lost` at once, at `until` or the reported position, the lower (`shared_stopped`). Tests: `until::` (hand dialer: J unknown with real nodes is not constructible in `Net`, so the stream differential does not reach it).
- Not built: backpressure on pushes beyond one page per step.

## One pass per label
- Every synced label has one pass (`passes`, `label_pass`, `run_pass`; its work set `SyncEngine::work`), whichever peers are connected: the own part judges this node's store once (placing in priority order, certificates, stamping and `local_promote` for writers, local deletions, reseal, a step when it has no sources), then each source (`sources`: a connected peer that cannot read L serving it as a store, whoever dialed, read to its end) is judged over what its stream showed (`known`): registration (`reregister`), entries it lacks stamped for its head (it pulls them), Deposit items, deletes. Readers are never sources: they pull what the own part placed. Details: notes/node-sync.md The pass.
- A label no peer serves still passes, so a sharer with no other node hands a direct-link redeemer a full K label (osis `redeem_direct_no_peer_in_process_*`).

## Complete passes and co-readers (spec/permissions.md §3)
`complete_pass` also waits for each source X the node received the label's entries from by a reader connection (`peer_received`), for a pass end there after the role change (`peer_passes`; The stream, Requesting), connected or not, while X still reads the label (judged at the check: an own node with its account), and at most `peer_pass_wait_ms` (5 min) after the actor's last remote role commit (`role_change`; from the engine's start, `started`, when none applied in this run). A source is what the connection's proofs identify (`pass_source` = `Proven::source`): an own node is itself, a node of another account is that account (it proves no node key here), so another account's nodes stand for each other (spec/permissions.md §3) and a pair's records, crossing first dials or a lost record leaving two, are no sources (a key per record left an orphan holding every pass the full wait). A peer known by nothing proves no source. Both maps are kept while the source is known (an own node on the roster, else `Judge::known`; `prune_pass_sources` when the engine generation moved) and per label until it is forgotten: bounded by own nodes and known accounts times labels, nothing per record or connection. A label with neither hosts nor such sources passes once the wait is over. So a removed co-reader, or one that stalls on purpose, cannot hold re-seals back past the wait.

## Driver (`sync/driver/`)
- Dials ephemerally. Every listener is the hub's (notes/serving.md, The hub in the node). `Control::Reach { urls, reply }` listens with the dialable secret at the first of `urls` that listens (for `P2p` the socket address to bind and the relays to register at; osis gives `[::]:p2p::PORT`, then `[::]:0`, each with `Config::relays`) and calls `publish_reach` with the listener's key and `reach_items` of its address (the routes read when its bound IP is unspecified) (the URLs remembered). `Control::Listen { urls }` binds more URLs under the dialable key in place of the last ones (`Osis::listen`), `Control::Socket` the unix socket.
- `Action::Reach` (a rotation): after the current event the driver drops the old dialable listeners (awaited: an in-process URL is free only once dropped; the sessions they accepted close with them, as a P2P listener's socket ends its connections), listens again at the remembered URLs with the new secret (a failure of the reach listener is logged and retried every `RELISTEN_RETRY`, 5 s: `reach.0` already names the new key), and rejoins the LAN at once.
- `Control::Lan(LanConfig { port, to, listen, watch, announce, join_repeats, join_gap })`: takes its announcement socket from the transport (`Transport::lan`; a failed bind is logged, the node runs on with no LAN loop). Sockets share the port (`SO_REUSEADDR`), so nodes on one Linux machine all bind it and all hear broadcasts; a unicast target reaches only one of them. A join announces at once and `join_repeats` more times `join_gap` apart and starts a `NetWatch` task (unless `watch` is off, in tests); its changes and `Control::LanRejoin` (an app's OS callback) make it rejoin at most every `LanConfig.rejoin_min` (`LAN_REJOIN_MIN` 30 s; a timer runs a deferred one), rotations at once. A rejoin drops the old LAN listener (awaited), listens at `listen` (default `peer://0.0.0.0:0 [::]:0`, IPv4 only if that fails) with `lan_secret()` and announces at once, then every `announce` (`LAN_ANNOUNCE`, 30 s) to each of `to` (default broadcast and ff02::1 at 47380), each family's announcement with the listener's port on that family (one dual-stack `[::]` socket: the same port on both); datagrams heard (a bounded queue, a flood is dropped) go to `lan_seen`.
- The osis handle's driver (`Net::spawn_driver`) runs on `Multi` (always `unix` for the client port; a test's in-process transport as `mem`, else QUIC and `P2p` over QUIC, relaying for co-readers, fed by `Control::PeerKeys` from `peers()` at ticks and local changes), sends `Reach` (P2P: `peer://[::]:<p2p::PORT>`, then `peer://[::]:0`, each with `Config::relays` as relay items; in process: `mem://<endpoint>-peer`) when `Config::direct_sync` (default on), and `Lan` (`LanConfig::default()` plus `Lan::On`'s targets) unless `Config::lan` is `Off`.
- Tests: by hand (notes/testing.md By hand, networking): node driver tests over P2P with the LAN loop on localhost, IPv4 and IPv6, through a relaying node, and relay plus LAN loop ending on the LAN route. Default tests: the LAN loop over the nat.rs model's segments (osis-node tests/it/driver.rs `driver_lan_*`); (osis tests/it/runtime.rs) first contact by a direct link with the host partitioned from the redeemer, the key unchanged and still dialed after the link ends; a rotation listened on without a restart.
