# Account forks: custody, roster, canonical merges
> Summary: Design closing the account-fork problems (a node that sided with the thief cannot switch back, a bare removal acting on D, a thing listing wrapped under the epoch it decides, the merge storm): custody of a secret follows the boxes held and never a judgment; D's roster, keys and removals are P's chain; a merge's epoch is a pure function of the tips it joins. Tess approved the design. Built on branch `fathom-custody-engine`: custody and its leave rule (phase 1), the chain as roster (phase 2), merges only join (phase 3), the engine-vs-model differential and its fixes (phase 4). Modelled in roles-model `custody` (every property holds, every negative control fails).

Tess's rule this serves (spec/permissions.md §5.4): every node, the account's and outside readers, follows the first device revocation it sees; a counter-revocation raises "account forked"; the user may switch sides and a switch sticks; nothing switches back by itself; D's roster is never an input of the side.

## For a reviewer
Status: Tess approved the design. Phases 1-4 built on branch `fathom-custody-engine`; next one review and one gate.

- **The problem.** An account P is several devices (nodes). D is P's devices actor (its roster). Removing a lost or stolen device X must stop X reading anything new and acting for P, with no server, devices offline at random, several devices changing things at once, and X fighting back with the old keys it holds (forking the chain, revoking honest devices). Terms: spec/permissions.md §1 (actors, roles), §5.4-5.5 (rotations, epochs, sides, the epoch rule); spec/crypto.md §3-5, §9 (epochs, seeds, boxes, wrapping).
- **Issues**: the merge storm and the sibling thing-listing cycle are closed (phase 3). Open: issues/switch-carries-words.md (the differential's one remaining disagreement), issues/thing-rekey-on-thief-side.md.
- **Rejected: patching the current design.** Each local fix to the current rules (first-seen sides, Waiting for D's lists, the leave guards) closed one case and review found the next; the three decisions under The wrinkle below keep producing them.
- **Evidence.** roles-model `custody` (core/test/roles-model/README.md, Custody; src/custody.rs): every property below holds over 8,000 random and 77,440 exhaustive small worlds, including a thief with every old key and lockstep schedules, and each of five deliberately broken variants fails. A model is not the engine: the engine gets the same check by differential (phase 4).
- **Plan.** (1) Custody: seeds boxed to each device's key, opened whatever counts; leave only when no held line keeps this device a node; seed re-seals. (2) The chain is the roster: D's keys derived from P's epochs, membership changes only inside P rotations. (3) Merges only join: a merge's seed a function of its tips. (4) Engine-vs-model differential, then one review and one gate. Phases 2 and 3 touch the same code and can be one build.
- **Built so far.** Phases 1-4 on branch `fathom-custody-engine` (the Built sections below).
- **Open questions a reviewer should press on.** Whether opening every held box (stale ones included) for reading leaks anything a removed or demoted device should not see; whether the merge-seed rule ("anyone holding all joined seeds can derive it") is no wider than the existing unaware-sibling limit; whether pairing as a rotation is acceptable (outsiders see one more rotation; on the branch it set off re-keying storms until boxes to an account went stale only at revoked epochs); whether the signing guard (sign only holding every removal a line names) can stall an account; how storage stays attributable (Tess: unsync everything → zero) for seeds and other accounts' chains.

## The wrinkle
Two decisions make all four problems.

1. **Judgment gates custody.** A secret a judgment needs to read its evidence opens only after that judgment counts:
   - P's seeds sit in D's `account.0` under D read epochs, which D's timeline establishes, which the epoch rule judges, which reads P's chain, whose side reads inner signatures, which open with seeds. Every engine fixpoint on this path (`rotations_due` taking seeds up as each opens the next D epoch, the account-rotation wrap exception, "counts once its inner signature opens") is this cycle. A node on the thief's side never counts the honest line, so never opens its seeds, so reads it as minted by no node: the switch cannot take.
   - A thing epoch is read only once its rotation counts (boxes at a closed account epoch are stale, a secret from a commit not counting is not established), while the listing that makes it count is wrapped under an epoch descending from it.
2. **D is a second source of the roster.** D has its own role timeline and key schedule, judged by the epoch rule, beside the chain's taken revocations. A removal is a D role commit apart from the P rotation carrying it, so it can act on D's roster and re-key D while its rotation is not held, and the two sources disagree. Fixing the roster to the chain alone stalled only because the seed sits under D's re-key, which reads the removal: decision 1 again.

The merge storm is a third, separate decision: **a merge carries its maker's choices** (a random seed, its takes and answers, its list), so two nodes merging the same heads mint two epochs, which fork again.

## Principles
- **Custody follows holding.** A node reads with every secret it can open from what it holds (a box to its own key, a seed or secret derivable from ones it holds), whether or not the commit carrying it counts or its epoch is open or stale. Judgment decides only what counts and what the node uses (signs at, encrypts under, boxes to others). So judgment is a function of held commits alone, never of a fixpoint with keys.
- **The chain is the roster.** At a node of P, who is a node on a line is read from P's rotations alone: D has no timeline of its own for membership and no key schedule of its own.
- **A merge is a join, nothing more.** Its epoch is a pure function of the tips it joins; anything else a node has to say goes in a plain rotation.

## Design
### Custody
- A rotation boxes its new seed to the KEM key of each device on its roster, in its D section (sealed under the D read secret of the epoch it is signed at, below). Any node holding a seed may later box it to a roster device lacking it (a seed re-seal, the existing re-seal rule applied to seeds). A device holds a seed iff it holds such a box to itself or derives it (Merges), whoever wrote the box and whether or not its rotation counts. A newcomer to a line (a device added, or one a joined fork added) gets the seeds its maker holds of the epochs on that line, none of a line beside it (else a node on X's side hands X's made-up device the honest line's seeds).
- An inner signature opens with the seed of the epoch it is signed at or of the epoch the item mints; a merge certificate's with the seed of any epoch it joins (a rotation seals it again under each: spec/wire.md §4). So a node of P reads who minted every rotation boxed to it on any line, and its side is a function of held rotations and its user's choice.
- A node signs at, rotates from or boxes only a seed of an open epoch it follows on which it is a node. Holding a line whole (with what it takes) already holds every removal on it, so "takes up a seed only where it holds every removal its writer held" is structural.
- Every box a node can open is opened for reading: a box to P at a closed or not-open epoch, a box in a commit that does not count (the reading-only rule, spec/crypto.md §4, widened to stale boxes). Stale still means "do not encrypt under, re-seal or rotate"; it never means "unread".
- A node removed on the line it follows keeps its device key, its seeds and D's sync IDs while any held line keeps it a node (the warning stands, its user may switch); it leaves (drops seeds and what it read as P) only when no held line keeps it: of the held rotations counting or whose seed it holds, no tip's line (under the removals on it) has it as a node. A rotation that merely boxes it nothing removes no one. A node off its roster, or not holding its preferred head's seed, signs nothing as P.

### Roster and D
- D keeps secrets of its own, rotated only inside P's rotations: each P rotation is also D's rotation of all four roles, boxed to that rotation's roster (no departs mark: D's departures are the chain's), so the general rotation wrap applies and nothing acts on D before its rotation is held. Rejected: deriving D's secrets from P's epochs like a plain thing's; a plain thing has no sync ID of its own (spec/sync-ids.md §2) and D's home is one, so it would need a new actor kind.
- Every change of D's membership is carried by a plain P rotation: its D section holds the removals and additions (a pairing is a rotation adding the device; a rotation's history box gives the new device the seeds its adder holds). No removal or grant exists apart from its rotation, so nothing acts on D before its rotation is held.
- Nodes on a line: the devices the creation record names, plus additions, minus removals, carried by rotations on the line. A removal of y carried by r hits every rotation minted by y that is not on r's line (made before it), unless it descends from a re-add of y on a line descending from r. A taken revocation from another line hits as now (spec/permissions.md §5.4). An addition by a removed node is in a rotation the removal hits, so it adds no one: forged and backdated grants, Z, and a grant at an unaware sibling's epoch need no rule of their own.
- D's roster at a node = the nodes of its preferred head's line under the taken revocations; a rotation's roster is that of the line it makes (the heads it joins) after its own changes, and devices on it not on the roster of the epoch it is signed at get every held seed. D bears no edge write: every engine read of a role on D (`Core::devices_holders`: role_now, rosters, key holders, own_node, card ends) is the chain's roster. The epoch rule never reads D for membership.

### Merges
- A **plain rotation** carries removals, additions, `takes`, answers (rotations it supersedes unnamed) and a fresh random seed, and names no fork. A **merge** carries nothing but the join.
- A merge's **tips** are the plain rotations maximal on the union of the lines it joins. Its seed is `kdf("merge seed", tips' seeds in epoch-name order)`, so its keys and epoch name (`[height, signing key]`, spec/crypto.md §3) are a function of the tip set. Structures name epochs by epoch name, not by the minting commit's ID.
- A merge commit is one **certificate** of that epoch: signed at a joined epoch, with each joined epoch's merge signature over the epoch name, the maker's inner signature, and seed boxes to roster devices that cannot derive the seed (a device added on one branch). Several certificates of one epoch are one epoch: not beside each other, closing the same epochs. The epoch counts iff some certificate of it is followed; a certificate minted by a node a taken removal hits (or by no node of its line) stands with nothing: it counts not, nor its boxes or D re-key, and it stops no node from certifying the merge itself. A certificate is signed at every epoch it joins (for the rejection rule's rotations signed at an epoch).
- Who can derive a merge seed: exactly the holders of every tip's seed, the intersection of the joined lines' custodies. A node aware of a removal merges only heads one of which carries it, so the removed node lacks that tip's seed and the merge's; a node that would have to merge lines lacking a removal it holds makes the plain rotation first.
- Policy, any node of P, at the end of a complete pass of D's sync IDs: if it owes a plain rotation, make one at its preferred head; then, if it follows more than one head and holds every tip's seed, make the merge of all of them; re-seal its heads' tip seeds to roster devices it holds no box to them for. It owes a plain rotation for:
  - a removal or an addition it makes;
  - an answer: a held rotation that does not stand under its taken revocations or stands with one it did not take, on no line a counting rotation answers, that it has not answered (superseded besides the epoch it was signed at: one it rotated on while following it answers nothing; a rotation waiting only on its parent epoch is owed nothing); a merge not counting only where it joins a line not followed (one of followed tips is certified instead);
  - a taken revocation its preferred head's line does not carry (on it, or taken by a rotation on it), only while it cannot make the merge: a taken revocation counts, so some head carries it, and the merge of all heads carries it.

### Why this ends the storm by structure
- Plain rotations are made only for carried content, each at most once per node per event (removal, addition, taken revocation, answered line). A merge creates no plain rotation. So the plain rotations of a run are finite, and merge epochs are subsets of them.
- Two nodes holding the same plain rotations make the same merge epoch, in lockstep or not; once every node of P holds them all, every node has one head. No timing, no designated merger, no wait on any one device: any node holding the tips' seeds makes it, and a second certificate is harmless.

## Properties
At every node and every prefix of its receipts, on top of roles-model `forks`'s (revocation holds, no flip, no false alarm, seeds stay away from X, settled):
- **Custody is monotone.** The secrets a node can open only grow with what it holds (but for its own leave); no secret's custody waits on a judgment.
- **A switch takes.** After a user's switch to a held line on which the node is a node, the node follows that line, signs at its head, and counts every honest write on it, whatever it followed before (its own removal taken first included).
- **One roster source.** D's roster at a node is a function of the rotations it follows and its taken revocations; no removal changes whom a node boxes unless its rotation is taken there (held, even with its chain, is not enough: a revocation whose line merges a fork its maker signed as a made-up key actor is held but not taken).
- **Bounded rotations.** Under every delivery schedule (lockstep, symmetric, random), honest nodes make at most one plain rotation per node per event and stop rotating once deliveries complete; merges made at once of the same tips are one epoch.
- **What decides opens without it.** On a thing with a holder outside P, a sibling's rotation of the thing at a closed epoch of P and the listing naming it open at the other node: its writes there count (the listing-cycle shape).
- **Merge secrecy.** No merge seed made by a node aware of X's removal is derivable from seeds X holds.
- **Certificates are interchangeable.** Holding any one of an epoch's certificates by nodes of its line instead of all of them changes nothing a node reads.

## Model
roles-model `custody` (core/test/roles-model/README.md, Custody): parties `forks`'s plus C (two removals made apart) and T reading only; thing t held by an outside reader; the thief holds every header and old key, forks, revokes, signs as a made-up key actor, adds, takes what it withholds, answers, certifies merges and re-seals. Random and lockstep schedules, an exhaustive small family. Negative controls, each failing: `custody-judged` (switches and held keys), `roster-bare` (one roster source), `merge-random` (lockstep never settles), `stale-shut` (held keys; the sibling's thing writes never count), `merge-carries` (certificates of one epoch read otherwise). The engine differential: `rotation::tests_custody` (Built, phase 4).

## Built (phase 1)
- Seed boxes in the rotation header (spec/wire.md §4, spec/crypto.md §9 Custody), opened whatever counts and kept in `records::SEEDS` (`flows::custody`); inner signatures open with every held seed; no seed fixpoint. A pairing is a rotation boxing the new device every held seed. D's deposit seed is fresh only in a rotation carrying a removal.
- Seed re-seals (`custody::reseal_seeds`, header field 16 `reseal`): at the end of a complete pass of D's sync IDs (`Engine::signing_due`, after the rotation due), per head whose seed the node holds, boxes of `[head seed, its line's held seeds]` to each other roster device no box in a commit counting here is tagged for. The line's seeds ride along because an inner signature opens only with the seed of the epoch it is signed at. The commit adds the head's signing key to D's `account.0`, so it travels under D's sync IDs.
- Stale boxes open: every held seed opens boxes to P (`Core::account_keys_by_name`), revoked or not counting included; the `stale_shut` test fault shuts them (`a_box_at_a_revoked_account_epoch_is_read_*` fails under it).
- Leaving (`signing::leave_if_removed`): of the held rotations counting or whose seed is held, no tip's line keeps this device a node. D's roster decides nothing there. A left node shares nothing as its account (`Engine::shared_here` reads `Core::member`).
- Pairing as a rotation exposed a regain bug: a thing regaining its own keys restarted from every current derived epoch, so one derived from a P epoch newer than its own rotation stayed current, unboxed, and every node re-sealed it to every holder (`apply::keys::refresh_own_keys` now keeps only the derived epochs the kept record knows).

## Built (phase 2)
- Built: perm `Chain` reads the roster from the chain alone (`Devices`: minter, adds, removes per rotation, creation device; hit, re-add and stands rules; `Chain::roster`, `roster_joining`); header `revokes` plus sealed `devices` (`osis_crypto::seal_devices`); creation nonce `kdf("first device", first seed, creation device)`; `account.0` a set of seed-boxing epochs' keys; removal and pairing each one plain rotation (`Engine::remove_device`, `pair`), D re-keyed in it to its roster; account departures (`rotation::keyset_departures`) from the chain; Waiting, `devices_word`, the wrap exception, `unpublished` and the line-whole signing guard gone; grants and removals on D refused. Engine tests rewritten to the mechanism (tests of the removed mechanism deleted); Spec rewritten (permissions §1, §3, §5.1, §5.4, §5.5; crypto §3, §4 blocked, §5, §9; wire §3, §4; data-model `account.0`).
- osis-sync green: a commit giving read epochs (rotation, grant, re-seal) wraps under an epoch they supersede (notes/crypto.md), so a link left out of a profile rotation reads its re-seal.
- Chain cost is linear in rotations and removals (`judging_the_roster_grows_with_the_rotations_*`: n and 10n rounds, a removal every fifth, at a node and outside): rotations placed as they become held (a count of needs per rotation); each line a persistent set sharing its parents' (`osis_perm` `PSet`, a treap whose shape is a function of its keys, so a merge's union costs the branches' difference); who is a node judged along the lines in one pass (`Chain::standing`), again under the taken revocations for the final pass; a revocation beside a line changes it only by hitting a rotation on it, so `Chain::hits` (per rotation, the revocations hitting its line from beside) says where a check walks a line instead. Outside the account what is followed is one line, the last followed rotation's. Left brute force: a walk (`stands_walk`) is quadratic in its line, taken only where a hit is; rejection pairs are re-read per placed rotation outside the account (only pairs off a line can reject, so it scales with forks, not rotations).

## Built (phase 3)
- Epoch IDs: a plain rotation's epoch is its commit ID; a merge's is `BLAKE3("osis v1 merge epoch" || signing key || tips ascending)` (`RotationHeader::mints`, `osis_commit::merge_epoch`), alike in every certificate. Rejected: naming every epoch by `[height, signing key]` in structures: the same identity for merges, but every `CommitId`-keyed structure (by_epoch, rotations_due, judged epochs, takes, supersedes) would change type for nothing a merge ID does not give.
- A certificate header: `names` = the tips but the one it is signed at (its lowest), `supersedes` = exactly its tips, no takes, `revokes` false, no devices, no thing lists; merge signatures over the merge ID (`validate`: anything else is bad input). The chain (`osis_perm::Chain`) takes one record per merge (`Rotation::is_merge`): lines hold plain rotations only, a merge is on a line iff its tips are (`Chain::on`), `tips` compares merges by that, a merge whose tips are not held plain rotations apart is no epoch; at a node of P a merge stands by a certifier (`Devices::certifiers`) that is a node of its line and not hit.
- Engine: `AccountView` groups certificates (`certs`, `epoch_of`, `minting`, `held_at`: an epoch's first receipt); a certificate counts as its epoch (`JudgedEpochs` carries an entry per certificate) and is answered as its epoch; `Core::rotation` finds a merge's keys through a held certificate; a Prove sends one certificate per merge.
- Seeds: `osis_crypto::merge_seed` (tips' seeds by ascending ID); `custody::derive_merges` keeps every merge seed whose tips' seeds are held, once per judging of this node's account. A certificate boxes the seed only to roster devices not seen holding every tip's seed (`custody::derivers`), its maker's own when none.
- Policy (`signing::rotations_due`, at D's pass end): a plain rotation (`rotate_in`) for an answer, or a taken revocation the preferred line lacks while no merge can be made; then `merge_heads` unless a certificate of that merge is held. Removals and pairings are plain rotations. Test: `merges_made_at_once_settle_in_lockstep_*` (lockstep: one certificate per node, one epoch; random: bounded), `concurrent_rotations_fork_and_their_merges_are_one_epoch_*`.
- The listing-cycle shape (`a_siblings_thing_rotation_and_its_listing_open_without_each_other_*`: `sibling_removals_apart_*` with a writer of t outside P, each node re-keying t at its own epoch) settles with every write counting at both nodes. It passes under the `stale_shut` fault too: node 0 reaches node 1's t epoch by a later box, so the engine test shows the shape settles, not which rule closes it; roles-model `custody`'s `stale-shut` control is that check.

## Built (phase 4)
- The differential (`rotation::tests_custody`, notes/test-properties.md): real engines against roles-model `custody`, per tick and per honest act; 1 failing world in 2000 at full size (issues/switch-carries-words.md). Controls planted in the engine (`Core::faults`, `stale_shut`) each fail it.
- Engine fixes it drove, each with a hand test in `rotation::tests_signing`: an epoch come to be held wakes what pends on it (a merge's ID is no commit's: `waits::Waits::epochs_held`); a rotation's inner signature sealed again under the epoch it mints and a certificate's under each epoch it joins (`osis_commit::Sigs::more`); a certificate standing with nothing counts not (`AccountView::standing_certs`, `osis_perm::Chain::standing_certifiers`) and blocks no merge; leaving reads line membership, not boxes (`AccountView::keeps`, `Chain::nodes_of`); answering means superseding besides the epoch signed at (`AccountView::answered_by`); merges answered only where they join an unfollowed line; newcomer boxes carry the line's seeds only; an account keyset come to be held reopens the boxes sealed to the account (`JudgedEpochs::keyed`: the rebuild-differs class); a node signs only on its roster and holding its open epoch's seed (`Core::writer`); a rotation takes only what its line does not carry; seed custody reads rotation headers, not D.
- Model fixes (the model contradicted the spec): the epoch rule's "held and not revoked" clause; the rejection rule's rotations signed at the rejector's epoch (a certificate at each it joins); merges answerable; a removal then the pass-end act; certificates boxed to their joined line's roster, newcomers given the line's seeds; boxes seen only in counting items; placement depth and ties as the chain's. The model re-keys t only after a node's own removal; the engine also on every taken revocation and where two of t's epochs are current (compared one way: issues/thing-rekey-on-thief-side.md for what that costs after a switch).

## What it removes once built
The account-rotation wrap exception (spec/crypto.md §5: a P rotation is wrapped under the epoch it is signed at, the general rule); D's own rotations; the `rotations_due` seed fixpoint; the "Who is a node" grant fixpoint and the D clause of removal frontiers (spec/permissions.md §5.1, Listed); the merge-storm limit (spec/permissions.md §5.5); the `forks-first-grants` and `forks-anyone` controls (moot by construction).

## Costs
- A pairing is a rotation of P (rare; readers outside see one more rotation, not whom it adds).
- A merge seed is held by everyone holding all its tips' seeds; for an unaware sibling's tips boxed to X that includes X, the existing unaware-sibling limit (spec/permissions.md §5.5), no wider.
- Reading stale boxes reads nothing the node was not boxed.
