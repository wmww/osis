# Slimming design
> Summary: The design for Slimmer things (notes/priorities.md item 2): today's measured cost of a dependent thing, a small commit and an independent thing over real sync; per candidate its cost, the concept it serves, the change at the model's level, what it deletes and changes on the wire and disk, and its risks; ordered into build jobs that do not collide, biggest structural win first; what was ruled out and why.

Principle (Tess): fixed overhead per thing, aspect, reference and commit is paid on everything forever, so whatever need not be there goes; nothing is assumed sensible as built. A change here must be sound at the model's level (one representation instead of two, a derived fact not stored, a concept merged) and usually deletes code; one that pays off only through a cache or a special case ranks low. Measurements and the byte-by-byte survey: notes/thing-cost.md.

## Today (measured)
Sync-harness world, node 0 makes, node 1 is its account's second node (rows by family and how to measure: notes/thing-cost.md, Survey).

| unit | wire 0→1 | wire 1→0 | stored, maker | stored, second |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one dependent, one small value, alone | 1182 | 333 | 2727 | 2996 |
| a 1-byte change | 814 | 333 | 1301 | 1475 |
| a small change, steady (compactions included) | 923 | 383 | about 880 | about 920 |
| one dependent of 100 in one commit | 670 | 5 | 958 + 410 blob | 1099 + 410 blob |
| one independent (marginal, 10 averaged) | 23.7 KB | 3.3 KB | 22.8 KB | 23.9 KB |

Where it goes, per node, for a small commit: sealed blob 478, its opened copy `c/` 361, `bp/` 101, the epochs it opened under `ou/` 224 (second node), the entry `E/` 224, `X/` 50, `S/` 50 (maker, per peer). On the wire the commit's Stream frame is 740 B and the peer echoes it back (259 B) with an Applied each way (74 B). Per thing in a batch: about 620 B of index rows for a 36 B record and a 4 B value (states 134, `refs` 178, `t/` 74, counters 102, `dw/` 59 at the maker, `ou/` 258 at the second node, the holder's element 74).

## Candidates
Each: cost now; why it is there (the concept); the change; what it deletes; wire and disk (spec sections); risks.

### A. A new thing pulls its unit anew (built for makes; attach open)
- **Built**: a minting of a thing arriving while the node held none, in the pass in flight with nothing of the thing forgotten since, exempts it (`Core::brought`, `Engine::ingest_carrying`; spec/sync-judgement.md §3 Dropped bodies), so a thing another node makes, dependent or independent (its rotation mints it, its content commit carries the strong later in the pass), pulls nothing anew at a reader (sync `growth::a_thing_another_node_makes_pulls_nothing_anew_*`, `growth::an_independent_thing_another_node_makes_pulls_nothing_anew_*`).
- **Open** (issues/attach-pulls-unit-anew.md): an attach pulls the enclosing unit whole.

### B. One stored form per commit: the record and its seal
- **Cost**: every node holds each commit twice: the sealed blob (478 B small, 846 a make) and the opened record in `c/` (361, 637), with `bp/` (101-117) relating them and, at a reader, `ou/` (224 per small commit, 480 per make: four 120 B rows, one per part and one per header opening, each repeating the delta hash). At the second node that is 803 of 1573 B for a small commit, 1443 of 3312 for a make; an independent thing's blobs are 9.4 KB of 24 KB.
- **Concept**: a blob is a function of the commit and its **seal**: canonical CBOR (spec/wire.md §1), Padmé padding from the length, AEADs with zero nonces under keys that are data. The seal is the prefix salt, the blob key, and per section either its key, wrap as sent and chain (its contents are the held commit's parts on its list, with their path keys where split) or the section verbatim (one not opened, or whose parts were not taken).
- **Built** (the check before any deletion): each `bp/` row (`BlobOf`) carries its blob's seal (`BlobSeal`: blob key, `osis_commit::SectionSeal` per section), kept at every opening (`open_entry`) and fresh encryption (`seal_body`, `seal_certificate`); `Engine::regenerate(b)` makes the blob again from the record and pending copy (`osis_commit::regenerate`, `osis_crypto::reseal_section`, `BlobKey::of_blob`) and checks its ID. Debug builds check every held blob with a seal at each engine's end of history (`Engine::check_seals`); sync `deletes::every_held_blob_is_made_again_from_its_seal` names it. Over the osis-sync suite about 70,000 end-of-history checks pass, none fail. A seal is kept only while the commit as held (record or pending copy) carries every part it keyed: one whose part leaves, or whose commit's record goes, loses it, the row rewritten shredded (`bodies::unseal`, from `Tx::put_commit` and `remove_commit`): a key left in a row opens the blob's bytes wherever they still are (the forensic test `a_purged_or_replaced_part_is_unrecoverable_from_the_sync_store_*` fails without the shred). Until the blobs go this costs storage: a small commit's `bp/` row is 265 B at either node (101 without its seal: blob key 32, one keyed section 132), so with `ou/` folded in (an epoch per keyed section) the seal row of a small commit is about 300 B, not the 110 first estimated, and the saving at a reader about 500 B a small commit, not 700. E's derived section key (no wrap, no section key: 104 B less) brings it to the estimate.
- **A strip is a record fact** (built): `bodies::strips` reads the record alone. A part dropped as covered strips once marked (`Dropped::strips`, bit 7 of the record head's drop flags), written by the purge-on-replace re-judge of its aspect (`retention::replace::mark_strips` in `settle_none`) when `bodies::strip_due` (a setting purges on replace and a held valid snapshot reaches it by its tables): `Core::none_due` already takes every input of that (a meta put on the aspect, a validity flip, a setting arriving, a role moving), so the strip is written in the transaction that makes it true. Debug builds check none is left unwritten at each engine's end of history, but on an aspect whose re-judge is still pending (`Engine::unwritten_strips`). A strip is for good: a reacher lost later does not put the part back into blobs (its bytes are gone; uncovered, it is missing and fetched as any). To check: a purge request naming a part already dropped as covered, on an aspect not under purge on replace, strips it neither by this rule nor before; if spec/commits.md §5 says its blobs must drop it, `purge::rejudge_purges` marks it the same way.
- **Held blobs with no seal** (census over the osis-sync suite, about 1,400 of 71,000), each to be made regenerable or keep its bytes before the blob bytes go:
  1. **Parts held only in a blob** (design, not built): the blob carries, in a section this node opened, a part the record does not hold: dropped as covered (not stripping) or while invalid, so the blob stays its refetch source and is served; opened but not taken (another holder's `secrets.0` piece, a part held `Missing`); or sealed into a fresh encryption from a held blob (`with_blob_parts`). The record keeps these as **kept parts** (plaintext part, with its thing's path key where split), one place for the bytes: added where a part leaves the record without stripping while a sealed held blob keys it (`Tx::put_commit` sees `leaving` and reads the old record), and where a sealed blob opens carrying a part the record lacks (`open_entry`); removed, shredded, where it strips (a Hold change or `mark_strips`: both record writes, so `unseal`'s check in `put_commit` sees it), where it comes back as a body, and with the record. `carried`, `Engine::regenerate`, `with_blob_parts` (no blob opened) and `seal_body`'s `want` read them.
  2. **The commit not held** (decided by the model, not built): a node that lost access keeps nothing it can no longer read (spec/requirements.md), and its own entries stay for the access to return (spec/sync-judgement.md §3 Restart and forgetting) as ciphertext, as a store keeping S unread holds them. So in the transaction that forgets the commit with lost access, each held blob with a seal is made again from the record before it goes, handed to the sync store as plain bytes, and its seal shredded (`unseal`).
  3. **Sealed fires** (`grant@~0` and the like): the payload is sealed to a KEM key with fresh randomness: stay blobs (`BlobOf::seal` `None`). Sync copies, record blobs and notices have no `bp/` seal and stay blobs too.
- **Left to build**, one branch, nothing landed before all of it (the seal rows alone grow every `bp/` row 101 → 265 B): (a) classes 1 and 2; (b) the sync store keeps no bytes of a blob with a seal: dropped once `open_entry` or a fresh encryption notes the seal (the entry row keeps its size), read through one door that asks `Engine::regenerate` first (sync's `Store::bytes` and `blob_len` callers: streams `serve`/`pull`, read `live`, inbox `offers`/`held`, store `follow`); (c) `held_sections` reads verbatim sections from seal rows, `with_blob_parts` the kept parts, neither opens a held blob; (d) `ou/` folds into the seal row (each keyed section's epoch; delta hash from the header); (e) the engine's holds of its own commit blobs (`G/`) go; (f) measure (notes/thing-cost.md survey rows) and set the figures above; spec only where it states the on-disk layout. The wire is unchanged (blobs served byte-identical).
- **Wire, disk**: no wire change. Engine store layout (notes/engine-store.md `bp/`).
- **Risks**: one re-encryption per serve (two XChaCha20 passes); a body read stays a record read. Rejected: the blob as truth with the record keeping head and header only (saves only the parts, about 250 B a make, and puts an AEAD open on every body read).

### C. A blob crosses each connection once
- **Cost**: (1) the echo: a reader streams back every entry it just took from its peer (259 B and an Applied of 74 B per commit per pair, 29% of a small commit's wire, one stamp verification at the maker, the maker's `S/` row of 50 B written from it); (2) a new unit's first sync sends inline blobs the requester already holds under other sync IDs: an independent thing's first sync carried A's key record (8,782 B) and an account-constant 1,070 B blob again, and its content commit (752 B) twice, once under A's Stream and once under its own: 10.6 KB of the 23.7 KB a reader receives.
- **Concept**: a source's `known` (spec/sync-judgement.md §3) is what its stream showed, "a write's acknowledgement, a send or a deposit counts for nothing until the stream shows it", so a reader proves it holds what it was sent by sending it back. And `known`, which keeps bytes off the wire, is per (sync ID, entry), though bytes are per blob.
- **Change**: one fact, by blob: a node knows what a peer holds by blob, from what the peer showed under any sync ID and from what it sent the peer in a message the peer reported applied and did not refuse (Applied and Refused already exist). Then a server lists no event to the peer it took it from (an identical Added: same blob, wrap and stamp; any other still flows) and inlines or answers no bytes of a blob the requester holds. Held elsewhere already counts a reader source's report (spec/sync-status.md §2); `known` gains the applied send.
- **Deletes**: the echo's frames and the work they cause at both ends (stamp check, `known` update, `S/` write); the second copy of shared blobs on a connection.
- **Wire, disk**: spec/sync-judgement.md §3 (`known`), spec/sync-streams.md §4 (Stream, Applying, Reports), spec/wire.md §11.2 (Pull's inline rule), spec/sync-status.md §2. Whole listings are unchanged (they list everything; they are rare).
- **Risks**: a store keeping S unread applies past what it refuses: its refusals must always be told (spec/sync-streams.md §4 Full, Refused) for an applied send to count, else it counts by its stream alone as now (the existing carve-out in Held elsewhere, which this keeps, not adds). Deletes judged from `known` at a store must not count a blob the store never stored. The sync models lag (issues/sync-models-lag.md): the streams model should take the rule before it is built.

### D. Index rows that repeat a stored fact
- **Built**: `node/metas` holds only what this node judged of a commit (part count, a byte per part, a split commit's decided paths); an open decodes each header from its record and takes the view from it (`View::of`): the view is the bulk of a header and every commit's is needed at open, so the row was the header twice. `a/` is gone: an aspect has a unit exactly while it has a state, so `aspects.0`, the open and a rebuild list units by key (`Transaction::aspects_of`, `all_aspects`; no state read). Saved per node: about 98 B per small commit, 182 per make, 27 per aspect with a state (engine `thing_cost::`; notes/thing-cost.md).
- **Kept** (each the only fast way to answer its reads):
  - `t/`: the node's creation records, all held before an open judges anything. A record learned from a record blob or an inbox payload (record chains) has no `created.0` part here, so deriving the rest from parts gives records two homes by origin (a part decoded per thing at open, a row for the others, a row written whenever a `created.0` part drops while its record stays, as at a destruction). The `created.0` state is another fact (the aspect's value, empty after a destruction); reading it from `t/` would be one aspect's carve-out.
  - `refs from`: the only per-source order of the reference index. The closure walk reads a thing's strongs from it (`units::strongs_from`): from the states that reads every state of the thing, O(its data) for O(its references). A signal's rows count fires that may be gone when its resync runs (`resync_fires` after a drop or forget), so its old rows cannot be read back from what is held. And the count that tells a row appearing or going lives there. `refs to` is the same index by target (backlinks).
  - `dw/`: records an act (this node sealed under a derived epoch while the thing was dependent here), not a function of held commits: the commit may be gone here, covered, while held elsewhere. The proposed replacement, the promotion listing every derived epoch of the mint admin since creation that the floor allows, misses a writer that seals under a derived epoch of a mint-admin epoch the promoter did not know (concurrent with the promotion, spec/crypto.md §4 Current): only that writer knows its rotation is due. A replacement must keep that duty; model it in the TLA rotation model before building.
- **Left**: F (entry rows, sync store), after B and C.

### E. The commit's own bytes
- **Cost**: a small commit's blob is 478 B (header 174 opened). Per thing touched a 72 B section wrap; per further aspect of one thing a repeated 16 B tid; per delete level a 35 B `[level, key]` in the header and a 33 B blinded commitment in the prefix, plus a 16 B blind (about 85 B with one level, 150 with two); the record's 32 B nonce behind a 15 B tid; the signature's 7 B agility wrapper; empty optional fields about 6 B.
- **Concept**: each is load-bearing in its job (notes/thing-cost.md, What each cost is (a)); the question is only its size.
- **Change** (each its own rule, all in the format): the section key derived from the thing's read secret, the commit ID and a 16 B nonce (`kdf("section", ...)`), a copy's from the blob key alike, in place of a wrap (saves 56 B per thing per commit, on the wire and in every seal row of B); the aspect list grouped by thing, one tid per group (16 B per further aspect); one blinded commitment per author within a sync unit (Tess: yes; about 33 B a commit); the blind derived from the blob key as linked blobs already do (16 B); a delete key named by its epoch where the reader holds it (the admin delete key from `keys.0`, the author's from its signing key or chain: about 32 B a level); the record nonce at 16 B.
- **Deletes**: the section wrap and its random section key (and their storage in B's seal row: the seal shrinks to salt, epochs and chains); nothing else of note.
- **Wire, disk**: spec/wire.md §3, §4, §5; spec/crypto.md §5, §7. About 120-150 B of a 478 B small commit.
- **Risks**: a derived section key is one key per plaintext: a strip's re-seal takes a fresh nonce; named delete keys need the reader to resolve the epoch before checking a delete (delete keys at stores keeping S unread read `keys.0` they may not hold: check notes/delete-keys.md before naming one); the per-author commitment lets a store tell which of a unit's blobs one author can delete (Tess accepted). Each item measured on the blob before and after.

### F. Entry rows
- **Cost**: per entry per node `E/` 224 B (key 50, wrap 57, stamp 64, the stamp verdict 33, the blob prefix's class and expiry again 9, its size 8, flags and sources), `X/` 50 (the blob's sync IDs, a refcount), `S/` 50 per peer at a maker.
- **Change**: the verdict by the head's index (about 29 B); class, expiry and size from the seal (B) or the blob; `X/` only for a blob under two or more sync IDs, the entry row naming its blob's sharing (50 B for the common one); `S/` gone with C (a peer's holding by blob, from what C keeps).
- **Deletes**: about 90-140 B per entry per node. Sync store layout only (notes/sync-store.md).

### G. Independent things
- **Cost**: 23-24 KB stored per node, 24 KB to a reader; the commits are near their floor (rotation 3,838 B opened: `keys.0` 1,387 with four keysets, sync ID, deposit seed and step keys, no KEM key; `secrets.0` 799 of history boxes and 635 of boxes to A; `sync.0` 458; content 432). The rest is storage: its rotation sealed twice (4,174 B under its own sync ID and a 4,046 B copy under A's: a role part on a thing other than the sync ID's actor always goes as a copy, `Body::to_sync_id`), `k/` 1,443 B, five per-role key index families (`node/keysets`, `boxed`, `sealed`, `boxes to`, `epoch intro`) 2.4 KB, seven entries 1.6 KB of `E/` and their `X/`, `S/`.
- **Changes**:
  - **The copy only where a section does not open** (spec/sync-judgement.md §1 already says so: the shared blob where S's readers can open every section). A newborn's rotation is wrapped under its mint admin's derived epoch (spec/crypto.md §5), which A's readers hold; so is every later rotation under the epoch it supersedes, which A's readers hold through A's boxes. The copy is needed for readers without that history (history-less grants, notes/history-less-grant.md) and for role parts on things outside the derivation: decide it by the section's epoch against what S's readers can hold, not by the part's class. Saves about 4 KB stored per node and 4 KB on the wire per independent thing in a unit. Model first: held-model's copy rules with a history-less reader of A.
  - **One row per (thing, epoch)** for the key indexes and `k/`, each role's secret, keyset body and boxes in it, in place of five families keyed per role: to measure what each family holds first (a cut of about 2-3 KB is the aim). Store only.
  - **One box record per holder actor per rotation** (issues/account-evidence-per-unit.md): a quarter of the 526 B a node an account's removal carries into every unit. spec/crypto.md §4 Box records.
- Rejected for now: roles sharing a keyset while their holders coincide (notes/thing-cost.md Open: the likeliest to add states; a grant would become a split rotation); role secrets derived down the role order (a holder actor's own role holders differ, so a box per role stays); dropping a newborn's history boxes (notes/thing-cost.md Rejected).

### H. Memory
- **Cost**: unmeasured, and possibly the largest: a two-node world's resident set grows about 120 KB per small commit, 40 times what both disks gain (issues/memory-per-commit.md). Known per held commit: a `Meta` with its `View` and its decoded header, kept for every commit for the engine's life.
- **Change**: none designed until attributed. If the engine's per-commit structures are the bulk, the direction is memory in the working set's size, not the store's: views and headers read through the store on demand (they are rows already, D keeps the header readable), with the in-memory indexes keyed sparsely.

## Jobs
In order; jobs on one line may run side by side (they touch different crates or layers). Each job recounts the figures above at its end in the default thing-cost tests (and makes `what_a_thing_costs_as_the_app_makes_it` run again: it is ignored, "sync rewrite: copies per (commit, thing)"), so the next ranks by real numbers.
1. **No pull anew for a thing just made** (A; engine `sync/units`): the biggest wire win, and it distorts every other count. Built for dependent things; independent things and attach are left. Beside it: **attribute memory** (H; a counting allocator, by-hand), a measuring job.
2. **One stored form per commit** (B; engine store, `CipherStore`): biggest storage win for every commit at every node; deletes two row families and the second copy. Beside it: **a blob crosses each connection once** (C; osis-sync streams and its spec), if job 2 keeps the sync store's "bytes of blob b" call as it is.
3. **Index rows that repeat a stored fact** (D; engine index), then **entry rows** (F; sync store), side by side.
4. **The commit's own bytes** (E; osis-commit, osis-crypto, spec): after 2, whose seal row it shrinks.
5. **Independent things** (G): the copy rule (held-model first), the key rows, box records.
6. **Memory** (H), as job 1's measurement directs; possibly earlier if it is the bulk.

## Ruled out
- **One stamp over a batch**: entries are stored and served one by one, so each keeps a proof, a Merkle path of 32 B per level, at least the 64 B saved from a batch of two up.
- **One signature for commit and stamp** (Tess asked): the two serve readers who must learn the signer (the commit's, inside the ciphertext) and stores that must not (the stamp, by the sync ID's write key); one signature would tell the stores who wrote or the readers nothing. Stays two.
- **A lone small delta embedded in the header** (no hash, no section, about 160 B): needs the commit to have one section whose audience is the blob's and no per-part purge: a special case. Low; reconsider after E.
- **A state stored whole when smaller than its body reference**: a few bytes per aspect, beside D's `created.0` change, which is the case that matters.
- **A verified-step memo** (sync-step verifications per make, notes/thing-cost.md Open): a cache; recount after job 1, which removes most of them.
