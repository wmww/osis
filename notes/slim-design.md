# Slimming design
> Summary: The design for Slimmer things (notes/priorities.md item 2): today's measured cost of a dependent thing, a small commit and an independent thing over real sync; per candidate its cost, the concept it serves, the change at the model's level, what it deletes and changes on the wire and disk, and its risks; ordered into build jobs that do not collide, biggest structural win first; what was ruled out and why.

Principle (Tess): fixed overhead per thing, aspect, reference and commit is paid on everything forever, so whatever need not be there goes; nothing is assumed sensible as built. A change here must be sound at the model's level (one representation instead of two, a derived fact not stored, a concept merged) and usually deletes code; one that pays off only through a cache or a special case ranks low. Measurements and the byte-by-byte survey: notes/thing-cost.md.

## Today (measured)
Sync-harness world, node 0 makes, node 1 is its account's second node (rows by family and how to measure: notes/thing-cost.md, Survey). Stored is logical bytes, engine rows + sync store rows + blob bytes: D's figures less the saving B's probe measured per row (B, Measured) [E].

| unit | wire 0→1 | wire 1→0 (before C: C, Measured) | stored, maker | stored, second |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one dependent, one small value, alone | 1182 | 333 | about 2090 | about 2360 |
| a 1-byte change | 814 | 333 | about 1040 | about 1210 |
| a small change, steady (compactions included) | 923 | 383 | about 860 | about 900 |
| one dependent of 100 in one commit | 670 | 5 | about 680 | about 825 |
| one independent (marginal, 10 averaged) | 13.2 KB | 3.3 KB | about 17.2 KB | about 18.3 KB |

Where it goes, per node, for a small commit: its record `c/` 358, `bp/` 273 (the blob's seal: its length, key, and the section's key and wrap; no bytes of the sealed blob are kept), `M/` 42, the epochs it opened under `ou/` 222 (second node), the entry `E/` 224, `X/` 50, `S/` 50 (maker, per peer). On the wire the commit's Stream frame is 740 B, answered by the peer's Applied (141 B with its anchor); nothing goes back (C). Per thing in a batch: about 620 B of index rows for a 36 B record and a 4 B value (states 134, `refs` 178, `t/` 74, counters 102, `dw/` 59 at the maker, `ou/` 258 at the second node, the holder's element 74).

## Candidates
Each: cost now; why it is there (the concept); the change; what it deletes; wire and disk (spec sections); risks.

### A. A new thing pulls its unit anew (built for makes; attach open)
- **Built**: a minting of a thing arriving while the node held none, in the pass in flight with nothing of the thing forgotten since, exempts it (`Core::brought`, `Engine::ingest_carrying`; spec/sync-judgement.md §3 Dropped bodies), so a thing another node makes, dependent or independent (its rotation mints it, its content commit carries the strong later in the pass), pulls nothing anew at a reader (sync `growth::a_thing_another_node_makes_pulls_nothing_anew_*`, `growth::an_independent_thing_another_node_makes_pulls_nothing_anew_*`).
- **Open** (issues/attach-pulls-unit-anew.md): an attach pulls the enclosing unit whole.

### B. One stored form per commit: the record and its seal
- **Concept**: a blob is a function of the commit and its **seal**: canonical CBOR (spec/wire.md §1), Padmé padding from the length, AEADs with zero nonces under keys that are data. The seal is the blob's length and key and per section either its key, wrap as sent and chain (its contents are the held commit's parts on its list, with their path keys where split) or the section verbatim (one not opened, or whose parts were not taken). With the prefix salt (`BlobOf::salt`) and the commit as held it gives the blob byte for byte.
- **Built**: each `bp/` row (`BlobOf`) carries its blob's seal (`BlobSeal`), noted at every opening (`open_entry`) and fresh encryption (`seal_body`, `seal_certificate`), kept only where it makes its blob again from the commit as held (record and pending copy), checked by making it once as it is noted (`bodies::put_row`), and never for a blob opened unchecked (its prefix not checked against the header's lists). So a blob carrying a part held here only in it, or another copy's signatures (a relay's copy whose signatures a true copy replaces), keeps its bytes. The sync store keeps no bytes of a sealed blob: noting a seal calls `Cipher::made` (the store's `M/` row with the length, its bytes dropped once the engine's store commits, noted for a crash), and every byte reader goes through `osis_sync::store::blob_bytes`, which asks `Engine::regenerate` first (notes/sync-store.md, Blobs the engine makes again). The wire is unchanged: blobs are served byte-identical.
- **One rule for a seal going**: before what makes a blob changes (`bodies::unseal_record` from `Tx::put_commit` and `remove_commit`, `unseal_pending` from every pending-copy write; a row going, `unnote_blob`), a seal whose reads of the commit change (`bodies::reads_same`: header, signatures, path keys, the parts its keyed sections list), or any seal of a commit no longer held, goes, shredded (a key left in a row opens the blob's bytes wherever they still are), and the blob is made a last time and its bytes given back to the store (`hand_back`, `Cipher::unmade`, applied before the engine's store commits) wherever an entry names it. That one rule covers a part leaving the record without stripping (dropped as covered, as invalid: the blob stays its refetch source), a part stripping (its entries leave by read's judgement as before), lost access (the record goes, the entries stay as ciphertext for the access to return, as a store keeping S unread holds them) and a release. A blob nothing names keeps no seal (`Engine::unnamed_blob`; a seal of one never logged, a Want's answer, goes the same way).
- **Checks**: debug builds check at each engine's end of history that every seal makes its blob again, to the store's bytes where it keeps any (`Engine::check_seals`), and `hand_back` asserts the blob is made. Engine `sync::tests_facts::a_seal_is_kept_only_for_the_blob_it_makes`; sync `deletes::every_held_blob_is_made_again_from_its_seal`, `deletes::a_reader_keeps_no_bytes_of_what_it_makes_again` (no bytes beside a seal, served byte for byte, a lost commit's entries keep their bytes), by hand `deletes::a_crash_at_any_write_leaves_each_blob_kept_or_made_again` (a power loss at every durable write of a seal noted and of lost access). A serve reads a blob's bytes only to send them: presence and inline checks read `Store::blob_len`, so a made blob costs one making per send, linear in its size.
- **Blobs keeping their bytes** (no seal): sealed fires, notices and record blobs; a blob opened unchecked; one carrying a part the commit as held lacks (opened but not taken: another holder's `secrets.0` piece, a part held `Missing`; or a part that left the record without stripping, given back as above).
- **A strip is a record fact**: `bodies::strips` reads the record alone. A part dropped as covered strips once marked (`Dropped::strips`, bit 7 of the record head's drop flags): by the purge-on-replace re-judge of its aspect (`retention::replace::mark_strips` in `settle_none`) when `bodies::strip_due` (a setting purges on replace and a held valid snapshot reaches it by its tables; `Core::none_due` takes every input of that, so the strip is written in the transaction that makes it true; debug builds check none is left unwritten, `Engine::unwritten_strips`), and by a purge request standing on it (`purge::purge_one`: its blobs are local copies of the named delta, spec/commits.md §5 Dropping a body; sync `deletes::a_purge_strips_a_part_dropped_as_covered_from_its_blobs_spec_commits_5`). A strip is for good: a reacher lost later does not put the part back into blobs.
- **Measured** (a throwaway sync-harness probe as in notes/thing-cost.md Survey, the same world on main and on B; logical bytes, engine rows + store rows + blob bytes, maker / second node): a 1-byte change 997 / 1169 (main 1260 / 1432); one dependent of 100 in a commit 618 / 817 (894 / 1091); an independent thing 14.0 / 15.1 KB (19.6 / 20.7 KB); a replace, steady over 30 with compactions, 862 / 898 (881 / 917): a replaced value's blob is given back as bytes once its part drops as covered, until compaction deletes its entry. A paired account's two nodes hold 104 / 108 KB after their first make (196 / 206 KB), 6 blobs of bytes left of 28. A sealed blob costs its seal in `bp/` (a small commit's row 101 → 273 B) and `M/` 42 B in place of its bytes.
- **Not built, and why** (reconsider on a measured workload that needs it):
  - **Kept parts** (the record keeping the bytes of parts held only in a blob, so such blobs stay made): they would keep made only the blobs of parts dropped without stripping, whose entries compaction deletes soon after (the steady replace above gains 19 B a change from all of B), at the cost of a second home for part bytes with its own shred rules.
  - **`held_sections` and `with_blob_parts` reading seals**: a fresh encryption's sections are read from held blobs made through the door and opened, a rare path; reading seals would be a second path to the same sections.
  - **`ou/` folded into `bp/`** (about 80 of each 120 B row): openings outlive seals (a seal goes when its blob is unnamed or a part leaves, an opening stays while its part is held, to pass it on), and copies' and header openings come from entry wraps, not sections; so openings would fold into the row, not the seal, keeping the per-thing index (`Indexes::opened_under`, `forget_thing`) and the in-memory-until-ingest timing. Its own job, with its own review against notes/sync-opens.md's passing-on invariant.
  - **The engine's `G/` holds**: the engine holds no commit blob's bytes (its holds are pages, chunks, list trees and pieces): nothing to remove.
- **Wire, disk**: no wire change. Engine store layout (notes/engine-store.md `bp/`), sync store `M/` (core/sync/src/store/rows.rs), spec/sync-ids.md §5.
- **Risks**: one re-encryption per serve (two XChaCha20 passes); a body read stays a record read. Rejected: the blob as truth with the record keeping head and header only (saves only the parts, about 250 B a make, and puts an AEAD open on every body read).

### C. A blob crosses each connection once
- **Built, the inline half**: a server inlines no blob its requester is known to hold under any sync ID naming it here, nor one it sent inline on the connection in a message not reported applied yet (`Serve::inlined`, `peer_holds_blob`; spec/wire.md §11.2 Pull, spec/sync-streams.md §4 Bodies); the requester takes the other entry with the bytes it holds. An independent thing's first sync no longer carries A's key record, the account-constant blob and its content commit again: 0→1 per independent make 23.8 → 13.2 KB (the probe below; sync `streams::a_blob_held_under_one_sync_id_goes_inline_under_no_other_spec_sync_streams_4`). Dependent makes and changes are unchanged.
- **Built, the echo's removal** (spec/sync-streams.md §4 Reports; notes/sync-streams.md, Sends counted; NetStream `S_echo`, `S_echorestore`): a server counts held at its requester each entry an `Added` carried in messages the requester's Applied reports, less those it told it does not hold (an event 4, code 14 where it has no refusal of its own, sent before the Applied); from the Applied on, the requester serves that peer no `Added` of what it holds as sent (same wrap and stamp) at or below its log end at the apply, outside a listing from the start, for the connection's life. The stamp check the echo carried: a requester that stamped the entry on taking holds another stamp, so its event goes and the server takes the stamp. The Applied carries the requester's log end (the anchor); the server's Pulls to it name the held anchor until a reply on the connection checked it, a store whose history lacks it answering from the start, and a newer anchor waits until that check (`SN_echoanchor`, `SN_echocheck`). Rejected: the count waiting for the requester's stream to pass its event (an empty push where the filter skipped): with both replies paged neither follows the other, a deadlock.
- **Measured** (probe below, merge base and branch, bytes per make): dependent alone 1→0 403 → 180, 0→1 1552 → 1501; a 1-byte change 1→0 370 → 202, 0→1 900 → 848; an independent 1→0 3297 → 1255, 0→1 13010 → 12938. An Applied with an anchor is 141 B (74 without).
- **Probe**: a throwaway sync-harness test as in notes/thing-cost.md Survey, `World::new(0)`, `add_node`, `pair(0)`, three warm-up makes, then the frames each way per make over 10 makes (`growth::makes`): a dependent alone, a 1-byte change on one dependent, an independent (`mint_independent` held by A's `holds.0`).
- **Wire, disk**: Pull and Applied gain `Opt(anchor)` (spec/wire.md §11.2); `known` counts reported sends (spec/sync-judgement.md §3); a record keeps its anchors (rows `ANCHOR`).

### D. Index rows that repeat a stored fact
- **Built**: `node/metas` holds only what this node judged of a commit (part count, a byte per part, a split commit's decided paths); an open decodes each header from its record and takes the view from it (`View::of`): the view is the bulk of a header and every commit's is needed at open, so the row was the header twice. `a/` is gone: an aspect has a unit exactly while it has a state, so `aspects.0`, the open and a rebuild list units by key (`Transaction::aspects_of`, `all_aspects`; no state read). Saved per node: about 98 B per small commit, 182 per make, 27 per aspect with a state (engine `thing_cost::`; notes/thing-cost.md).
- **Kept** (each the only fast way to answer its reads):
  - `t/`: the node's creation records, all held before an open judges anything. A record learned from a record blob or an inbox payload (record chains) has no `created.0` part here, so deriving the rest from parts gives records two homes by origin (a part decoded per thing at open, a row for the others, a row written whenever a `created.0` part drops while its record stays, as at a destruction). The `created.0` state is another fact (the aspect's value, empty after a destruction); reading it from `t/` would be one aspect's carve-out.
  - `refs from`: the only per-source order of the reference index. The closure walk reads a thing's strongs from it (`units::refs_from`): from the states that reads every state of the thing, O(its data) for O(its references). A signal's rows count fires that may be gone when its resync runs (`resync_fires` after a drop or forget), so its old rows cannot be read back from what is held. And the count that tells a row appearing or going lives there. `refs to` is the same index by target (backlinks).
  - `dw/`: records an act (this node sealed under a derived epoch while the thing was dependent here), not a function of held commits: the commit may be gone here, covered, while held elsewhere. The proposed replacement, the promotion listing every derived epoch of the mint admin since creation that the floor allows, misses a writer that seals under a derived epoch of a mint-admin epoch the promoter did not know (concurrent with the promotion, spec/crypto.md §4 Current): only that writer knows its rotation is due. A replacement must keep that duty; model it in the TLA rotation model before building.
- **Left**: F (entry rows, sync store), after B and C.

### E. The commit's own bytes
- **Cost**: a small commit's blob is 478 B (header 174 opened). Per thing touched a 72 B section wrap; per further aspect of one thing a repeated 16 B tid; per delete level a 35 B `[level, key]` in the header and a 33 B blinded commitment in the prefix, plus a 16 B blind (about 85 B with one level, 150 with two); the record's 32 B nonce behind a 15 B tid; the signature's 7 B agility wrapper; empty optional fields about 6 B.
- **Concept**: each is load-bearing in its job (notes/thing-cost.md, What each cost is (a)); the question is only its size.
- **Change** (each its own rule, all in the format): the section key derived from the thing's read secret, the commit ID and a 16 B nonce (`kdf("section", ...)`), a copy's from the blob key alike, in place of a wrap (saves 56 B per thing per commit, on the wire and in every seal row of B); the aspect list grouped by thing, one tid per group (16 B per further aspect); one blinded commitment per author within a sync unit (Tess: yes; about 33 B a commit); the blind derived from the blob key as linked blobs already do (16 B); a delete key named by its epoch where the reader holds it (the admin delete key from `keys.0`, the author's from its signing key or chain: about 32 B a level); the record nonce at 16 B.
- **Deletes**: the section wrap and its random section key (and their storage in B's seal row: the seal shrinks to salt, epochs and chains); nothing else of note.
- **Wire, disk**: spec/wire.md §3, §4, §5; spec/crypto.md §5, §7. About 120-150 B of a 478 B small commit.
- **Risks**: a derived section key is one key per plaintext: a strip's re-seal takes a fresh nonce; named delete keys need the reader to resolve the epoch before checking a delete (delete keys at stores keeping S unread read `keys.0` they may not hold: check notes/delete-keys.md before naming one); the per-author commitment lets a store tell which of a unit's blobs one author can delete (Tess accepted). Each item measured on the blob before and after.

### F. Entry rows
- **Cost**: per entry per node `E/` 224 B (key 50, wrap 57, stamp 64, the stamp verdict 33, the blob prefix's class and expiry again 9, its size 8, flags and sources), `X/` 50 (the blob's sync IDs, a refcount), `S/` 50 per peer at a maker.
- **Change**: the verdict by the head's index (about 29 B); class, expiry and size from the seal (B) or the blob; `X/` only for a blob under two or more sync IDs, the entry row naming its blob's sharing (50 B for the common one); `S/` gone with C (a peer's holding by blob, from what C keeps).
- **Deletes**: about 90-140 B per entry per node. Sync store layout only (notes/sync-store.md).

### G. Independent things
- **Cost**: 23-24 KB stored per node, 24 KB to a reader; the commits are near their floor (rotation 3,838 B opened: `keys.0` 1,387 with four keysets, sync ID, deposit seed and step keys, no KEM key; `secrets.0` 799 of history boxes and 635 of boxes to A; `sync.0` 458; content 432). The rest is storage: its rotation sealed twice (4,174 B under its own sync ID and a 4,046 B copy under A's: a role part on a thing other than the sync ID's actor always goes as a copy, `Body::to_sync_id`), `k/` 1,443 B, five per-role key index families (`node/keysets`, `boxed`, `sealed`, `boxes to`, `epoch intro`) 2.4 KB, seven entries 1.6 KB of `E/` and their `X/`, `S/`.
- **Changes**:
  - **The copy only where a section does not open** (spec/sync-judgement.md §1 already says so: the shared blob where S's readers can open every section). A newborn's rotation is wrapped under its mint admin's derived epoch (spec/crypto.md §5), which A's readers hold; so is every later rotation under the epoch it supersedes, which A's readers hold through A's boxes. The copy is needed for readers without that history (history-less grants, notes/history-less-grant.md) and for role parts on things outside the derivation: decide it by the section's epoch against what S's readers can hold, not by the part's class. Saves about 4 KB stored per node and 4 KB on the wire per independent thing in a unit. Model first: held-model's copy rules with a history-less reader of A.
  - **One row per (thing, epoch)** for the key indexes and `k/`, each role's secret, keyset body and boxes in it, in place of five families keyed per role: to measure what each family holds first (a cut of about 2-3 KB is the aim). Store only.
  - **One box record per holder actor per rotation** (issues/account-evidence-per-unit.md): a quarter of the 526 B a node an account's removal carries into every unit. spec/crypto.md §4 Box records.
- Rejected for now: roles sharing a keyset while their holders coincide (notes/thing-cost.md Open: the likeliest to add states; a grant would become a split rotation); role secrets derived down the role order (a holder actor's own role holders differ, so a box per role stays); dropping a newborn's history boxes (notes/thing-cost.md Rejected).

### H. Memory
- **Cost** (`tools/memprof` over sync `memory::memory_per_commit_probe` at 50 and 500 small commits, both nodes, debug build; live heap bytes per commit): 60.2 KB, 20 times what both disks gain (2.85 KB). Linear in commits held, nothing flattening by 500. By owner:
  - **Followers of every held commit, about 19.3 KB, unbounded**: the sync `Read` follows each held commit for its life (`bodies`, `local`, `judged` and `toward` per body, `freed`), and the engine's cutoffs `sync_bodies`, `sync_body_rows`, `read_facts` and `held_fates` and blob lists per body: about 8 followed keys a commit a node. Per followed key: its `Followed` slot with the key and its value (about 5.5 KB a commit); its reads, a 40 B `memo::Dep` and its index entry, about 100 B a read, 46 reads a commit a node (9.4 KB); its row (2.5 KB). The reads are the commit's own facts: what is the thing's comes from one row per thing (below, Built).
  - **Bounded caches, about 18.5 KB until full**: the stores' decoded-unit caches (`StoreConfig::unit_cache_bytes`, 16 MB per store, two stores a node: 10.6 KB) and `DecodedCommits` (`DECODED_MAX`, 16 MB per engine: 7.9 KB; 1.5 KB of it debug-only copies of the stored bytes and parts it compares against). 96 MB per node at their caps: a phone's budget question for notes/arbitrary-values.md, not a leak.
  - **Engine per-commit structures, about 12 KB**: perm's delta facts per content write (`MemStore`, the engine's timelines' backing: 4.1 KB); evidence (`kept_evidence`, `copy_parts`) 3.7 KB; the `Meta`'s decoded header (`apply_verified`, about 600 B a node), `Commits::insert` and journal maps about 4 KB. `View::of` is 200 B: copying header fields into the view is not worth a change.
  - The test disk (`MemoryBackend`'s sealed units) 2.85 KB, the disks' own size. Debug checks keep no growing state beyond `DecodedCommits`' copies and 8 B per followed key (`Followed::moved`).
- **Built**: `Effects` holds each followed key once, in its slot by want sequence (found by a keyed hash), and names it by that sequence in its dependency index (one set of (hash read, key) per container) and pending set, so neither holds a copy of a key. A read kept (`memo::Dep`) is 40 B. `sync_bodies` is a `Cutoff` and `sync_body` a row per body taken from it, so sync read's bodies and each body's facts read one row instead of re-recording the derivation's reads. `Bodies` is a sorted slice: a commit's one or two bodies in a `BTreeMap` took a node of eleven (4.6 KB). The per-thing rows (`Core::thing_syncs`, `secret_actors`, `keys_opens`, `read_floors`; notes/engine.md, Per-key rows): every commit's bodies and facts read the same facts of the things its parts are on (how the thing syncs, its secret actor, whether the sync unit's actor reads it, the actor's read floor), each a derivation of about 25 to 40 reads (the record chain, epochs and foreign keysets, the role walk); copied into each commit's rows they were 120 reads a commit a node, as rows 46 (`sync_bodies` 51 to 13, `read_facts` 47 to 10.5) (72.7 KB to 61.5 KB a commit); each key held once, 61.5 to 60.2 KB.
- **Change** (in order of bytes): (1) the principle the followers should meet: memory in what is live or changing, not in what is held. A held commit whose judgement settled still keeps every key, list and index entry so that any later change can find it; following by coarser keys (per sync ID or thing, re-judging its commits when one of its few inputs moves) would make the per-commit cost a value, not a reactive record. Model and measure before building. (2) perm's delta facts out of memory: no lever, a new layer (notes/perm-paths.md, The delta-facts seam: the engine's indexes are all in memory, so a store-read `paths::Store` is the first index read on demand). 4 KB.

## Jobs
In order; jobs on one line may run side by side (they touch different crates or layers). Each job recounts the figures above at its end in the default thing-cost tests (and makes `what_a_thing_costs_as_the_app_makes_it` run again: it is ignored, "sync rewrite: copies per (commit, thing)"), so the next ranks by real numbers.
1. **No pull anew for a thing just made** (A; engine `sync/units`): the biggest wire win, and it distorts every other count. Built for dependent things; independent things and attach are left. Beside it: **attribute memory** (H; `tools/memprof`): measured.
2. **One stored form per commit** (B; engine store, `CipherStore`): built, but `ou/` (B, Not built). Beside it: **a blob crosses each connection once** (C; osis-sync streams and its spec): built.
3. **Index rows that repeat a stored fact** (D; engine index), then **entry rows** (F; sync store), side by side.
4. **The commit's own bytes** (E; osis-commit, osis-crypto, spec): after 2, whose seal row it shrinks.
5. **Independent things** (G): the copy rule (held-model first), the key rows, box records.
6. **Memory** (H): measured; the index fix, the `sync_bodies` cutoff, a 40 B `Dep`, `Bodies` as a slice and the per-thing rows built; settled commits followed by coarser keys next (a design question), the bulk of what a node keeps per commit.

## Ruled out
- **One stamp over a batch**: entries are stored and served one by one, so each keeps a proof, a Merkle path of 32 B per level, at least the 64 B saved from a batch of two up.
- **One signature for commit and stamp** (Tess asked): the two serve readers who must learn the signer (the commit's, inside the ciphertext) and stores that must not (the stamp, by the sync ID's write key); one signature would tell the stores who wrote or the readers nothing. Stays two.
- **A lone small delta embedded in the header** (no hash, no section, about 160 B): needs the commit to have one section whose audience is the blob's and no per-part purge: a special case. Low; reconsider after E.
- **A state stored whole when smaller than its body reference**: a few bytes per aspect, beside D's `created.0` change, which is the case that matters.
- **A verified-step memo** (sync-step verifications per make, notes/thing-cost.md Open): a cache; recount after job 1, which removes most of them.
