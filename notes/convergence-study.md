# Convergence: retroactive validity against dropped data
> Summary: Why validity flips meet dropped data (the one class of honest-use read divergence), the rule that makes it converge on delivery (R1: a body dropped other than as covered counts as never received, a copy that arrives again joins), the repair backstop (suspect flags, a writer's compaction snapshot), why no removal rule removes the class (rejected: give up retroactivity, key epochs, bounded reach-back, grace windows), the holes left, and the unbuilt label digests with their cost. Tess signed off the requirement sentences.

## 1. The shape
Validity (spec/permissions.md §5.2) is a function of the held set, and that function is **non-monotone**: adding an explicit demotion D to a node's held set turns writes it had applied invalid. A node that applied X's write before it held D un-merges on D (spec/crdt.md §4.6), and un-merge is "the join of every body and residue the node holds, minus the invalid writes". A node that dropped a body it would need (purge, expiry, pending part dropped at T, a cover that itself turned invalid) computes a different join than a node that kept it. So the class is **(a validity flip) × (a non-vouching drop under the flipped write)**.
- Every flip is an explicit demotion arriving late (a removal, a demotion, a revoke of a snapshot's or cover's author; `until` and self-removals audit nothing and are judged at the write's own `ts`, so they never flip). Late means the demoted writer's write reached the node first: an unsynced or offline revoke, P2P-only edits, in-flight uploads.
- Compaction is a vouching drop and does not cause the class: a valid snapshot vouches for what it carries (notes/permissions.md Snapshots and history), so a write compacted before the compactor learned of the demotion stays at every node alike. The divergent drops are the non-vouching ones: expiry, purge, a pending part dropped at T, a right-less drop of a delta breaking a required retention.
- In honest use the class needs an aspect with retention bounds (or expiry/purge), written by a holder later demoted with a frontier not covering the write. Rare, but each new dropping mechanism re-opens a shape of it, which is why the fix is a rule, not per-case patches.

## 2. What is built
- **R1** (spec/commits.md §5, spec/permissions.md §5.2; `retention::takes_late`, node-sync's `DeletedBody` admitting a body live again): a body dropped other than as covered (a part missing when purged) counts as never received; a copy that arrives again joins and is judged again. The cover rule (a body dropped as covered whose cover turns invalid) is the same rule with the writes stripped. Memory parts stay final. So nodes holding the same bodies read alike; a node lacking a body gets it from any holder that still has it, a reader included (deposit, then promotion); only when no node holds it is it gone, and then alike. Privacy holds: expired and purged copies still arrive value-less or stripped. The order oracle asserts equality after the nodes exchange held bodies (`alike_exchanged`).
- **Requirement sentences** (Tess's sign-off): Decentralization says nodes holding the same bodies read alike, a node missing a body reads as if it never had it until it arrives.
- **A part pending at its expiry is dropped** and counts as never received (`retention::pending_drops`, `strip_pending`, also for parts waiting aside; a purged pending part is held value-less, `strip_purged`), and the aspect is flagged suspect.
- **Repair backstop** (notes/engine.md Suspect repair): a node flags an aspect it cannot compute right (an un-merge or flip where it holds a body dropped other than as covered, a pending part dropped at T, a snapshot's writes removed where their bodies are gone) in its `seen.0` heartbeat as (tid, ak, digest), the digest keyed by the thing's dedup secret (reader-only, no recompute at rotation). A writer whose digest differs writes an ordinary compaction snapshot of the aspect (`Engine::snapshot`), a `Repair` diagnostic per judged entry. A flag names the bodies it is about and clears per body: held again, or listed as included by a valid snapshot that joins; dormancy is judged when the flags are read, from the held valid snapshots (`suspect::dormant`), since a settling snapshot can turn invalid later; a pending-expiry flag is never dormant. `sync_more::suspect_aspect_repaired_by_writer_snapshot_spec_permissions_4`.
- Why a compaction snapshot and not `replace` as the repair write: a `replace` is a reset at the repairer's `ts`, pruning every concurrent honest edit and making the repairer's view win over a richer one. A snapshot's copies join with held originals by the copy order, two writers' snapshots join to their union, it carries expiring writes value-less and purged ones stripped (so it never resurrects them), and it carries a keep-at-most-dropped value exactly when it is live at the snapshotter. If the repairer's view is the poorer one its snapshot changes nothing and the richer writer's converges everyone. Readers cannot write snapshots; they only report. Repairs hide bugs, so each is a diagnostic and the oracles run with repair off.
- Repair's premise is that a snapshot listing a delta carries its writes (value-less for an expired one): a record a valid snapshot keeps keeps the valid writes nested in it (spec/crdt.md §4.6).

## 3. Rejected: removal rules
No removal rule removes the class: convergence is decided by what the node dropped, not by the rule. Keep the frontier model.
- **Give up retroactive invalidation** ("a removal stops only writes arriving after it"). By arrival at the judging node: validity depends on arrival order, breaking Deterministic and Convergence outright. By arrival at a host: a sequencer, against "no central or trusted coordinator" and "nothing trusts a host's word", undefined with several hosts and P2P. By the author's claimed `ts`: the rejected timestamp rule, and still retroactive (an honest 10:05 write accepted over LAN flips when a 10:00 removal arrives). Any rule in which a removal invalidates a write some node already applied is retroactive there; the only non-retroactive rules are arrival-dependent. And the removed writer plus any reader would keep permanent write power (deposit, promotion), breaking "readers only read".
- **Bind each write to its key epoch**: holding an epoch's write secret proves only a lower bound on write time ("after the epoch existed"), never the upper bound removal needs; a removed writer keeps the secret and backdates into any epoch it held. It would lose more honest writes and still flip.
- **Bounded reach-back by a first witness** (a writer's signed receipt frontier, or an upload stamp under a write-key epoch the author never held): sound and deterministic as a least fixpoint, but still retroactive; it reduces loss (writes the revoker had not received), closes no divergence, and adds a receipt frontier to every delta. The lever for that loss if receipts-only reads prove insufficient.
- **Reach-back window** on `ts`: backdated vandalism is what removal prevents. **Demoter compacts first**: not needed with R1; its size bound would be a convergence switch.

## 4. Holes left
Classes: **F** forged copies by an authenticated writer (bounded, out of scope by requirements; nodes converge whenever both hold the same valid snapshots); **C** clock or arrival, transient; **L** nodes read alike but something is lost or never applied.
- Sticky drop in the role timeline: a drop whose cause is later dropped stays (D only grows within a walk); alike at every node holding the same role commits; a re-grant recovers (notes/permissions.md Role timeline). L, about 1e-4 of random histories with offline revokers.
- A valid snapshot's copy differing from a held delta's (forged) joins by the total copy order wherever held, so nodes holding the same bodies agree; the forger picks the value, no worse than writing it: the original's body stays held, uncovered by any snapshot holding another copy (notes/permissions.md Snapshots and history). F, no divergence.
- Equivocation by a valid writer whose first commit's writes are all dead: nodes that held the first void both, nodes that forgot it count the second (issues/session-sources.md). F.
- A forged snapshot listing a part makes the commit show partly until the part arrives (notes/engine-validity.md Snapshot authority). F.
- A keep-at-least bound or an edge's `until` straddled by two nodes' clocks: delete and re-place while clocks differ (notes/sync-convergence.md Limits). C, seconds.
- A value reads past its T until the next call or Tick (notes/ephemeral.md Expiry). C. A relay-only signal delivered twice after a restart, or late within `MAX_DRIFT` (spec/sync.md §9). C.
- A cross-group commit at a receipts-only partial node pends (issues/receipts-only-mode-obsolete.md). L, until policy lifted.
- Session-sources double removal: a write left out of A's frontier because Y's snapshot Z covered it, then Y removed by a frontier not covering Z: lost alike (issues/session-sources.md). L; a demoter listing every commit avoids it.
- Writes the revoker had not received (unreachable hosts, P2P-only edits, in transit): lost alike until re-grant (spec/requirements.md Permissions). The designed semantics.
- Every writer dropped what only a reader kept: lasts to the next write (spec/requirements.md Deterministic; notes/engine.md Suspect repair).

## 5. Not built: label digests
Detection of unknown divergence: a set-hash (XOR of BLAKE3) of the commits applied under a label (`held`) and one over per-aspect state digests of the label's closure (`state`) in the heartbeat; two co-readers with equal `held` and unequal `state` have diverged, reported as a diagnostic only. Cost found: a per-aspect digest kept current means hashing on every state write, page-wise for paged states (the store keeps no per-page hash), plus a per-label set-hash over the closure that node-sync keeps as things enter and leave a label; a few hundred lines in the engine's write path and node-sync's work sets. Suspect entries compute their digest on demand instead. It would be the first field signal for divergences the oracles missed.
