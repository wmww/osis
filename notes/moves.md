# Moving a thing
> Summary: A thing never changes its record and nothing moves it (Tess): a move is grant then remove, with no tid change. A plain copy needs nothing in the protocol; a copy with imported history is rejected, with what made it costly.

1. **Grant then remove**: grant the new party admin, remove the old one. The tid stays; a large set moves by handing over one shared actor (the share-through-one-actor rule, spec/requirements.md Generality).
2. **Plain copy** (possible today, needs nothing in the protocol): the mover creates M under the new first admin and writes T's current values as its own writes; T stays. Loses original authorship, edit history and others' purge rights over the copied values.
3. **Copy with imported history**: rejected. An import commit re-sourcing another thing's writes onto M (with `was.0`/`moved.0` links resolving references at read time) interacts with purge, retention, validity and arrival order everywhere, while nothing first-party moves. If an app ever needs it: give imported writes a source kind of their own and one origin per import commit; that removes the hardest rules (imported dots sharing `Commit(C)` with a commit-form C's own dots, multi-origin imports). The earlier design is in git: `git log -S 'Import commit' -- spec/permissions.md`.
