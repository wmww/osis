# Keys: history boxes and establishment
> Summary: Why history boxes, establishment by names, passing on content, what a `secrets.0` snapshot vouches for, uncovered forks rotating and withdraws are what they are (spec/crypto.md §4-§5), with the establishment rules that failed; the superseded-epoch wrap (why a removed party reads role parts of commits giving read epochs until the step, what closing it costs, Tess's decision). Coverage (the pin): notes/keys-coverage.md; floors: notes/history-less-grant.md.

Principle: **nothing is written under an epoch until this node holds every departure its rotator knew of** (establishment); every link is first hand, so nothing follows another rotator's list.

## History boxes
- **History boxes**: holding a role's current secret opens every older one its holders have; a holder removed before epoch n opens nothing newer; a join costs one box per role, not one per epoch. A grant or re-seal writes the history box of each epoch it boxes again (what the live entries list plus what the writer holds), because the recipient could not otherwise fetch it: the rotation commit carrying it is wrapped under the epoch the recipient lacks (TLC R3). 32 bytes per earlier epoch per grant.
- **A history add is a new entry beside the others** (`put_secrets` with `new_only`, a may-grant grant's shape): a reader may add, and a put resetting the rotator's entry would let one reader hide the genuine entry; several live entries are each tried (`HistoryAlts`). Every entry of one epoch carries the rotation's names (the adder copies a met entry's, a bound one first), so an added entry is bound by the keyset's digest like the rotator's and establishment reads nothing weaker from it.
- **Only established epochs' secrets go in a history box** (TLC R9), rotations, grants, re-seals and adds alike: a node re-sealed at e before e's commit reached it holds e's secret but not the departure e carries, and would otherwise rotate over e and hand e's secret to the holder e removed. What an entry lists is copied as it is (anyone with the key read it already).
- **Floors and state at a cut** (grants without history, spec/crypto.md §4 Floors): notes/history-less-grant.md.

## Passing on content
R9's content twin (spec/crypto.md §5 Passing on): what a node read only under epochs not established at it, it passes on only as the ciphertext it came in. Mechanism, invariant and who writes each input: `sync_ids::opened`'s module doc. Model: core/test/tla/rotation/Reseal.tla (sealing under the opened epoch itself is safe too, but a snapshot carries many writes under one epoch, so the rule is stated on the node's choice). Tests: `rotation::tests_reseal`. Sync's one caller: `Engine::seal_body` (notes/sync-facade.md Sealing).
- A copy's content counts as opened under the epoch its entry's wrap opened under, of the sync ID's thing T: a fresh encryption or snapshot of the part's own thing never takes it, while a wrap under T does (`opened_under_wrap`).
- An opening row names its thing: an epoch's (n, nonce) names none where the node lacks its secret, and any admin of T may write a rotation commit of T naming another thing's epoch, which a row keyed by epoch alone let count as an opening under T.
- Floor clause: a part goes under an epoch of floor f > 0 only where an epoch it was opened or written under has floor at least f (`opened::note_own` for own parts).
- **Every read notes its epoch; none is exempt** (fail closed, Reseal.tla `RS_closed`). Rejected: passing a part read in no section as the node's own (`RS_exempt` leaks): a sealed fire is such a read, and a node lacking the removal its keyset names put the fire under its own epoch, which the removed reader holds (`sealed_fire_passes_on_only_once_its_keyset_is_established_spec_crypto_5`).
- Content read only from copies under another thing's sync ID (a thing read through a role on its independent, which that sync ID does not carry) is passed on under no epoch of its own thing until this node opens a section of it (its writer and nodes that opened its section do); an aspect holding a write read only under an epoch not established here (a withheld removal, a withdrawn epoch) is not compacted by this node while so (other writers' valid snapshots still drop its bodies, spec/sync-compaction.md §1); a wrap whose epoch is not among the thing's own or mint-admin-derived epochs held (a thing whose `created.0` is not here, opened by a key actor's derived secret) records no header opening.
- Reseal.tla models section openings; the header, copy and fire openings are the same rule on another reader of the same epoch, not modelled apart.

## Establishment
- **Establishment** (spec/crypto.md §4 Established and current) is the one notion behind "may encrypt under", "current", what a history box carries and where a re-seal may be made. Each rotation's history box lists, from its rotator's own timeline, the lowerings on the thing it held and the void writes giving a role that count (and itself, by `ts` and author, when it departs someone); its keyset publishes their digest. A node uses the epoch only once it holds them all, each judged as the rotator judged it (notes/keys-coverage.md, The pin), so it never passes on an epoch whose rotator knew of a departure the node lacks: its own roster then has the holder departed and boxes it nothing. Unestablished secrets read; nothing is written under them.
- **Rejected establishment rules**: "settled" chains, where each rotation's keyset listed the rotation commits of what it superseded and a node followed the chain down to an established epoch: every link is someone else's word, and each way it lies needs its own patch (a writer's own list skipping a removal, a forged valid rotation listed beside a genuine unsettled one, a snapshot standing in for a removal, invalid commits passed through by name, re-grants hiding the chain, injected forged claims). Also: a departed-holder set in the history box (hearsay: every boxing path must consult it, and a writer could bar any holder); re-sealing only to holders the rotator boxed (closes re-seals, not the rotation path); an age rule (clock-dependent); a roster hash from the rotator (detects a mismatch, cannot repair it); establishment from the node's own timeline without names (a node cannot know what it has not received).
- **What a `secrets.0` snapshot vouches for** (spec/crypto.md §4): a snapshot keying an epoch bases it at every receiver, so it carries only records at epochs established at its writer, names aside (`osis_keys::unvouched`, `Fragment::leave_out`); names are each holder's own judgement, so a withdrawn epoch's records are carried. A record left out (a reader's history add or a writer's box at an epoch no valid rotation made, a removed member's backdated one, or a genuine grant's box that reached the writer before its rotation commit) adds itself back on join, so the commit writing it is not covered (spec/commits.md §5) and stays beside the snapshot, not in its `Absorbed` either (a commit writing both a carried and a left-out record would else read covered at a node holding the snapshot but not the body, which then never fetches it: `mixed_commit_not_absorbed_spec_crdt_8`). So compaction never waits on what the node cannot attribute to a valid rotation, never loses a record, and never bases an epoch nothing vouched for. A plant costs its own commit and entry, for good: the same order as a reader's junk entries at a genuine current epoch, so no new growth class. Model: core/test/tla/rotation/Planted.tla. Tests: `rotation::tests_planted`.
  - Rejected: judging an entry at an epoch no valid rotation made invalid (validity is judged alike at every node from the commit and the role timeline, while whether a rotation of e is held is arrival-dependent, and after compaction only a snapshot keys e, so a genuine grant box before its rotation would fall: `P_drop`'s loss; held pending instead, it costs the same storage with more machinery); gating compaction until every keyed epoch is verified (one plant stops `secrets.0` compaction at every node for good); carrying everything (a plant gets based at every receiver: a reader forces rotations, `P_all`); leaving records out but covering their commits (a genuine grant's box is lost where it is current, `P_drop`); basing a snapshot's epochs only where a held keyset writes or lists them (once `keys.0` compacts only the newest keyset is left, so an older epoch loses its basis at a fresh node, and with it passing on content written under it, `P_keyset`).

## Uncovered and unsuperseded epochs
- **Uncovered forks rotate** (promise 3): a node that may rotate rotates while any current epoch, not only the newest, misses a departure it knows (`rotation_due`, `osis_keys::uncovered`), before it next encrypts: a fork made without knowing the departure otherwise stays current and may win the (n, nonce) order. A current epoch whose secret the node lacks is judged by order (`rotation::uncovered`, as a holder actor's keyset is): ordered before a known departure, it cannot cover it and the node rotates over it unread; ordered after, its rotator may have known, and only the rotators boxed at it, reading its history, can tell, so they judge it (a node that lacks it is stranded and asks a re-seal, spec/permissions.md §4). Why: an epoch a node cannot read is no cover of its to judge. Rotating over every epoch it lacks loops wherever rotators cannot read each other's epochs (each reads the epoch it made only: two writers through a dependent holder actor, or rotators sealing to a holder actor at keysets the others lack), each new epoch uncovered at the next for good (`a_rotator_lacking_an_epoch_leaves_it_to_its_readers_spec_crypto_4`); and it made a lockout's recovery hang on an unrelated departure being known (a vandal still a holder is recovered by removing it, `chat_by_record_spec_perm_1`). A fork made without knowing a departure but ordered after it is rotated only once one of its readers knows the departure; until then only its readers encrypt under it. An orphaned epoch has no reader to judge it: it makes the rotation due where the new epoch boxes a holder that may rotate the role (`rotation::boxes_rotator`), never a loop. A rotator whose every path runs through a holder actor with no keyset is boxed by no rotation, so such rotators stall until an admin of the holder actor gives it secrets again (`own_secrets_candidates`, notes/engine-roles.md Role flows; `orphaned_rotations_do_not_trade_spec_crypto_4`).
- **Unsuperseded and withdraw** (spec/crypto.md §4): names gate establishment only where the secret is held, so an epoch whose names its holders do not meet (it names a removal by an admin demoted concurrently, which applies nothing once the demotion arrives) is current at nodes lacking its secret and at no holder of it, and no fork rule fires where the secret is held. So a rotation lists, and counts for n, every unsuperseded epoch, and the epoch's maker withdraws it: it rotates while an epoch it made is unsuperseded but not current at it. A maker's own names come from its own timeline, so they go unmet there only when later arrivals refute them, never while a named commit is in flight; anyone withdraws when the maker may not rotate or is unknown. The withdraw alone loops (n never passes the unmet epoch, which stays unlisted). Bounded: each withdraw answers one epoch; withdraws by several nodes fork once, and the fork stands. Rejected: merging over current and unsuperseded epochs at any node (a rotation commit arriving before its named commits would be rotated over). Test: `rotation::tests::unmet_epoch_withdrawn_by_its_maker_spec_crypto_4` (fails without either half).
- **A withdrawn epoch's secret goes nowhere**: established nowhere it is held, it goes in no history box, so what was written under it reaches only its box holders, never the holder its rotator left out nor later holders. Only an admin fight triggers it (the demotion and the removal both need admin), and what is lost is what writers holding the removal but not the demotion wrote in between (an admin could remove the holder outright, so nobody gains by it). No rule closes it: "refuted" is never final (a re-grant of the remover ordered between its demotion and the removal, or an unseen demotion of the demoter, makes the removal apply again), and while the admin holding that write is away the others hold the same commits in a world where the left-out holder is entitled and one where it was removed (Withdrawn.tla: passing it on leaks, waiting for the re-granter leaks through another admin's unseen demotion, waiting for every admin stalls). Passing it on would also leak ongoing content: a node holding the re-grant keeps the epoch current and writes under it until the withdraw reaches it. Its cause is required, not incidental: a demotion voids the demoted admin's writes its author had not received (the only cut a backdating admin cannot forge), and that can flip any number of times as earlier writes arrive. Its boxes stand for good, and a `secrets.0` snapshot carries them.
  - Rejected: re-keying its content under the withdraw's epoch (the re-keyer holds no more than the others: leaks as passing it on); addenda vouching for the removal (shrink the case without closing it); an explicit re-grant of the holder unlocking it (safe, but nothing tells an admin to make one); boxing it to later holders only (safe and live, Withdrawn.tla `W_joiners`, but a box class at superseded epochs that every grant and re-seal consults by names: the rejected hearsay pattern, for a rare case). Tests: `withdrawn_epoch_content_reaches_its_box_holders_only_spec_crypto_4`, `withdrawn_epoch_compacts_judged_alike_spec_crypto_4`.

## The superseded-epoch wrap
A commit giving read epochs (rotation, grant, re-seal) is wrapped under an epoch they supersede (spec/crypto.md §5), so a party X removed at the rotation minting e, holding the superseded epoch's roles secret, opens the role parts of every commit giving e: `roles.0` (who was granted), `keys.0` (keysets, the fresh deposit seed), `secrets.0` (box keys: who is boxed; the boxes and history entries stay sealed). issues/removed-device-reads-later-grants.md.
- **The window is the step, not the next rotation.** X reads entries of S only while it opens S: once S's step for e lands at a peer, X's credential there gets only the walk to the new head (spec/sync-ids.md §3), so it fetches nothing more there. Everything X reads past its removal under S (ciphertext, stamps, these role parts) lasts until the new step reaches the peers it uses: briefly for an admin's removal (rotation logged, then the step), until an admin next writes for a writer's (registration is admin-only, notes/keys-rotation.md). A blob logged under several sync IDs (derived things) is read through whichever of them still serves X's old step.
- **The chicken-and-egg.** A recipient lacking e (at a rotation: every holder; at a grant or re-seal: the grantee or re-sealed holder) learns e only from its box, and the box sits in `secrets.0` inside the role section inside the blob, whose key is in the entry's wrap. So the wrap and that section must open with something the recipient holds before it holds e, and the only thing every such recipient holds that X does not is its own KEM key.
- **Candidates.**
  - *Wrap the role parts under the new epoch, the boxes outside*: `secrets.0` under the superseded roles secret (the recipient opens its box there), `roles.0` and `keys.0` under the given epoch's. Closes `roles.0` and the commit's own `keys.0`, not the box keys (who was granted), the header or the deposit seed (`keys.0` goes whole as a certificate under every sync ID needing the thing's keys: the issue's Certificates window). A shrink, not a close, and needs an audit of every non-holder reader of a thing's own `keys.0` first.
  - *Per-holder KEM wrap*: each boxed holder gets the blob or section key sealed to its KEM key outside the blob. Found by holder tid it shows X who was boxed, as the split does. Anonymous it needs trial decapsulation of every box by every recipient: O(holders^2) per rotation, against fast by construction; plus a new entry form and the boxes carried twice (about 1.2 KB each) or `secrets.0` holding references.
  - *Derive the wrap from e's secret*: the same chicken-and-egg; resolving it pulls the boxes out into an outer layer, which is one of the two above.
  - *Plain rule plus delivery by inbox* (wrap under e, recipients lacking e take their box by inbox): at a rotation every holder lacks e, so every rotation becomes one sealed delivery per holder; link keys have none (a key actor has no inbox, spec/sync-inbox.md §1).
- **Conclusion.** Nothing structural closes it at a cost in line with the rest of the key machinery: any design where a recipient lacking e finds its box without X learning that it was boxed needs anonymous per-holder delivery and trial decryption. Decided (Tess): within the requirements' key-step rule, bounded by the step (a removed party reads, until S's new step reaches the peers it uses, the role parts of commits giving the epoch that removed it: who is granted or re-sealed, keysets, the deposit seed; never a secret or content); judged again at A5 (issues/removed-device-reads-later-grants.md). So a deposit-seed refresh stops a departed party only once it reads no sync ID certifying the thing and S's step has moved (issues/removed-device-reads-later-grants.md). Building the split alone is rejected: it shrinks the leak and adds a role section class every reader and copy path must know.
