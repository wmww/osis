# Keys: history boxes and establishment
> Summary: Why history boxes, establishment by names, passing on content, what a `secrets.0` snapshot vouches for, uncovered forks rotating and withdraws are what they are (spec/crypto.md §4-§5), with the establishment rules that failed. Coverage (the pin): notes/keys-coverage.md; floors: notes/history-less-grant.md.

Principle: **nothing is written under an epoch until this node holds every departure its rotator knew of** (establishment); every link is first hand, so nothing follows another rotator's list.

## History boxes
- **History boxes**: holding a role's current secret opens every older one its holders have; a holder removed before epoch n opens nothing newer; a join costs one box per role, not one per epoch. A grant or re-seal writes the history box of each epoch it boxes again (what the live entries list plus what the writer holds), because the recipient could not otherwise fetch it: the rotation commit carrying it is wrapped under the epoch the recipient lacks (TLC R3). 32 bytes per earlier epoch per grant.
- **A history add is a new entry beside the others** (`put_secrets` with `new_only`, the invite grant's shape): a reader may add, and a put resetting the rotator's entry would let one reader hide the genuine entry; several live entries are each tried (`HistoryAlts`). Every entry of one epoch carries the rotation's names (the adder copies a met entry's, a bound one first), so an added entry is bound by the keyset's digest like the rotator's and establishment reads nothing weaker from it.
- **Only established epochs' secrets go in a history box** (TLC R9), rotations, grants, re-seals and adds alike: a node re-sealed at e before e's commit reached it holds e's secret but not the departure e carries, and would otherwise rotate over e and hand e's secret to the holder e removed. What an entry lists is copied as it is (anyone with the key read it already).
- **Floors and state at a cut** (grants without history, spec/crypto.md §4 Floors): notes/history-less-grant.md.

## Passing on content
R9's content twin (spec/crypto.md §5 Passing on): what a node read only under epochs not established at it, it passes on only as the ciphertext it came in. A fresh encryption of another's commit, the wrap of a blob carrying its parts and a snapshot carrying its writes put them under an epoch the node chose, so each needs an epoch the node opened a genuine section of that part under to be established there now (then the node knows every departure that epoch covered, and its own epochs cover them). Model: core/test/tla/rotation/Reseal.tla (sealing under the opened epoch itself is safe too, but a snapshot carries many writes under one epoch, so the rule is stated on the node's choice). Tests: `rotation::tests_reseal`. Sync's one caller: `Engine::seal_body` (notes/sync-facade.md Sealing).
- Mechanism: `Engine::open_commit` notes each opening (`sync_ids::opened`: per part, its epoch with the thing it is an epoch of, and delta hash), and `passes_on` judges at use (a later demotion unestablishes an epoch). The wrap of any blob of the commit needs every held content part opened so and, once an entry's wrap of the commit opened here, one such wrap's epoch established (`note_unwrapped`).
- A copy's content counts as opened under the epoch its entry's wrap opened under, of the sync ID's thing T: so a fresh encryption or snapshot of the part's own thing never takes it, while a wrap under T counts an opening under an established T epoch of floor at least the wrap's (`opened_under_wrap`).
- An opening row names its thing: an epoch's (n, nonce) names none where the node lacks its secret, and any admin of T may write a rotation commit of T naming another thing's epoch, which a row keyed by epoch alone let count as an opening under T.
- A held section goes verbatim where none of its parts is held stripped or dropped. Floor clause: a part goes under an epoch of floor f > 0 only where an epoch it was opened or written under has floor at least f (`opened::note_own` for own parts).
- A part read in no section passes as the node's own: no sync path carries another's content so; fail-closed is safe and live in the model, but LocalPeer's plain delivery (the test harness) would need sections. Open: issues/reseal-under-own-epoch-leaks.md.

## Establishment
- **Establishment** (spec/crypto.md §4 Established and current) is the one notion behind "may encrypt under", "current", what a history box carries and where a re-seal may be made. Each rotation's history box lists, from its rotator's own timeline, the lowerings on the actor it held and the void writes giving a role that count (and itself, by `ts` and signer, when it departs someone); its keyset publishes their digest. A node uses the epoch only once it holds them all, each judged as the rotator judged it (notes/keys-coverage.md, The pin), so it never passes on an epoch whose rotator knew of a departure the node lacks: its own roster then has the holder departed and boxes it nothing. Unestablished secrets read; nothing is written under them.
- **Rejected establishment rules**: "settled" chains, where each rotation's keyset listed the rotation commits of what it superseded and a node followed the chain down to an established epoch: six rounds of fixes each closed one way a link was someone else's word (a writer's own list skipping a removal, a forged valid rotation listed beside a genuine unsettled one, a snapshot standing in for a removal, invalid commits passed through by name, re-grants hiding the chain, injected forged claims). Also: a departed-holder set in the history box (hearsay: every boxing path must consult it, and a writer could bar any holder); re-sealing only to holders the rotator boxed (closes re-seals, not the rotation path); an age rule (clock-dependent); a roster hash from the rotator (detects a mismatch, cannot repair it); establishment from the node's own timeline without names (a node cannot know what it has not received).
- **What a `secrets.0` snapshot vouches for** (spec/crypto.md §4): a snapshot keying an epoch bases it at every receiver, so it carries only records at epochs established at its writer, names aside (`osis_keys::unvouched`, `Fragment::leave_out`); names are each holder's own judgement, so a withdrawn epoch's records are carried. A record left out (a reader's history add or a writer's box at an epoch no valid rotation made, a removed member's backdated one, or a genuine grant's box that reached the writer before its rotation commit) adds itself back on join, so the commit writing it is not covered (spec/commits.md §5) and stays beside the snapshot, not in its `Absorbed` either (a commit writing both a carried and a left-out record would else read covered at a node holding the snapshot but not the body, which then never fetches it: `mixed_commit_not_absorbed_spec_crdt_8`). So compaction never waits on what the node cannot attribute to a valid rotation, never loses a record, and never bases an epoch nothing vouched for. A plant costs its own commit and entry, for good: the same order as a reader's junk entries at a genuine current epoch, so no new growth class. Model: core/test/tla/rotation/Planted.tla. Tests: `rotation::tests_planted`.
  - Rejected: judging an entry at an epoch no valid rotation made invalid (validity is judged alike at every node from the commit and the role timeline, while whether a rotation of e is held is arrival-dependent, and after compaction only a snapshot keys e, so a genuine grant box before its rotation would fall: `P_drop`'s loss; held pending instead, it costs the same storage with more machinery); gating compaction until every keyed epoch is verified (one plant stops `secrets.0` compaction at every node for good); carrying everything (a plant gets based at every receiver: a reader forces rotations, `P_all`); leaving records out but covering their commits (a genuine grant's box is lost where it is current, `P_drop`); basing a snapshot's epochs only where a held keyset writes or lists them (once `keys.0` compacts only the newest keyset is left, so an older epoch loses its basis at a fresh node, and with it passing on content written under it, `P_keyset`).

## Uncovered and unsuperseded epochs
- **Uncovered forks rotate** (promise 3): a node that may rotate rotates while any current epoch, not only the newest, misses a departure it knows (`rotation_due`, `osis_keys::uncovered`), before it next encrypts: a fork made without knowing the departure otherwise stays current and may win the (n, nonce) order. A current epoch whose secret the node lacks is judged by order (`rotation::uncovered`, as a holder actor's keyset is): ordered before a known departure, it cannot cover it and the node rotates over it unread; ordered after, its rotator may have known, and only the rotators boxed at it, reading its history, can tell, so they judge it (a node that lacks it is stranded and asks a re-seal, spec/permissions.md §4). Why: an epoch a node cannot read is no cover of its to judge. Rotating over every epoch it lacks loops wherever rotators cannot read each other's epochs (each reads the epoch it made only: two writers through a plain holder actor, or rotators sealing to a holder actor at keysets the others lack), each new epoch uncovered at the next for good (`a_rotator_lacking_an_epoch_leaves_it_to_its_readers_spec_crypto_4`); and it made a lockout's recovery hang on an unrelated departure being known (a vandal still a holder is recovered by removing it, `chat_by_record_spec_perm_1`). Limit: a fork made without knowing a departure but ordered after it is rotated only once one of its readers knows the departure; until then only its readers encrypt under it. An orphaned epoch has no reader to judge it: it makes the rotation due where the new epoch boxes a holder that may rotate the role (`rotation::boxes_rotator`), never a loop. A rotator whose every path runs through a holder actor with no keyset is boxed by no rotation, so such rotators stall until an admin of the holder actor gives it secrets again (`own_secrets_candidates`, notes/engine-roles.md Role flows; `orphaned_rotations_do_not_trade_spec_crypto_4`).
- **Unsuperseded and withdraw** (spec/crypto.md §4): names gate establishment only where the secret is held, so an epoch whose names its holders do not meet (it names a removal by an admin demoted concurrently, which applies nothing once the demotion arrives) is current at nodes lacking its secret and at no holder of it, and no fork rule fires where the secret is held. So a rotation lists, and counts for n, every unsuperseded epoch, and the epoch's maker withdraws it: it rotates while an epoch it made is unsuperseded but not current at it. A maker's own names come from its own timeline, so they go unmet there only when later arrivals refute them, never while a named commit is in flight; anyone withdraws when the maker may not rotate or is unknown. The withdraw alone loops (n never passes the unmet epoch, which stays unlisted). Bounded: each withdraw answers one epoch; withdraws by several nodes fork once, and the fork stands. Rejected: merging over current and unsuperseded epochs at any node (a rotation commit arriving before its named commits would be rotated over). Test: `rotation::tests::unmet_epoch_withdrawn_by_its_maker_spec_crypto_4` (fails without either half).
- **A withdrawn epoch's secret goes nowhere** (spec/requirements.md Permissions, the voided-removal Limit): established nowhere it is held, it goes in no history box, so what was written under it reaches only its box holders, never the holder its rotator left out nor later holders. Only an admin fight triggers it (the demotion and the removal both need admin), and what is lost is what writers holding the removal but not the demotion wrote in between (an admin could remove the holder outright, so nobody gains by it). No rule closes it: "refuted" is never final (a re-grant of the remover ordered between its demotion and the removal, or an unseen demotion of the demoter, makes the removal apply again), and while the admin holding that write is away the others hold the same commits in a world where the left-out holder is entitled and one where it was removed (Withdrawn.tla: passing it on leaks, waiting for the re-granter leaks through another admin's unseen demotion, waiting for every admin stalls). Passing it on would also leak ongoing content: a node holding the re-grant keeps the epoch current and writes under it until the withdraw reaches it. Its cause is required, not incidental: a demotion voids the demoted admin's writes its author had not received (the only cut a backdating admin cannot forge), and that can flip any number of times as earlier writes arrive. Its boxes stand for good, and a `secrets.0` snapshot carries them.
  - Rejected: re-keying its content under the withdraw's epoch (the re-keyer holds no more than the others: leaks as passing it on); addenda vouching for the removal (shrink the case without closing it); an explicit re-grant of the holder unlocking it (safe, but nothing tells an admin to make one); boxing it to later holders only (safe and live, Withdrawn.tla `W_joiners`, but a box class at superseded epochs that every grant and re-seal consults by names: the rejected hearsay pattern, for a rare case). Tests: `withdrawn_epoch_content_reaches_its_box_holders_only_spec_crypto_4`, `withdrawn_epoch_compacts_judged_alike_spec_crypto_4`.
