# Keys: who rotates and when
> Summary: Why write or above rotates its roles and below (decided with Tess), lazily, with no periodic rotation; per-role secrets and the patterns they cover; dependent things deriving secrets until shared; the nesting cost; the stale `keys.0` window; a removal and its rotation in one role commit; what a removal reaches; re-seals (a SHOULD with a trigger) and the re-seal invariant with orphaned epochs. Rules: spec/permissions.md §3; key structure: notes/keys.md.

Principle: **a removal rotates lazily: the next node that may rotate and is about to encrypt does it**, and the right to rotate is write or above (notes/keys.md, The right to rotate is keyed).

## Writes, reads and roles
- Writes are node-signed plus a chain of role grants. Sharing a thing's signing key among holders erases which holder wrote (revocation needs the author) and makes every removal a key rotation; it is reserved for deliberate anonymous writing (notes/permissions.md).
- Reads are key wrapping, per role and every epoch: a later writer needs old write epochs to sign deletes of old `writer`-purgeable entries; a later reader needs old read epochs for old wraps.
- Per-role secrets cover the patterns without extra machinery: sharing (read and write wrapped to the grantee, admin only to the admins), demotion write -> read (rotate write only), removal. A writer's rotation of read and write leaves the admin secret, its boxes and the sync ID delete key where they were (notes/keys.md, Epochs per role).
- **A `secrets.0` record needs the role its key names** (validity `secrets_role`): a map record acts only on the entry of its key (spec/crdt.md §5.6), removals included, so the role and the removed box are read from the delta alone; a key-erased record needs admin. Rejected: looking up the removed entry's creation key (its answer depended on which bodies a node had held). `roles.0` is read alike: its perm adaptation (`edge_ops`) names a removal's holder by its entry's creation (`holder_keys`) and ignores a reset carrying another key.

## Who rotates
- **Write or above rotates its roles and below** (decided with Tess). With admin-only rotation, every thing an account holds a non-admin role on would keep wrapping new content to a lost node until its admins happened to rotate, and the account could do nothing. So the account's own node rotates every thing it writes to when it removes the lost node, and elsewhere the next writer to write does. Readers rotate nothing: a rotation sealed to an incomplete roster locks holders out, worse than the storage waste a reader may cause; a thing the account only reads keeps wrapping to a lost node until a writer or admin of it next writes. A writer's bad rotation is bounded like its other vandalism (the next honest rotation forks past it). Eager rotation by many holders forks harmlessly but costs boxes per rotator.
- Registration stays admin-only (the delete key): if the write key signed steps, every ex-writer could race to lock a sync ID at a node keeping it unread, which only ex-admins can (notes/sync-access.md). Until an admin registers the newest epoch, a removed node keeps the old sync and write keys there: ciphertext it cannot open, stale stamps and level-3 deletes that holders heal. Residue: a holder that has not heard of the removal wraps under the old epoch, and the lost node reads that one blob (as with Signal sender keys).
- **No periodic rotation, no rotation policy** (Tess). A node compromise is either known, and the removal rotates before the next write, or unknown, and the node is still a holder, so a scheduled epoch seals the new secrets to it as well. Healing a silent copy would need the node's KEM key to change from randomness the thief lacks; the seed is the node, so a thief holding it is the node. History boxes mean a compromise yields all stored history anyway; "no history for new holders" and bounded history are app or future choices.

## Dependent things and nesting
- **Dependent things derive secrets** until they have a holder other than their mint admin, so nothing is stored; then explicit secrets, so removing that holder rotates the thing alone and not its mint admin. The switch is an ordinary rotation from the derived epochs (history boxes carry the derived secrets, one-way from the mint admin's), so wraps, delete keys and sync ID steps need no special case. Content of the derived era keeps the mint admin's delete keys: a later admin of the thing who is no admin of the mint admin cannot delete it by key at nodes keeping S unread, only by the sync ID delete key (accepted, Tess). Derived epoch numbers: notes/keys-impl.md, Derived epochs.
- Explicit secrets do not stop the cascade the other way: every rotation of a mint admin is caused by a removal, and the removed holder could open every explicit secret its roles reach (its dependents, grant targets, transitively), so all of those rotate too, lazily. The `keys.0` keysets of rotated roles are written by the rotator, a writer included, or sync IDs above would keep sealing to the stale KEM key until an admin acted.
- **Nesting cost** (accepted by Tess): removing a holder rotates every thing with explicit secrets whose secret it could open, so a lost node costs one lazy rotation per shared thing the account reaches; the usage rule is to share sets through one thing that holds the role. A thing's admins learn what it holds roles on because every grant whose holder is the thing is also published under its own sync ID. Per rotated thing: one sealed box (about 1.2 KB) per direct holder per rotated role, one re-registration per sync ID per node keeping it unread. An organization of 50 shared spaces with 10 sync IDs each kept unread by 3 nodes: 50 boxes and 30 signed messages per account removal. Inherent to lazy rotation (spec/requirements.md, Cost).
- **What a removal reaches**: the things the removed party's roles opened, the things minted with them and grant targets transitively, linear in the graph: the sync IDs a removal is logged under.

## Removals
- A removed node erases what it can no longer read, so a later compromise exposes nothing more; not enforceable against a hostile node. If the removal is undone, the node re-syncs. The removal reaches the removed holder in its key part where the remover's node writes it, else as a `grant@~0` fire on its inbox (spec/sync-inbox.md §5).
- **Stale `keys.0` window**: until an account's new `keys.0` reaches the sync ID of a thing the account holds a role on, rotations there seal to the lost node's old keyset and its steps list its old claim key, so the lost node reads new content via an honest node keeping S unread. Hence the MUST to log the removal and new `keys.0` under every sync ID the removed party could read in the same sync as the removal (spec/permissions.md §3); what remains is rotations racing that sync.
- **A removal and the rotation it requires on the target are one role commit**, so the rotation and the departure travel together (a sender holding the keys may still split them, spec/commits.md §4), and an omission in a held rotation can only mean the rotator did not know the holder. Without it (TLC, core/test/tla/rotation R1) a node receiving the rotation before the removal re-seals the removed holder, who reads post-removal content from an honest node keeping S unread (it holds the current sync key). It costs nothing: the remover had to rotate before encrypting the removal commit anyway. The inbox copy to the removed holder and the certificate copies carry the grant delta only. Cascade rotations of other things stay lazy; they exclude nobody directly. A later rotation by a node that knows the removal carries no removal; establishment by names covers it (notes/keys-establishment.md).

## Re-seals
- **Re-seal is a SHOULD with a trigger** (a current holder without a box at a current epoch, or a stranded report): without the trigger a grant raced by a rotation was a silent permanent lockout. A missing box marking a deliberate exclusion looks exactly like one marking an unknown grant, hence the guards: a re-seal decision comes at the end of a complete pass (every source's node log read to its end: cheap to know, and what makes the post-compaction case sound), only at established epochs, and only to current holders. A re-sealer makes the rotations and finalizes it may first, re-seals even while a departure stays uncovered (notes/keys.md, The three promises), and boxes nothing to a holder whose grant it may finalize. A late joiner holding only a snapshot rotates instead (a rotation boxes everyone it thinks current). The remover logs the rotation before registering the step (notes/sync-access.md), so a holder listed in the step never strands merely because the commit is not there yet.
- Residue (notes/sync-access.md Re-seal): after compaction a node's view of the roles no longer travels with the epoch chain, so a node boxing a holder at a snapshot-established epoch from an incomplete view (a source that never got the removal, an interrupted first sync followed by an eager rotation) can box a removed holder; healed by the next rotation of a node that knows.
- **Re-seal invariant** (`reseal_candidates`): once re-seals settle, every node counts the same current epochs and every holder with key access holds every current epoch any node holds; once history adds settle too, every holder of a current epoch holds every earlier superseded established secret any of its holders holds (both checked by `rotation::tests_property`). A re-seal is made only if it adds a box the trigger asks for (`forked_role_reseal_reaches_fixpoint_spec_permissions_3`; without that, churn looped); requiring every fork's secret of a re-sealer stranded a holder when no node held them all. The trigger's re-seal boxes only the epochs the holder lacks a box at (`osis_keys::reseal_holder`): it rewrites no box another holder may hold differently; `reshare` (stranded, by hand) rewrites every current box.
- **Orphaned epochs**: a current epoch whose secret a rotator lacks and that boxes no holder that may rotate its role (its maker since lowered to read) is orphaned: no node can re-seal a holder it left out, so it is due a rotation, and its holders add it to the next epoch's history (`orphaned_epoch_rotated_spec_crypto_4`). Judged from the boxes held, it is not fooled by a rotation arriving without its boxes: an epoch is established only with its rotation's `secrets.0` delta, which boxes the rotator. So an epoch is orphaned only once every may-rotate holder boxed at it is lowered (an admin's act, or a self-demotion), and each such rotation's epoch boxes its own maker: a re-seal box not yet arrived costs at most one extra rotation, never a loop a peer or member can drive. Rejected: judging by the maker alone (`epoch_intro` author): unknown for a snapshot's epoch, and rotates where another boxed rotator could re-seal.
