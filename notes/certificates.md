# Certificates
> Summary: Why signed role commits are the certificates (spec/permissions.md §4), what they leak, why a copy counts per sync ID, current `keys.0` and its measured cost, chained writes (what a chain certificate holds) and the rejected alternatives.

Principle: **signed role commits are the certificates** and travel under the sync IDs where they are needed: checking a write on N needs the role grants of N's record chain and of the author's own actors, which only holders can read. No verify role, no certificate format, no extra key.

## What travels
- Leaks: a reader of L learns, for every author under L, every path it ever had to the things it writes there (every chained write between things on those paths, applied or not, each `created.0` record and hence that thing's dedup secret), never whole rosters. Author identity is inherent anyway.
- **Per sync ID, not per node**: a certificate delta already under the sync ID counts whoever logged it, so a joiner copies only its own chain (11.5 KB per sync ID in a chat) and holders do not all re-copy a rotated actor's new `keys.0`. Sound because a copy counts only once this node checked every delta of it against the header's delta hash (opening checks the prefix only, so before the check anyone able to deposit under the sync ID could post the header with garbage deltas and stop the real copy; the same check guards `carried_by` and the duplicate and strict-subset judgements), only while held in the node's own store and at every source of the sync ID (spec/permissions.md §4), and, for a rotation commit of the sync ID's actor, only under the newest epoch. Older epochs otherwise count: a newcomer gets the history boxes with its grant and opens older copies like older content; counting only newest-epoch copies made every holder re-copy everyone's chain after each rotation (40 KB per holder per chat removal). Residue: a holder of a link key alone (newest secret, no history box) reads none of the older entries. Deletion power is the same either way (a copy commits to its commit's delete keys).
- Deltas the sync ID's bodies carry (closure things its actor reads) are never copied; a chain's deltas on closure things it may not read are. Another node's demotion or `keys.0` may be left to its author for `SETTLE_MS` (30 s) after this node received it (the remover must log them): otherwise every holder learning of a rotation before the remover's copy arrived copies it (3.3 KB per holder per removal); measured from receipt, not the commit's `ts`, which its author sets. Not built: issues/sync-unbuilt.md (facade).
- A removal of G's grant goes under G's sync IDs because the target may have left G's closure, the removal ends G's reading of it, and G's inbox may reach only G's admins.
- The revokes logged under an uncovered epoch (spec/permissions.md §4) let A's rotators learn of the departure. The departed party can read them: those actors' new public keys and box keys, M's `roles.0` deltas and the writes' authors' chain certificates, not the boxes.
- **Current `keys.0`** (`Engine::current_keys`): an actor's newest `keys.0` commit may be an inbox or deposit seed write, so certificates carry that and the rotation commits of its newest keyset per role. A keyset then often reaches a node before any secret of its actor: `Core::unboxed_epochs` gives its first admin's nodes the derived read epochs, reading only, so they still open the actor's sync ID. Cost: one more commit per actor whose newest `keys.0` carries no keyset. Joiner's bytes down, measured on a chat: 100 members syncing with one peer, about 2.0 MB (the extra commits add 0.2%); 30 members each having added a saved peer, 795 KB, of which the extra commits are 139 KB (21%).
- **Kept while needed** (spec/commits.md §5 Forgetting, What a held thing needs; `retention/needed.rs`): a chain a node took (records, `keys.0` commits, rotations, an inbox's record) stays while a held thing needs it and goes whole, as never received. No count of its own: the needs are references (strong, weak or ticket) in the durable state of a thing in the node closure (`refs::to`; a thing kept only as a certificate needs nothing by what its state names, so needs stay bounded by what the node holds), an account's inbox (its `keys.0`), devices actor and profile, `Held::named_on` (records naming it first admin, edge writes naming it holder), the held commits it authored (`Indexes::by_epoch`) or acted in (`by_actor`), header alone included, and the pending commits it signed or whose record or chain they await (`Indexes::pend_by`, `pend_on`), by the things they write on, and a held commit on it with a part missing, by the things those parts are on (`Meta::missing_parts`; what that part brings may reference it, so a commit received in part keeps the things it makes until it is whole; a part stopping missing notes the commit's things, `Core::note_on`). **Dropped when the pass that brought it ends**: what arrived (a record learned, a record awaited) or lost a need (a reference row gone, a signed commit moved or left pending, a holder unnamed) is noted in `Core::pass_due` and judged by `Engine::settle_needs` (sync: each complete pass's end, `status::live`; tests: `LocalPeer::sync`, `sync_all`, `spread`, the order harness's delivery end; the first call after an open judges every held and awaited record); a release judges what it released in its own transaction. So what a node holds outside its closure at quiet is a function of what it holds, never of arrival order. Not per transaction: an ancestor's record (another account's root, a chain) arrives before what needs it, and judging each transaction dropped it at once and forced a refetch into every one-shot delivery. The judgement walks needs up to the first rooted thing, O(1) while needed, brute force over the unrooted component when not (cycles of holder actors go together). A pending commit wholly on things that go leaves with them (`forget`). Keeping an account on purpose is a reference: a contact or a peer to grant is a weak from the account root's `holds.0` (the harness's `Peer::know`; `LocalPeer::of`, `sync`, `sync_all` and `flows::spread` introduce their peers); a node being paired keeps the account it joins as its own (`Engine::joining`, in memory); a followed link keeps its K. A stranger's chain goes with its last fire (a fire's references count only while it is held); a co-admin's with the last held thing it wrote, holds a role on or a held thing references.
- Accepted: revocation reach is eventual (L's readers accept a revoked node until some holder of the revoking actor syncs L); chain depth equals nesting depth, at most `MAX_CHAIN_DEPTH` (64) record links (deeper nesting would make every standing query cost more).
- Rejected: a transitive "verify" role (outside collaborators learn full rosters; every rotation re-wraps to everyone below); per-commit certificate chains (a few hundred bytes per commit against a 300-byte floor).

## Chained writes
- **Chain certificates** (what a demotion frontier lists; spec/permissions.md §4): for (n, t), the things on any path that ever existed from n to t over chained writes (record edges included) and every chained write between two of them, applied or not, closed under authors.
- **Chained** (`cert::CertIndex::of`): one pass in timeline order; a write is chained when its author could have had the authority it needs (admin or invite on the target, admin on the holder, write for a removal) over the edges chained writes of earlier commits set, at their highest role and any invite, never removed, `until` disregarded. Every applied write is chained, so it is a superset of every walk's paths; O(edge writes × a search from a target) (`scale::chained_writes_found_linear_in_edge_writes_spec_permissions_4`). It reads the held set alone, never the walk, trust set or arrival order, and only grows, so honest nodes name the same chains whatever side of a fork they take, and a write never leaves one. A forger (a writer granting a private actor Y on its thing, a stranger) chains nothing, so no honest node logs Y's roster for it. A removed admin's later writes stay chained (a node siding with it applies them, and it could have backdated them).
- Point-free, so it holds whatever any walk had at any point: a verifier must see every path an earlier walk had (drops are sticky: a write it audited without dropping stays), every demotion of the node on a path gained after its write, and an edge the node left itself. A dropped demotion is kept because it can still have dropped a write on a projection.
- Rejected: the walk's applied writes (node-dependent through trust sets, shrinks as audits drop writes); `Held::ever_admins` (named by any holder: misses an invite holder through a holder actor); authority at any time, a least fixpoint by passes (no walk applies a write ordered before its authority; a chain of keys each backdated before its own grant cost a pass per write, cubic: 1.7·10^8 search steps at 1000 writes); certificates by the roles at a point, closure at a point with later-demotion rounds (they reveal about as much, and each round needed another failing seed and cost 0.2 s at 10^4).
- Projection oracles check applied, dropped, the author's standing per write and each node's role on each thing at every write's point from its own certificates against the full evaluation, sticky residue skipped.
