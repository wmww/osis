# Coordination
> Summary: How a coordinator session runs osis work: one opus (or, for gnarly or already-failed problems, fable) worker per task in a worktree, then a fresh opus reviewer who gates; fast-forward main; at most three agents; what goes in a brief; the periodic checks and their cadence; the watchdog that reviews how the session went; the complaints directory agents write to; two-box rules.

The build and test mechanics are in notes/build.md; this note is the process around them. Starting a session: read notes/handoff.md first when it exists (what the last coordinator left in flight); delete it once its items are taken. What is in flight is the local task branches (`git branch`; never pushed) and their worktrees; a coordinator ending a session with work open writes notes/handoff.md. Merges need no progress record: git history has them.

## The task loop
- `wt claim <branch>` for an opus worker; then a fresh opus reviewer on the same worktree: correctness review of the diff, targeted differentials for the invariant the branch changed, small fixes committed, `git rebase main` then `tools/gate.sh`. Then the coordinator fast-forwards main from the local branch (`git merge --ff-only`, push main, `git branch -d`; task branches are never pushed or fetched: worktrees share the repo's branches), then briefs the next task.
- `wt release` is the agent's very last step, after its last commit: a released worktree is free for the next claim, and a second agent there commits onto the wrong HEAD.
- At most three agents at once, workers and reviewers counted together; two while the box runs at load above about 20 (waiting on builds is the main cost: at load, gates took 440-594 s against 162-171 s quiet, a third of that rebuilding after a rebase that brought osis-crdt/-store/-base changes). Keep two worktrees warm (`wt trim 0`/`1` after a release makes the next claim cold).
- Re-review: a reviewer change bigger than a small fix gets its own fresh reviewer before merge (an unreviewed reviewer rewrite once produced four bugs the next periodic run found).
- Third round rule: a branch whose second review changes the rule, not a line, goes back to the coordinator for a simpler rule before a third reviewer (branches that kept patching a rule spent four rounds on rules later cut).
- Re-gate the rebased branch before the fast-forward, not a checkout at main's tip (there HEAD equals the merge base, so the gate runs everything, kit and app included). A re-gate after a rebase that brought other branches' code costs the same as the reviewer's (the sweep is chosen by the branch's own diff); skip it only when what came in is notes-only or in crates the branch's crates do not depend on.
- A background gate wrapper ends with the gate's code: `tools/gate.sh > $L 2>&1; e=$?; echo gate=$e >> $L; exit $e`, and the log's `== gate passed` is read before merging (`...; echo gate=$?` exits 0, and failed gates were pushed that way).
- A cargo run stopped partway (TaskStop, a killed gate) can leave a crate's incremental cache unable to link (`rust-lld: undefined hidden symbol anon.*`): prefer letting a stale gate finish; else `cargo clean -p <crate>` for the crate that fails to link, not the whole target.
- Hung agents: an agent quiet far past its task's usual length is asked (SendMessage) or stopped. Seen: agents sat for hours on a permission prompt (a foreground `rm` in the scratchpad), and one ended its turn waiting on a `Monitor` whose last event never woke it.
- One intricate incremental change (fast path, window, cache with a from-scratch fallback) per area in flight at a time.
- Seed chases: a second seed-found fix in one area stops patching; the next task is a study of the class (what rule is missing or wrong). Every chase so far ended with such a study after 10-30 patches (notes/testing.md (Arrival-order differential), notes/crypto.md (Frozen coverage)).
- Simplification pass (Tess, standing): about every six merges, one behaviour-preserving simplification/refactor pass on the area that changed most since the last, with its own reviewer; it counts as a task.

## Standing rules (Tess)
- Simplicity: keep implementations simple; when a subtle aspect of a design adds a lot of complexity or bug surface, look for a way to avoid it, and in extreme cases bring it to Tess as a possible requirements compromise. Say so in design briefs.
- Cut tentacles (Tess): when an edge case has grown a wing of fixes, extensions and their own bugs, replace it with the rule that actually works, or a stated limit, or bring it to Tess as a requirements compromise. Cut cruft freely: slow or badly designed tests, notes describing how things aren't, issues stating unfixable facts (e.g. no recovery of intentionally permanent deletes). Tess is often away: don't block on her; take the reversible option and note the question.
- Options keep their property (Tess): where users choose between options with different trade-offs (keep history forever, drop it when convenient, purge immediately), each option keeps its defining property and pays its own cost, stated. Never quietly blend one toward another to dodge its cost ("gone once replaced" became "within an hour" that way). If an option's honest cost is too high, ask Tess whether to offer it.
- No typical use case (Tess): examples are not workloads. Nobody knows how osis will be used (a few-page document in a text aspect is one thing people *could* do, not the typical aspect). Don't design or tune for an assumed common case at the expense of others; aim for consistent, predictable properties across a wide range of sizes and edit rates, and state costs in those terms. Worst case so far: "an always-on server holding encrypted backups" grew into separate node types and the whole host concept, still being removed (issues/node-host-unify.md).
- Performance is the coordinator's: "a little slower than optimal but much simpler, more correct or more verifiable is a good trade". Budgets: notes/testing.md (Performance budgets). A new incremental path needs a measured miss of a budget in a realistic scenario; simple from-scratch code that meets the budget wins.
- Growth: a change that adds a new kind of permanent data (a row, record, table entry or synced field never dropped) adds or updates a row in notes/growth.md; reviewers check.
- Hostile-only shapes inside data the writer may write (notes/core-triage.md §1 D): very low priority; fix only when easy, with no added complexity.
- App work (libs/kit/, app/) is planned with Tess in a separate UX session (notes/app.md) and counts toward the agent limit. When no app session is active, the core coordinator fixes app breakage (failing app tests, API adoption) itself. Whatever kit/app needs from core becomes an issue tagged `core-ask` (plus the component, and `tess-call` if the fix is not obvious); app work uses a workaround meanwhile and never edits core.
- Names: each coordinator picks its own name and pronouns once it knows the codebase (check the registry for collisions), records both here and says them in chat. Sub-agents are `<coordinator>-<branch>` (e.g. `lodestar-grant-keyset`), stated in the brief's first line and used in reports and complaints.
- Registry: **Lodestar**, she/her. **Tiller**, he/him. **Wren**, they/them. **Sorrel**, they/them. **Fathom**, they/them.

## Test budget
Seed breadth belongs to the periodic checks, not to branches (Tess: cut testing time without letting issues slip). Of about 200 merged tasks' bugs, reading and new targeted tests found about 225, per-branch oracles about 25, periodic and wide runs about 10, rerunning already-run ranges about 2 (notes/testing.md (What finds bugs)).
- Worker: tier 1, clippy, its own tests, the task's repro, a quick targeted check of what it changed, and the instant checks (`python3 tools/policy.py && python3 tools/spec_map.py --uncited && python3 tools/spec_map.py --gaps`: uncited tests, MUSTs without a RULES anchor and unlisted `OSIS_*` variables are what failed reviewer gates). Then commit; no gate (the reviewer gates after rebasing; worker gates cost hours of agent wall and load everyone else's).
- Reviewer: the per-branch oracles of notes/testing.md for the area the branch touched, at plain sizes (a tenth of full, `osis_base::testenv::large`; never `OSIS_FULL=1` per branch) on a fresh offset, plus any differential it writes, then the gate. The gate is its only full tier-1/clippy run.
- Never periodic-sized ranges in a branch (300/200/1000/3000 seeds cost 30-60 min per agent and duplicate the next periodic run; one `_large` harness at 300 seeds was 19,450 CPU-s). Before briefing, add up the oracle set's CPU-s (testing.md costs) and keep it at default sizes. A branch that changes an invariant the per-branch set does not reach gets one targeted larger run, saying why in the brief.
- A branch that adds or rewrites a randomized harness runs it once at `OSIS_FULL=1` before merge (plain sizes have passed while full size failed 4.6% of seeds).
- One all-core test run (oracle, sweep, tier-2 set) at a time per box: three at once ran each at a third of the speed.
- Reviews are where bugs are found, through targeted differentials and reading, not re-running default ranges. Keep the reviewer brief on the invariant the branch touched, and ask for a differential test of it.

## Briefs
Standard footer: CLAUDE.md, notes/build.md, `claudewait`, no wait loops, commit, `wt release`, report.
- Subagent shells lack the plugins' `bin` on PATH: briefs start with `export PATH=/home/ai/.claude/plugins/cache/wmww-agent-skills/reasonable-rust-builds/0.5.1/bin:/home/ai/.claude/plugins/cache/wmww-agent-skills/claudewait/0.1.0/bin:$PATH`.
- Say `wt claim <branch>` prints the worktree; never predict which one.
- Name the agent's own scratch dir (`<scratchpad>/<branch>-<random>`) and say never `rm` in the scratchpad (a foreground `rm` there hangs on a permission prompt; use python `shutil` or leave files). Where /tmp is tmpfs (`findmnt -T /tmp`; on ostrich it is the root disk) scratch binaries are RAM: many copies overwrite one name.
- A worker brief names no per-branch oracle the reviewer will run, unless that oracle is the task's repro; then the worker runs that one test at its default size on one fresh offset, and the reviewer brief says to skip it.
- Put the reviewer's "check these cases" questions in the worker brief too (a case asked only of the reviewer cost a second round). Say "do not loosen an existing test: report it".
- Security or convergence fix: the worker writes the invariant the fix relies on as a code comment, plus a randomized differential (or an extended oracle) that fails with the fix disabled; the reviewer attacks that argument. Every new test is checked to fail with its fix disabled.
- Ask the simplicity question in the worker brief ("is there a rule with no points/windows/caches that meets the budget?"), not first in review.
- Say "first re-run the issue's repro on current main" (issue seeds go stale).
- Name sections of the big notes, not whole files (testing.md, permissions.md, ephemeral.md, sync.md are each around 100 KB or more).
- Grep the rule's words before citing a spec section or env var (a wrong env var name silently runs the default seeds).
- A brief asking for TLA results names the instances (`run.sh all` and the C3 instances are periodic-sized: an hour each).
- The three git footguns: squash with `git reset --soft $(git merge-base HEAD main)`, never `main`; wip-commit before a fix-disabled probe, so the `git checkout <file>` that undoes it loses nothing; `git fetch && git rebase origin/main` (local main lags when the other box merges), without a pipe, and read its result before gating (gates have passed on half-rebased trees; tools/gate.sh does not detect a rebase in progress).
- Command rules agents broke most (all in notes/build.md, repeated in briefs): never bound tier 1, the gate or an oracle with `timeout` (it kills tier 1 with a green-looking log): `run_in_background`, then one `claudewait` per call. Edit and build in separate calls. Narrow node runs with `-p osis-node --test it -- <filters>`, never `--workspace --test it` (a `0 passed` line per crate hides the one that ran) and never two filters before `--`. Use the task ID the tool printed, never `ls -t` on the shared tasks dir. Never `pkill -f`/`pgrep -f` (it kills the caller's own shell): TaskStop. The reviewer commits before starting oracles and runs them from a copied binary (agents edited sources under running builds). Deep proptest runs pass `PROPTEST_CASES=N` (`.cargo/config.toml` sets 32, which overrides a test's own `cases:`). `--ignored --exact` on a tier-1 env-ranged test (walks, deaths, undoexp) runs nothing and says ok: run those without `--ignored`.
- Networking per-branch check: `cargo test-net -- --ignored driver:: runtime:: quic relay lan` (unfiltered it runs all of tier 2 incl. timing benches).
- A reviewer who needs main's behaviour for comparison gets main's test binary with `tools/bin-at main <out>` (notes/build.md) and runs old and new side by side; under load compare CPU seconds, not wall time.

## Periodic checks
The seed-breadth runs that catch what the default ranges never will. Draw every offset (`--from`, `_START`, `OSIS_*_SEED`) at random below 10^12 (`python3 -c 'import random; print(random.randrange(10**12))'`), so ranges never repeat without a log; reviewers' fresh offsets the same way. After a run, update the last-run line below and the counts in notes/testing.md (What finds bugs); file an issue per new failure.
- Last full run: main 1fd92068 (sim sweep, order alike and exhaustive, perm, crdt, walks, TLC small, whole tier-2 set at `OSIS_FULL=1`); dozens of merges since, so the next one is due.
- `cargo run --bin sim-sweep -- --seeds 150 --from <F> --budget-secs 3600` (46 scenarios; at load 30-45 it reached 78 of 150 seeds in 3600 s). A sweep cut by its budget says so only in its per-scenario seed counts: log the seeds that ran.
- `OSIS_FULL=1` on every tier-2 test named below that takes its plain default.
- `OSIS_ORDER_START=<S> OSIS_ORDER_SEEDS=300 cargo test -p osis-node --test it -- --ignored order::arrival_order_alike_large_spec_permissions_5_2` (2 min on 16 threads), and `OSIS_ORDER_SEEDS=200` for `order::arrival_order_exhaustive_large_spec_crdt_4_8` (13 min).
- `OSIS_PERM_SEED=<P> cargo test -p osis-perm` (only osis-perm reads it) and `OSIS_PERM_SEED=<P> cargo test -p osis-perm --test it -- --ignored differential:: queries:: incremental::` (2.4 min).
- `OSIS_CRDT_SEED=<C> cargo test -p osis-crdt -- --include-ignored --skip repro_ --skip budget --skip bench:: --skip scale:: --skip hostile_moves --skip shrink_script` (the skipped timing tests run 30+ min in debug).
- `OSIS_WALKS_START=<W> OSIS_WALKS_SEEDS=1000 cargo test -p osis-node --test it -- --ignored --exact walks::open_equals_rebuild_random_histories_large` (1 min on 16 threads; every seed-ranged harness has a `_large` twin, notes/testing.md Seed runners).
- `core/test/tla/run.sh small`.

How:
- Harness runs with an overridden range print it even when passing (notes/build.md); check that line, not only `1 passed`.
- Run the whole script under `nice -n 19`: at normal priority it held the box at load 25-35 for 2 h and every gate in that window took 250-530 s instead of 80-150. At nice 19 beside load 30-50 a harness can run 30x slower than its CPU-s say, so judge a slow item by its CPU-s (`/usr/bin/time`), not wall time, before suspecting a regression. nice does not shield a laptop: a niced run still takes SMT siblings and the shared power budget.
- Scope: each check belongs to a component (today all core). Run a component's checks only after significant work on it; the same holds for the dev loop (working on one component must not pay for others). A new component gets its own per-branch oracles and periodic checks, and tools/gate.sh selects by what the branch touched.
- When: in the background, in a spare worktree, with at least five times the last run's wall time of coordinator work between one run's end and the next's start (at most a sixth of the clock), never a fixed hour or merge count; whenever about six branches merged or when idle, and only while at most one agent is building or testing. If they grow, first make them faster without losing coverage (the order harness went from 12 min to under 2 with a thread per core), then run them less often. Before a release, run them at full length.

## The watchdog
When the periodic checks finish, spawn one opus agent with this brief, and act on its report (fix tooling or notes yourself, file issues, or brief a worker): "Review how this coordinator session's agents have been working, not what they built. Run `tools/agent-times.py --long 3` (defaults to the session that last ran agents; `--since` the last watchdog run) and read the gate logs the coordinator kept. For each agent: was its wall time reasonable for its task, how much was model time against waiting on builds, tests and benches, did anything hit the 180 s subagent cap repeatedly, time out, or write a wait loop; did any build or test take far longer than notes/build.md's expected costs (3x at low load is a bug: find out why rather than blame load); did agents run checks that are not on the per-branch list (notes/testing.md); did any agent, gate run or periodic check pay for a component nobody was working on (app tests during core work, core sweeps after app-only work, a whole-workspace run where a component's own would do): that shape of waste is a standing thing to look for; did reviews find bugs and what kind of test found them. Check disk: `du -sh .worktrees/*/target` and `df -h .`; a warm worktree target settles at 11-12 GB by `du` (notes/build.md); one well above that holds stacked artifact generations (after a profile, layout or toolchain change: delete it and rebuild) or a stray artifact set (`-p`, `--features`, env flags: find the command in the transcripts); more worktrees than agents means `wt trim` was skipped; also look for stray `target/` dirs outside the worktrees (`find ~ -maxdepth 4 -name CACHEDIR.TAG -path '*target*'`) and scratch dirs over a GB. Read every file in notes/complaints/ and the worker and reviewer reports the coordinator appends to this brief. Verify every claim about a file or a tool against the current main checkout before reporting it. Then report: what is fine, what cost time for a fixable reason with the evidence (which agent, which command, how long, why), patterns across complaints worth fixing, and complaints not worth fixing (delete those files; fold the worth-fixing ones into your report and delete them too, so the directory holds only what the coordinator has not yet seen). Do not change code; notes and the complaints directory only. Commit and release your worktree if you used one."

## Complaints
`notes/complaints/<branch>.md`: low-priority workflow mishaps, mistakes, footguns and slow spots an agent met, a few lines, appended during the task and committed with the branch. Anything that blocked it, a tool that gave a wrong answer, or a check far over its documented cost goes in the report too. The watchdog reads the directory, keeps the patterns and deletes what it consumed; with two coordinators at once, each watchdog deletes only its own session's files (commit author and offset tell them apart).

## Two coordinators at once
One coordinator by default. When two run at once (each on its own machine): neither is in charge (Tess); they talk over the osis channel (notes/dogfood.md) and split by issue area (Tags), anything unclaimed going to whoever claims first. Protocol: post `claim: <issue> (<branch>)` before `wt claim` and `merged: <sha> <branch>` after each push; `git pull --rebase` before every push; in shared notes add your own section or row, never rewrite the other's; relay Tess's decisions verbatim with `tess:`; a pinned-binary bump is `bump? <sha>`, answered `bump ok`, before either side rebuilds. Each runs the periodic checks for its own areas.
