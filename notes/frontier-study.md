# Frontier study: what a demotion's frontier leaves out
> Summary: Recommended rule for spec/permissions.md §5.1 under the owner's requirement that a kick never scales with history: writes are split by retention into **permanent** deltas (keep-at-least forever, no keep-at-most, no expiry; their headers are never dropped, purge residues included) and the rest. For permanent deltas the frontier lists one head per (aspect, demoted device) and coverage follows a chain of per-session predecessor links the author writes in its own headers; for the rest it lists commit IDs, leaving out exactly what the demoter's own compaction would drop now. Kick cost: one entry per permanent aspect the device wrote, plus the uncompacted tail and the keep-at-least window of the others. Closes frontier-clobbers-history and item 219's carry-overs; keeps writer-rotates-to-itself recoverable by sanctioning a deliberate omission. Per-session hash chains as proposed (one chain per session across all aspects) are rejected: unwalkable across labels.

Reading only; nothing run. Code references: `Engine::frontier` and `covered_here` (core/engine/src/apply/receipt.rs), `drop_covered` (core/node/src/sync/local.rs), `chunk_frontiers` (core/engine/src/engine/large.rs).

## 1. The problem
Today's MAY (§5.1) lets A leave out any commit a valid snapshot covers. A snapshot carries live content only, so the covered commit's superseded writes turn invalid for good, whatever their retention; any writer can write that snapshot just before a kick. The covered filter was also the only thing keeping frontiers small. Requirements to meet together: no clobbering of kept history; a kick's cost bounded independently of history (owner, issues/frontier-clobbers-history.md); validity a function of the held set; a removed writer cannot forge into counters it once used (owner decision (a), round 13); the author's own `ts` never decides; new devices never fetch compacted headers and nodes forget them (session-sources rules 8, 9).

## 2. Why no compact frontier exists for compacting aspects, and why one does for permanent ones
A frontier is a compact encoding of "the set of X's writes A had received". Compact means a bound per source (counters or chain heads), and a bound is safe only if every commit under it stays detectable: a forged commit re-signed on counters whose original every node has forgotten is indistinguishable from the original. Headers are forgotten exactly where bodies are dropped: compaction (covered), expiry, keep-at-most, and purge residues dropped as covered. So:
- Where bodies are dropped, the only unforgeable encoding is the commit-ID list, and the honest list is the uncompacted tail (plus the keep-at-least window): bounded by compaction cadence and retention, not by history. The demoter can compact first (session-sources step 3) to make it about empty.
- Where bodies are never dropped (keep-at-least forever, no keep-at-most, no expiry: the "history is the data" class, design E), every header is held by every node forever. A re-signed commit on used counters meets its original and both void (spec/commits.md §4, hurting only the forger), so bounds are safe there. The one leak is purge: a purged delta leaves a header-only blob that is covered and may be dropped; the rule below keeps it.

So the retention class decides the encoding, and the class is on the wire per delta (spec/commits.md §5), judged alike everywhere.

## 3. Recommended rule
**Permanent delta**: retention keep-at-least `abs` forever, no keep-at-most, no `expires`. Role-class deltas are never permanent (fixed retention). A header-only blob left by purging permanent deltas is not covered: it stays must-hold wherever those deltas were (it is design E's tombstone anyway).

**Predecessor link** (header `Aspect`, new optional field `prev`): the first delta of a session on aspect `a` in session form names its predecessors: the newest commit the device holds on `a` from each of its own earlier sessions on `a` that is not an ancestor, through such links, of another it names (usually one; several after a restore before syncing). Any other delta carries none. A writer rule; a receiver checks only that each named commit is by the same author on `a`. For session S on `a`, S's **bound given by** a link naming commit p in S is p's `hi` on `a`.

**Frontier** (§5.1, replacing the "A MAY leave out" sentence; the role and chain-certificate IDs stay as written):
> For each aspect in question and each demoted device X, the frontier carries either X's commit IDs or a **head**. By ID: the IDs of X's commits with a delta on the aspect that A had received as of T, except that A MAY leave out a commit when, for every delta its header lists, A holds the delta or dropped it as covered, and each delta on an aspect in question is one A's compaction would drop now or already dropped as covered (spec/commits.md §5: covered by a snapshot A holds valid and had received as of T, for a dropped delta every cover on the way; past its keep-at-least bound as A dates it; purgeable by A). By head, only when every delta of X on the aspect A had received is permanent: `(tid, ak, session nonce, ctr)` for each session of X on the aspect whose newest received commit no received link of X reaches, `ctr` the greatest counter A had received in it. A write of X on the aspect is **covered** iff its commit's ID is listed, or its session is a listed head's with its counters at or below `ctr`, or its session is reached from a listed head through held links with its counters at or below the bound the reaching link gives. A MAY also leave out a received commit, or list a lower head, on purpose: the frontier is A's word, receivers judge by it alone, and such an omission is an admin's repudiation of those writes (the lockout recovery of §3 needs it), exposed by a client only as an explicit act. A lists no ephemeral commit.

Short form: **the frontier omits what compaction could erase anyway, and names history that can never be erased by one head per aspect and device.**

Companion edits: §3 names the lockout recovery (remove or demote the rotator with a frontier that leaves its rotation out, rotate from the epochs held); spec/commits.md §5 the permanent class and its uncovered purge residue; spec/wire.md §4 `Aspect.prev`; session-sources rule 7 and rule 8 (a permanent delta's header is never forgotten); notes/growth.md's frontier row (bounded by permanent aspects written and the compaction tail).

## 4. Why it holds
- **No clobbering.** A kept body is either permanent (covered by head and chain, whole, dead writes included) or inside its keep-at-least window (listed by ID). An omitted ID is a body every right holder may drop now: its live writes are in the covering snapshot (vouched, §5.2), its dead writes are what compaction deletes.
- **No forgery.** A fresh commit after T: by ID, unlisted; by head, either a new session (no link from a received commit reaches it; links are in X's own signed headers, fixed before T) or counters above a bound. A re-signed commit on used counters of a permanent delta meets the original's header (never dropped, purge residue kept) and both void. Counters of a non-permanent delta are never under a head (writer rule; a mixed aspect falls back to IDs).
- **New devices read alike.** On permanent aspects they fetch every header (nothing is compacted away); elsewhere coverage is by ID. Links name commits on the same aspect and thing, so they never cross labels; a session's first commit on `a` is found by `lo = 1`.
- **Deterministic.** Coverage is a function of held headers and the frontier; a link whose target is not yet held leaves the write uncovered until it arrives (then re-judged, like any late arrival). "Droppable now" uses A's own dates and only decides what A lists.
- **Restore and clones.** A restored device that writes before syncing links to a stale predecessor; the lost session's later commits are unreached until its next session links them (it then holds them); a kick in between loses them, the existing restore-before-sync hazard. Two live copies are two sessions, each linked at their next starts. A session link to an earlier session of another device is bad input.
- **Role layer unchanged.** Role commits are commit form, listed by ID, kept `ROLE_KEEP`; "list unless droppable" lists every rotation within 90 days and the role snapshot establishes epochs after. Item 219's carry-overs dissolve: role commits follow the same rule, and a commit with a delta A does not hold is listed.
- **Hostile admin lists nothing**: stated, not hidden; out of scope by requirements. Bodies stay must-hold, re-grant revives; a history read may show held invalid bodies as disputed (API).

## 5. Costs
- **Kick**: per demoted device, about 60 B per permanent aspect it wrote (one head; two after an unhealed restore), plus 32 B per uncompacted or keep-at-least-window commit on other aspects, plus the role and chain-certificate IDs. Design E: a few heads per kicked member (one message aspect per year), independent of messages. Device removal: one head per permanent aspect the device wrote. The Cost goal (spec/requirements.md: one entry per aspect it wrote) holds again.
- **Header**: `prev` on the first delta of a session per aspect: 33 B per (aspect, session), nothing on other commits.
- **Storage**: permanent deltas' headers were already kept with their bodies; new is the purge residue (header-only blob, about 300 B) of a purged permanent delta, kept forever.
- **CPU**: building the frontier is one droppability check per held non-permanent commit of X in scope (the `drop_covered` predicate) and one scan of X's sessions per permanent aspect; judging a write on a permanent aspect walks the session chain once per (aspect, device, frontier), cached as session -> bound, O(sessions) header lookups; by ID one set lookup.
- **Caps**: with heads, frontiers stop growing with history, so `MAX_LIST_BYTES` (8 MiB) and `MAX_LINKED_FRONTIERS` (64 MiB) stay resource bounds that honest use does not approach; no change. Issues/frontier-certificate-cost.md shrinks to the non-permanent tails and may close after a measurement.

## 6. Rejected candidates
- **Per-session chain, one across all aspects** (devbox): a session spans every thing the device writes; a verifier under label L sees only headers touching L's closure, so the chain from the head back to a commit passes commits it cannot see. Unwalkable regardless of forgetting.
- **Per-(aspect, session) counter bounds without links**: sound for permanent deltas with no header change, but one entry per (aspect, app launch that wrote), which for one message per launch is one per message. Kept as the simpler fallback if the owner accepts launches as the unit (see §8).
- **Chains or bounds on compacting aspects**: forgetting and new devices leave gaps wherever a later session's commits were dropped while an earlier one is kept (map and set aspects under relative keep-at-least, expiring deltas, purge residues); an honest write then reads as uncovered, which is the same clobbering narrowed. Closing the gaps needs drops in chain order plus exclusions: three rules for a class whose honest list is already bounded by cadence.
- **`ts` against the cutoff with sessions bounding the lie**: a session gives counter order, not time; a removed device appends to its last received session with any `ts` up to T. Adds nothing over counters; the owner rejected `ts` rules.
- **Per-aspect summaries other than bounds** (set hashes, accumulators): a verifier needs a membership proof per commit, which the kick would have to produce per surviving write.
- **Node-form counters for permanent aspects** (continuous across sessions, max read from held headers): reintroduces the `Node` source kind step 4 deletes, and a restore before sync voids both the old and the new writes instead of orphaning until healed.
- **Listing every kept write by ID** (the first version of this study): correct and simple, 32 B per kept commit, about 6% of what kept history costs its holders, but a kick then scales with history and the frontier caps fail the 0/infinity test. Rejected by the owner.

## 7. What stays open
- Double-removal residual (round 13, accepted): c left out because Y's snapshot Z covered it; Y later removed with a frontier not listing Z; c's live writes go even where c is held. Narrower now (c had to be droppable).
- Loss of writes the revoker never received (pre-sync, hole 19): unchanged.
- issues/writer-rotates-to-itself.md: the writer-side flow still refuses to stamp without a current secret; after the omission the previous epoch is current again at the demoter, so the flow must judge under the new frontier before choosing epochs to rotate from.
- issues/equivocation-after-snapshot.md: an un-merge bug, untouched by any frontier rule.
- Mixed aspects (some writes permanent, some not, per-write override): fall back to IDs; a schema may want to forbid the mix.

## 8. Build steps
Prerequisite: session-sources step 2 (session form for durable content), since links are per session. Independent of steps 4, 5, 6; step 6 gains the "never forget a permanent delta's header" clause.
- **F1 Spec and notes**: §3 recovery sentence; §5.1 as in §3 above; commits.md §5 permanent class and purge residue; wire.md §4 `prev`; crdt.md §8 unchanged; notes/permissions.md Revocation; session-sources rules 7, 8, step 3; growth row.
- **F2 ID part**: `covered_here` becomes `droppable_here`, sharing `drop_covered`'s bound and right predicate (`body_keep(..).at_least + MAX_DRIFT_MS <= now`, `may_purge`) so compaction and the frontier cannot disagree; a commit with a delta on a thing this node does not hold is listed. Tests: the e-review attack on a keep-at-least aspect (writer snapshot, kick, the superseded write stays valid at a holder and absent at a new device, both read alike); a rotation within `ROLE_KEEP` under a role snapshot is listed; existing `new_device_alike`, backdated-cutoff and vouching tests keep their verdicts. Mergeable before step 2.
- **F3 Permanent class**: `Retention::permanent()`; `drop_covered` and must-hold keep a header-only blob of permanent deltas; header forgetting skips them.
- **F4 Links and heads**: writer sets `prev` on the first delta per (aspect, session); perm `Frontier { commits, heads }`, schema `frontier` gains field 1; `Engine::frontier` lists heads for all-permanent aspects; `delta_valid` walks links (cached per (aspect, device)); ledger `Tenure.frontier` carries both. Tests: head coverage of a whole permanent history after compaction-free kicks at a fresh node; a re-signed commit on used counters voids both; a post-kick new session is invalid; restore-before-sync orphan healed by the next session's link; a mixed aspect falls back to IDs. Differential: random permanent histories with kicks, a fresh node from headers and the kick equals a full-history node.
- **F5 Lockout recovery**: `demote_in` takes an omission set; flow judges under the new frontier before rotating; test from writer-rotates-to-itself.

## 9. Owner questions
1. Finite keep-at-least (say 1 year after replaced): the kick lists that device's kept writes within the window by ID, so it scales with rate x window, not with total history. Acceptable, or must finite windows get heads too (that means never forgetting their headers, 300 B per write forever, which defeats finite retention)?
2. Permanent deltas: a purged one leaves a header-only blob kept forever (about 300 B, design E's tombstone). Accept?
3. Heads via predecessor links (one entry per aspect and device, a small header field, a chain walk) or the simpler per-(aspect, session) bounds (no header change, no walk, one entry per aspect and app launch that wrote)? Recommended: links.
4. Sanction the deliberate omission in §5.1 (an admin repudiating received writes; the only recovery from a writer rotating to itself), or prefer a crypto-flow fix (rotate a fresh fork from held epochs, ignoring the hostile one), subtler and touching label-step registration?
