# Frontier study: what a demotion's frontier leaves out
> Summary: Recommended rule for spec/permissions.md §5.1: a demoter lists every received commit of the demoted node in scope except those its own compaction would drop now (covered by a snapshot received by the cutoff and past keep-at-least, purgeable), plus a sanctioned deliberate omission (repudiation). Closes frontier-clobbers-history, dissolves item 219's role-commit and partial-hold carry-overs, keeps writer-rotates-to-itself recoverable. Per-session hash chains (devbox) are rejected: a session chain crosses labels and compacted headers, so verifiers cannot walk it. Cost: 32 B per kept commit of the demoted node, about 6% of the content bytes that history already costs; the frontier caps then fail the 0/infinity test and must give way to paged lookup.

Reading only; nothing run. Code references: `Engine::frontier` and `covered_here` (core/engine/src/apply/receipt.rs), `drop_covered` (core/node/src/sync/local.rs), `chunk_frontiers` (core/engine/src/engine/large.rs).

## 1. The problem in one line
Today's MAY (§5.1) lets A leave out any commit a valid snapshot covers. A snapshot carries live content only, so the covered commit's superseded writes turn invalid for good, whatever their retention. Any writer can write that snapshot just before a kick. The covered filter was the only thing keeping frontiers small, so fixing it reopens frontier size.

## 2. Recommended rule
Spec-ready replacement for the "A MAY leave out" sentence of §5.1 Frontier (the rest of the bullet stands):

> A lists every such commit it had received as of T, except that it MAY leave out a commit when, for every delta its header lists, A holds the delta or dropped it as covered, and each delta on an aspect in question is one A's compaction would drop now or already dropped as covered (spec/commits.md §5: covered by a snapshot A holds valid and had received as of T, for a delta dropped as covered every cover on the way; past its keep-at-least bound as A dates it; purgeable by A). A MAY also leave out a received commit on purpose: the frontier is A's word, receivers judge by it alone, and such an omission is an admin's repudiation of that write (the lockout recovery of §3 needs it), exposed by a client only as an explicit act. A lists no ephemeral commit.

Equivalent short form: **the frontier omits exactly what the demoter could erase by compaction anyway.** Everything whose body any node is entitled to keep (before keep-at-least, `nobody`, uncovered) is listed by ID.

Companion edits:
- §3: one sentence naming the recovery from a rotation sealed only to its author: the admin removes or demotes the author with a frontier that leaves that rotation out and rotates from the epochs it holds (issues/writer-rotates-to-itself.md).
- issues/session-sources.md rule 7: "not covered by a held valid snapshot" becomes "not droppable now"; step 3 (compact first) shrinks only the uncovered-but-past-keep-at-least part, so its "no for now" stands.
- notes/growth.md demotion-frontier row: bounded by the demoted node's kept commits in scope, not by compaction cadence.

## 3. Why this rule
- **Correct by construction.** A commit left out is one whose body every right holder may drop now. Its live writes are in the covering snapshot (covered means joining it changes nothing), vouched by §5.2; its dead writes are exactly what compaction deletes. Nobody loses anything they were entitled to keep. A thing set so admins cannot erase history (`nobody`, or keep-at-least forever) lists everything, as the owner wants.
- **One rule for content and role commits.** Role-class deltas are `admin`, keep-at-least `ROLE_KEEP` after replaced. A rotation is droppable only 90 days after a later rotation replaced it and a valid `secrets.0` snapshot covers it; by then the snapshot establishes its epoch (spec/crypto.md §4). Within `ROLE_KEEP` every rotation, grant and keyset write is listed. Item 219's carry-over "always list role commits" is not needed and would block the lockout recovery.
- **Partial holds.** A commit with a delta A does not hold (a thing A does not sync) is listed: A cannot judge that delta droppable. Item 219's second carry-over closes.
- **The hostile-admin limit is stated, not hidden.** A receiver cannot check a frontier against A's receipts, so an admin can leave out anything. Saying so makes the deliberate omission legal and keeps hostile admins where requirements put them (out of scope). Bodies stay must-hold (`must_hold` reads no validity), re-grant revives.
- **Determinism untouched.** The frontier is data; "droppable now" uses A's own death dates and clock, which only decide what A lists. No new picked number: it inherits compaction's `MAX_DRIFT` margin by reusing its predicate.
- **No wire change.** `frontier = struct { commits: set<blob> }` stays.

## 4. The hash chain (devbox) and the other shrink candidates
Rejected, in order of how far they get.
- **Per-session chain** (each commit names its session predecessor; the frontier lists heads, 32 B per session). A session spans every thing the device writes. A verifier holding c under label L can only see headers of commits that touch L's closure; the chain from the head back to c passes commits on labels it does not read. Unwalkable regardless of header forgetting. Dead on arrival.
- **Per-aspect chain** (a predecessor per delta). Walkable within an aspect, but the walk needs every header between c and the head. Header forgetting (session-sources rule 8) and "new devices never fetch compacted headers" leave gaps wherever a later commit of the session on that aspect was dropped as covered while an earlier one is still kept: under relative keep-at-least on a map or set (per-key lifetimes), and around expiring commits. A gap makes an honest c unverifiable, which is the same clobbering narrowed. Closing it needs drops in chain order plus exclusions for expiring deltas, 32 B per aspect per commit in every header forever, and the frontier is still one entry per (aspect, session).
- **Per-(aspect, session) counter bounds for never-dropped deltas.** Sound without a header change: a counter entry applies only where every delta of that session on that aspect up to the bound has keep-at-least forever, no keep-at-most, no expiry (then no node ever forgets its header, so a re-signed commit on its counters voids both, hurting only its author). The writer rule is needed because counters are shared across retention classes: a forgery claiming keep-forever on a compacted droppable delta's counters would otherwise stand. Saves 2-10x on hot history aspects (messages per app launch); reintroduces a second coverage kind. Kept as the fallback if frontiers measure large, not recommended now.
- **Equivocation and concurrent sessions** (asked about the chain): under IDs a second commit on the same dot is unlisted hence invalid where the demotion is held, both void where both are held (one dot, one write); two sessions of one device are just two nonces. The chain would treat an equivocation as a fork whose unlisted branch is invalid. Nothing here favours either. issues/equivocation-after-snapshot.md is an un-merge bug, unchanged by the frontier rule.

Conclusion: without a header change, the commit-ID list is the smallest unforgeable encoding of "what A received" for history that is kept, and the owner already chose IDs over counters (round 13).

## 5. What it closes and costs
Closes: issues/frontier-clobbers-history.md (the rule); the e-review hazard "any snapshot hides a kicked member's past"; item 219 carry-overs 1 (role commits) and 2 (partial holds); the frontier side of issues/writer-rotates-to-itself.md (its flow fix stays, §7).

Bytes: 32 B per listed commit. Listed = the demoted node's commits in scope that are uncovered, or covered but before keep-at-least, or `nobody`. For an aspect with no keep-at-least (the untyped default, most content): the tail since the last compaction, as today. For keep-at-least D after replaced: about D of the node's writes. For keep-forever history (design E chats, audit logs): every commit ever. A kept commit costs its holders at least about 300 B of header plus its body, so the frontier is at most about 6-10% of what that history already costs, and it goes to the same nodes. The owner's Cost goal "never to content or history" (spec/requirements.md) no longer holds literally; see §8.

Growth: per removed holder, 32 B times its kept commits in scope, in the demotion's header (chunked above `CHUNK_THRESHOLD`), in the tenure ledger of every later roster snapshot of the target (forever), and as a chain certificate wherever that demotion is on a verifier's chain. The last two are issues/frontier-certificate-cost.md, which this rule makes pressing rather than optional: certificates and ledger projections should carry the role part (chain-certificate IDs) and the content IDs should travel only where the demoted node's content is. `chunk_frontiers` writes a fresh random-keyed tree per roster snapshot, so re-carrying is not deduplicated at hosts today; linking the same blobs across snapshots (or keying the tree by content) would make the ledger's re-carry free.

CPU: building the frontier is one droppability check per held commit of the demoted node in scope, the same predicate `drop_covered` already evaluates; judging a write is one set lookup. No change in order.

Caps: `MAX_LIST_BYTES` (8 MiB on resolving the header's frontier, about 2.6 x 10^5 IDs) and `MAX_LINKED_FRONTIERS` (64 MiB per roster snapshot's ledger) now fail notes/arbitrary-values.md: the honest size is proportional to kept history, which requirements say no horizon ever drops, so at any finite value an admin eventually cannot remove a prolific holder, or the owner chain can never snapshot the roster again. Neither is a resource bound against hostile input (only admins write frontiers, and hostile admins are out of scope); both are correctness switches. The fix is a frontier judged by lookup: a sorted ID list in pages with a first-key per page (frontier-certificate-cost step 4), every page held before the demotion applies (store-bounded like any content, memory per page), then no total cap. Until built, raising the caps only moves the wall.

## 6. What stays open
- Double-removal residual (round 13, accepted): c left out because Y's snapshot Z covered it; Y later removed with a frontier not listing Z; c's live writes go even where c is held. Narrower now (c had to be droppable), still there.
- Hostile admin lists nothing: accepted. Whether a history read shows held invalid bodies as disputed is an API choice (`valid_at` exists per delta), not a core rule.
- Loss of writes the revoker never received (hole 19, pre-sync): unchanged.
- issues/frontier-certificate-cost.md: the split and page lookup, now needed.
- issues/writer-rotates-to-itself.md: the writer-side flow still refuses to stamp without a current secret; the omission makes the previous epoch current again at the demoter, in the same transaction, so the flow must judge under the new frontier before rotating.

## 7. Build steps
Independent of session-sources steps 2, 4, 5, 6; step 5 changes `retention::covered` (rule 4) and step 6 forgets only headers of droppable commits, which this rule never lists, so both compose. Do F1 and F2 before steps 5 and 6.
- **F1 Spec and notes**: §5.1 sentence above; §3 recovery sentence; notes/permissions.md Revocation (the "leaves out commits a snapshot covers" paragraph); issues/session-sources.md rule 7 and step 3; notes/growth.md row.
- **F2 Engine**: `covered_here` becomes `droppable_here`: today's cover test (coverers received by `as_of`) and the bound and right tests of `drop_covered` (`body_keep(..).at_least + MAX_DRIFT_MS <= now`, `may_purge`), shared as one retention predicate so compaction and the frontier cannot disagree; a commit with a delta on a thing this node does not hold (header aspect with no held or dropped part) is listed. Tests: the e-review attack (writer snapshots a keep-at-least aspect, admin kicks, the kicked node's superseded write stays valid at a holder and absent at a new device, both read alike); a rotation within `ROLE_KEEP` under a role snapshot is listed; `nobody` lists everything; existing `new_device_alike`, backdated-cutoff and `frontier_lists_uncovered_snapshot_vouches_covered` tests keep their verdicts (no keep-at-least there).
- **F3 Lockout recovery**: `demote_in` takes an omission set; the flow judges the target's epochs under the new frontier before choosing the epochs to rotate from; test from issues/writer-rotates-to-itself.md.
- **F4 Certificates**: frontier-certificate-cost's split (role IDs in certificates and ledger projections, content IDs where the content is).
- **F5 Paged frontier judged by lookup**; remove `MAX_LIST_BYTES` for frontiers and `MAX_LINKED_FRONTIERS`; update notes/arbitrary-values-table.md rows. Can wait for a measurement of real frontier sizes after F2; F1-F2 do not depend on it.
