# Keys: the osis-keys implementation
> Summary: How osis-keys holds epochs (`ActorEpochs`/`RoleEpochs`): establishment computed on read, current and supersession, history keys opened whole, names, reading-only secrets and their caps, derived epochs and their cap, merging derived writes after the own-secrets switch, `rotate` and its helpers, persistence, differentials. Why the rules are so: notes/keys.md and the notes it maps.

Principle: **epoch state is a function of the held commits**, recomputed on read; nothing about an epoch is stored that a verdict flip could leave behind.

## Epochs
- `ActorEpochs`/`RoleEpochs` per actor per role; `EpochInfo`: supersedes, rotations, snapshot, names, established, secret, reading, order.
- **Established** is `established: Option<Provenance>`: `Fixed` (`key_actor`) is set directly and persisted; `Derived` is computed on read from the base actor's establishment, never stored (stored at ingest, it froze the base's establishment of that moment: reopen differed from rebuild, `derived_epoch_follows_base_establishment_spec_crypto_4`); `Rotated` is computed by `RoleEpochs::establish(held)` and never stored: base `rotations` (held valid rotation commits with their keyset's list) or `snapshots` (held valid `secrets.0` compaction snapshots keying it; a flag set once outlived its snapshot's verdict, `invalid_snapshot_keys_nothing_spec_crypto_4`), within headroom, and, its secret held, some entry of `names` met under `held` (the engine's `NamesHeld`). The engine's view is `Core::own_epochs` (established on read), so establishment needs no invalidation when commits or verdicts change. There is no own-rotation provenance: the rotator's commit applies locally before `rotation::record` stores the secrets, so its epochs fall when the commit turns invalid. `Rotation::preview` (account creation) marks the new epochs established in a scratch view.
- **History keys are opened whole**: `names` and the history-listed part of `supersedes` are what the live entries of the epoch's history key say, opened under its secret (not reading-only). `open_histories` sets them per key it opens (`RoleEpochs::set_history`, every live entry tried), and an ingest first clears them for every epoch whose key its read stands for (`engine::secrets::SecretsRead`; a pending commit's boxes clear nothing). So an entry stripped with its invalid delta, or overwritten, names and supersedes nothing, alike at a node that opened it while live and one that never did (`stripped_history_entry_leaves_nothing_spec_crypto_4`); an accumulator plus per-commit cleanup could not tell which body carried an entry. A lying entry names only planted epochs, never established, or, written by a holder of the epoch, names less (a secret once shared cannot be un-shared).
- **Current** = established and not in an established epoch's `superseded_epochs`. A rotation commit is told from a grant or re-seal by its `keys.0` write: only history boxes at epochs whose keyset the commit sets count (`rotation_commit`; counting a re-seal's re-written history boxes was R9). `keys.0` vouches for no secret, and nothing is inferred from the applied `secrets.0` state. A `keys.0` keyset names no epoch in the held epochs: epochs come from box and history keys, rotation commits, snapshots and secrets, so the index does not depend on whether `keys.0` or `secrets.0` joined first. Suites ride on keysets and boxes, not on `EpochInfo`.
- A sync ID entry box's read secret is held when it names its epoch (`Engine::learn_secret`); it establishes nothing.
- Persisted by `engine::secrets::encode_epochs` (rotations as `[commit, [epoch]]`, names as their wire value, reading as `[author, commit]`).

## Reading-only secrets
- **Reading-only secrets**: unestablished secrets read and open (`registered_epoch` matches any held secret; stamps, steps and deletes check `is_established`). `reading` holds the pending commits (author node, commit) an epoch is known from when known only from such; non-empty, the secret is reading-only (`secret`/`held` skip it; `reading_secret`, `held_newest_first`, `registered_key` include it), and what it opens by history inherits its sources, supersedes nothing and records no names. A commit stops being a source when it applies or is dropped as bad input, and a secret it was the last source of goes (`apply::keys::forget_unchecked`; an actor left with no secret goes on the lost-access work list).
- Caps: `MAX_READING_PER_AUTHOR` (64) per author, `MAX_READING` (256) per role (each costs a trial decrypt per wrap, since wraps name no epoch); authors whose `created.0` the node lacks share one budget (`UNKNOWN_AUTHOR`), so throwaway identities gain nothing; at most `MAX_READING_ACTORS` (64) actors through reading-only secrets alone. Accepted limit (Tess): an insider's bogus pending commits under throwaway authors can fill the unknown-author pool, so a genuine grant whose granter's chain the node lacks reads only once the grant applies (one sync later; nothing exposed or lost). Tests `bogus_flood_bounded_by_caps_spec_crypto_4`, `genuine_grant_applies_after_flood_spec_crypto_4`.

## Derived epochs
- **Derived epochs**: `ActorEpochs::derived(base, tid)` maps each base-actor epoch whose secret is held to the thing's epoch at the base actor's n capped at `MAX_FREE_N` − 1 (`MAX_FREE_N` = 2^62), named by the derived secret, and keeps a base epoch without a secret as a placeholder at that n whose nonce hashes the whole base epoch (base nonces alone could collide through made-up or capped epochs), so what it supersedes stays superseded.
  - The cap: a writer of the base actor may jump its n past `MAX_FREE_N`; uncapped, a thing's switch rotation landed above the headroom line with nothing at n − 1 that a holder holding no base actor secret could establish. Not n 0 for all: derived epochs must order as the base actor's, or a base actor rotation leaves a plain thing's sync ID head "newer" only by nonce luck and a writer picks a fork other than the base actor's newest. Past the cap (attack only) they keep that order through `order` (a rotation's wrap picks by n, spec/crypto.md §5). Accepted (insider only, who can rotate anyway): above the cap two derived epochs with one secret collide, and an insider can leave a plain thing's derived epoch unestablished.
  - `adopt_derived` (in `Core::own_epochs`) establishes a thing's derived epochs after its own-secrets switch only where the base actor's secret is held, and gives them what they supersede from the base actor's view, on read: the record holds only what the thing's own `secrets.0` says. The base actor is the next thing up the record chain (`CreationRecord::up`); derivation, the section chain (spec/crypto.md §3) and the delete base all walk through first-admin links.
- **Derived writes after the switch** (spec/crypto.md §4 Established and current; Depth0 `D0_fa_dmerge`): a writer lacking the own-secrets rotation seals the thing under a derived epoch the switch's history does not carry, and a blob once sealed is reused when logged under the thing's own sync IDs later, so without a merge only holders of the base actor read it. `rotation::merge`: `Engine::seal` notes, for a section of a plain thing, the derived read epochs it seals under (`Indexes::derived_written`, local records `dw/`, not rebuildable); `adopt_derived` adds those to the thing's view once it has secrets of its own, so such an epoch is current until a rotation's history carries it, and `rotation_due` rotates read, whose history box carries the derived secret; the keyset lists no derived epoch. Kept per (thing, epoch) until superseded here (`merge::settle`) or until a destruction keeps nothing of the thing (`merge::forget`); an emptied key stays in memory until the next open, as removing a key moves every memo that read an absent one. Any node that sealed under it merges. Only epochs actually sealed under count: every derived epoch of the base actor's later epochs would rotate the thing on each base rotation. Test: `rotation::tests::derived_write_after_own_secrets_is_merged_spec_crypto_4`.

## Rotating
- `rotate`: n+1 over the current epochs (held or not), the epoch named by the fresh secret, history of established held secrets only (`held_established`) with the names, removes role-r boxes at the epochs its history carries, lists in the keyset every current epoch; the rotator's own record supersedes what every other node reads from the commit. Helpers: `history_boxes` (per current held epoch: its live entries' list plus the established earlier secrets held, with a met entry's names), `box_holder` (grants and re-seals), `history_adds`, `reseal_needed`, `unvouched` (what a `secrets.0` snapshot leaves out), `newest_secret` (newest established held: a stranded wrap, a rotation's), `newer_than_head` (also needs the head admin epoch established: the step signs under it). Writes are `SecretsWrite { boxes, removed, history }` / `KeysWrite`, turned into map deltas by the node. Recipients: `HolderKeys::{KeyActor, Actor}`; a holder actor's at the keyset Seal gives, symmetric when the writer holds that established epoch's secret.
- Names are computed by `rotation::departures::names` (notes/keys-coverage.md, The pin); `rotation_into` fixes the commit's `ts` beforehand when it departs someone. The named commits are logged with the rotation.
- Residual: a malicious valid rotator leaks what it holds (names it omits, or a second history entry naming less, only hand on epochs it held).
- Differentials: osis-keys `names::names_establish_by_the_held_set` (random histories with withheld commits, forged entries, invalid commits, snapshots, forks, flipping verdicts, against a direct reading of the rule); osis-engine `rotation::tests_property` (end to end: no departed peer opens an epoch whose rotator held its departure). Tests for the TLC traces: `tests/it/established.rs`.
