# Purge rights and the history setting
> Summary: Why purge-by is per delta with default `admin` (Tess), why purge rights are judged at the request's time as validity, immutable fields, lasting headers, a purge following the role timeline both ways (no settling window), what reverses a purge, and the history setting `retention^.0` (exact AKs, no validity rule, the current setting decides, an ordinary aspect). Rules: spec/commits.md §5, spec/permissions.md §5.2.

Principle (Tess): **a purge setting is a promise; deletion that is rightful really deletes; a wrong one must not destroy what honest nodes hold.** The right to purge is exactly validity of a delta needing the role at the request's key.

## Purge rights
- Purge-by is per delta (spec/commits.md §5), decided with Tess. Default `admin` (Tess, notes/engine-retention-history.md Tess's decisions, 2): admins may always purge, and no writer can fill a thing with content they cannot remove. `author_or_admin` lets a writer take back its own writes (chat's messages); `writer` is for aspects whose history is disposable. No set is closed to admins: an unpurgeable delta would let its writer keep content from the thing's admins.
- Purge rights are judged at the request's time, so an author who lost write on the thing can no longer purge its old content at honest nodes (Tess's call; a node keeping S unread approximates it, notes/sync-reasons.md Delete keys). A re-grant gives back a gap request's right as it does its writes (else a re-grant revives the reset but brings the purged value back).
- **Purge author right**: the delta's purge author, the thing its commit's path names by its purge-author index (spec/wire.md §4 field 2; spec/permissions.md §6), held by any node with write on it while it has write on the thing. `PurgeBy` (osis-base) is three named sets, no scale. A purge acting for a client actor drops what the node and the client actor both may, judged at receivers. A promise covers what honest nodes keep; nodes keeping a thing unread follow its sync IDs, so taking a thing off a sync ID deletes there whatever its purge-by (Tess's latitude, coordinator's call).
- Keep-at-least binds compaction but not purge requests, so "history never compacted, authors may purge their own" is `author_or_admin` with at least forever; a deliberate purge is a signed commit while compaction is routine, which the protocol already tells apart.
- Fields are immutable: a change would need the blob re-encrypted anyway (commitments are fixed by blob ID), so purge and re-write; the price is that dead history cannot be made more permanent after the fact (apps export to a new permanent thing). Residue: a delta by another author that carries part of the content (a typo fix inside pasted text) needs that author or an admin. A purge also voids the write inside every snapshot carrying it, else the compactor's copy would outlive it.
- **Lasting headers** (spec/commits.md §5): a purged permanent delta's commit keeps its header at the sync ID for good (about 300 B), and so does the purge commit naming it (without it a new node never applies the header-only blob). Tess: "if history is on, the cost of keeping history is expected". The header-only blob is logged only when no other body of the commit is wanted at the sync ID.

## A purge follows the role timeline
- `retention::rejudge_purges` runs at the end of `rejudge_once` per aspect with a held request on a re-judged thing (`p/`): a part a request comes to drop is purged, a part dropped for one that no longer does goes through `reopen` to `missing`, the one mechanism `uncover` uses. Not kept per body: own-write parts read the gone list, others one flag bit.
- No settling window: at 0 the demotion-first holder restores, at infinity a rightful purge never deletes, so a value could only turn "usually" into "more usually" (fails notes/arbitrary-values.md). A node syncs a sync ID before it deletes under it, so the window is only a demotion not at that source yet, the same a hostile admin before removal has. Lost for good: only a body every holder dropped before any heard of the demotion. Judgments are functions of the held set, so a holder logging a must-hold body and a stale node dropping it end once that node syncs the demotion, which lies at the same source.
- A holder that saw the delete still logs the body though the body's writes are carried (`Engine::named_by_a_fallen_purge`): the delete-keeps-it-out rule assumes a keep bound passed. A stand-fall-rise with no copy left must leave what a node never seeing the fall has, or an older value resurfaces: a delta's `w/` row outlives the fall and counts only while its writes are gone; a snapshot is never dropped by a purge (it has no writes of its own), so it needs no such row.
- A frontier lists requests by `requests_resting_on`, not by the things where the class falls: the right by author runs signer, actor, thing, and the signer may keep its own grant on the thing (a node removed from its account). At the list bound the requests received last go, so a heavy moderator stays demotable and a flood lands last. A header-only blob left from a fallen purge stays at the sync ID as may-hold (300 B each, spec/sync-judgement.md §2): deleting it needs every node's agreement that it is not lasting.
- The gate is separate: a missing delta counts as held on the word of a possible right holder (`Held::ever_admins`, `ever_writers`, monotone), or a demotion's place in the order would decide whether a commit applied; `known_purged` still decides the stripping. A body stripped for carrying stays held through a fall, its copies of the fallen request's writes removed: a whole copy brings them back wherever one joins. Making it missing instead lost the other writes a carrying snapshot covered. Rejected: `reaches`-style rules using a snapshot's kept frontiers (dropped without the body they judge differently from a node that saw it: order::purge seed 890023).
- **What reverses a purge**: only a demotion whose frontier omits the request. A demoter's frontier lists every request of the demoted node it received on a thing in question, for a fall below admin too (`Engine::frontier`, `apply/receipt.rs`), so an honest demotion reverses only a request the demoter had not seen. A hostile current admin can omit on purpose or backdate: the purges of the one it demotes fall, and the deleted values return only from a holder that kept the bytes, as a holder could show them anyway; hostile admins are out of scope. A demoter that never held the request (a fresh node past `ROLE_KEEP`, non-permanent delta) makes it fall too; the body is gone at honest nodes, so only a node still holding it brings a message back.
- `snapshot_right` shares the rule without the listing; it counts only for purge on replace's duty (`replace::coverer`). A cover's drop asks no right and must-hold reads the history setting before any cover (spec/sync-judgement.md §2), so a demotion moves neither; drops made under a fallen writer's snapshot stay dropped.

## The history setting
`retention^.0` (spec/commits.md §5 The setting):
- **By exact AK, plus `default`**: aspect names are static (notes/api-design.md), so naming each governed aspect is enough; no matching language.
- **No validity rule**: the setting decides what history keeps and what a writer fills in (expiry, `final`), never whether another delta is valid; a writer ignoring it can only give its own writes another expiry or purge-by (notes/engine-retention-history.md Tess's decisions, 5).
- **The current setting alone decides**: the held valid body with the newest write by `(ts, author, commit ID)`, so every arrival order agrees and a later node judges history without the older settings. A setting's arrival, fall or drop re-judges its thing's history (`retention::settings_changed`), never a delta's validity. Rejected: judging a body by the setting current when it was replaced (every node would need every past setting, so none could compact; only purge on replace needs the past, and the floor carries it).
- **Purge on replace is one way, by a floor**: the only past that matters is that content written under purge on replace stays under it. Each entry's `purge_before` carries it; a writer of a setting writes the floor joined over the settings it holds (`carry_floors`), and a node joins the floors of every held setting (`joined_floor`), so the newest setting need not know the others. While a held setting's floor is not carried by the current one (`floors_unabsorbed`: concurrent admins, or a writer that missed one), every held setting stays in history (notes/engine-retention-history.md).
- **An ordinary aspect**: admin-only to write, otherwise like any aspect: purge-by, expiry, snapshots, compaction, and its own history under the setting it holds. Cost: the current body, plus replaced ones the setting keeps.
- **Whole value, read a body at a time**: judging needs no CRDT replay. Writers turn `set` into a replace; a hand-made partial delta only hurts its admin.
- **A setting by one not a possible admin binds nothing**: the author alone counts (a path naming an admin as actor saves nothing). It goes like any invalid body but at its author and nodes keeping S unread (`setting::writers_setting_keeps_nothing_spec_perm_6`), bounded by `MAX_RETENTION_ENTRIES` per body.
