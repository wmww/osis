# Time limits
> Summary: The time limits (the tables tools/limits.py reads) and their tooling; what overruns them and why (recurring patterns per stage and box), and the investigations. Hits themselves live in each box's limits.log.

Each box's checks go to its own `~/.cache/osis/limits.log`, the record of every hit (time, box, stage, CPU-s, wall, load). `tools/limits.py summary` lists that box's hits and flags the investigation triggers (notes/coordination.md, Time limits).

## Limits
Every timed run has a CPU limit (tight: other work barely moves CPU-s) and a wall limit (looser: it also catches waiting, idle cores, a demoted process), as multiples of the box's base unit `u` (policy: notes/coordination.md, Time limits). `u` is the CPU-s of one fixed work quantum of `tools/baseunit.c` with every hardware thread busy; a CPU limit is `k u`, a wall limit `k u 16/threads`. Measure a box's with `tools/heavy tools/limits.py unit` (median of 5; varies 2-4% with load) and add its row. `tools/limits.py` reads both tables below: keep their shape.

`gate` and `gate.hold` are Tess's 5-minute cap, never raised. Every gate stage is a share of one of them (`share of`), each whole's shares summing to at most it with 1 u wall and 10 u CPU left for the steps between (`tools/limits.py split`, a gate instant check), so a gate over its cap names the stages over their share. The shares follow ostrich's cost when set and are all under what their stage takes today, so gates hit them until issues/test-time.md is done: such hits belong to that issue. A hit is reported and logged, never fatal.

- `tools/limits.py check NAME CPU WALL` prints `limit NAME: CPU a/b s, wall c/d s` and a line per limit exceeded (`over 2x` starts an investigation): `LIMIT CAP NAME ...` over `gate` or `gate.hold` (Tess's cap), an indented `over share: NAME ...` for a stage over only its share, `LIMIT NAME ...` over any other limit; `summary` tags each hit and trigger `(CAP)` or `(share)`, and appends to `~/.cache/osis/limits.log`; it never fails the run. The gate checks each stage and itself (its lock wait left out); `tools/heavy` checks each hold (`--limit NAME`, default `heavy`). A whole over its limit prints `over its share of WHOLE:` for each part over its share.
- `tools/limits.py run NAME CMD` wraps a by-hand run; `tools/limits.py tests [--jobs N]` runs every default test as its own process and times again alone those past half the `test` limit (under the lock, about half an hour); `tools/limits.py table` prints every limit in seconds on this box; `tools/limits.py summary [--since H] [--all]` lists the box's hits (`--all`: every check) and flags investigation triggers.

## Hits
Every hit is in the box's limits.log; none is copied here. A reviewer whose gate goes over a limit names in its report the pattern below that fits, or the cause when none does. The watchdog (`summary`) adds a new pattern here or updates a pattern's numbers.

## Patterns
Both boxes have 16 threads (fastbox-0 u 1.69, ostrich u 2.3) and run other agents' work beside a gate, usually at load 15-40.
- **ostrich: every gate over `gate` wall and the default tests over their share, CPU and wall.** On the rebuilt sync after test-optimizer rounds 10-16: `gate` 4400-7600 CPU-s, 675-1220 s against 4094/299, the hold 2300-5700 CPU-s, 225-860 s against 3795/242; default tests 2320-2600 CPU-s, 220-470 s (1610/115), properties 1700-3160 with engine properties selected, 155-385 s (1219/78); sim 65-80 CPU-s, 4-15 s, within. The same stages cost fastbox-0 about 2.2x fewer CPU-s while the units differ 1.36x: per u, ostrich spends about 1.6x the CPU on the same work, so its CPU limits are too tight for this box, not the work for the limits: u holds at 2.2-2.3 through 2.5 min of back-to-back all-core runs, so the quantum, not a burst clock, misreads this CPU's cost of compiled Rust tests (Investigations, 2026-11-15 ostrich). During a hold the rest of the box is the coordinator's own agents' unlocked builds and crate tests (`tools/heavy --report`: own 40-70%, box 85-100%; one coordinator session and no other project on ostrich): load 30-60 is three agents each running a 16-thread test binary beside the hold, so wall runs 1.5-2.5x CPU/16. Not worth changing: nicing them moves wall from the workers to the gate, zero-sum while lock waits stay small (20 min over 13 holds), and a wider lock serializes their crate runs (waits of 7-30 min when it did). Properties by selection (each alone, CPU-s): a roles-model change 900 (perm's ten 606, roles-model's two random searches 143, the engine's custody property 137), an engine change about 2140 (`held_set_decides_exhaustive` 705, `_plain` 408, rotation 228, custody 137, deep 121), perm 606, crdt 236, held-model 71. issues/test-time.md for the rest.
- **fastbox-0: CPU at its shares, wall over by load.** CPU within or up to 1.2x (default tests 1150-1350 CPU-s against 1183, drifting 1.01-1.10x over it on main, sim 600-780 against 693, properties 900-1150 against 896 with every large property selected); wall 1.1-3.9x, tracking load (default tests 110-300 s against 84, sim 38-105 s against 34, properties 60-110 s against 58): at load 30 a hold's tests get about 9 cores of 16. Most fastbox-0 gates at load 20-60 are over `gate` wall (232-850 s against 220), none over its CPU but by a rebuild. A warm gate at load under 15 fits (cleanup-cli 141 s, purge-tombstones-perm 177 s). Wall over with CPU within is load; CPU over 1.2x is worth a look.
- **Builds after a low-crate change.** Any change below osis-engine since the target's last build, the branch's or main's (osis-base, -crypto, -blob, -crdt, -commit, -store, -schema, -keys, -perm), recompiles every dependent's test binaries. Floor, warm target (one comment line in osis-base, `cargo test --no-run`, 240 units): 330-390 CPU-s, 41-53 s at load 9-14 on fastbox-0, about 4x the `build` share's CPU and 3x its wall; its critical path is osis-engine compiled twice (lib 26 s, lib with its in-src tests 27 s), osis-crdt's lib tests (25 s) and the largest test binary (23 s), with only 4-7 units running at once. Gates observed: `build` 290-1350 CPU-s, 37-370 s on fastbox-0 against 93/17; 440-2600 CPU-s, 76-500 s on ostrich against 126/23 (an osis-keys or -schema change: 1100-1600); clippy 2-6x with it (a full re-check). Above the floor is a target warm for an older commit (a reviewer gating in another worktree than its worker's: 3 of 4 code gates on 2026-11-14, up to 54 commits behind) and, for wall, load. Only a gate with nothing below its own top crates changed fits (8-108 CPU-s). A rebase over a main change reaching the GUI (osis, osis-store, osis-kit) also recompiles the GUI lint from cold: `gate.gui-lint` 210-480 CPU-s against 8-12. Investigations, 2026-11-14 fastbox-0 build; issues/test-time.md item 7.
- **A manifest or lock change selects everything.** Any `Cargo.toml`, `Cargo.lock` or `.cargo/` change (a new dependency, a comment) selects all 26 crates and runs the unification check, which rebuilds the GUI: `gate.unify` 900-2200 CPU-s, 17-150 s on fastbox-0 against 34/5; 1700-4400 CPU-s, 60-180 s on ostrich against 46/7, its `gate.gui-lint` about 500 CPU-s against 12. By design (notes/gate.md, Paths outside a crate).
- **The reviewer's own builds in the gating worktree.** `tools/bin-at` or a by-hand variant build leaves the target at another revision; the gate after it rebuilds every dependent (`build` 7-10x). An edit after the reviewer's last build is compiled by `gate.clippy` (36-90 s wall against 7).
- **`gate.gui-tests` compiles inside its stage.** The build stage prebuilds core's test binaries only; the GUI tests compile kit, app and masonry test binaries (10-13 s of 10-21 s wall, up to 60 s at load 50 when a GUI test was edited) against a 3 s share: issues/test-time.md.
- **Tiny wall shares trip on load.** `gate.deny`, `gate.gui-lint` and `gate.checks` (1-3 u wall: 2-5 s on fastbox-0) go over by wall in most gates under load (1.2-7x) with CPU within (`tools/policy.py` is about 1 s at load 7 on ostrich): issues/test-time.md.
- **A storm seed or a slow tail in the sim.** `gate.sim` wall 3-5x with CPU 1.3-1.7x: a seed running to the 5000-delivery storm cutoff, 2-3 min single-threaded. Check the stage's slow and failing seeds before blaming load. Without a storm, the seed offset alone moves `gate.sim` CPU around its share (Investigations, cover-once-quiet).
- **By-hand holds over `heavy`.** 300-seed sweeps of `order::held_set_decides_plain` (12,000-14,500 CPU-s), several runs in one hold (A/B, plants: 5000-11,000 CPU-s), roles-model exhaustive families (`custody-exh` about 2,900 CPU-s) and by-hand TLC configs (700-3400 s wall; liveness checking runs on one thread, so wall-bound; `run.sh depth0` at one worker 646 s, its old instances slower than the README says: `D0_nc3` 185 s against 16 s; `netopen` with the walk about 550 s at load 40; Cut.tla's `X_*` instances at 16 workers 6500-7500 CPU-s each, to shrink at the model's next change) go 1.04-5x over `heavy`, which fits a gate-size hold. The custody differential's `_large` at `OSIS_FULL` (2000 worlds) is about 10,000 CPU-s, 667 s on ostrich: a release-size run, never a gate stage; the 3-connection `run.sh netopen` configs (O_rk3 351 s) carry the module's limit, not their own. Size by design, not an investigation; log what ran.
- **Single tests over `test`.** `tools/limits.py tests` finds the default tests over the limit alone: `order::held_set_decides` (115 CPU-s, 17 s), `shred::past_expiry_and_purge_alike` (13-14), held-model's repair properties (12-13), `rotation_holds_spec_crypto_4` (11-12): issues/test-time.md.
- **The disk fills.** A gate can abort at its build on a full disk while worktree targets are a normal 9-16 GB each: the rest is elsewhere (stray targets, scratch dirs: the watchdog's disk check, notes/coordination.md, Watchdog). Not a limit: a gate that fails to build reruns.
- **A hung test.** A default test with no test of its target finishing for 10x the `test` wall limit is stopped and failed "hung" (notes/gate.md, Hung tests). A property seed can also run to its 600 CPU-s timeout (`gate.properties` wall about 2x).

## Investigations
One entry per investigation: date and box, the trigger, what was tried, the outcome (fixed, or a raised limit with why no good solution was found). Tess reviews every raise.

- 2026-11-10 ostrich, the gate against the 5-minute cap (`gate`, `gate.hold` 4.3x): issues/test-time.md (what was measured and profiled, what was fixed, what each remaining fix would save). Every gate since meets a trigger (over 2x, or 5 hits in 3 hours) for the same cause and belongs to it. No limit raised.
- 2026-11-12 fastbox-0, cover-once-quiet review (`gate.sim` CPU 926/693 s, wall 117/34 s, 3.46x at load 14): the same sim sweep (15 seeds from 138571068065) under `tools/heavy` at load 11-20 cost 935 CPU-s, 116 s on origin/main and 949 CPU-s, 130 s on the branch: main's sim at that seed offset is over its share (a long tail of runs, no storm seed), so `gate.sim` CPU over its share varies with the seed offset, not only the branch; the rest was load. No limit raised.
- 2026-11-13 ostrich, every gate over its limits by 1.3-2x CPU and 2-5x wall (Fathom's nine review gates, 08:25-13:32 local): compared each stage's CPU-s with fastbox-0's gates the same day (same suite, same sizes): default tests 2490-2910 against 1150-1280, sim 1395-1530 against 600-700, about 2.2x, against a unit ratio of 1.36. Ostrich is a laptop (Ryzen 7 PRO 6850U, ThinkPad, amd-pstate-epp, platform profile `balanced`, EPP `balance_performance`); at load 10-12 its 16 threads averaged 2.2-2.6 GHz of 4.77 max. `tools/limits.py unit` times five runs of about 2 s each, short enough to run boosted, so u reads the burst clock while gates run at the sustained one. Not tried: a unit timed after a few minutes of all-core load, and the `performance` profile (needs root: Tess). No limit raised.
- 2026-11-14 fastbox-0, `build` over 2x in every code gate of the day (cleanup-schema 1142/93 CPU-s, 205/17 s, 12x): one comment line added to osis-base on a fully warm target (a no-op build 0.2 s first), `cargo test --no-run` at load 9-14: 386 CPU-s, 53 s, and 328 CPU-s, 41 s removing it again (`--timings`: 240 units, engine lib and lib-test 26 + 27 s, crdt lib-test 25 s, node `it` 23 s, 4-7 at once under `jobs = 8`). So no gate after a low-crate change fits the share, and the stated warm `rebuild` (about 15 s; limit 118 CPU-s, 22 s) is 2.8-3.3x low: osis-engine's sources grew 29% and node's tests 24% since it was set, and the measure was never logged. Not the cause: `wt release`'s prune (it marks the standard commands first, so a claim is warm at its released commit) or `wt trim` (only free worktrees beyond two). The rest of each overrun: the reviewer's worktree was warm for an older commit (cleanup-schema's wt13 54 commits behind, with crypto, crdt and store changes; thin-client's 541 CPU-s for an osis-only branch), and load for the wall. Fixed: the reviewer works in the worker's still-claimed worktree (notes/coordination.md, The task loop). Not tried: unit tests out of engine and crdt `src/` (drops one compile of each from the critical path, about 25 s), dropping `debug = "line-tables-only"` (a comment line moves spans downstream; Tess's call). No limit raised.
- 2026-11-15 ostrich, every gate of the day 1.5-4x wall, up to 2.2x CPU (Fathom, fathom-gate-cost): each of the 35 properties alone at one gate's offset under the lock (load 10-22, 5376 CPU-s): roles-model's `small_family_holds_large`, the custody model's exhaustive small family, 2581 CPU-s, 309 s wall, more than the whole properties share and half of every property together; it is the `custody-exh` search, so it is by hand (core/test/roles-model/README.md, Custody), the gate keeping 2000 random custody worlds, which fail under each of the controls (`roster-bare` 46, `merge-carries` 144 failing checks at 2000). A roles-model change also selected all 13 engine properties through osis-engine's dev edge, where one file (the forks property) uses the model; a crdt-model change selected 19 crates through osis-crdt's `cfg(fuzzing)` edge. Fixed: a crate reached only by a dev or fuzzing edge runs only the properties in files reaching that dependency (notes/gate.md, The selection): a roles-model gate's properties go from about 5200 CPU-s to about 900, a crdt-model gate's from 19 crates and the sim to four crates. Tried: the unit after 2.5 min of all-core load (60 back-to-back runs at load 20-30: 2.21-2.30, as measured cold): the 2026-11-13 burst-clock reading is wrong; ostrich's 1.6x per-u gap is the quantum's mix against this CPU, so a unit timed on real work (a fixed test binary) would read it. Not raised: the default tests (2500-3200 CPU-s against 1610) are the engine's per-change cost (issues/test-time.md); no limit raised.
