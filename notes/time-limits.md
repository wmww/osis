# Time limits
> Summary: The time limits (the tables tools/limits.py reads) and their tooling; what overruns them and why (recurring patterns per stage and box), and the investigations. Hits themselves live in each box's limits.log.

Policy (Tess): notes/coordination.md, Time limits. Each box's checks go to its own `~/.cache/osis/limits.log`, the record of every hit (time, box, stage, CPU-s, wall, load).

## Limits
Every timed run has a CPU limit (tight: other work barely moves CPU-s) and a wall limit (looser: it also catches waiting, idle cores, a demoted process), as multiples of the box's base unit `u`: the CPU-s of one fixed work quantum of `tools/baseunit.c` with every hardware thread busy. A CPU limit is `k u`, a wall limit `k u 16/threads`. Measure a box's `u` with `tools/heavy tools/limits.py unit` (median of 5; varies 2-4% with load) and add its row. `tools/limits.py` reads both tables below: keep their shape.

`gate` and `gate.hold` are Tess's 10-minute cap on ostrich, raised only by her. `gate` leaves out the gate's `build` stage (the rebuild after a rebase swings by up to 1,300 CPU-s between gates of one branch, set by what main changed below the branch, not by its tests), which has its own limit. Every gate stage is a share of one whole (`share of`); each whole's shares sum to at most it, leaving 1 u wall and 10 u CPU for the steps between (`tools/limits.py split`, a gate instant check), so a gate over its cap names the stages over their share. The shares are all under what their stage takes today, so gates hit them until issues/test-time.md is done: such hits belong to that issue. A hit is reported and logged, never fatal.

- `tools/limits.py check NAME CPU WALL` prints `limit NAME: CPU a/b s, wall c/d s` and a line per limit exceeded: `LIMIT CAP NAME ...` over `gate` or `gate.hold` (Tess's cap), an indented `over share: NAME ...` for a stage over only its share, `LIMIT NAME ...` over any other limit, and for a whole over its limit `over its share of WHOLE:` per part over its share. It appends to limits.log and never fails the run. The gate checks each stage and itself (its lock wait left out); `tools/heavy` checks each hold (`--limit NAME`, default `heavy`).
- `tools/limits.py run NAME CMD` wraps a by-hand run; `tools/limits.py tests [--jobs N]` runs every default test as its own process and times again alone those past half the `test` limit (under the lock, about half an hour); `tools/limits.py table` prints every limit in seconds on this box; `tools/limits.py summary [--since H] [--all]` lists the box's hits (`--all`: every check), tags each `(CAP)` or `(share)`, and flags investigation triggers (over 2x, or 5 hits of one limit in 3 hours).

## Hits
Every hit is in the box's limits.log; none is copied here. A reviewer whose gate goes over a limit names in its report the pattern below that fits, or the cause when none does. The watchdog adds a new pattern here or updates a pattern's numbers.

## Patterns
Both boxes have 16 threads and run other agents' work beside a gate, usually at load 15-40. Wall over with CPU within is load.
- **ostrich: every gate over `gate` wall, and the default tests over their share in CPU and wall.** Ostrich spends about 1.6x fastbox-0's CPU per u on the same work (its CPU-s are about 2.2x fastbox-0's while the units differ 1.36x; u holds at 2.2-2.3 after minutes of all-core load, so the quantum's mix, not a burst clock, misreads this CPU's cost of compiled Rust tests): its CPU limits are too tight for this box, not the work for the limits. Of a hold's wall over its limit, about half is box load (about 3.3 s per unit of load), a sixth CPU-s over the share, and a third the share assuming 16 busy cores where a hold keeps 12-13 (binaries run in turn, each ending on its slowest tests). The rest of the box during a hold is mostly the coordinator's own agents' unlocked builds and crate tests; not worth changing: nicing them moves wall from workers to the gate, and a wider lock serializes their crate runs. `gate.properties` by selection, in gates: an engine change 2,700-3,600 CPU-s, within its share (the order harness's two sizes 1,500-1,700, a fifth of it history dating: issues/test-time.md lever 1a; rotation 340-380, deep 230-240); a roles-model or held-model library change 4,400-5,300, 1.1-1.4x the share in every such gate, and a change reaching both the models and the engine's rotation properties (24 of 25 crates) 5,800-6,000, 1.5-1.6x. issues/test-time.md for the levers.
- **fastbox-0: CPU at its shares, wall over by load.** CPU within or up to 1.2x; wall 1.1-3.9x, tracking load (at load 30 a hold's tests get about 9 cores of 16), so most gates at load 20-60 are over `gate` wall, none over its CPU but by a rebuild. A warm gate at load under 15 fits. CPU over 1.2x is worth a look.
- **Builds after a low-crate change.** Any change below osis-engine since the target's last build, the branch's or main's (osis-base, -crypto, -blob, -crdt, -commit, -store, -schema, -keys, -perm), recompiles every dependent's test binaries: at best 330-390 CPU-s, 41-53 s on fastbox-0, several times the `build` share (critical path: osis-engine compiled twice, osis-crdt's lib tests and the largest test binary, with only 4-7 units running at once). In gates `build` runs 290-1350 CPU-s on fastbox-0, 440-2600 on ostrich, and clippy 2-6x with it. Above the floor is a target warm for an older commit and, for wall, load. Only a gate with nothing below its own top crates changed fits. A rebase over a main change reaching the GUI (osis, osis-store, osis-kit) also re-lints the GUI from cold (`gate.gui-lint` 210-480 CPU-s). issues/test-time.md, Properties and builds.
- **An engine change.** A branch touching osis-engine (most code branches) pays its edit twice, the library and its lib tests (`#[cfg(test)]`, 616 tests in 45 files of `src/`), plus sync, sim, osis, chat and cli: on ostrich a one-comment edit, warm, 246-260 CPU-s, 43 s at load 8-19 (the lib tests 100 CPU-s and the 42 s critical path; the library 25 s beside it); a few engine modules edited 550-1,270 (the redeem-blob gate 553 at load 6; replayed at load 16, 1,270); engine from scratch 765 / 124 s. Over the `build` share in every engine gate, by the engine's size (69,000 lines of `src/` when the share was measured, 91,000 now) and ostrich's CPU per u; the lever: issues/test-time.md, Properties and builds.
- **A manifest or lock change selects everything.** Any `Cargo.toml`, `Cargo.lock` or `.cargo/` change selects every crate and runs the unification check, which rebuilds the GUI: `gate.unify` 900-4400 CPU-s, `gate.gui-lint` about 500. By design (notes/gate.md, Paths outside a crate).
- **The reviewer's own builds in the gating worktree.** `tools/bin-at` or a by-hand variant build leaves the target at another revision, and the gate after it rebuilds every dependent (`build` 7-10x). An edit after the reviewer's last build is compiled by `gate.clippy`.
- **`gate.gui-tests` compiles inside its stage.** The build stage prebuilds core's test binaries only; the GUI tests compile kit, app and masonry test binaries inside the stage: issues/test-time.md.
- **Tiny wall shares trip on load.** `gate.deny`, `gate.gui-lint` and `gate.checks` (1-3 u wall) go over by wall in most gates under load with CPU within: issues/test-time.md.
- **A storm seed or a slow tail in the sim.** `gate.sim` wall 3-5x with CPU 1.3-1.7x: a seed running to the 5000-delivery storm cutoff, 2-3 min single-threaded. Check the stage's slow and failing seeds before blaming load. Without a storm, the seed offset alone moves `gate.sim` CPU around its share.
- **By-hand holds over `heavy`.** Size by design, not an investigation; log what ran. 300-seed sweeps of `order::held_set_decides_plain` (12,000-14,500 CPU-s), several runs in one hold (A/B, plants: 5000-11,000), roles-model exhaustive families (`custody-exh` about 2,900), by-hand TLC configs (700-3400 s wall; liveness checking is single-threaded; Cut.tla's `X_*` instances 6500-7500 CPU-s each, to shrink at the model's next change) and the custody differential's `_large` at `OSIS_FULL` (about 10,000 CPU-s) go 1.04-5x over `heavy`, which fits a gate-size hold. The 3-connection `run.sh netopen` configs carry the module's limit, not their own. A hold's own agent running crate tests beside it adds the rest. Back-to-back holds starve the unlocked runs beside them (other agents' `cargo test -p osis-engine` 3-5x its usual wall). A by-hand failure hunt replays the quoted seed first and sweeps only when that does not reproduce.
- **A one-core tail in a hold.** A model search whose result printed but whose process went on shrinking on one core held the lock for up to an hour at 6-9% of the box while every other agent queued. Such a hold yields after 5 quiet minutes (notes/build.md, Heavy lock); roles-model shrinks only failures (`vouches`) and only in runs of at most 20,000 worlds (`paths`). Under 100 CPU-s goes `--short`.
- **The full-size sync differential.** `OSIS_FULL=1` `differential::histories_hold_their_closure_plain` in two holds of 50 seeds: 1,300-1,600 CPU-s and 120-310 s each on ostrich (6-8 cores of 16: the slowest seeds' tail sets the wall). At load a few seeds reach the 600 CPU-s seed timeout, so a sweep's hang count depends on the box. Every engine or sync landing runs it while it is driven to 0 (notes/coordination.md, Briefs), 6-10 min of lock per landing. Within `heavy` by design; a hold of 25 seeds wastes more of its tail.
- **Mutation runs pay a whole test binary.** `tools/mutate.py` reruns the binary per plant (osis-sync `it` about 90 s each) and a plant low in the graph relinks every dependent's binary (an osis-blob plant: sync's `it`, about 5 min a build): batch plants into one call, which builds once.
- **Single tests over `test`.** `tools/limits.py tests` finds the default tests over the limit alone: issues/test-time.md.
- **An end gate** (`--all`, or a branch equal to origin/main). Every stage runs, unification and GUI included, against shares sized for a branch: 2-3x the `gate` cap. Expected; read its failures, not its times.
- **The disk fills.** A gate can abort at its build on a full disk while worktree targets are a normal 9-16 GB each: the rest is elsewhere (stray targets, scratch dirs: the watchdog's disk check, notes/build-internals.md, Disk). Not a limit: a gate that fails to build reruns.
- **A hung test.** A default target with no test finishing for 10x the `test` wall limit is stopped and failed "hung" (notes/gate.md, Hung tests). A property seed can also run to its 600 CPU-s timeout (`gate.properties` wall about 2x).

## Investigations
One entry per investigation: date and box, the trigger, what was tried, the outcome (fixed, or a raised limit with why no good solution was found). Tess reviews every raise. Raised: `gate.checks` CPU (2026-11-21, below).

- 2026-11-10 ostrich, the gate against the 5-minute cap (`gate`, `gate.hold` 4.3x): issues/test-time.md (what was measured and profiled, what was fixed, what each remaining fix would save). Every gate since meets a trigger for the same cause and belongs to it.
- 2026-11-12 fastbox-0, `gate.sim` 3.46x at load 14 (cover-once-quiet review): the same 15-seed sweep cost the same on origin/main and the branch, both over the share: `gate.sim` CPU varies with the seed offset (a long tail of runs, no storm seed), not only the branch; the rest was load.
- 2026-11-13 ostrich, every gate 1.3-2x CPU and 2-5x wall: each stage's CPU-s about 2.2x fastbox-0's the same day against a unit ratio of 1.36. Ostrich is a laptop (Ryzen 7 PRO 6850U, amd-pstate-epp, platform profile `balanced`); at load 10-12 its threads averaged 2.2-2.6 GHz of 4.77 max. u reading the burst clock does not explain it (2026-11-15). Not tried: the `performance` profile (needs root: Tess).
- 2026-11-14 fastbox-0, `build` over 2x in every code gate (up to 12x): a one-comment osis-base edit on a fully warm target costs 330-390 CPU-s, 41-53 s, so no gate after a low-crate change fits the share, and the stated warm `rebuild` (about 15 s) was 2.8-3.3x low as the sources grew.
