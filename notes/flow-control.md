# Flow control and stall bounds
> Summary: How sync connections stay bounded and live: reply allowances (`max_reply`, `SMALL_REPLY`, `FIRST_REPLY`), the client's request window, push holding by bytes taken, stall timeouts and resends, the drivers' queue caps, and the host hub's per-input bounds with turns by source (measured costs, rejected alternatives). Rules: spec/sync.md §6 Flow control. Neighbours: notes/sync-protocol.md (the engines), notes/serving.md (the hub and driver), notes/networking.md (transport windows).

## Reply allowances
- A Sync or Fetch reserving `MAX_MESSAGE` (9 MiB) of `WINDOW` (16 MiB) would allow two per round trip, so Sync and Fetch carry `max_reply`, Path pages hold 48 steps, and every small reply's allowance is `SMALL_REPLY` (16 KiB): about 1000 such requests per round trip.
- A first Sync asks `FIRST_REPLY` (64 KiB, plus room for its codes and wants), 256 per round trip; later pages `REPLY_BUDGET` (`MAX_MESSAGE` − 64 KiB). A page under a small cap may hold no event when the first does not fit, and only then does the client not call it stuck. A Fetch asks `FIRST_REPLY` per ID (a batch of 144 fills the budget); an empty reply is asked again under `REPLY_BUDGET`. (At `SMALL_REPLY` a role commit, about 15 KiB with its boxes, would be asked twice.)
- A host answers neither by reading a blob that does not fit `max_reply`: with a thousand small requests per round trip that would be 8 GiB read per trip for no reply bytes. Reconcile keeps `MAX_MESSAGE`: rare per label.
- A stream Fetch is sized as any Fetch (`FIRST_REPLY` per ID, the rest of an answered one under `REPLY_BUDGET`), so a mesh's bodies of many labels share the window rather than taking a `REPLY_BUDGET` each (two per round trip).
- Pipelined Fetches over the sim's lossy links (a cut, or 2% per message, loses all in flight) cost rounds. Test: `loopback::many_labels_sync_in_few_round_trips`.

## The client window
- Every request goes through one `client::Window`: queued in order, sent while the bytes in flight (frame plus reply allowance per unanswered request, `client::allowance`, the one definition both sides use) are under `WINDOW`, so a session's own requests (at the node) and replies (at the peer) never pass `WINDOW_QUEUE` (`WINDOW + MAX_MESSAGE + 4`). `ClientSession` sends all its requests by it, the node's own (`Command::Peer`: the stream's Pulls and Fetches, the shared set's) in the same order; a `ServingSession` sends the node's own requests to the dialer (`ServingInput::Peer`) by one of its own, so both sides of a peer connection pull within it. An answer in the node's range (`for_node`) frees its request's room before the node sees it. A node-kind request a `ServingSession` hands up (`ServingOutput::Peer`) keeps its frame and reply allowance against `MAX_UNANSWERED` until the upper layer answers it (`node_asked`, as client-port requests in `asked`; a second unanswered one with its id is Error 2), so input after input a peer's node requests held up count as its store requests do (proto `serving::tests::node_requests_count_until_answered_spec_sync_6`). On a hub session the window, its stall and its close bound are the hub's session's: node sync's requests go down into it, and the hub closes a session past its `stall_at` at its tick (osis-host `hub::a_stalled_node_request_closes_spec_sync_6`). The drivers' caps (`MAX_QUEUED` 64 MiB for dialed connections, the hub's `max_queued_bytes` 64 MiB for served ones) only bound peers that do not read.
- Reason behind the spec's two close bounds (§6): within `WINDOW`, unwritten requests and unwritten replies stay below `WINDOW + MAX_MESSAGE + 4` bytes and unwritten pushes below `MAX_MESSAGE + 4`; requests received before any is answered were all in flight at once. Between nodes both sides request, so a side's queue holds its replies and pushes (`SEND_QUEUE`) plus its own unanswered request frames (below its window's bytes in flight: an answered request was delivered): `ServingSession`'s close check allows `SEND_QUEUE` plus that, `MAX_QUEUED` is at least `SEND_QUEUE + WINDOW_QUEUE` (test `serving::tests::own_requests_unwritten_close_nothing`). Hosts send no requests.
- **Stall** (spec/sync.md §6): one rule for every requester, whichever side dialed, kept by `client::Window` (so both a `ClientSession` and a `ServingSession` with node requests have it): the oldest request sent and not answered must move within `STALL_TIMEOUT_MS` (2 min), else the connection closes and the node redials (`ClientSession` closes itself, `Timeout`; the hub closes a served session past `ServingSession::stall_at` at its tick). It moves by its own events: when sent with none out, when the one before it is answered, when a reply frame to it arrives or starts arriving (`FrameDecoder::partial`, `msg::peek_head`: kind and id are its first bytes), and when the transport takes bytes up to its frame's end (`ClientInput::Taken`/`ServingInput::Taken`, cumulative against the session's `written`). A reply to any other request moves nothing (`msg::is_reply_kind`). Any other bytes received (pushes, Notifies, the peer's own requests) move it while at most `MAX_AHEAD` of them came since its last own move (`Window::frame_in`/`tail_in`: a frame's bytes count once its head is in, so each is judged by its kind): a peer answering nothing while pushing or asking is closed one stall time after `MAX_AHEAD` bytes, one answering only later requests or sending nothing at all at the stall time.
  - `MAX_AHEAD` (`SEND_QUEUE + MAX_UNANSWERED`, about 68 MiB) is what an honest peer can have ahead of its reply: the Closing bound plus the most it has in flight of its own (spec/sync.md §6). Ahead of the oldest's reply there are no replies (earlier ones were answered, later ones follow), so the Closing bound's reply part (`WINDOW_QUEUE`) is slack that covers the transport's buffers (QUIC 1 MiB per stream, a relay's hops less). Below it an honest reply queued behind a full push backlog on a slow link (under about 80 KB/s) would close; no rate or time is picked.
  - Oldest, not any reply: a peer answering cheap later requests cannot hold an older one (were they progress, a host dropping one reply while answering a request each pass would hold the connection, and the node's idle state, indefinitely). Honest peers answer in arrival order (the hub, a node's session and stream handlers answer on arrival); a client-port `settle` is answered once its Notifies went (replies to later Calls may pass it and move nothing; the Notifies count).
  - Drivers compare `LinkTx::taken` (the writer pump hands the transport 16 KiB pieces) before each tick and report moved connections (`Event::Sent`, `Hub::sent`). `taken` means delivery only because QUIC `write_all` waits for stream credit (`STREAM_WINDOW` 1 MiB): a peer that stops reading stops it within 1 MiB. A taken byte past the oldest request's frame is not its progress, so a peer reading this side's replies to its own requests does not keep it. The in-process transport never blocks a write, so `taken` jumps at once there; driver tests cannot show flow control (`slow_link` models the queues).
  - Any reply speed passes: a reply trickling one byte per stall time keeps the connection, by design, so a hostile peer trickling a reply frame (or pushes, within `MAX_AHEAD`) holds that request and every later one on its connection. What that holds is its own connection, nothing beyond: a body fetch (of a host or a reader) unanswered for `STALL_TIMEOUT_MS` is asked of the next source that has it too (notes/peers.md, The stream: Bodies). A rate floor would close tricklers but is a picked number. Endless churn ahead of a client-port `settle` still closes (notes/client-port.md).
  - Rejected: answering before pushes (a push frame being written cannot be overtaken, so the worst case stays; a `settle` must follow its Notifies and a Stream its PullReply's cursor; it needs two-lane, frame-aware send queues in every driver).
  - Tests: proto `loopback::stalled_requests_time_out` (what moves the clock), `loopback::slow_replies_keep_the_connection_spec_sync_6`, `loopback::a_listener_request_stalls_while_the_dialer_answers_nothing_spec_sync_6`, `loopback::a_slow_reply_behind_a_full_push_backlog_keeps_the_connection_spec_sync_6` (18 MiB of pushes at 16 KB/s), `loopback::a_pusher_answering_nothing_is_closed_after_max_ahead_spec_sync_6`, `loopback::answering_later_requests_holds_no_older_one_spec_sync_6`; node `stream::a_silent_co_reader_holds_a_body_for_the_stall_time_only_spec_sync_6` (both sides dialing), `stream::a_trickling_co_reader_holds_a_body_for_the_stall_time_only_spec_sync_10` (sim `trickle` links).
- **Resends**: uploads, deposits and deletes in flight are remembered per connection (`Conn::req`: `sent`, `deleting`, beside fetches in flight and blobs the host did not deliver; dropped with the connection), so passes before the answer do not send them again; unanswered after `RESEND_MS` (30 s) they go again, but only an entry whose request was answered (`ClientSession::answered(tag)`: every request of the commands tagged up to `tag`, follow-ups included, got its reply; `Requests` stores the tag of `commands()` after the batch's commands went, `UNTAGGED` until then), so a held-back queue that drains sends nothing twice.
- Serving-side back-pressure is by input turns only: a served connection holding more than its share of the node's output bound gets none (notes/serving.md, Connection memory), so its requests wait unread in the transport. Not built: a pausable `ServingSession` that reads requests and answers them only within room (it would make that bound strict; the client still needs its request window either way).

## Pushes
- With `ServingInput::Taken` (cumulative `LinkTx::taken`) reported, a push goes only while the bytes written and not taken, it included, fit `REPLY_BUDGET` (or none are unwritten). Otherwise the label goes to `owed` (FIFO; while any label is owed a `Changed` only queues its label) and continues from its `subs` position at a later `Taken`, labels in turn: one held again goes to the back, since a set walked in ID order lets a busy label starve the others. Relay-only pushes without room are dropped. Without reports (the sim, test drivers: instant delivery) nothing is held.
- Drivers report before a connection's input bytes and at ticks; `held()` connections ask for a tick every `PUSH_POLL_MS` (1 s; hub `next_due`). Caps: `SEND_QUEUE` = `WINDOW_QUEUE` + `PUSH_QUEUE` (one push frame).
- The upper layer's pushes: on a served connection client-port Notifies and node sync's stream pushes wait for room (`WantRoom`, then `Room { budget }` once no host push is owed and the backlog leaves at least `SMALL_REPLY` of `REPLY_BUDGET`), one grant at a time in the order the two asked (notes/peers.md, Served connections); a stream page is cut to the budget, one page per granted room, and the next asks again at a later step (`push_room`, `room_used`). A relay-only push (a Stream without cursor) handed down without room is dropped by the session, as its own relays are. Pushes on dialed connections go unheld until issues/net-one-peer.md 3i puts them on hub sessions.
- Tests: proto `loopback::node_requests_stay_within_the_unanswered_bound_spec_sync_6` (a Pull and Fetches of several labels made at once, one input, both directions), `loopback::held_back_node_requests_keep_their_order_spec_sync_6` (a Pull that would fit waits behind held Fetches, both sides: fails with a first-fit window), `loopback::a_silent_node_request_times_out_spec_sync_6`, node `stream::mesh_bodies_of_many_labels_fetched_at_once_spec_sync_6` (sim `batch_reads`: what a link carries before delivery is one input, as a driver's read; the sim otherwise delivers each send alone and never charges requests together).
- Tests: proto `slow_link::*`: 72 MiB each way at 64 KB/s and 2 MiB at 1 KB/s, both on one session with the real cap sizes (fails with the window off); 72 MiB pushed to a 640 KB/s subscriber within `SEND_QUEUE` (fails without `Taken`); held labels take turns; a random push differential (labels, sizes, rates) against the log; requests held back for hours stay unanswered.

## Hub stall bounds
The hub thread serves every connection of a host, so per-input work must be bounded and comparable to honest work.
- An Open costs a signature and at most three lookups (claims indexed as `H` rows, never a claim-set load). A message's signatures try older keys only after one failed under the head's key (spec/wire.md §11.2), so at most one older-key scan per message. Sync entries are capped at 1024, like Upload. One store commit per input. A relay goes to subscribers only.
- A connection whose unwritten replies pass `SEND_QUEUE` within one input is closed before its further requests run (`ServingSession::unsent`). Requests in one input are all unanswered when the last was sent, so their frames plus reply allowances stay within `MAX_UNANSWERED` (`WINDOW + 2 × MAX_MESSAGE + 4`) for a compliant peer, and one passing it is closed before that request runs: at most three `MAX_MESSAGE`-allowance requests (Reconcile, Fetch, Sync, Path) per input.
- That bounds the two requests whose reads follow the label rather than the message: Reconcile, and Sync from an old cursor (Sync skips log records that yield no event, up to `log_keep`; issues/sync-log-scan.md). Measured (release, redb, pages decrypted from the host's `Store`): a whole-space range reads every live ID, about 125 ms at 10^5 live entries, 1.06 s at the 2^20 per-label cap; 1024 puts in one commit about 0.45 s at 10^5, about 1 s at 2^20. Without the bound one 64 KiB input of about 1600 such 40-byte requests cost a minute at 10^5. An honest reconciliation also reads up to the label once per message, so per input the two are alike.
- **Turns** (osis-node `sync/driver/hub.rs`, `Turns`): those are bounds per input (one read, at most 64 KiB), not per second; an opener can send input after input and keep the hub busy, as an honest peer with a full window may. So a connection's reader hands the driver one read, and the next only after the hub took it; the driver serves waiting reads round-robin by source (`Connection::source`), then by connection within a source; reads that arrived are queued before each turn. A connection over its share of the output bound is parked with its read until the hub resumes it (`Hub::waits`, `take_resumed`; notes/serving.md, Connection memory). With S sources waiting and P the per-input bound, a read waits at most (S − 1) × P once it reached the driver (k × S × P if its source has k connections waiting), plus the bookkeeping between turns (accepts, closes, ticks: a sweep is 1024 rows) and the node sync work the driver's loop runs between them (the hub shares that loop); a message spanning several reads waits that per read, and each read moves the sender's stream (transport progress, so stall timeouts see it). One opener with any number of connections from one source delays others by one input per turn: about 0.4 s at 10^5 entries, about 3.2 s at the 2^20 cap, against `STALL_TIMEOUT_MS` 2 min; many sources each get a turn (issues/host-hub-stall.md, Sources).
- Tests: osis-node `hub_loop::flood_does_not_delay_others` (a flood's reads queued behind one reader delay another's Sync by about 12 reads, the probe's round trip), `sync::driver::hub::tests::turns_by_source`. Throughput: one session's 64 MiB upload then download (16 KiB blobs, QUIC loopback, release) costs the same CPU with one read handed over at a time as with a 1024-slot queue (the link's 16-read read-ahead keeps the pipe full).
- Rejected for now: per-subtree fingerprints in the store (O(log n) per range) cut honest and hostile cost alike but need an order-statistic index persisted beside the `E` rows in three stores, and a 256-range message with splits would still cost thousands of row reads; not needed while the per-label cap (2^20) bounds a scan.
- Not done: decode and verification off the hub thread; a host-wide relay rate (a relay's fan-out is bounded by the subscribers' own queues and the per-label rates).
