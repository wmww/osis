# Role timeline: model findings and role history
> Summary: What `core/test/roles-model` found about the role timeline (layer 1): third-party witnesses fail, the demoter-frontier rule chosen, sticky drops and their residue, self-removals audited, other checked properties; role history (`roles.0` never compacted) with measured bytes and the rejected roster snapshots. Revocation: notes/revocation.md.

Principle: **the timeline is a deterministic procedure over the held role commits**, not a fixed point of end-of-walk roles: layer 1 is inherently non-monotone in receipts, so several stable answers are normal and only a procedure picks one.

## The model
`core/test/roles-model` (Rust, independent of any implementation; a library whose modules follow spec sections, `src/lib.rs`, plus a search binary; osis-perm and osis-engine dev-depend on it as their differential oracle; the module map with each module's differential is `src/lib.rs`). `cargo test` runs the scenarios; `cargo run --bin roles-model -- [samples] <mode>` searches (modes in `src/main.rs`; how to run them: notes/test-properties.md, Models): exhaustive up to 3 edge writes (first admin, 2 admins, 1 writer, skewed receipts, offline nodes) plus millions of random histories (up to 4 admins, 2 writers, 8 writes). Layer-1 modules: `edges` (projected rosters, links, invites, per-op judgment, record-chain writes, finalizes), `joint` (the joint walk over several actors: order independent, terminates in at most 4 walks in 4M random histories, nothing dropped with nobody offline), `content` (layer 2: per-delta validity across partially synced things, snapshot authority at `ts`, the snapshot-of-snapshot divergence), `trust` (trust sets and disputes: notes/disputes.md, Model results), `addenda` (notes/addenda.md).

## Findings
- **Third-party witnesses judged by end-of-walk roles fail**: about 14% of random histories never stabilize (period-2 loops), e.g. an admin removing itself that nobody received in time. Several stable answers are normal (1–2%).
- **Every third-party-witness variant has a self-witness hole**: an ex-admin's backdated grant whose only receiver is its beneficiary stands, since the beneficiary is a holder by that grant; two forged grants can vouch for each other. Closing it needs a lot of rule for rare writes. If third-party receipts for edge writes are ever wanted: `strat` (admin witnesses) and `stratw` (write+ witnesses), both needing a self-witness fix; write+ witnesses also let a writer plus an ex-admin hold admin power.
- **Demoter-frontier rule (chosen, agent's call)**: every expected outcome holds (mutual removal resolves to the earlier timestamp; the first admin's further-backdated revoke cancels a stolen node's; re-grant validates content; a cycle of removed witnesses validates nothing; self-witness and mutual-vouching attacks fail; a demoter's testimony falls with the demoter). Terminates everywhere (at most 6 walks in 4M histories), order independent, nothing dropped when nobody is offline. Drops are sticky within one evaluation; a from-scratch re-evaluation can oscillate.
- Consequences: a stolen admin node's backdated removals stand iff the node removing it had received them before its revoke time; an admin removed by a node that had not synced loses the grants that node had not seen, even if other admins saw them (re-grant recovers). Demotions of a grant never audit record-chain writes.
- **Sticky drops leave residue**: if a demotion drops a grant and that demotion is itself later dropped, the grant stays dropped (about 1 in 7,000 random histories); likewise a used single-use link can revive until the sharer finalizes. Re-grant repairs it. Drops that last only while their cause applies remove the residue but loop forever on removal rings (about 0.04%), so drops stay sticky. Also residue (Tess-accepted): a drop whose dropped write made its demotion a fall stays after a restart where the demotion is no fall (D only grows). Every node holding the same role commits computes the same residue, so it diverges nothing. Pinned: perm `sticky_residue_of_dropped_grant_spec_permissions_5_1`, `unseen_leave_stands_spec_permissions_5_1`.
- **Self-removals are audited like any write** (Tess, simplicity): they keep only their looser authority (admin on the holder, none on the target) and carry no rotation. One through admin on holder M is attributed to the author's class on M and listed as a write on M; one lowering the author's own grant, to its class on the target. Never dropping them let a node removed from account P write backdated removals of P's grants everywhere that stood. Accepted cost: a leave the demoter had not received is dropped and gives access back until someone removes it again (re-seal covers the missing boxes). The honest demoter covers it: `Timeline::demotion_scope` adds the targets of the demoted node's applied self-removals through each thing its class falls on. Writes in one commit are judged one by one: all-or-nothing would make L's self-removal depend on the redeemer's roster entry, which a verifier of L does not hold.
- Concurrent invite grants for one actor: the first in timeline order wins, even with a lower role or shorter `until`.
- Also checked: projected rosters compute the same roles as full rosters (0 mismatches in about 320M checks, given per-write judgment inside a commit); `until` and single-use finalization (a backdated join after `until`, or a forged earlier redemption, stands until the sharer's removal of L drops it: `until_link_backdated_grant_void_spec_perm_7`); plain record-chain writes (a removed node's late write is never valid; validity is order independent and survives compaction and projection); invite power (no joiner or link holder can remove, demote or change anyone; self-granting admin, mutual vouching, ex-admin plus invitee, and re-adding during the inviter's own revocation all fail; a removed inviter's unseen grants drop).

## Role history
- `roles.0` is never compaction-snapshotted: every role commit stays, and a node that starts holding a thing fetches and judges all of it (spec/requirements.md Scale states it). `secrets.0`, `keys.0` and `sync.0` compact like any aspect (compaction snapshots page, so a big actor's boxes and steps never have to fit one delta).
- Bytes: 364-371 B per role write, plus 34 B per commit a frontier lists. A 1000-member chat after a few thousand joins and leaves is about 10^4 role writes (3.6 MB). A joiner ingesting them one by one does counted work linear in the history, and a write at the end of it and its arrival flat work: perm places each arrival without walking (`Timeline::append`), and the node re-judges only the commits `Changed::commits` names. Evaluating the timeline anew was 1.5-2.4 s at 10^4 (release); the wall time of a 10^4 join on the flat per-arrival paths is unmeasured.
- The `roles.0` state keeps a tombstone per ended entry (spec/crdt.md §5.6, never compacted) and is written whole up to `PAGE_TARGET`, paged above: per write at most 64 KB, then flat. Accepted: every state under `PAGE_TARGET` is rewritten whole (notes/engine-paged.md), and the tombstones are the same order as the role commits kept forever. Nothing reads that state (the roster is the timeline's, `Engine::get`), so dropping it is the fix if it ever matters.
- **Rejected: roster snapshots** (a record-chain roster snapshot with a ledger of ended tenures and their frontiers). Every write+ tenure and frontier had to stay forever (a write anywhere below may rest on any of them), so it saved only 1.1-2.6x for writer groups (20x only for big read rosters), a kick-heavy group at 10^4 could not write one ("the commit does not fit a blob"), a chat's took 294 CPU-s to write (release), and it cost epoch-start rounds, linked frontiers and a second residue class. A join is O(role history) with or without snapshots (the ledger is), so the Scale item on permission history is needed either way. Linked frontiers save nothing in these shapes, since each kick lists the kicked member's own commits, disjoint. The frontier that does grow without bound is a single kick of a heavy writer (`TooLarge` at 3*10^5 commits).
