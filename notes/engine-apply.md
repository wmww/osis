# Engine: receiving and writing commits
> Summary: How the engine examines, pends, applies and re-examines commits (`apply/mod.rs`, `apply/waits.rs`, `apply/atomic.rs`) and what its writer refuses: sessions and counters, the HLC, how a part waits alone, the chain-certificate and owner-depth readings, junk and bad parts, late parts, the sync set, the re-examination cache, the writer-side rules, and apply and first-sync costs. The rules themselves are spec/commits.md §2-§4; this note says how the code keeps them. Not here: validity and re-judging (notes/engine-validity.md), retention on apply (notes/engine-retention.md), role commits (notes/engine-roles.md), streamed joins (notes/engine-paged.md).

## Sessions, counters and the HLC
- **Counters** (spec/crdt.md §2.1): every content commit writes under this open's session (`Core::session`, a random nonce per `Engine::create`/`open`); expiring memory commits under a second one (`Core::expiring`), so an expired commit forgotten with its counters leaves no gap in `session`'s ranges that a snapshot frontier could not pass. Counters per (aspect, session) are memory overlay rows (`Store::counter`/`reserve_counters`, never on disk; rolled back with an aborted transaction, so a session leaves no gap), reserved in the transaction that applies the signed commit.
- A restored or cloned store is a new session, so nothing it writes overlaps what the lost or sibling copy wrote: no restore gate, no counter adoption, no `OverlappingCounters` on honest nodes (osis `sync::restored_store_writes_at_once_spec_crdt_2_1`, node `crash::two_live_copies_of_one_store_converge_spec_crdt_2_1`, sim `restore_backup`). Two live copies still answer for one dialable key (`reach.0`) and LAN tag: a connectivity flap (LAN receivers keep the first source address they verified), not a correctness problem.
- When an own write of the current sessions turns invalid, later writes go under fresh sessions (`Core::fresh_session`: same random first 8 bytes, next count), past the hole, with an `OwnWriteInvalid` diagnostic.
- Rejected: counters per (aspect, node) under a per-node source with a restore gate. It costs one frontier entry per node that ever wrote an aspect in every later snapshot, two live copies void each other, and a lying frontier can push a restored copy to commit form for good.
- Role commits and snapshots are commit form (`write::commit_in`, `retention::snapshot_if`); `Mutation::commit_form` asks for commit form on a content commit.
- **HLC** (`engine/clock.rs`): a writer exhausting `logical` within one ms moves to the next ms instead of waiting.

## Examining a commit (`apply::examine`)
spec/commits.md §4 gives the rules; the code's shape:
- **Invariant**: what applies is a function of the parts held and everything else held, never of arrival order or which parts came together. A header or signature fault refuses the commit; each part is judged alone from the signed header, its bytes and held data; every wait ends only as the held set grows, so the applied parts are the least fixpoint of a monotone rule (`parts::split_delivery_*`, `parts::parts_differential_*`).
- A commit pends whole only for its author's record, drift, an unresolved header list (the held author's signature is checked first, so a false one costs no list), or when every held part waits. A commit with no part held applies as its header alone.
- **A part waits alone** (`Aside`, `thing_wait`, `Waiting`): for its thing's record, its owner chain, the author's reach to the chain, its pages (`WaitBlobs`), or parked. The commit's other parts apply, so a commit can be applied and listed by `pending()` at once (the waiting parts, stored under its ID). Only parts the blob holds can wait: a thing whose part is not held (another's private thing in a mixed commit) makes nothing wait, and its delta joins late if it comes. Nothing in apply reads what the node syncs. A commit with unchecked boxes keeps them until its last part applies (`forget_unchecked`). Accepted cost (notes/whole-commits.md): a node syncing two labels fresh may show one thing's part before the other thing's record arrives.
- **Chain certificates missing** (`MissingChain`): the author's or an owner chain's `created.0` is missing, or the author is not on the thing's owner chain and no path of held edge writes and owner links leads from it to a thing on that chain (`validity::reaches`: searched backward from the chain, from a thing to its owner and its named holders (`Held::holders_of`), as perm's `roles_to_any`, so the author's owned things never enter it). Revoked authors keep their certificates, so their commits apply, judged invalid; a re-grant re-merges.
- **Owner depth** (spec/permissions.md §1): `validity::too_deep` makes every delta but `created.0` invalid on a thing whose chain is not known to reach its key actor within `MAX_CHAIN_DEPTH` links (deeper, or a record on it not held: forgetting a record never makes a deep delta valid); per delta, from creation records alone, so nodes holding different parts agree. `thing_wait` stops waiting for the chain there (`ChainStep::TooDeep`). A deep thing's `roles.0` writes never reach perm (`add_role_commit_in`); perm gives such things no ownership authority (`Owners::chain` returns `None`); the writer refuses such a commit.
- **Junk and bad parts**: a part not hashing to its header entry, or on an aspect of the wrong storage class, is dropped alone (diagnostic) and the commit applies without it; a blob holding only such parts is refused. Before its thing's record is held the hash cannot be read, so the pending blob keeps the first copy of each slot, junk or not; when one proves junk the commit is named (`Core::redeliver`) and node-sync ingests the commit's other held copies again (`SyncEngine::redeliver`), so the rule holds at the node, not at one `ingest`. A part that hashes but is bad by itself (`judge_part`: the author signed garbage) is dropped alone, unmarked: only those bytes can ever hash there. A node that could open only a bad section holds nothing of that commit, where a node opening none applies its header.
- **Prefix vs header** (spec/commits.md §4 last rule) is checked by node-sync where blobs are opened, for linked header lists once resolved (the pass); `ingest` takes plaintext.
- **`created.0` minting** (spec/commits.md §2): `carried_records` refuses a durable commit whose `created.0` part is not exactly one minting (`write::is_minting`: one source, one stamp, equal to `mint_created` under the `creation` view); several commits' mintings join (two nodes persisting one draft). Header validation refuses `created.0` in a durable snapshot, and `Engine::snapshot` refuses it, so no junk can enter a state kept forever. A writer delta already cannot carry slots or foreign stamps (codec); the minting rule adds refusing resets and untyped structure.
- **Role-class content rules** are writer-side only: a received commit breaking them is applied, and perm judges each edge write at its point.

## Late parts (`apply::late_parts`, `atomic::join_late`)
A part of an applied commit arriving later: one that waits is kept pending under the commit's ID and joined once it can be; an invalid one joins and un-merges as on apply. Late `created.0` records and `roles.0` parts reach perm as on apply (the commit's edge writes re-added from every held part: a certificate copy may bring a commit's `roles.0` part after its `created.0` one), then held deltas are re-judged. Late `secrets.0` parts establish epochs with the commit's held `keys.0` like a whole commit's (`with_keys`; notes/engine-roles.md). A snapshot part dropped while valid that comes back judged invalid takes back what it gave (`given_by`), as a held snapshot turning invalid does.

## Sync set (`Core::syncs`)
`ix.syncing` holds only what the node was asked to sync (a group root per label, asked for at the first entry of its label the node opens, or its first pass); `syncs` adds what `groups.of` puts in those groups, so the set follows `group.0` in the transaction, validity flips included. `ephemeral_synced` reads `syncs` of the thing and of the groups it is in, so a window in a group that is an item of a labeled group stays synced.

## Re-examination (`apply/waits.rs`)
- `reexamine` runs after every applied commit, at open and on ticks. Examining every pending commit again each time (read, decode, signature) makes a first sync where commits wait for their thing's `created.0` O(commits × pending): at 10^4 commits it took 540 of 580 s (`scale.rs first_sync_cost`).
- So a pending commit's examination is kept (`Core::waits`, a cache dropped by a rollback that changed anything, a clock offset change, a reload) when it waits for a record (`UnknownCreated`, `MissingChain`), its header is inline, and it fits the byte bounds (`MAX_WAIT_BYTES` 64 KiB each, `MAX_WAITS_BYTES` 16 MiB in all; a record wait keeps its things and records only, since `MissingChain(author)` comes before the signature check and junk from a peer gets there). The module doc lists what each kind of wait reads.
- `waits::candidates` takes only what may have changed: commits without a kept examination; record waits whose record was added (`Held::take_records`); a wait on its author's certificates when a grant or record moved on a thing its backward search reaches (`reach_watch`, `Held::take_moved`); a wait with boxes when the epochs its boxes read changed (`Wait::openers`, `Waits::secrets_changed`) or the reading-only budget got room for it (`wake_refused`); every pending commit when the clock is below the latest one any wait was judged at (drift). A purge request drops the waits of commits with a part on its things (examined in full: the part is held value-less, `pending_drops`). Candidates keep the (role commits first, `ts`) order.
- Debug builds examine every commit left out in a rolled-back transaction and assert it still waits for the same reason (`Engine::check_unchanged`); that examination can emit diagnostics (sent at once, not rolled back), so rate-limit slots differ between debug and release.
- Tests: tests/it/waits.rs (clock back, 1000 junk commits bounded), receive.rs `pending_not_reexamined_while_unchanged_spec_commits_4`. Remaining cost: commits pending for other reasons (drift, blobs, parked, header lists by link, past the byte bounds) are examined at every `reexamine`.

## Writer side
- **Writer-side role rules**: at most one `roles.0` grant per role commit (excepted: a redemption's grant plus self-removal; an explicit removal of several others' grants on one target with a frontier, `revoke_joiner`); a frontier only on a commit that lowers a grant, a removal of an absent or expired grant included; a removal or demotion of another holder on a thing with a `keys.0` keyset carries its `secrets.0` and `keys.0` deltas (its rotation), a self-removal none (`validity::self_removal`: the author's own grant, or admin on the holder with no admin on the target without the holder's grant). The rules take a remove + put of one grant as its new grant, so a raise carries no rotation. A local commit breaking them, or with any delta invalid at this node, fails before it leaves.
- **Commit form encoding**: own writes of a commit-form delta are re-sourced to a commit placeholder before encoding (`build_and_apply`), so entries order as a reader decoding with the real commit source orders them (own commit first, spec/crdt.md §3.2); encoding them under the signer's own session would break canonical bytes when foreign dots share a map (a link redemption re-writing history boxes).
- `Mutation::dead_resets`: map tombstones for entry dots whatever their liveness (`osis_crdt::reset_entries`: a removal of an absent grant).

## Costs
Release, osis-node `--test it scale::`, run one at a time on a 16-core box at load about 11 (numbers vary ±50% with load):
- `role_change_and_open_cost`, with 10^4 content commits held: writing them 2.9 s; a new thing 0.57 ms; a grant to a new holder 0.56 ms; open 24 ms (rebuilding the index instead: 126 ms; open costs: notes/engine-store.md).
- `first_sync_cost`, a reader's first sync of 10^4 content commits written while it syncs, through one hub: 15.2 s (reader 7.3 s, writer 6.1 s, hub 1.7 s); writing them 3.6 s; the same 10.1k commits ingested directly 2.5 s (0.25 ms each: store B-tree work, apply, signature). Both nodes' engine phases in the sync (`osis_node::costs`): `ingest` 2.8 s for 10.1k commits (apply 0.64 s, of which joins 0.27 and commit records 0.11; 14.7k examinations 1.36 s, of which signatures 0.41), `reexamine` 0.72 s over 5.6k calls (choosing candidates 3.8 ms), the pass 7.6 s, 0.35 M transactions (sync's reads and flushes) with 1.14 s of store commits. Without the wait cache the same sync took 580 s; without the pass work sets 58-65 s.
