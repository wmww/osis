# Engine: receiving and writing commits
> Summary: How the engine examines, pends, applies and re-examines commits (`apply/mod.rs`, `apply/waits.rs`, `apply/atomic.rs`) and what its writer refuses: sessions and counters, the HLC, how a part waits alone, the chain-certificate and chain-depth readings, junk and bad parts, late parts, the sync set, the re-examination cache, the writer-side rules, and apply and first-sync costs. The rules themselves are spec/commits.md §2-§4; this note says how the code keeps them. Not here: validity and re-judging (notes/engine-validity.md), retention on apply (notes/engine-retention.md), role commits (notes/engine-roles.md), streamed joins (notes/engine-paged.md).

## Sessions, counters and the HLC
- **Counters** (spec/crdt.md §2.1): every content commit writes under this open's session (`Core::session`, a random nonce per `Engine::create`/`open`); expiring memory commits under a second one (`Core::expiring`), so an expired commit forgotten with its counters leaves no gap in `session`'s ranges that a snapshot frontier could not pass. Counters per (aspect, session) are memory overlay rows (`Store::counter`/`reserve_counters`, never on disk, rolled back with an aborted transaction), reserved in the transaction that applies the signed commit.
- A restored or cloned store is a new session, so nothing it writes overlaps what the lost or sibling copy wrote: no restore gate, no counter adoption, no `OverlappingCounters` on honest nodes (osis `sync::restored_store_writes_at_once_spec_crdt_2_1`, node `crash::two_live_copies_of_one_store_converge_spec_crdt_2_1`, sim `restore_backup`). Two live copies still answer for one dialable key (`reach.0`) and LAN tag: a connectivity flap (LAN receivers keep the first source address they verified), not a correctness problem.
- When an own write of the current sessions turns invalid, later writes go under fresh sessions past the hole (`Core::fresh_session`: same random first 8 bytes, next count), with an `OwnWriteInvalid` diagnostic.
- Rejected: counters per (aspect, node) with a restore gate: one frontier entry per node that ever wrote an aspect in every later snapshot, two live copies void each other, and a lying frontier can push a restored copy to commit form for good.
- Role commits and snapshots are commit form (`write::commit_in`, `retention::write_snapshot`); `Mutation::commit_form` asks for it on a content commit.
- **HLC** (`engine/clock.rs`): a writer exhausting `logical` within one ms moves to the next ms instead of waiting.
- Own dots are written under a zero commit ID placeholder from the start (`write.rs` `own_source`), so the writer's own source sorts above foreign dots (spec/crdt.md §3.3 rule 2).
- **Clock**: every node runs on its own wall clock; nothing a peer sends moves it, so a wrong clock harms only its holder (spec/requirements.md): fast, its writes park elsewhere until real time catches up; slow, it parks others' writes and its own sort early. A node's own commits (`Origin::Local`, a held key actor's included) are never refused for drift: its HLC may run ahead of a clock set back. A peer's clock offset feeds only the `ClockSkew` diagnostic. Rejected: correcting the clock from peers' offsets (a median, a cap, a quorum, own nodes first): any correction lets other nodes move this one, and each bound needs picked numbers and special cases (one connection, one peer on two connections, an own node with a dead clock battery).

## Examining a commit (`apply::examine`)
- **Invariant**: what applies is a function of the parts held and everything else held, never of arrival order or which parts came together. A header or signature fault refuses the commit; each part is judged alone from the signed header, its bytes and held data; every wait ends only as the held set grows, so the applied parts are the least fixpoint of a monotone rule (osis-engine `order::held_set_decides`).
- A commit pends whole only for drift, its author's epoch or an unresolved header list (the held author's signature is checked first, so a false one costs no list), never for its parts' waits: with every held part waiting (pages, parked) it applies as its header alone and they wait under its ID, so whether it applies does not hang on which copy came first (a header-only copy, or a destruction that drops its waiting part and then falls). Its author's or actor's record unknown: thrown out (`Exam::Awaits`).
- **A part waits alone** (`Aside`, `Waiting`) for its pages (`WaitBlobs`) or parked; a part `thing_wait` holds back (record, chain, author's reach) is thrown out (`Parts::unjudged`). The other parts apply, so a commit can be applied and listed by `pending()` at once. Only parts the blob holds can wait: a part not held (another's private thing in a mixed commit) makes nothing wait and joins late if it comes. Nothing in apply reads what the node syncs. A commit with unchecked boxes keeps them until its last part applies (`forget_unchecked`). Accepted cost (notes/whole-commits.md): a node syncing two labels fresh may show one thing's part before the other thing's record arrives.
- **Chain certificates missing** (`MissingChain`): the author's or a record chain's `created.0` is missing, or the author is not on the thing's record chain and no path of held grants and record links leads from it to a thing on that chain (`validity::reaches`, searched backward from the chain through first admins and granted holders (`Held::granted_on`: edge writes putting a grant, of any validity), as perm's `roles_to_any`, so things below the author never enter it). A removal names its holder (`Held::holders_of`, kept for dependents and closures) but leads nowhere: counting it let a thing's admin, by removing an absent grant of G, open a way for G's admins to have forged commits on the thing held. Revoked authors keep their certificates, so their commits apply, judged invalid; a re-grant re-merges.
- **Chain depth** (spec/permissions.md §1): `validity::too_deep` makes every delta but `created.0` invalid on a thing whose chain is not known to reach its key actor within `MAX_CHAIN_DEPTH` links (deeper, or a record on it not held: forgetting a record never makes a deep delta valid); per delta, from creation records alone, so nodes holding different parts agree. `thing_wait` stops waiting for the chain there (`ChainStep::TooDeep`). A deep thing's `roles.0` writes never reach perm (`validity::add_role_commit` skips them); the writer refuses such a commit.
- **Junk and bad parts**: a part not hashing to its header entry, or on an aspect of the wrong storage class, is dropped alone (diagnostic) and the commit applies without it; a blob holding only such parts is refused. Hashes are checked, and unjudgeable parts thrown out (`throw_out_parts`), before a pending copy is stored (drift, epoch); only a commit whose header lists are unresolved keeps the first copy of each slot unchecked, and when one proves junk the commit is named (`Core::redeliver`) and sync ingests its other held copies again. A part that hashes but is bad by itself (`judge_part`: the author signed garbage) is dropped alone, unmarked: only those bytes can ever hash there. A node that could open only a bad section holds nothing of that commit; a node opening none applies its header.
  - A slot counting as never received (`Engine::settled_slots`: body dropped, part past its expiry) is not brought back by a held copy: only a new copy does that, or a restart would change what a node reads. A copy with a junk part or signature is no source even for its true parts: a sender holding the true part could withhold it anyway. Limit: a true copy deleted everywhere while junk holds the slot waits for a holder's new copy (keeping such copies while the commit pends would let one sender grow one commit's copies by flood-and-delete).
- **Prefix vs header** (spec/commits.md §4 last rule) is checked by sync where blobs are opened, for linked header lists once resolved (the pass); `ingest` takes plaintext.
- **`created.0` minting** (spec/commits.md §2): `carried_records` refuses a durable commit whose `created.0` part is not exactly one minting (`write::is_minting`: one source, one stamp, equal to `mint_created` under the `creation` view); several commits' mintings join. Header validation and `Engine::snapshot` refuse `created.0` in a durable snapshot, so no junk enters a state kept forever. The codec already keeps slots and foreign stamps out of a writer delta; the minting rule adds refusing resets and untyped structure.
- **Role-class content rules** are writer-side only: a received commit breaking them is applied, and perm judges each edge write at its point.

## Late parts (`apply::late_parts`, `atomic::join_late`)
A part of an applied commit arriving later: one that waits is kept pending under the commit's ID and joined once it can be; an invalid one joins and un-merges as on apply. Late `created.0` records and `roles.0` parts reach perm as on apply (the commit's edge writes re-added from every held part: a certificate copy may bring `roles.0` after `created.0`), then held deltas are re-judged. Late `secrets.0` parts establish epochs with the commit's held `keys.0` (`with_held`; notes/engine-roles.md). A snapshot part that comes back judged invalid gives nothing: the parts dropped under its cover alone go missing again (`retention::uncover`), as when a held snapshot turns invalid.

## Sync set (`Core::syncs`)
`ix.syncing` holds only what the node was asked to sync (a group root per label, asked at the first entry of its label the node opens, or its first pass); `syncs` adds what `groups.of` puts in those groups, so the set follows `group.0` in the transaction, validity flips included. `check_synced` (`Mutation::require_synced`) reads the labeled set for the thing and the groups it is in, so a window in a group that is an item of a labeled group stays synced.

## Unjudgeable commits and parts (`apply/waits.rs`; notes/expiry-creation-design.md §3)
- A commit or part that cannot be judged (`Reason::MissingChain`, `UnknownCreated`) is thrown out after its boxes are read (`Ingested::Awaits`); nothing of it is stored. Sync keeps `Kind::Awaits` and fetches it again on `Changed::wakes`; one awaiting a record a later batch of a fill brought is queued again at once (`open_entries`), so it is asked before the fill's next batch: what judging needs comes first (spec/sync-judgement.md §2), also when an order-0 commit (an admin's purge request) arrives ahead of its thing's creation. A part of an applied commit stays in `m.missing`, its commit named in `Wakes::woken`.
- Reading-only secrets a commit's boxes gave are sourced from it (`Waits::reading`, rebuilt at load) and go at its last part's apply (`forget_unchecked`), when it is judged bad, or when its last entry leaves. A refused box is read again when the budget frees (`Wakes::reread`).
- `reexamine` examines what is left pending (`Drift`, `WaitBlobs`, `Parked`) at every call.
- Tests with no sync layer fetch through `local_peer::Peer::receive`, driven by the same `Wakes`; `Peer::tick` and `Peer::reexamine` wake them too (a drift-parked creation that runs at a tick brings the record a thrown-out commit awaits). Test peers run what is due through those, never `<peer>.engine.tick()`/`reexamine()` (`tools/policy.py`); a test ingesting straight into `engine` (no fetch stand-in) may reexamine through an `&mut Engine` it holds.

## Writer side
- **Writer-side role rules**: at most one `roles.0` grant per role commit (except a redemption's grant plus self-removal, and an explicit removal of several others' grants on one target with a frontier, `revoke_joiner`); a frontier only on a commit that lowers a grant, a removal of an absent or expired grant included; a removal or demotion of another holder on a thing with a `keys.0` keyset carries its `secrets.0` and `keys.0` deltas (its rotation), a self-removal none (`validity::self_removal`: the author's own grant, or admin on the holder with no admin on the target without the holder's grant). A remove + put of one grant counts as its new grant, so a raise carries no rotation. A local commit breaking them, or with any delta invalid here, fails before it leaves.
- **Commit form encoding**: own writes of a commit-form delta are re-sourced to a commit placeholder before encoding (`build_and_apply`), so entries order as a reader decoding with the real commit source orders them (own commit first, spec/crdt.md §3.2); under the signer's own session, foreign dots sharing a map (a link redemption re-writing history boxes) would break canonical bytes.
- `Mutation::dead_resets`: map tombstones for entry dots whatever their liveness (`osis_crdt::reset_entries`: a removal of an absent grant).

## Costs
- **A commit's parts are read once** (`apply::PartsRead`): each `keys.0` part's keysets (`keys::KeysBody`: typed `KeysWrite`, each keyset with its keys parsed, `KeysetRec`) and perm's reading of its `roles.0` parts (`EdgeParts`) are read on first ask and shared by everything an apply, a late join (`with_held`: late and other held parts, read once for judging and joining) or a rebuild notes from them: required roles and proofs (`note_required`, invite grants from `EdgeParts`), keyset parts, keysets and rotations (`before_join`), perm's edges and the `node/edges` row.
- Counted (osis-engine `scale::`, notes/test-scale.md Scale checks): a grant does the same work with n and 10n content commits held (`grant_is_flat_in_content_commits`); an open from the persisted index rebuilds nothing (notes/engine-store.md). A first sync's ingests grow linearly with no pending commit read again (without the wait cache every pending commit would be examined again after each applied one, quadratic): its check waits for the new sync (notes/test-scale.md, Checks to restore).
