# What one thing costs
> Summary: Measured breakdown of a plain thing and a thing with own keys (stored rows by family, bytes downloaded, commits, KEM and signature work, maker and readers), why each line exists, why the big lines are what they are, what is open (one waiting on Tess) and rejected, how a state is stored as its one held body, and the record riding in a newborn's own-keys rotation.

Principle: **a thing costs its record, its content and what its readers need to open it, once each**. A second copy of the same bytes in the store, or a commit whose only job is ordering inside one transaction, is a defect; key material is paid per role a holder can be boxed at (notes/keys.md, A KEM key per role).

## Measured
Tests print the tables with `THING_COST=1`: engine `thing_cost::what_a_thing_costs`, `thing_cost::what_a_reader_costs`; osis `thing_cost::what_a_thing_costs_as_the_app_makes_it`. Stored bytes are logical rows (key plus value, `Engine::census` over `Transaction::census`); the osis test also gives both stores' physical bytes. KEM and signature counts are `osis_base::costs` counts in osis-crypto (`kem: keygen`, `kem: encapsulate`, `kem: decapsulate`, `sign`, `verify: *`).

Engine, the maker and its account's second node (`account_two_nodes`), each kind made once first:

| commits | 1 (content: record, content, the root's `holds.0`) | 2 (rotation: `created.0` 88 B, `keys.0` 6288 B, `secrets.0` 1409 B, `sync.0` 451 B; content) |

Keyed, stored rows at the maker: commit records `c/` (the two commits whole); aspect states 66-69 B each (`keys.0`, `secrets.0`, `sync.0`, `created.0`, content: each its one body, States stored as their body); `node/keysets` 948; `k/` (opened epochs) 1443; `node/metas` 603, `node/sealed` 592, `node/epoch intro` 408, `node/boxes to` 400, refs 354, `node/edges` 66; small local rows about 300. Plain: `c/` 789, `node/metas` 255, refs 178, states 330, `t/` 74, `a/` 56, counters 104, states 136 (by body).

The KEM public keys (1216 B each, one per role, four roles) are 4864 B of the 9402 B a reader downloads and of the 14.9 KB stored at every holder (in the commit record; the `keys.0` state is that body until a second one joins).

Through the API, as the app makes them (osis, per thing over 10 makes; home is a plain thing under the account root): a plain thing under home (record, `retention^.0`, home's `holds.0`) 1.5 commits, 1.9 KB stored (2.0 KB physical), 1.2 KB downloaded; a keyed thing held by home (made under the account, as `osis create --admin me` does) 3.5 commits (its rotation carrying its record, home's `holds.0` with its `retention^.0`, the account root's ticket for it, `tickets.0`, about 640 B, then compactions of home's `holds.0` and the root's `tickets.0`), 16.4 KB stored (18.6 KB physical), 10.9 KB downloaded, 8 signs, 14 sync-step verifications at the maker and 15 at the second node.

Another account granted read on a keyed thing (engine `what_a_reader_costs`): one role commit of 3975 B (`secrets.0` 3093 B: sealed boxes at read and sync, each about 1.2 KB, and the history boxes again), 3 KEM encapsulations at the granter, 2 decapsulations at the reader; the reader stores about 31 KB for the thing (its content commit included) and the grant (its `keys.0` and `secrets.0` states hold two bodies, so they are whole); the granter's grow by the same, about 7.5 KB.

## Why the big lines are what they are
- **The keyset index names its body, not its KEM key** (`HeldKeyset::kem_in`, the earliest body carrying one; `apply::keys::keyset_kem` reads the key from that body or its dropped record when sealing, a rare path): a copy would cost 4.9 KB stored per keyed thing at every node, and the same in memory (`Indexes::keysets` is in memory). The key sealed to is still one a valid noted body of the keyset carries. Earliest by body order, so two bodies of one epoch naming different keys (a writer re-publishing a keyset with junk) give every node the same key in any arrival order (`parts::` index checks compare a rebuild with the live index).
- **A state is stored as its body** (States stored as their body, below): a second copy would cost 8.4 KB per keyed thing and 0.16 KB per plain one, at every node.
- **The record rides in the own-keys rotation** (The record in the rotation, below): one commit and two signatures fewer per keyed thing. Content cannot join it: a commit is of one kind (spec/commits.md §2), and role commits travel as certificates under other sync IDs, where content would leak. So a keyed thing made with content is two commits (a minted actor, made without, is one).
- **Plain things**: 1.6 KB is the commit record (789 B, of which the header is 357 B: three aspect entries with their delta hashes, the delete-key commitment, blind and signatures), the metas row (255 B) and reference rows (178 B). Nothing big left.

## Open
- **Tess call: KEM keys only for actors that can hold roles.** The four KEM keys are half of what a reader downloads and two copies stored. Every keyed thing publishes them so that any granter, offline and holding none of its secrets, can box it as a holder; most keyed things (chats, notes in a shared tag) are never holders. A thing declared a holder kind at creation (accounts, teams, tags, realms) would publish them; others not, and granting such a thing a role would be refused. −4.9 KB downloaded and −9.7 KB stored per non-holder thing. It restricts Generality (any actor may hold), so it is a requirements question. Publishing lazily when a thing is first granted something does not work: a granter that is not its admin cannot write its `keys.0` and would wait on one that is. The variants that keep Generality (one ML-KEM key per actor with an X25519 key per role; roles sharing a key while their holders coincide) were declined (notes/keys.md).
- **CPU: sync-step verifications** (14 per keyed make at the maker, 15 at the second node, constant in what is held): the new sync ID's steps verified again at each read. A verified-step memo keyed by step ID would make it one; about half a millisecond per make.

## Rejected
- **No history boxes in a newborn's own-keys rotation** (about 800 B). They are not empty of use: keysets never list derived epochs (spec/crypto.md §4 Lists), so the history box is what supersedes them; every later holder boxed at the first own epoch opens the rotation commit itself (wrapped under the derived epoch, §5 Who wraps under what: its keysets, boxes, record and `sync.0`) by the derived roles secret that history carries, and a grant writes again only what live entries list or the writer holds as superseded; and a node that wrote under the derived secret before the rotation reached it (TLA Depth0 `Plain`, `dhist`) is read through it. Dropping them needs a "first rotation supersedes its derived epochs" rule and grants carrying the derived secrets anyway, so the bytes move into every grant. The roles-model has no first-rotation notion (custody's thing t starts at its epoch 0), so no search applies.
- **The account root's ticket in the making commit** (osis path, about 640 B downloaded and 1 KB stored per keyed thing held outside the root's unit). Same author, so signing is no bar; the bar is order: the ticket duty (`flows::inbox::write_account_ticket`, driven by sync's account-ticket effects over the closure feed) reads how the node closure enters the thing (`entered_outside`), which holds only once the making commit's strong applied and the closure settled at the transaction's end. Folding it in means predicting the closure for things made in that transaction: a special case of the duty.

## The record in the rotation
- **Rule** (spec/permissions.md §3): a thing made under an account by a commit writing content on it carries its record in its own-keys rotation (`write::keyed_first`), and so does `Engine::mint_actor`'s actor. `rotation::own_secrets_new` judges the creation as its commit would, holds the record ahead of the commit (`apply::atomic::learn_record`, timelines updated) so the rotation is built exactly as for a held plain thing (derived epochs, holders by the record edge, the first admin's own secrets first), then the rotation mutation `create`s it. `commit_or_build` refuses a create only where a minting is held or the held record differs, so a record held ahead passes once. An expiring first write keeps the record alone with its expiry (a role commit never expires).
- **Not for a link's K, nor a thing made without content** (`Engine::create_with_admin`): their record goes alone, the rotation after. K's readers are its redeemers, who read K's sync ID by entry boxes at its own epochs and never hold the derived one the rotation is wrapped under; a node paired later reads K by the account's holder piece under D (notes/sync-facade.md), untried with K's record moved into the rotation. A records-only commit keyed at the transaction's end lets a maker key it itself first (K's sync ID listing L).
- **Who reads it**: the rotation is wrapped under the derived epoch it supersedes (spec/crypto.md §5), so the record section opens for the base actor's holders and holders granted with history, not for a ticket's, a link key's entry-box, a cut's or a sync-only holder's. They take the record as a record blob: `Engine::records_of` counts a closure body as carrying a record only where a minting of it gives no epochs (`minted_plainly`), as spec/permissions.md §4 Logging certificates counts a rotation's blob only where it opens under the newest read epoch.
- **Rejected**: the record riding with the first content commit instead (the rotation would pend unjudgeable at every receiver until it came, making the rare pending path the common one, and a thing without content would need the record commit anyway).

## States stored as their body
An aspect unit's state is one of: empty, whole (`state::encode`), paged (`pages::open`), or `[2, commit ID]` (`state::Stored::Body`): the state is that commit's part on the aspect, as its record holds it. A keyed thing's `keys.0`, `secrets.0` and `sync.0` states and most `created.0` and content states are one body each, so they cost a 66-70 B row instead of a second copy.
- **Rule**: at each transaction's end (`Tx::settle_states`, before the index persists), every unpaged state written in it (`Core::written`) is stored as a body equal to it that is held whole (not a leftover) and valid: the one it names already, else the aspect's newest (`by_aspect`); otherwise whole. Equality, not reasoning about which writes could make them differ: whatever normalised the state (value-less strips, a snapshot's copies, a dropped char), a reference reads exactly what was cached. Writers (joins, rebuilds, covered drops, strips, purges) write whole as before; one decision point, so no writer has a special case. Cost: the body's decode (`Core::decoded`, per record write) and a compare per written state, O(state) as the write's encode already is. Paged states stay paged: their point is reading a page, and a reference reads the body whole.
- **One reader**: `Tx::load_lazy` resolves it (`apply::stored_fragment`: the record's part, decoded once per record write). Every state read (cache misses, an open's `sync.0` and `account.0` states, the index check) goes through it.
- **A body leaving its record** (`Tx::put_commit` without a part the index holds whole, `unindex`): `Tx::pin_states` first reads each state naming it into the cache and notes it written, so the transaction reads it as before and `settle_states` re-decides it. A valid body's drop rebuilds or rewrites its state anyway (shredded); where it does not (forgetting keeps role-class states, a part set missing again), the state is stored whole from the cache. So no stored reference names a body not held.
- **Shred**: a reference holds no content, so dropping or purging its body leaves no copy in the state; the drop's rebuild writes the unit shredded as before (path rotation covers earlier whole versions). `drop_aspect` (destroy, forget) removes the unit. The covered-drop page rewrite touches paged states only; an unpaged covered drop rewrites whole, then settles.
- **Index**: a reference names a record, not an index row, so a rebuild of the index leaves it valid (rejoined states settle like any write). `apply::check_states` (debug) reads each checked state from the store through the reader, not the cache, against a rebuild from the held bodies.
- **Rejected**: deciding in each writer (the apply joins before its record is stored, so the body is not there yet); a second store of states; references for paged states.
