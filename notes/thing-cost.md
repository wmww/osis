# What one thing costs
> Summary: Measured cost of a dependent and an independent thing (rows by family, wire and stored bytes, commits, KEM and signature work, maker and readers), of a commit on a public thing per referring unit and of an account's card, of a small commit by each signer form, and of an account's pairing, removal and per-unit evidence at n and 10n nodes; the survey of fixed overhead per commit, thing, aspect and reference with every byte placed and the ranked candidates to remove; why the big lines are what they are, what is open and rejected, states stored as their body, and the record riding in a newborn's own-secrets rotation.

Principle: **a thing costs its record, its content and what its readers need to open it, once each**. A second copy of the same bytes in the store, or a commit whose only job is ordering inside one transaction, is a defect; key material is paid per role a holder can be boxed at (notes/keys.md, A KEM key per role).

## Measured
Tests print the tables with `THING_COST=1`: engine `thing_cost::what_a_thing_costs`, `thing_cost::what_a_reader_costs`, `thing_cost::what_a_small_commit_costs` (default tests, their bounds hold the engine figures below); osis `thing_cost::what_a_thing_costs_as_the_app_makes_it` (ignored: sync rewrite, copies per (commit, thing); its figures are to measure again). Stored bytes are logical engine rows (key plus value, `Engine::census` over `Transaction::census`); the osis test also gives both stores' physical bytes. These tests hand commits over in their opened form: "downloaded" there is that form, not the wire, and they count neither the sealed blob nor the sync store's entry rows, about half of what a node really sends and keeps for a small commit (Survey, below). KEM and signature counts are `osis_base::costs` counts in osis-crypto (`kem: keygen`, `kem: encapsulate`, `kem: decapsulate`, `sign`, `verify: *`).

**A public thing per referring unit** (engine `thing_cost::a_commit_on_a_public_thing_costs_an_entry_per_referring_unit`, an account's public profile P strongly referenced by n independent things of the account, each a unit of its own as an importer's account is; n = 2 and 20): a strong to a public thing brings it whole into the referrer's unit (spec/sync-set.md §2 T's keys open), so every commit on P is a body under P's own sync ID, the account's and each referrer's: 4 entries at n, 22 at 10n, exactly one per referrer; the commit itself is 279 B for a small value whatever the referrers. The accepted cost of the built unit (notes/redesign-plan.md risk 6), paid per commit at every node logging each unit.
- **The account's card** on its profile (A's and the inbox's `keys.0` commits with their evidence and records): its value 18.0 KB; the commit rewriting it after a rotation of A 10.8 KB (opened package); carried as any commit on P, one entry per referring unit. A card therefore costs every unit carrying the thing it is on: on the profile, every contact's account.

Through the API, as the app makes them (osis `thing_cost::what_a_thing_costs_as_the_app_makes_it`, ignored on the sync rewrite): its figures predate accounts as things (they had home a dependent of a separate per-account thing, and an independent thing a record per make on it); to measure again once it runs. Expect the engine's figures above plus `retention^.0` and compactions of A's `holds.0`.

Another account granted read on an independent thing (engine `what_a_reader_costs`): one role commit of 3968 B (`secrets.0` 3093 B: sealed boxes at read and sync, each about 1.2 KB, and the history boxes again), 3 KEM encapsulations at the granter, 2 decapsulations at the reader; the reader stores about 38 KB for the thing (its content commit included; `keys.0` carries the read KEM key twice, in the keyset and the step keys) and the grant once the offer's fire is past its keep (`INBOX_LIFETIME`), about 59 KB while the offer is held (the fire's value carries the role commit's copy, its evidence and records), 4.5 KB of it the held writes of `secrets.0` (below) (its `keys.0` and `secrets.0` states hold two bodies, so they are whole); the granter's grow by about 23 KB, the held writes' 4.5 KB among them.
- **Held writes at the first death** (notes/engine-retention-dating.md Rows): a replaced role-class body is kept `ROLE_KEEP` after replaced (spec/commits.md §5 Fixed retention; evidence and the required set say where a role commit is held, not when a replaced one may go), so a role aspect's bodies are dated like any kept aspect's. An aspect on which nothing was replaced keeps no row (a thing's creation costs none); the grant is the first death on the thing's `secrets.0`, whose held writes are then 4,460 B in 5 pages at granter and reader each: 50 writes, 89 B a write (each write's row, its position's top and step, its body's record of it, with a page's repeated sources, commit IDs, stamps and position hashes written once; 480 B a write before that). Every later write on it adds its own rows.

A small commit (one value on a dependent of A, `what_a_small_commit_costs`; no epoch, no inner signature): opened 275 B (header 174 B), sealed blob 478 B. By the node minting A it states the empty path (`[0]`, 2 B: its record edges descend to the thing); by a paired node `[0, A]` (18 B), 16 B more. Signed by a key granted on the thing, the signer takes its second form `[tid, signing_key]` (40 B more: the key is a `signing_key` agility value, 39 B, in a two-item list) and the commit form (1 B) in place of the node's session form (19 B), no session of the key's being open: 22 B more in all.

## Accounts at n and 10n nodes
Engine `scale::pairing_a_node_spec_permissions_8`, `removing_a_node_spec_permissions_8`, `evidence_per_unit_spec_permissions_4`, over `account_nodes` (peer 0 makes A and pairs the others in), n = 2 and 20 nodes besides peer 0. Counts, not time.
- **Pairing** (peer 0 pairs a fresh node): one grant commit of 7091 B (header 287, `secrets.0` 6115: A's boxes to the node, four roles; `keys.0` 515: their box records; `roles.0` 95), 4 KEM encapsulations, 1 sign, flat. The offer is 8.6 KB, flat: the grant's edge piece of the node (its `roles.0`, its `secrets.0` piece and the rest piece of history boxes, no `keys.0`: spec/permissions.md §4 Copies), its evidence (A's minting and the pairer's node thing's minting, each cut to its edge-class parts and the `sync.0` placing listed keys) and two records; A's `sync.0` copies only where that evidence lacks them (none: A's minting is its one `sync.0` write). The new node taking it: 8 decapsulations, 8 keygens (its boxes' roles), flat in everything. The pairer's grant and a sibling taking it judge A's roster, linear in its holders (pairer: roles walked 41 to 167, keysets walked 36 to 180, state pages loaded 9 to 51). The new node's first sync from the pairer is linear in A's history (applies 12 to 48) but for the counts that judge each role commit against its whole component, quadratic: grants judged for key access 13 to 310, roles walked 49 to 886, held-writes pages 18 to 250 (issues/perm-eval-cost.md).
- **Removal** (peer 0 removes a node, erase): one commit, A's rotation, 16.3 KB at 2 nodes left, 135 KB at 20: about 6.6 KB per node left (four boxes), 4 encapsulations per node but the remover (it boxes itself symmetrically). One node's edge piece of it (its `secrets.0` piece and the rest, spec/wire.md §4 Pieces) is 7.1 KB at 2 nodes left, 7.7 KB at 20: about 34 B a node more (its piece's hash in the header), what a unit carrying that node's piece pays. A sibling decapsulates 4, flat; it judges A's roster, linear. A's inbox and profile, whose boxes to A went stale, are re-keyed by the remover's next role duty, two rotations whatever the size; the sibling taking them re-keys nothing.
- **A notice per held removal** (spec/permissions.md §4 Notices; osis-sync `holding::a_removal_its_writer_made_blind_*`, one removal of an account's read on T by a sibling node that never held the grant): one entry under T's sync ID of about 6 KB (the removal's edge-class copy, its evidence copies of the remover's path and their records, under a blob key wrapped as an entry's and sealed to the holder's KEM key: 1,168 bytes of box), kept for as long as the removal is held, at every reader of T; readers logging it concurrently each seal one, the duplicates beside the lowest blob ID unlogged as sync copies are. Paid once per removal of a reader, not per node left.
- **Evidence per unit** (an independent thing T minted with admin A; certificates at its minter, each copy cut, spec/permissions.md §4 Copies): with one paired node writing, 5 copies, 4 records, 12.3 KB at n; 23 copies, 4 records, 30.4 KB at 10n: every node's grant rides in T's unit as A's public key record (its `keys.0` box records with the `roles.0` they are judged by), about 1 KB a node whether or not it writes there (issues/account-evidence-per-unit.md, a tess-call). With every node writing, 13.2 KB at n, 47.3 KB at 10n (42 copies, 23 records): each writer's node thing's minting rides as a `sync.0` copy (the step placing the delete key its writes list) with its record, about 900 B a writing node.

Every figure is marked: **[M]** measured, **[C]** computed from spec/wire.md (each computed total matched the measured one to the byte), **[E]** estimated. **To measure again**: the survey's [M] figures (wire, sync store rows, rows read, verifications) were taken on a build before the sync rewrite, whose evidence and grant delivery differ; the engine's own rows are held by the tests above. ### CPU and memory
- **Signatures [M]**: a small commit is 2 signs at the maker (commit, stamp; engine measured: 1, the stamp is sync's), 1 commit verify at the second node and 2 stamp verifications in the two-node world; no KEM.
- **Rows read [M]**: engine 12 at the maker and 7 at the second node for a 1-byte change, 31 and 32 for a make. Whole world (both nodes, engine and sync store): 1365 rows read for a 1-byte change against 633 for an idle step, rising by about 30 per commit (1365, 1369, then 1464, 1468 after the compaction): the sync side reads some 40 times what the engine does per small commit. Not traced to its readers.
- **Sync-step verifications [M]**: none for a single small commit; 525 world-wide for 100 makes in 100 commits (7 in a step whose make compacted D's `holds.0`). Not traced.
- **Decodes [M]**: each part decoded once per node in the engine (`part decode` equals the parts).
- **Hashes [C]** (not counted by `costs`): at the maker, a delta hash per part, the commit ID, the account digest, the blob ID, the stamp's entry digest; at a receiver, the blob ID, the entry digest and the verdict hash per stamp check, the commit ID, a delta hash per part.
- **Memory per thing held [E]**, from the structs: a `Meta` per commit with its `View` (an `AspectView` of about 120 B per aspect), entries in `by_aspect`, `ranges`, `by_thing`, `by_actor`, `commit_blobs`, `blob_parts`; `Held::created` and `mint_admin_of` per thing; `derived_written` per dependent thing at its maker: of the order of 1-2 KB per small thing made alone, about half batched. Counter rows (51 B per aspect written) stay in memory until the session ends.

### What each cost is
(a) a job nothing else does, (b) a second copy or a pointer bigger than its data, (c) a job something present could do, (d) removed by a decided change.
- (a) Load-bearing, keep: `blind` (unlinks a commit's commitments across blobs, spec/crypto.md §7); the delete key and its prefix commitment (Delete and shredding by level); the delta hash per aspect (a part dropped or purged alone leaves a header that still verifies, spec/commits.md §5); the delta nonce of a sealed part (readers of the commit's other sections must not confirm a guessed small value from its hash); padding (size leakage); the stamp (a node keeping the sync ID unread checks a write came from a writer without learning who); the blob wrap (one ciphertext serves several sync IDs); the section chain (derivation without the record); the prefix salt; signatures.
- (b) The sealed blob and `c/` (two copies of each commit at every node, `bp/` relating them); `node/metas` (header fields again); `ou/`'s delta hash; `bp/`'s salt; `E/`'s expiry and class; `t/`; a state row naming a body smaller than itself; the entry echoed back; the blob ID beside an inline blob; `refs from` beside the state it indexes.
- (c) the tid in every aspect entry of one thing; the section wrap where a key could be derived; `dw/` (derivable from the mint admin's epochs); `X/` for a blob under one sync ID; the 33 B stamp verdict; the record's 32 B nonce behind a 15 B tid; the `created.0` delta nonce (the record carries its own).
- (d) Stated paths cost 2 B (`[0]`) plus 16 B per listed thing for a commit whose things are all written along one path, whatever their number (none for what the node minting A writes on A's dependents, its record edges descending to them; `[0, A]` for a paired node's, 16 B more), and in the split form (notes/path-form.md, Bytes) 3 B plus 115 B per thing in the header (32 B of it the entry's key commitment) and 38 B per section (its thing's path key) only where one path does not serve; their evidence is not counted here.

### Ranked candidates
Savings per unit; [C] unless marked.
1. **Store each commit once.** Keep the sealed blob (served byte for byte: its ID and stamp cover it) and read bodies by opening it; `c/` keeps its head and what only it holds (leftovers, dropped parts), `bp/` its non-default cases. Saves about 700 B per small commit per node ([M] `c/` 511-787 against the blob). Changes the engine store layout; the engine already holds blobs (`G/`). Risks: a purge or shred must reach the one copy; commits under no sync ID and pending ones; an AEAD open per body read (state-as-body reads go through it); re-encryption (`Engine::relink`).
2. **`ou/` per (commit, thing, epoch), not per part.** An aspect bitmap; the delta hash only where it was not checked against the header. Saves about 350 B per one-value make and 130 B per small commit at every node but the maker ([M] 480 and 224 B). Risk: the passing-on invariant (`sync_ids::opened`): keep the epoch exact (its nonce), keep unchecked openings' hashes.
3. **No entry echoed to the peer it came from.** Saves about 276 B of wire per commit per peer pair, and the maker's `S/` row (50 B per entry per peer) if Held elsewhere is read from the Applied cursor. Risk: spec/sync-status.md §2 needs the peer to have stored the entry, and a changed wrap or stamp must still flow (event 0's replacement).
4. **Derive the section key** from the thing's read secret, the commit ID and a 16 B section nonce, instead of a 72 B wrap: saves about 57 B per thing per commit. Risk: one key per plaintext: re-sealing stripped parts must take a fresh nonce; copies keep the blob-key wrap.
5. **Embed a lone small delta in the header** where the commit has one section and its audience is the blob's: no hash, no delta nonce, no section. Saves about 50 B per aspect and about 112 B of section. Risk: per-part purge needs the hash, so only for a commit whose purge drops it whole; readers of another section never see it.
6. **`node/metas` from the record**: 150-250 B per commit per node. The in-memory `Meta` per held commit is the deeper question: an open reads every commit's.
7. **One tid per thing in the aspect list**: 16 B per further aspect of a thing in the header, 15 in `node/metas`. Wire format only.
8. **`refs from` read from the state**: 49 B per reference per node. Risk: dropping an aspect must read its state before clearing `refs to`.
9. **No `t/`**: 74 B per thing per node; the `created.0` state, keyed by tid, already reaches the record. Risk: hot record lookups go through the state reader.
10. **No `dw/`**: 59 B per dependent thing at its maker, derived from the mint admin's epochs since the thing's creation. Risk: a rotation's history must not carry epochs below a floor (spec/crypto.md §4 Current).
11. **Entry row**: the stamp verdict by head index, not 32 B (about 29 B); expiry and class read from the prefix (9 B); `X/` only for a blob under several sync IDs (50 B). Per entry per node.
12. **No blob ID beside an inline blob** on the wire (34 B per entry): the receiver hashes the blob anyway.
13. **Small items**: the record nonce at 16 B (16 B per thing in each copy); empty optional header fields (about 6 B); the signature's enum wrapper (7 B).

Not candidates: **one stamp over a batch** never pays: entries are stored and served one by one, so each keeps a proof, a Merkle path of 32 B per level, at least the 64 B saved from a batch of two up. **The session nonce** (18 B per commit against commit form) stays until session counters' job is studied: it is what lets one source span commits.

To study (CPU): the sync side's row reads per small commit (about 730 world-wide against the engine's 19) and the sync-step verifications in a burst of makes.

### Floors
- **Empty dependent thing [E]**, from the format with 4-6, 8, 10-12. Batched, its record (about 36 B: admin 15, nonce 16) embedded as `created.0` with its aspect entry (about 20 B) and an element of its holder's `holds.0` (about 20 B): about 80 B on the wire, about 200 B stored per node (record and entry in the stored commit, state rows, `refs to`). Alone in a commit, add the commit floor below: about 550 B on the wire, 800 B stored per node. Today: 1278 B (365 back) and 2.7-2.9 KB.
- **Small commit [E]**: blob about 320 B (prefix 62, tag 16, header about 170 with the value embedded, signature 66, padding); on the wire about 470 B with its entry (wrap 57, stamp 64, sync ID and heads 30) and no echo; stored per node about 600 B (blob, entry row about 150, record head and meta about 100). Today: 990 B (365 back) and 1.7-1.9 KB. Below that, each further byte is the author's signature, the stamp, the delete key with its commitment, or the wrap: jobs nothing else does.

## Why the big lines are what they are
- **The keyset index names its body, not its KEM key** (`HeldKeyset::kem_in`, the earliest body carrying one; `apply::keys::keyset_kem` reads the key from that body or its dropped record when sealing, a rare path): a copy would cost 4.9 KB stored per independent thing at every node, and the same in memory (`Indexes::keysets` is in memory). The key sealed to is still one a valid noted body of the keyset carries. Earliest by body order, so two bodies of one epoch naming different keys (a writer re-publishing a keyset with junk) give every node the same key in any arrival order (`parts::` index checks compare a rebuild with the live index).
- **A state is stored as its body** (States stored as their body, below): a second copy would cost 8.4 KB per independent thing and 0.16 KB per dependent one, at every node.
- **The record rides in the own-secrets rotation** (The record in the rotation, below): one commit and two signatures fewer per independent thing. Content cannot join it: a commit is of one kind (spec/commits.md §2), and role commits travel as certificates under other sync IDs, where content would leak. So an independent thing made with content is two commits (a minted actor, made without, is one).
- **Dependent things**, engine rows only: 1.6 KB is the commit record (789 B, of which the header is 357 B: three aspect entries with their delta hashes, the delete-key commitment, blind and signatures), the metas row (255 B) and reference rows (178 B). The sync store's sealed blob and entry add as much again (Survey).

## Open
- **Fewer KEM keys held** (Tess: each is fine to do where a worker finds it works and is worth it; she doubts keys are where most bytes go, so measure first). A holder's five KEM keys (four keysets and the step keys' read key) are about 60% of what a reader of it downloads and, after its first grant, stored twice (the commit record, and a `keys.0` state holding two bodies whole).
  - **Store them once** after the first grant: about 6.7 KB per holder, no design change.
  - **Keep them from those who never seal to the thing**: only a granter sealing a box to a thing needs its KEM keys; judging its writes needs none. Matters more if a sync unit carries the evidence for every actor that could write in it (issues/actors-one-mechanism.md).
  - **Only things something seals to publish them**: built (spec/crypto.md §3 Published KEM keys; Measured, above). A grant to a thing not externally grantable by someone who may not write it is refused, never left waiting (spec/permissions.md §2).
  - **Roles sharing a key while their holders coincide**: up to 3.6 KB for a thing with one level of holder; a split when a lower holder first appears. The likeliest to add states.
  - Not wanted (Tess): one ML-KEM key per thing with an X25519 key per role. It saves about 3.5 KB and leaves the separation of roles inside the thing resting on the classical key alone: a wrinkle in the security posture for bytes.
- **CPU: sync-step verifications** (14 per independent make at the maker, 15 at the second node, constant in what is held): the new sync ID's steps verified again at each read. A verified-step memo keyed by step ID would make it one; about half a millisecond per make.

- **Per entry, per sync ID a blob is under** (spec/wire.md §5, §11.2): blob ID, wrap and stamp travel and are stored beside every blob in every stream (measured in the Survey: the `E/` row is 224 B, the frame about 170 B). Candidates there (11, 12); one stamp over many entries does not pay (Survey, Not candidates).

- **Tess, for the slimming work**:
  - A small commit should be one signed chunk of bytes per sync unit. Today it is one blob (header, the signer's signature, sections) plus an entry with a second signature, the stamp. The two serve different readers: the commit's signature is inside the ciphertext for readers; the stamp is for nodes keeping the sync ID unread, which must not learn who wrote. Ask whether one signature can serve both, or one stamp a batch.
  - **Where the data is smaller than what points at it, embed the data**: a copy in every place beats the pointer however many places there are. Seen so far: a state row of 66-70 B naming the commit that holds a 28 B value (States stored as their body, below, wins only above that size); a 32 B delta hash in the header for a delta shorter than a hash; two reference rows of about 89 B for a 15 B tid; a 57 B wrap for a 32 B key.

- **Delete keys per commit** (Tess asked; ideas from reading notes/delete-keys.md, none studied): each delete level a commit's deltas call for costs a `[level, key]` in the header (about 35 B) and a blinded commitment in the blob's prefix (33 B), plus one 16 B blind per commit: about 85 B with one level, 150 with two (the author-or-admin default), 220 with three. Chunk, tree and page blobs carry the commitments again. Candidates:
  - **Name the key, do not carry it**: a reader already holds the admin delete key (the delete base's `keys.0`) and the author's (its signing key or chain); the header could say which epoch's in a byte or two. About 32 B per level.
  - **No level-1 commitment where it equals the sync ID's**: admins of the sync ID's thing already delete at nodes keeping it unread by the sync ID's delete key; a blob of a thing whose delete base is that thing lists the same right twice. About 68 B per such commit.
  - **Derive the blind** from the blob key, as linked blobs already do (the link blind): 16 B.
  - **One blinded commitment per author within a sync unit** (Tess: yes): about 33 B a commit. A node keeping the unit unread can then tell which of its blobs one author can delete; across units the blind still differs, so nothing links an author's blobs from one unit to another, which is what the blind exists to prevent (a node keeping units unread, with any contact's help, linking an author across them).
  - Hashed tokens in place of signatures under blinded keys were decided against by Tess (a writer that is no admin could not list the admin level).

## Rejected
- **No history boxes in a newborn's own-secrets rotation** (about 800 B). They are not empty of use: keysets never list derived epochs (spec/crypto.md §4 Lists), so the history box is what supersedes them; every later holder boxed at the first own epoch opens the rotation commit itself (wrapped under the derived epoch, §5 Who wraps under what: its keysets, boxes, record and `sync.0`) by the derived roles secret that history carries, and a grant writes again only what live entries list or the writer holds as superseded; and a node that wrote under the derived secret before the rotation reached it (TLA Depth0 `Dependent`, `dhist`) is read through it. Dropping them needs a "first rotation supersedes its derived epochs" rule and grants carrying the derived secrets anyway, so the bytes move into every grant. The roles-model has no first-rotation notion, so no search applies.

## The record in the rotation
- **Rule** (spec/permissions.md §3): `Engine::mint_independent`'s things carry its record in its own-secrets rotation. `rotation::own_secrets_new` judges the creation as its commit would, holds the record ahead of the commit (`apply::atomic::learn_record`, timelines updated) so the rotation is built exactly as for a held dependent thing (derived epochs, holders by the record edge, the mint admin's own secrets first), then the rotation mutation `create`s it. `commit_or_build` refuses a create only where a minting is held or the held record differs, so a record held ahead passes once. An expiring first write keeps the record alone with its expiry (a role commit never expires).
- **Not for a link's K, nor a thing made without content** (`Engine::create_with_admin`): their record goes alone, the rotation after. K's readers are its redeemers, who read K's sync ID by entry boxes at its own epochs and never hold the derived one the rotation is wrapped under; a node paired later reads K by the account's holder piece under D (notes/sync-facade.md), untried with K's record moved into the rotation. A thing a records-only commit makes becomes independent at the transaction's end, which lets a maker make it independent itself first (K's sync ID listing L).
- **Who reads it**: the rotation is wrapped under the derived epoch it supersedes (spec/crypto.md §5), so the record section opens for the mint admin's holders and holders granted with history, not for a link key's entry-box, a cut's or a sync-only holder's. They take the record as a record blob: `Waits::records_of` counts a closure body as carrying a record only where a minting of it gives no epochs (`minted_dependent`), as spec/permissions.md §4 Logging certificates counts a rotation's blob only where it opens under the newest read epoch.
- **Rejected**: the record riding with the first content commit instead (the rotation would pend unjudgeable at every receiver until it came, making the rare pending path the common one, and a thing without content would need the record commit anyway).

## States stored as their body
An aspect unit's state is one of: empty, whole (`state::encode`), paged (`pages::open`), or `[2, commit ID]` (`state::Stored::Body`): the state is that commit's part on the aspect, as its record holds it. An independent thing's `keys.0`, `secrets.0` and `sync.0` states and most `created.0` and content states are one body each, so they cost a 66-70 B row instead of a second copy.
- **Rule**: at each transaction's end (`Tx::settle_states`, before the index persists), every unpaged state written in it (`Core::written`) is stored as a body equal to it that is held whole (not a leftover) and valid: the one it names already, else the aspect's newest (`by_aspect`); otherwise whole. Equality, not reasoning about which writes could make them differ: whatever normalised the state (gone writes removed, a snapshot's copies, a dropped char), a reference reads exactly what was cached. Writers (joins, rebuilds, covered drops, strips, purges) write whole as before; one decision point, so no writer has a special case. Cost: the body's decode (`Core::decoded`, per record write) and a compare per written state, O(state) as the write's encode already is. Paged states stay paged: their point is reading a page, and a reference reads the body whole.
- **One reader**: `Tx::load_lazy` resolves it (`apply::stored_fragment`: the record's part, decoded once per record write). Every state read (cache misses, an open's `sync.0` and `account.0` states, the index check) goes through it.
- **A body leaving its record** (`Tx::put_commit` without a part the index holds whole, `unindex`): `Tx::pin_states` first reads each state naming it into the cache and notes it written, so the transaction reads it as before and `settle_states` re-decides it. A valid body's drop rebuilds or rewrites its state anyway (shredded); where it does not (forgetting keeps role-class states, a part set missing again), the state is stored whole from the cache. So no stored reference names a body not held.
- **Shred**: a reference holds no content, so dropping or purging its body leaves no copy in the state; the drop's rebuild writes the unit shredded as before (path rotation covers earlier whole versions). `drop_aspect` (destroy, forget) removes the unit. The covered-drop page rewrite touches paged states only; an unpaged covered drop rewrites whole, then settles.
- **Index**: a reference names a record, not an index row, so a rebuild of the index leaves it valid (rejoined states settle like any write). `apply::check_states` (debug) reads each checked state from the store through the reader, not the cache, against a rebuild from the held bodies.
- **Rejected**: deciding in each writer (the apply joins before its record is stored, so the body is not there yet); a second store of states; references for paged states.
