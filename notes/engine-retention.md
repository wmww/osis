# Engine: retention and dropped bodies
> Summary: How the engine keeps spec/commits.md §5-§7 (`retention/`): the one-drop principle, the thing's history setting and its one door, body classes, when a body was replaced for keep-for-D (a function of the held set), purge on replace as the replacing writer's cover (cost, privacy), own replaces and forgetting, snapshot frontiers, covering, invalid bodies, bodies dropped and coming back, leftovers and the value-less list, purge, waiting parts at expiry or purge, undo's pre-state, and the costs. The spec states the rules; this note keeps reasons, invariants, Tess's decisions, footguns and numbers. Not here: validity and uncovering (notes/engine-validity.md), suspect flags (notes/engine-suspects.md), host deletion judgment (notes/sync.md), memory-class retention (notes/ephemeral.md).

## Principle
What each deletion kind promises users (erasure vs storage reclaim): notes/deletion.md.

A body drops in one of four ways: as covered once out of history when nothing reads it on its own (a held valid writer's compaction snapshot covers it, or two of its author's own later whole replaces do, the purge right or not: nothing promises to keep it, Tess's decision 4, History below), by expiry, by purge, or as invalid (it gives the state nothing, spec/permissions.md §5.2). A node without write drops nothing on its own but expired and invalid content and what writers' covers cover. Every rule below follows from it:
- the cover rule (Fathom): a cover drops a body when it carries every live write of it and nothing reads the body on its own (`bounds::read_alone`), judged per part as a cover drops one part (`drop_part_for`; judged across the body class, a creation commit's live parts kept its replaced purge-on-replace part, and writers without the right re-covered it forever: sim `snapshot_rights`). A held durable delta none of whose writes is dead (`replace::replaced`) is read on its own: a purge by name, a fallen purge bringing it back and listings read it, so it stays, covered or not, until purge, expiry or invalidity (what keeps "only a purge request removes a permanent delta's body" true). A delta with a dead write, a compaction snapshot (copies) and an in-memory body (no purge, host copy or per-delta listing) have no such reader, so the same rule drops them. Every covered-drop path asks it after the cover and keep bound: node-sync's `drop_covered`, `own_covers_every_part`, `settle_none`, memory's `drop_covered`. Cost: a snapshot changes nothing for a body read on its own, so node-sync judges it only when one of its writes dies: every durable join computes its killed writes (`retention::Killed`), `mark_killed` marks their commits and adds them to `Core::cover_candidates`; a snapshot's arrival puts only the aspect's candidates and snapshots in the work set (`Core::cover_judged_on`, `work::prepare`; `scale::aspect_pass_is_flat`), and a delta found read alone leaves the set. A rebuild (`mark_bounded`) makes every held delta of the aspect a candidate again (it read them all), an open every held delta (the set is not stored: `index::load`), and a drop takes the part out (only held parts are candidates). Limit: a write dying by a rebuild re-judges an unkept aspect's bodies only at the next snapshot or full pass, as a rebuild marks only bounded bodies changed (kept longer, never dropped early); test tombstone.rs `a_body_replaced_before_a_restart_drops_under_a_later_cover_*`. Rejected: any writer's cover dropping a live delta out of history (a refused purge's re-snapshot then dropped a body a fallen purge must show again: tombstone.rs, sim `purge_falls`). Tests: tombstone.rs `a_live_body_survives_a_covering_snapshot_*`, sync_more `flip_after_drop_a_writers_cover_keeps_the_killer_*`;
- what history keeps is the thing's admins' setting (`retention^.0`, History, below), never the write; it only limits when a covered drop may happen, at every node alike;
- purge on replace is a duty to write the cover: the writer whose transaction replaced the body covers it, and the cover drops it everywhere (`retention/replace.rs`);
- no history promised has neither: writers compact when they like (node-sync, notes/sync.md);
- a node holds no body it judges invalid past the transaction that judged it (Invalid bodies, below): every read is a function of the valid writes held (a sequence's order included, spec/crdt.md §6.1), so the drop changes no read;
- a purge never drops a snapshot: it has no values of its own, so no request names one (`purge::names`; it never reads purged, whether held whole, as its leftover or dropped as covered, so a listing stays a function of the held set, not of a node's compaction), and its copies of purged values go as any carrying body's (held as its leftover). A cover therefore falls only by turning invalid, its author's edit under a demotion that did not list it (spec/permissions.md §5.2 Limit);
- covering is the join and nothing else: a body is covered when the snapshot carries every live write of it. A snapshot carries an expiring delta's values value-less (spec/crdt.md §8), so the join covers such a delta once none of its values lives, before T as after (nothing visible of it can return: its writes are dead); "expiring is never covered" is not a rule, only what the join says while a value lives;
- keep-for-D needs only when the body was replaced, a function of the held set judged when asked (Replaced, below): nothing about it is stored, dated per node or replayed;
- the fixed bounds are keep at least, so they too only delay a covered drop: `ROLE_KEEP` after replaced for role-class bodies, a purge request's floor on its commit's blobs, `created.0` and `retention^.0` never out of history;
- a signal's body (`~` without `+`) is in no state, so nothing covers it: it is replaced when written (`bounds::part_replaced_at` returns its own `ts`; `body_class` keeps it apart from other deltas) and kept at least to its keep bound under the setting's entry for its AK (Tess: keeping history means how long a write is exposed, never a deletion promise); a writer refuses a fire no setting it holds keeps (`write.rs`). Past the bound it is past every bound, so a node may drop it; this engine does, whole or as its leftover (at once when past at apply or late join, else by its aspect's walk, Leaving history below, or a `T_KEPT` timer where its commit's purge floor keeps it longer; `drop_part_for` takes a signal's bare leftover too), so data nobody promised to keep is not kept for good and needs no compaction. Convergence with a node that keeps it: reads judge by the bound from the header, held or not (`listing::past_due`); no copy past the bound is taken (`takes_late`: an offered copy is a duplicate, so no resend loop, `signals::signal_dropped_past_its_bound_not_taken_again_*`), and nothing makes it missing again (`past_signal_bound` in `valid_again` and `purge::rejudge_purges`, as expiry: no refetch, no suspect flag that nothing could clear); node-sync never counts a signal part established uncovered, so it is must-hold only while kept (`must_hold`). No join, rebuild, snapshot or cover touches it (`join_part`, `rebuild`, `rebuilt` and `Engine::snapshot` return early), so a validity flip only changes which bodies count;
- expiry and purge are the principle's two exceptions and hold a body as its value-less leftover, which then leaves only as covered (past T) or with its thing; a waiting part keeps its body until it applies (no cover can judge it), and goes before only by those two.
Outside the four: a thing's bodies go whole when the node loses read access to it (spec/permissions.md §3, `retention/forget.rs`), and memory-class bodies vanish when superseded or at a delete here (notes/ephemeral.md Divergences left). There is no keep-at-most bound; the only timers are expiry, one per (thing, AK) at the next body leaving history (Leaving history, below: a fire's body drops, the engine's choice; a value part is only told to commit listeners and node-sync), a purge request's header keeping a part (`T_KEPT`: its commit's purge floor, per part like an expiry; a purged fire's tombstone) and a purge request's floor (`T_FLOOR`: a covered purge-on-replace body of a commit carrying a request waits for it, spec/commits.md §5 Purge).

## History: the thing's setting, one door (`retention/history.rs`)
- The setting: `retention^.0`, admin-only, fixed retention (`nobody`, never out of history, never snapshotted or undone), written whole. `ix.retention` holds each thing's held valid settings by key (kept on apply, validity flip and drop, rebuilt at open); `validity::settings` reads them. A setting's arrival, flip or drop marks its thing (`Core::settings_due`), and `retention::settings_changed` judges again at the end of the transaction, per aspect of the thing: the bodies that leave history or come back by the new timeline (Leaving history, below), and purge on replace where some setting asks for it (`none_due`: a setting arriving can make a writer owe a cover). node-sync's cached keep bounds of the thing's commits go with it (`Core::settings_moved`, `Engine::settings_moved_after`: per thing, so a change costs node-sync the thing's bodies, not the node's). Cost: the bodies that move and O(log) per aspect; no body read (`scale::setting_change_and_window_passing_are_flat_spec_commits_5`). An aspect turning dated has every held part dated once (O(its history), the first keeping setting of an aspect with held bodies).
- The pure rules are osis-commit's `retention::Timeline` (`out`, `kept_until`, `in_force`, the one-way rule), matching held-model's `thing_history`: a setting acts from its own stamp, bodies of an aspect leave in replaced order, purge on replace keeps nothing at any moment (AMBIGUITIES 68).
- Every reader asks `history::of(core, tid, ak) -> Kept` (the thing's timeline read for the AK; fixed retention: `ROLE_KEEP` after replaced for role-class and `addenda.0`, never out for `created.0`/`retention^.0`, nothing for `purge.0`): `leaves_at(replaced)`, `dated`, `keeps`, `purge_on_replace(r)`, `kept_whenever_replaced`, `purges`, `forever`; `history::permanent` on top. Readers: `bounds::kept_until` (with the purge floor: `body_keep`, `body_keeps`, `signal_bound`, listing), `bounded`, listing, undo, memory `superseded`, `replace::under_purge`, node-sync through `Engine::body_keeps`, `keeps_unreplaced`, `Engine::permanent`. `View` stores nothing that depends on a setting: its flags are the header's alone (`index.rs` debug-asserts the meta row equals `View::of(header)`).
- The wire carries no history: a part's retention is its purgeable-by alone. A snapshot's is `writer`; its history is the setting's like any body's.
- The writer fills a write's expiry from the newest valid setting it holds (`settle`), refuses a replace under a `final` entry, and refuses a fire no setting keeps.
- Principle: a thing's admins decide what replaced content is kept and shown, and for how long; the setting never decides who may drop a body (purgeable-by, per write) nor deletes anything promptly (purge, expiry). Every judgment reads only the settings and bodies a node holds and the clock, nothing dated by arrival, so nodes holding the same bodies and settings show the same history; a late or fallen setting moves bodies in or out only where still held, and no resend loop arises (a body out of history is neither must-hold nor fetched, one in history at a node that dropped it is not fetched; settings are must-hold for good). Under a dispute each side's timeline is the settings valid under its trust set.
- At hosts the setting is the promise: a body in history is must-hold whoever's snapshot covers it (node-sync `must_hold` reads `part_kept` before any cover), so a label its thing joins and a host that lost its copy get it again from any holder; out of history a covered body is may-hold and goes under the cover. No loop: a cover never drops a kept body, and a right holder deletes at hosts only past the bound plus `MAX_DRIFT`; a holder judging the bound later (other bodies held) does not place it again while the label's snapshot carries its writes (`delete_stands`). Test `deletion::second_label_gets_kept_bodies_spec_sync_6` (a plain writer's cover, a new label, a wiped host).
- Tess's decisions: (1) "never comes back" is a property of the timeline: a setting arriving late may re-show a body a node still holds; no per-node marks. (2) Admins may always purge: purgeable-by is `admin` (the default), `author_or_admin` or `writer` (`nobody` only for `created.0` and fixed retention); no per-write keep-forever; shortening needs only the admin right and reaches every body, which leave an aspect in replaced order (one threshold per thing and AK). (3) Purge on replace is one way: where an earlier setting purges an AK on replace, a later `keep` there reads as no history (model-checked: without it late dating lets purged content show). (4) Out-of-history bodies drop under any valid writer's cover: data nobody promised is not kept for good. (5) Who-may-purge, edits off (`final`) and the expiry default are writer defaults only, no validity rule: a commit is valid with what it carries, and a change reaches later writes only.
- Security: keep is promised only by nodes that hold the setting. Beyond purge, an admin can only shorten windows (letting writers' compaction drop bodies and any node drop a signal's entries); it cannot touch live values or force a prompt deletion, and a backdated setting ends no more than one stamped now. A writer ignoring the setting can only give its own writes another expiry or purgeable-by.
- Model: held-model `thing_history.rs` and its properties (`tests/it/spec_thing_history.rs`, also through `evaluate`): bodies leave in replaced order, a setting stamped after a body left never brings it back, a late setting may re-show a held body, purge-on-replace content is never history however late dated (and is without the one-way rule: a test), an eager-dropping node shows a subset of a keep-everything node in every arrival order.
- Replaced (keep-for-D): a body counts from the latest death among its writes, a snapshot's among its writes no held valid snapshot stamped later carries (`bounds::kept_until` takes the date): each write's history is kept by the last snapshot carrying it, so compacting a kept write while live loses none of it (held-model `snapshot_replaced_at_last_death_no_later_snapshot_carries_*`). Counting every carried write instead kept every snapshot of an aspect whose long-lived writes keep dying within D (each carries them all): held snapshots grew with the edit count, not the D window.

## Leaving history (`retention/leaving.rs`)
- Bodies of one aspect leave in replaced order, so per (thing, AK): the places in replaced order, a cursor (every place at or before it is out) and one timer at the first place past it (`Indexes::leave_from`, persisted `hf/`; `leave_due`). Places: a signal's fires by `ts` (`by_aspect`; a held kept fire makes or moves the cursor, `fire_held`); a value aspect's rows (`Indexes::leaving`, persisted `hr/`; `dated` by part), one per held valid part replaced on a dated aspect at its replaced-at, a purged part at its request's `ts`, in history or out (a late setting may bring one back). Leaving: a value part is told to listeners and marked for node-sync; a held fire drops, or at its commit's purge floor if later (`T_KEPT`).
- A tick walks the due aspects' places that leave; a setting change walks those leaving by the new timeline and, on a value aspect, back from the cursor over those it brings back. Each O(the bodies that move); no body read, no walk of one that stays.
- Rows only time events; every read judges from the held set when asked. `date_due` (a transaction's end) dates the parts `mark_killed`/`mark_bounded` marked (`Core::to_date`) and every part with a row or stripped whose meta changed, the aspect's held bodies read once for all (`Core::held_writes_memo`); a moved row is told and marked. So a kill on a dated aspect reads the aspect's held bodies once in the transaction as well as in node-sync's judgment. A date moving earlier with no kill (an earlier superseder of an already dead write arriving) leaves its row late: told and marked at the row's time, after the body left.
- Rows are kept whoever listens, so a listener added later (or a thing joining a listened group) needs no arming. Rejected: a timer per body (a setting change re-armed every body of the thing, reading each; stale timers fired at each old bound).
- A purge request's header keeps its commit's parts to its purge floor, a time no setting moves: a header event like an expiry, so a per-part `T_KEPT` timer, armed for a value part when it arrives (`on_parts`; told then if its listing ended, `listing::kept_passed`), live or not and on any aspect (an aspect no setting keeps has no rows, yet a replaced part stays listed to the floor: `listing::purge_floor_part_told_at_its_floor_spec_commits_5`). Timeline-dated events are the cursor's, header-dated ones per-part timers. A purged fire's tombstone (`edits::settle`, at its request's `ts` on the timeline) stays a per-part timer, not a place: signal places are fires by their own `ts`, and a setting change moves it only later (rearmed when it fires) or leaves it late.
- Never early (debug check in `left`, sampled per aspect and generation): a value row leaving finds its part out of history by a fresh judgment. Rows may be late, never early.
- Cost per kill: dating reads the aspect's held bodies once per transaction (`held_writes`): one overwrite of a kept register with n held bodies reads n+1 (21 at 20, 201 at 200), on top of node-sync's own judgment. O(the aspect's held history) per edit, not O(change).

## Bodies
- A body is the commit's one blob (notes/whole-commits.md). Listing is per part: a part is current by its own writes only (`delta_live` in `listing::in_kept_history`, `keep_bound`, `listed`) and in history by its own aspect's keep (`body_history_keep`); another live part of the same body keeps the bytes, not this part's listing (Fathom; setting.rs `a_body_class_keeps_by_its_kept_aspect_*`). Retention's `body_class` still groups its deltas by (reader actor, retention, delete-key indices): its keep bound is the latest of its parts' own (`class_keep`): each part dated by its own writes' deaths alone and kept by its own aspect's timeline alone (`part_replaced_at`; a part on an aspect no setting keeps adds no bound), so the body stays while any part is current or inside its own keep (Fathom: a setting governs its aspect's data, nothing else; Dated only where history is kept, below), and a kill marks bodies for judging only on an aspect a setting keeps (`bounded`), a part drops with the rest of its class, and the blob is rewritten without it. The class depends on what is held (the reader actor, `Core::reader_of`: up the record chain through first-admin links to the nearest actor with secrets of its own), so nothing kept may be keyed by it. Expiry is per commit; purge drops single deltas (the commit is kept without them).
- A held body is judged "held" by the stored blob holding the part (`apply::stores_part`), not by meta alone: during a join the commit is indexed before it is stored, and a rebuild must not count its own delta as held then. A part still to arrive (`Meta::missing`, a late part) is not held (`retention::held_part`): it is not judged and not dropped before it arrives.

## Replaced, for keep-for-D (`retention/bounds.rs` `part_replaced_at`, `dated_replaced_at`)
- Tess's rule (issues/retention-modes.md): a body under keep-for-D is not dropped until D after the earliest valid replacing write the node holds, a function of the held set. Judged when asked (node-sync's `body_keeps` per commit judged, `drop_from` before a compaction drop, `Engine::replaced_at`), never stored: no death records, no replays, no crediting. Whether a write is dead, and when, is a function of the held bodies, never of how a part is paged.
- **Which writes died** (`deaths`): the part's winning writes (`Fragment::winners`, spec/crdt.md §4.8: by position, kind and stamp, never by stamp alone, since one op stamps many writes) that do not win once the part joins, whole, a copy of the state at its positions (`Tx::state_part_at`, `Fragment::part_at`: O(part), not a copy of the state). Winners, not absence: a position write beaten as `cur` stays as `prev`, a deleted text creation stays in its record, and a write beaten by one in another record of its map dot stays in its own; `winners` leaves all three out, and is checked against a pairwise from-scratch definition (osis-crdt `tests_winners`, which also checks that a write dead in a join of two dies by a write the other side holds, any clocks). The part's own superseded writes are not its winners, so a superseder it holds kills nothing of it, and the state is the un-merged join of the held bodies: another held body holds a superseder of every dead write (none: `Error::Corrupt`). None dead: past a relative bound. Cost: the part decoded whole, O(part) memory for every body judged, live or dead (notes/engine-paged.md Left whole).
- **When** (`HeldWrites`, spec/crdt.md §4.8 When): a dead write dies at the earliest `ts` among the held valid writes superseding it alone (the pairwise rule of `supersedes` in osis-crdt `winners.rs`, as held-model's `replaced_at`), read from any held valid body other than the part's own holding one: its own commit or a snapshot carrying it. Always the write's own stamp, never the holding body's: a valid snapshot's copy of a write whose delta is held invalid here (vouched) counts as the write itself (Fathom). Dating a vouched copy at its snapshot was rejected: dropping that snapshot under a later cover redated the write to the cover's stamp and brought a body out of history back (setting.rs `vouched_copy_dates_at_its_write_*`). The cost is the writer-`ts` limit every write has (a writer stamping early shortens what history keeps), here storage reclaim only, never erasure (notes/deletion.md; spec/commits.md §5 Bodies and deaths). A text element's creation dies only by a delete stamped after the latest undelete of the element held (`ByWrite::first_superseding`), so dropping the first delete of a delete, undelete, delete moves nothing. The aspect's held bodies are read once into an index by write (`osis_crdt::ByWrite`): every write each body holds, winning there or not (`Fragment::held_writes`: a deleted text creation still supersedes position writes below it), as the state joins it (invalid writes left out as un-merge does), with the two earliest bodies by `(ts, ID)` holding each (one is not the part judged); `ByWrite::first_superseding` reads the positions on the dead write's path in stamp order and stops at the first stamp past the best date found. Every body dates at the latest death of its writes, deltas too: a body drops only after every dead write it alone holds has left history. A snapshot first leaves out the dead writes a later held valid snapshot holds as winners, exactly (`HeldWrites::carried`, each snapshot's winners whole: that one keeps their history; holding one as `prev` or deleted keeps none). Holding more bodies dates no later; holding the same dates alike. A text delete and undelete are two kinds of flag write (`Kind::Delete`, `Kind::Undelete`), so only a delete supersedes its element's creation.
- **Invariant: a valid covered drop never changes whether any held body is in history**, nor its listing, nor the keep bound of a held body in history, nor the replaced-at of a part in history (a part out of history may redate, staying out, inside a body another part keeps); only arrivals (bodies, settings, validity changes) and the clock do. A drop is valid only past the body's keep bound (`drop_from <= now`, D4); a forced drop of a body in history (`Engine::drop_body` skips the bound) is outside it. Every date a judgment reads, and why such a drop of body B (out: `out(r_B) <= now`) cannot move it (`out` keeps replaced order):
  - a superseder's `ts` (D1): the write's own stamp from whichever held body holds it (`HeldWrites::holds` only says one other than the part judged does); a live write of B stays in the cover with its stamp; a write W only a dead write d of B dated redates to at most death(d) <= r_B (supersession is transitive), so W was out and stays out;
  - a snapshot's dead writes a later held valid snapshot S carries (D2, `HeldWrites::carried`): S dropping adds back deaths <= r_S; a body in history has r > r_S, so its date stays; one out stays out;
  - a text creation's latest held undelete (D3): an undelete's body drops only once the undelete is dead, by a delete no earlier than the creation's date, so the creation was out and only dates earlier;
  - the state (`deaths`, `body_live`): a cover carries every live write of B, so the state is unchanged;
  - headers, which outlive every drop: a fire's own `ts`, the purge floor, a purged part's request `ts` (purge-request commits are never forgotten), `kept_whenever_replaced`, undo's bound; the settings timeline (`retention^.0` is never out of history, so never covered-dropped); the clock (`clock_keep`). Forgetting is outside it (spec/commits.md §5 Forgetting, Limit; Fathom): it drops the header of a commit whose parts all went under its author's own cover, and undo then counts the aspect's forgotten `ts` (`undo::replaced_kept`), so an undo excused by that part's age or a snapshot reaching it is refused after. Forgetting may narrow what undo allows, never widens it and never brings back data. It is local only: the undoing node's own refusal, never a rule a receiver applies (spec/crdt.md §4.6), so no two nodes disagree on a commit by it; keeping more to excuse the part would be a lasting trace (notes/deletion.md).
  Each argument is per aspect: a part is dated by its own aspect's writes and kept by its own aspect's timeline (Dated only where history is kept, below), so a drop on one aspect moves nothing of another's parts.
  Tests: setting.rs `covered_drops_move_nothing_*` (random histories on a kept and an unkept aspect, some commits writing both in one class, with removals no addendum answers, so snapshots vouch; a node holding every body drops each covered body past its bound in random order and checks every held part's listing, history status, keep bounds and replaced-at; vouched copies dated at their snapshot failed 3 of its seeds, a class dated across aspects 1), `an_unkept_part_dates_no_class_*`, `vouched_copy_dates_at_its_write_*`, `a_covered_drop_redates_nothing_*`, `a_body_dies_at_its_latest_death_*`, `text_creation_dies_at_delete_after_undelete_*` (each drops what node-sync would, in several orders, and checks every held body's listing and keep).
- Late arrival: a body arriving after its earliest superseder dropped is dated by what is held, so later, and may show until that leaves history where a node holding both would not: an arrival, as a late setting is (Tess's decision 1, History; held-model's any-order property allows exactly that class).
- The index (`Core::held_writes`, `HeldWritesCache`) is kept per aspect while the engine's `generation` and `memory_generation` stay, built and read only in a transaction that changed nothing yet (`Core::dirty`): every change to a held body, a validity or a state forgets it, so a pass judging every body of an aspect reads each body once. `HELD_WRITES_MAX` (2^20 writes) bounds the others kept beside the aspect read last. Debug builds check the 1st, 4th, 16th, ... cache hit against a fresh read.
- Limit: a snapshot dropped while a later one carried its dead writes is not brought back if that later one turns invalid (its cover falls) before their D passes: those writes' history goes with it, as a body dropped as covered by a snapshot that falls (never received, not fetched).
- Debug check (osis-engine's tests): `deaths` against the part joined into the whole state (`oracle::deaths`).
- Cost counted, not timed: a judgment walks the part judged for its winners a fixed number of times, never once per death (`retention::tests::reset_judged_walks_parts_once_*`, `Core::death_walks`); the index reads each held body of the aspect once per change (`deaths: a body`), so judging every body of an aspect of n and 10n bodies reads about one body per judgment (`scale::judging_every_body_reads_each_once_spec_commits_5`). A judgment right after a change still reads the aspect's held bodies: O(the aspect's history) per change that kills a write.
- A snapshot on a frequently edited kept aspect (`scale::kept_snapshots_stay_within_their_window_spec_commits_5`): every held snapshot judged after each death, as `mark_killed` asks, and one past its bound dropped as covered: the snapshots held stay at the D window (3 at 8 and at 80 edits), death walks and replaced reads flat; judging one reads it whole, O(the set's state). Counting every carried write held all of them.
- node-sync judges a body again when it may have been replaced: an apply's join marks the commits owning the writes it superseded (the state's winners at the delta's reach and the delta's own, against the join's, `retention::Killed`) and the aspect's snapshots (`mark_killed`; a reset joined by pages, not judged write by write, marks every bounded body); a rebuild (flip, un-merge, drop, expiry) every held bounded body of the aspect (`mark_bounded`).
- **Dated only where history is kept** (spec/commits.md §5 Keep bounds): a covered drop needs the body out of history on the node's clock, `out(r) <= now`, and `out(r) >= r`, so a replacement dated after the clock is not judged until the clock reaches it (`bounds::clock_keep` adds `r` to the drop bound: `body_keep`, `body_keeps`, so `drop_from`, `settle_none`, memory drops). Per part: a part is dated only where some held setting keeps history for its own aspect (`bounded`, in `part_replaced_at`), by its own writes alone, and kept by that aspect's timeline alone; elsewhere nothing is at stake and the part adds no bound, ahead of the clock or not, whatever the thing's or the body's other aspects keep (`replaced_ahead_of_the_clock_waits_only_where_kept_*`, `a_body_class_keeps_by_its_kept_aspect_*`). The body leaves when every part may (`class_keep`, the latest). Rejected: thing level (an aspect waited or not by unrelated aspects); a class dated by the latest death of all its parts and kept by each aspect's timeline at that one date (a covered drop of an undated body on an unkept aspect redated the class's kept part, so a body out of history came back: setting.rs `an_unkept_part_dates_no_class_*`; the invariant's argument compares dates on one aspect's timeline). Listing reads `body_history_keep` (the part's own history and the purge floor alone: purge on replace and no history list nothing, AMBIGUITIES 68). Limit: a wall clock set back is not noticed, so node-sync's cached keeps judged before it stand.
- **Host delete margin** (Fathom): `MAX_DRIFT` applies to host deletes only. A local drop changes only this node's reads, which follow its clock, so it happens at the keep bound; a right holder deletes the body at hosts `MAX_DRIFT` later, once every node's clock agrees it left. `drop_part_for` records it as the dropped record's `Dropped::hosts_from` (the keep bound judged while the body is still held, plus `MAX_DRIFT`; 0 when it had none); node-sync's delete judgment (`judge`) waits for it through the pass's note time. No timer or row of its own: it lives and goes with the dropped record (`deletion::host_delete_waits_drift_past_history_*`).
- Rejected: dating a superseder by its own commit's header (the header outlives the body). The earliest superseding write may be held in no body any more (the cover holds a later write); a header names the aspect, not the writes it superseded. Stored per-node dates: dated by arrival. Keeping a dropped-by-cover body while it dates a held write: conflicts with purge on replace dropping it at once.
- `settle_none` drops an out-of-history covered body at its keep bound, as node-sync's `drop_from`.
- Rejected: per-node death dates stored per body (dated by arrival order, with crediting of carrying snapshots, recomputes after flips and a text re-delete rule: the class of bugs Tess's rule removes); dating at the superseder the state holds now (the latest: a register's old bodies would stay kept for as long as it keeps changing within D); walking the later bodies per judgment, joining each against the part (a pass quadratic in the aspect's bodies and their sizes); deaths reported by the join (a stamp per death: a stamp names many writes, so counting reports per stamp dated snapshots early, and a second definition beside `winners` to keep in step).
- Cost (`scale::late_delta_deaths_are_flat_spec_crdt_4_8`): a late delta older than n and 10n held one-element commits, or 5 commits late, applied and the first body judged: every counted phase flat (death walks, replaced reads), in an unbounded set, a kept one and a compacted kept one. Judging is paid per node-sync judgment of a bounded body, which reads the body for covering anyway; stored dates would cost a recompute per flip and a record read per judgment.
- Tests: retention.rs `snapshot_carried_per_write_not_per_stamp_*` (one stamp, values dead at different writes), `snapshot_dated_by_deleted_creations_not_carried_*`, `snapshot_not_carried_by_a_beaten_position_*`, `skewed_clocks_dated_at_earliest_superseder_*`, `deaths_dated_from_first_superseder_held_*`, `snapshot_replaced_at_latest_death_not_at_copies_*`, `deaths_never_early_random_*` (random histories against a node keeping every body: dated never earlier, a node lacking bodies may date what it does not; the same judgment on more bodies, not an independent truth; `OSIS_STRAYS_START`/`_SEEDS`); the independent truth is held-model's `replaced_at` (order.rs `arrival_order_kept_*`, `kept_snapshot_replaced_at_latest_death_any_order_*`: no keep bound before D after it), `snapshot_dated_through_*`, `text_delete_replaces_at_earliest_delete_held_*`; order.rs `replaced_alike_in_any_order_*`, `text_redelete_replaced_at_delete_after_undelete_*`, `snapshot_after_late_dated_death_not_credited_*`; pass_marks `body_judged_once_per_body_*` (`Engine::replaced_reads`).

## Purge on replace (`retention/replace.rs`)
- The writer's cover, the principle's one drop (Tess, issues/retention-modes.md): at the end of every transaction (`Engine::run`, `settle_none`), over the aspects whose purge-on-replace bodies or snapshots changed (`Core::none_due`, filled by `Core::put_meta` for any commit on an aspect some setting of its thing purges on replace (`Core::note_none`), by a rebuild, by a setting changing (`retention::settings_changed`), and by a timeline change on the thing: a grant can make this node a writer owing a cover, retention.rs `none_granted_writer_covers_what_it_holds_replaced_*`): a node that may write a snapshot of the aspect (`snapshot::may_snapshot` over `write::may_write`, the write's own checks: the thing exists, `writer_for`, `snapshot_role`, the thing's minting and delete keys; every writer holds the `writer` right) writes one from its whole state (`snapshot::write_snapshot`, at most one per aspect per transaction) when a held body under purge on replace (the setting in force at its replaced-at, `under_purge`) is replaced and no held valid snapshot holding the right over it covers it (`coverer` with `right`; below for a writer without the right); then every covered body of the aspect out of history drops as covered (any writer's cover), at every node (readers too: the snapshot's commit holds the right). The cover is written in a `Tx::step` (`Step::Cover`): a failure (the write's own check refusing after a removal sweep moved its stamp past an `until`) leaves the body to the writers that may, is reported (`OwnWriteFailed`) and never fails a receive. A function of the held set: the order things came in changes when, never whether.
- **Waiting to write** (`MayWrite::Later`: role held, minting or delete keys not yet here): the thing goes into `Indexes::cover_waits` under every thing on its record chain (journaled with the indexes, so a failed transaction gives back a wait it ended: one lost to a rollback would never wake); at each transaction's end while any wait, `replace::wake_covers` reads what the transaction changed (the journals of `ix.commits` for `created.0`/`keys.0`/`secrets.0` commits, `ix.secrets`, `ix.own_keys`, `ix.delete_hints` after `promote_hints`) and re-judges only the waiting things under those, marking the aspects of each that may now write due. A role, a record edge or an epoch's establishment moving comes through the timeline trigger instead (`Core::timelines_changed` marks every purge-on-replace aspect under a moved roster). So the last to arrive of body, replacement, role and keys triggers the cover, and the end is a function of the held set (retention.rs `writer_covers_once_its_delete_keys_come_in_any_order_*`). O(what changed), nothing when nothing waits; in memory only (the first transaction after an open settles every held purge-on-replace aspect, refilling it). Rejected: marking every purge-on-replace aspect below a base whose keys changed (O(the base's aspects) at every rotation); remembering per base whether its keys are held (as many inputs to track as the journals already record).
- **Replaced** (`replaced`): the body is valid, holds a live write by dot the state does not (or one of its winning writes wins no longer in the join), and joins into the state's part it meets with its canonical bytes unchanged: dead, not un-merged under an invalid record (which would make a snapshot that cannot cover it, every transaction). Not "has a dead write": a root reset dies once a later write implies one yet canonical form keeps it (held-model-4). A delta with an expiry held with its values is judged against the state with its values value-less, as the cover will carry them: replaced only once none of its values lives, so the duty arises exactly when a snapshot of the state covers it (no snapshot every transaction for a body no snapshot can cover).
- Every replacing write brings a snapshot: the replaced body drops under it; the new body stays (live, read on its own: the cover rule) until it is replaced in turn, so an aspect under purge on replace holds its live deltas and one snapshot at the writer after each transaction. Readers keep replaced bodies until a writer's snapshot arrives: some writer must hold both the body and its replacement (the editor need not hold the body, a concurrent write; every writer fetches both in time); with none online they keep them (no timer: a node without write acts on its own only for expiry).
- A writer whose own snapshot would hold no right over the body (`purge::would_hold_right`) owes none while another held valid snapshot covers it: that cover drops it here already, and its own would neither drop more nor keep the promise at hosts. Without this, under a narrow purge set (`admin`, `author`) every plain writer wrote a useless cover on every replacement, as the replaced body, live until then, is covered only by snapshots without the right (sim `snapshot_rights`: 3 covers per edit, each snapshot's header kept for good, Forgetting, ran a node over its state bound).
- Cost (spec/requirements.md Scale, the cost the app chooses): every replacing edit costs a snapshot of the aspect, O(aspect state), at every writer holding the replaced body uncovered; in practice the editor alone: it uploads its snapshot with the edit, and node-sync ingests a batch's compaction snapshots before its plain commits (`open_entries`), so a writer receiving both finds the body covered (by ts order the edit applied first, and every writer wrote its own). Duplicates (a writer that applied the edit before the snapshot arrived) are harmless (spec/sync.md §7). Measured in notes/growth.md (purge on replace).
- Rejected: a keep-at-most bound and its timers, carriers written by node-sync before a timed drop, readers dropping at the bound with no carrier, invalid bodies counted dead at their own `ts`: each needed death dating, a live-write judgment per body and a carve-out per kind of body (snapshots dropped live, never received after a flip, oracle excuses); the cover rule needs none of them.
- Rejected: writer-side purge per edit (Tess's fallback if this proves costly): a purge cannot express a partial replacement of a body, a purging commit keeps its blobs `ROLE_KEEP`, every edit leaves a leftover.
- Privacy (spec/commits.md §5): erased data persists in its body at a writer for no transaction, at a reader until the writer's snapshot arrives, at a host until a right holder next syncs. The read state loses it at the kill, and the drop shreds like any.
- Liveness (`retention::live`, `Engine::delta_live`; `body_live` is any delta of the class) stays for node-sync's host deletes (a body with a live write goes from a label only with a carrier, spec/sync.md §7): judged per delta on the current state, cached per delta and forgotten when its aspect's state changes (`Core::forget_live`; `Fragment::holds_owned_write`: an element's or entry's tombstone holds no write of its creator, order seed 64197). A deleted text element's creation stays in the state as its anchor, so a text body fully erased is live too.
- Tests: retention.rs `none_*` (the cover in the replacing transaction, readers dropping on arrival), setting.rs (a late purge-on-replace setting makes holders cover), held.rs model comparison, sim `snapshot_rights`.

## Snapshots: frontiers, covering (`retention/snapshot.rs`)
- Frontiers list the sessions occurring in the state (`osis_crdt::sources_of`; a session with a write left but no contiguous prefix applied gets a 0 entry, spec/crdt.md §8), each covering its counters contiguously applied and valid on the aspect; `Absorbed` lists the commit-form commits occurring in it.
- `covered` judges a held body by the join, with the snapshot's copies under the body's dots stripped first so a differing copy (forged, greater or not) never covers; the body's value-less writes are left out of that strip (`Fragment::valueless_dots`): a snapshot whose writer held a purge this node does not (a fork, or one that fell) holds a live write value-less, and later snapshots hold it valued, so the strict compare would keep that snapshot for as long as the write lives. Tables speak only for one not held (an absent session or a 0 entry says nothing of what joins). Canonical-bytes comparisons there must stay encodings, not `Fragment ==`/`contains`: a deleted text element's char survives a join in memory but not in canonical bytes (deletion::text_partly_erased). An expiring delta held with its values is judged by the join (covered once none of its values lives: retention.rs `dead_expiring_delta_covered_before_its_expiry_*`, `purge_on_replace_covers_dead_expiring_write_*`, ephemeral.rs `dead_expiring_ephemeral_write_covered_before_expiry_*`); one not held and not value-less is not covered (the frontier alone would say it is). `receipt::settled` (suspect flags) is any snapshot of the aspect stamped after the commit, valid here.
- Out of history a compaction drop needs only a held valid writer's snapshot covering the body (`bounds::drop_from`: no right asked; node-sync's `drop_covered`): nothing promises to keep it, and dropping it is no purge. The purge right counts where a promise is kept: purge on replace's duty (`replace::coverer` with `right`), deleting at hosts (delete keys), purge requests (`purge::snapshot_right`).
- A snapshot's retention is `writer` (or the aspect's fixed one): what history keeps of it is the thing's setting, like any body's (`osis_commit::retention::snapshot_retention`).
- Copy indexes (`Core::copy_index`, `copies::copy_index`): a held snapshot's writes by dot, built from what `each_held_piece` reads (its whole body, or its leftover: keys erased, values gone) and cached per commit, forgotten whenever the commit's stored body or leftover changes (`put_commit`, `remove_commit`, `leftover::put`/`remove`) and on rollback. Debug builds check every cache hit against an index built anew: a stale index vouches for records from a creation copy the strip erased, which a fresh one does not, so a reopen would change what an un-merge keeps. In the un-merge (`Unmerge::vouches`) a record a valid snapshot holds key-erased keeps every record of its dot, the state's own key-erased record (asked with no key) included: nested writes of other deltas live there.
- Known schemas, one per app AK, never from the wire (`Engine::register_schema`, which replaces one of a lower or equal version for each AK it declares, or a local typed write, which replaces only a lower version; kept under `d/` + AK, read at open) serve undo's typed views and large write-back after a restart, not snapshots. `osis::Osis::aspect_key` types only by this session's registrations, so the CLI's `schema rm` untypes.

## Own replaces and forgetting (`retention/snapshot.rs` `own_*`, node-sync `drop_covered`, `forget_own_covered`; spec/commits.md §5 Own replaces, Forgetting)
- A plain whole replace (root reset or register write, `Meta::whole`) is its own cover: no compaction step. `Indexes::own_held` and `own_replaces` key held plain parts by (thing, aspect, author), so finding a commit's covers never walks the aspect. `covered` asks for two valid held own replaces later than the body (the writer's previous replace stays until the next lands: one late replace that later counts for nothing loses nothing), each passing the join.
- A commit goes only whole (`own_covers_every_part`: every held part covered by own replaces, and past every part's keep bound): a reader cannot rewrite a blob, so dropping one part would leave it unable to relay the rest. Such a commit stays while any part is uncovered (issues/zero-growth.md Left). Out of history the author need not hold the purge right over a part: a single writer of an `admin` aspect stays flat.
- A cover that turns invalid or drops other than as covered gives back what it alone covered (`uncover`, as for snapshots), until the body is forgotten: a node forgets a commit, ID included, once every part dropped under its own author's cover, no entry or delete within its tombstone names a blob of it. Event-driven (`State::forget_due`: last part dropped, entry gone, tombstone expired), one look at every commit after an open.
- Forgetting reads as never having received: a removal frontier cannot list a forgotten commit, and the cover stands for it (accepted loss: two late covers in a row lose what only they carried, roles-model `forget.rs`).

## Invalid bodies (`retention::drop_invalid`; spec/permissions.md §6)
- `Core::drop_due` takes every commit with a part held and invalid (`Core::index_validity` for a flip or an arrival, `Core::put_meta` for a late part joining invalid); at the end of `Engine::run`, after `answer_removals` (this node's addenda vouch first) and before `settle_none`, each such part goes (`DropFor::Invalid`, persisted as bit 32 of the dropped flags), each aspect rebuilt once (`run_batched`: the one path), then held deltas are judged again if the drop marked any (a `keys.0` part's keyset rebuild marks its commits; nothing flips). Kept (`goes_invalid`): role commits whole (the timeline, trust set, disputes and possible holders read every held edge write, applied or not; keeping only the `roles.0` part split a commit a label holds whole, so a writer never promoted an invalid role commit's deposit, sim `link_race`), this node's own commits (`OwnWriteInvalid`: its user may write it again from the body, `osis chat resend`; its author also uploads it, must-hold, so a re-grant finds it), memory parts (Memory, notes/ephemeral.md Divergences left).
- What the body judged stays in `Dropped` (role, overreach, keysets, shown), as for any drop. An invalid addendum's drop marks nothing stale (it is outside the fixpoint, its list counted for nothing); a `retention^.0` part's drop takes it out of `ix.retention` and judges its thing's history again (a setting not held is in no timeline). An invalid snapshot dropped stands in for nothing (`stands_in` only for `DropFor::Other`).
- While invalid a copy arriving again is refused (`takes_late`: only `DropFor::Other` takes one), so a relay costs nothing. Valid again (`rejudge_once` collects the parts rising while `dropped_invalid`, after its debug check), `valid_again` makes each missing (`reopen_to_fetch`, `Changed::undropped`) and flags the aspect `Why::Uncovered`; node-sync turns its entries `Unknown` and fetches them where present (`reopen_undropped`, which also forgets bytes still held when the drop and the rise fell in one step: else nothing re-ingests them).
- Refetch source: no right deletes an invalid body at hosts. node-sync leaves alone (may-hold, `judge` → `None`) any blob holding a part it dropped as invalid and not purged or expired since (`Engine::left_at_hosts`), unless a right dropped another part of it (purge, expiry, cover): that drop wins and the blob goes, the invalid copy with it (else a purged sibling stayed at the host, `purge_beside_invalid_delta_leaves_host_*`). Such a part alone does not make the blob hold nothing (`holds_nothing`) nor its chunks dropped (`dropped_chunks`). So hosts keep what was uploaded while valid, and the author keeps its own. Nobody deletes a never-valid body at hosts either (junk a reader or ex-writer uploads): only its expiry, a purge or the thing leaving the label take it, within the host's per-label cap (issues/invalid-junk-at-hosts.md). A body no holder has stays gone, at every node alike (`regrant_with_no_holder_reads_alike_*`).
- Cost: a part invalid on arrival is stored, then dropped, two rebuilds of its aspect; a fall drops its bodies with a second rebuild (`fall_of_many_one_pass_*`). A part that arrives before what makes it valid (a grant on another label, an addendum) is fetched twice: rare-path brute force.
- Tests: retention.rs `invalid_write_drops_regrant_fetches_it_*`, `revoked_writers_late_edit_goes_*`, `regrant_with_no_holder_reads_alike_*`; setting.rs `writers_setting_keeps_nothing_*`; held-model `Fate::MustDrop(DropWhy::Invalid)`.

## Bodies dropped and coming back
- **Never received** (spec/commits.md §5): a body dropped other than as covered (a part missing when purged) counts as never received: `retention::takes_late` admits its late copy (not one dropped as covered while a cover carries it, not a memory part, and a value-less one only without a residue), `reopen` clears its `Dropped` (and its `dropped_snapshots` row), the copy joins and `on_late` judges it again (purged or stripped again while the request stands). Nothing fetches it on its own; a holder that judges it must-hold re-uploads it (a fresh encryption past a host tombstone).
- A body dropped as covered whose covers no longer carry it is missing again (`uncover`, spec/permissions.md §5.2) and fetched where present; compaction deleted it at the hosts, so it is often held only by readers, who upload nothing, and its live writes are lost (the stated limit, spec/permissions.md §5.2); the parts harness (`parts::parts_differential_*`) gives a node the commit of any part missing again, as node-sync fetches it (parts seed 79).
- **Dropped snapshot liveness**: a snapshot not held (dropped or never received) is live while the state holds a write stamped at or before its `ts` or one without a `ts` (a sequence record whose creation is gone): an over-approximation, so its delete at hosts waits for a carrier (`snapshot_live`; a dropped-as-covered one has its coverer). Test `deletion::dropped_snapshot_waits_for_lost_carrier_spec_sync_7`.
- A drop removes the snapshot from `Core::snapshots` (`apply::forget_snapshot`): an index entry derived from a dropped body goes, since a rebuild cannot read it and an incremental node must not keep them either (order seed 1291).
- `drop_part` keeps a dropped part's rotation role, overreach, keysets and shown possession (`Dropped::role`, `overreach`, `keysets`, `shown`), so re-judging it and a rebuild need no body; `reopen` (never received again) forgets them. A bare leftover is never dropped (it stays for un-merge); one holding values (`Meta::valued`) drops as a whole body.

## Leftovers and the value-less list (`retention/leftover.rs`, `w/`)
- An expired or purged part, a snapshot carrying purged values, or a waiting part a request names is held as its leftover (`make_leftover`: its body stripped, in `w/`, `Meta::stripped`): a held body for every reader (`each_held_piece`, `held_parts_at`, `stores_part`: rebuilds, keep bounds, copy indexes, covering, the rebuild check), no body for node-sync (`Meta::body_gone`). A leftover holds stamps, dots, resets, flags and positions, never a value.
- **Invariant** (`strip_leftover`, the one strip of purge, expiry and arrival): no held body holds a listed write's value, and none loses a value of a write not listed: every listed write it holds loses its value (`Fragment::strip`), so a rebuild reads the same with it. A body carrying a copy of one (a snapshot's; a key or undelete char is its own writer's value) is held as its leftover with only the copies value-less (`strip_carried`; `rebuild` finds them while the list changed, `drop_gone` on arrival), and a leftover still holding own values (`Meta::valued`, persisted) is a body for keep bounds (`Meta::bare`: the value-less ones are exempt); node-sync deletes its blob as a dropped body with live writes (snapshot first).
- The value-less list `v/` stays as the one join-time predicate (`apply::strip_valueless`: what joins and what a rebuild gives are stripped by it). Without it a snapshot's stale copy would reach the state, and deriving it per join would walk the aspect.
- A part dropped as covered and then purged takes its leftover from its valid coverers' copies and is held again from then on. A part missing when purged has no leftover: the list alone (a copy joins stripped and becomes the leftover, `drop_gone`).
- On its purge's fall a part stripped of its own values counts as never received: its writes leave the list, the part is missing with its leftover row set aside (`unstrip`), the aspect is rebuilt without its writes and the node refetches (`refetch`, `Changed::undropped`); a copy joins whole (`forget_joined_leftovers` drops the stale row); held value-less again first (a rise, or its expiry), `make_leftover` adopts the row, so it holds what it left as a node that never saw the fall does. An expired part's writes never leave the list. A body stripped only for carrying others' values stays held as its leftover through the fall: its value-less copies only hide, the valued copy wins wherever one joins (spec/crdt.md §4.7 Join), and a whole copy arriving replaces it (`takes_late`). Making it missing instead lost the other writes a carrying snapshot covered, deleted at hosts on its strength.

## Purge (`retention/purge.rs`; spec/commits.md §5 Purge, spec/permissions.md §6)
- Every question about a request comes down to two: does it name a delta (`names`: an item on its aspect reaches it, stamped before the request), and does it have the right? The right has two readings: `holds_right` by the role timeline (demotion frontiers included, so a backdated request by a removed admin drops nothing), which what a request drops follows (`stands`, `rejudge_purges`); and `signers_could`, by roles of any validity at any time (`Held::ever_*`), for what must not turn on arrival (`could_stand`, `counts_as_purged`). A header never names role-class deltas.
- Requests are kept per aspect or per named commit (`p/`, `pn/`), listed before the request's commit joins (`note_requests`), so deltas arriving later are purged on arrival and a body killed by the request's own commit is purged, not dropped: its leftover is bare, so no cover drops it (`kill_and_purge_in_one_commit_keeps_leftover_*`). A body dropped as covered before a request naming it arrived takes its leftover from its coverers (Leftovers). A request by name costs other deltas nothing; an aspect-wide one is judged in one walk of the aspect per class and actor. A named commit never held names nothing yet (its header gives the right and the dots).
- When a role change flips a verdict (`rejudge_purges`): a part a request comes to drop is purged as on arrival; one held value-less for a request that no longer stands counts as never received (above); a body stripped for carrying others' values stays held (above). Cost: the aspect's requests and what they name, its snapshots' views, and with an aspect-wide request a walk of its parts (`scale::purge_rejudge_is_linear_in_requests_spec_permissions_6`; a flip rebuilds once per request: issues/purge-flip-rebuilds-per-request.md).
- A purged write a node never held leaves it no leftover (counts as never received), so it reads what the write replaced where a node that held it reads its value-less leftover: the requirements limit for expiring or purged edits a node never held. The repair is a writer's re-snapshot after applying the request (spec/commits.md §5 Purge), carrying the write value-less: `purge::apply_request` hands node-sync each named aspect where the request dropped something here (it stood on a named delta whose header is held; a refused or no-op request changes nothing and writes no snapshot: tombstone.rs `a_refused_purge_writes_no_snapshot_*`) with the named bodies it dropped applied here before (held, or dropped as covered: their writes are in the state through the cover; `Changed::purges`), and node-sync, first in `engine_events`, writes a snapshot where it may write unless it holds one valid snapshot of the aspect the purge left whole (not stripped: a stripped one goes at hosts; `Engine::holds_valid_snapshot`) covering every one (`purge_resnapshots`, sync/pass/delete.rs). With none applied any whole valid snapshot of the aspect will do, and with none held it writes one: a vacuous skip there let a fresh node read an older value (order seed 632724249436). The order harness's purger follows the same rule (`order::resnapshot`). It is apart from `snapshot_first` (spec/sync.md §7 No carrier: a dropped body with live writes needs a carrier before it leaves a host, for expiry as well as purge); after a purge `snapshot_first` finds the re-snapshot as the carrier. Rejected: purging writes out of every state entirely (swaps this for the invalid-delta limit, large) and fetching purged bodies to form leftovers (a race against the purger's host deletes).
- A purge that dropped a permanent delta on a thing is noted (`z/`, `retention::lasting`): a new node needs the request to hold a copy of the delta that comes back as purged; noted, not walked, since a writer's aspect-wide request naming nothing it may purge would walk the aspect on every pass.
- A memory delta purged has vanished whole and stays gone.

## Waiting parts at expiry or purge (`retention/pending.rs`)
- A part pending at its expiry goes at T (`pending_drops`, `strip_pending` from the T timer; parked copies included) and counts as never received. Whether it went is a function of the arrivals and the clock, never of when ticks ran: before any ingest or `reexamine` reads a stored pending copy, its parts past T go as their timers would (`apply::expire_stored`, `retention::expire_waiting`, sharing the tick's `run_timer`), so a part woken past T before the next tick does not apply as a value-less leftover (`waits::part_waiting_at_expiry_goes_whatever_the_ticks_spec_commits_5`); the commit is examined again without it; a part waiting aside (`pend_aside`) is judged the same way, and only that aspect is flagged suspect (`Why::PendingExpiry`). Expiry cannot strip: a part usually waits for its thing's record at T, and without it the part cannot be decoded.
- A pending part a held purge request names is held value-less, not dropped (`strip_purged`, from `pending_drops`; a purge request's apply wakes the commits on its thing, and holds a part waiting aside of an applied commit it names value-less at once, `purge_pending`): once its thing's record is held it is decoded, stripped by the delta's `Owned`, kept as the part's leftover (`w/`), and its pending slot holds an empty-list delta (`retention::emptied`, never hashing; `examine` trusts it only with the leftover row, joins it as a whole part, `Parts::stripped`, and leaves it out of the stored blob); once joined, `stripped_joined` (before `on_apply`/`on_late`) holds it as its leftover, as purge does an applied part, so every arrival order holds the same state (`shred::purge_of_a_waiting_part_reads_alike_spec_commits_5`, `purge_of_a_part_waiting_aside_reads_alike_spec_commits_5`). No flag. With a page away or parked it cannot be stripped: it goes at once, flagged (`Why::Uncovered`), refetched by `fetch_fallen` if the request fell before the commit applied. Snapshot parts (no own values) wait whole; a memory commit's purged waiting part is dropped as never received (notes/ephemeral.md Divergences left).
- node-sync forgets the blobs of an expired or stripped part at once (`SyncEngine::dropped`, `Engine::spent_copy`): a blob also holding another part is kept while that part differs from the pending copy and is not provably junk (a junk copy may hold its slot, the blob the only true copy); the same for an applied commit with parts waiting aside (else the waiting part is lost for good, `parts_relay::copy_kept_for_a_junk_held_slot_of_an_applied_commit_spec_commits_5`), and every copy of a commit named for redelivery is kept; `redeliver` re-checks the commits it visited. An applied commit with nothing waiting keeps the cheap rule (any part dropped: forget). A blob holds one expiry (one per commit), so the kept-blob case is purge-only. A blob parked whole (its header unreadable by this build) cannot be stripped.

## Undo (`undo/`)
An undo's pre-state is kept history only (`kept_before`, spec/commits.md §7): the held valid parts before the target that are live (`body_live`) or within their keep bound (`body_keep`), joined; nothing is stored per local commit. Its cost is O(aspect history held) per undo, a rare path. Whether the inverse needs replaced content is the target's op kinds (`osis_crdt::restores`); whether that content is kept is `replaced_kept`: every valid part of the aspect before the target, by header, is in kept history, or its own retention would keep it now counted from the target's `ts`, or a kept part supersedes it: a root reset stamped after it (the walk stops there), or a snapshot whose `Frontiers`/`Absorbed` reach it (`retention::reaches`; a part the tables do not name is not reached: they cannot tell one whose writes all died from one the writer never applied; an expiring part is never reached: a snapshot holds its writes value-less). A snapshot part is judged like a delta and only a later reset excuses it: no table names a snapshot, and a node may hold a snapshot without the headers of the parts that wrote it (covered, not lasting). A write does not record what it overwrote, so an unkept part in that span the target did not replace (it died earlier) blocks too: judging its body would make the answer depend on whether it is still held (accepted limit, issues/undo-unkept-part-blocks.md). A part not fetched yet blocks; a forgotten commit (memory, or durable under its author's own cover) leaves its `ts` in `Indexes::forgotten` (newest per aspect; durable ones persist as `f/` rows, gone with their thing) and blocks unless a kept reset is stamped after it (no snapshot can be shown to reach a commit whose header is gone). The memory compaction trigger weighs no body a keep bound holds, and the memory cover drop waits for `body_keep` (notes/ephemeral.md Undo).
