# Engine: retention, deaths and dropped bodies
> Summary: How the engine keeps spec/commits.md §5-§7 (`retention/`): the one-drop principle, history modes, body classes, deaths dated per node for keep-for-D, purge on replace as the replacing writer's cover (cost, privacy), snapshot frontiers, covering and inherited retention, bodies dropped and coming back, leftovers and the value-less list, purge, waiting parts at expiry or purge, the undo buffer, and the costs. The spec states the rules; this note keeps reasons, invariants, Tess's decisions, footguns and numbers. Not here: validity and uncovering (notes/engine-validity.md), suspect flags (notes/engine-suspects.md), host deletion judgment (notes/sync.md), memory-class retention (notes/ephemeral.md).

## Principle
Every drop of a body that is not expiry or purge is a drop as covered: a valid compaction snapshot whose commit holds the right over the body covers it. A node without write drops nothing on its own but expired content. Every rule below follows from it:
- keep history (until T, D after replaced) only limits when a covered drop may happen, at every node alike;
- purge on replace is a duty to write the cover: the writer whose transaction replaced the body covers it, and the cover drops it everywhere (`retention/replace.rs`);
- convenient (no history) has neither: right holders compact when they like (node-sync, notes/sync.md);
- the right-less drops of spec/permissions.md §6 (a delta breaking a required retention, a `required.0^` by a non-admin) are the purge section's: junk a node MAY drop; they count as never received and come back fetched if they turn valid.
There is no keep-at-most bound and no drop of an invalid body for its invalidity; the only timers are expiry and a purge request's floor (`T_FLOOR`: a covered purge-on-replace body of a commit carrying a request waits for it, spec/commits.md §5 Purge).

## History modes (`osis_commit::retention::KeepRule`)
The wire carries a history mode (spec/commits.md §5). Keep history is a keep at least: `until` absolute, `after` D after replaced, the later. Purge on replace has no bound. A role-class aspect's wire retention is `admin` with no history; `KeepRule` gives it a relative keep at least of `ROLE_KEEP`, so role bodies are kept as before (compactor policy in the issue's terms, applied by every node).
- Replaced (keep-for-D): a delta's body counts from its first death so far, a snapshot's from its last (`body_keep`): the snapshot keeps the history of every write it carries for D after that write died, so compacting a keep-for-D write while live loses none of it (held-model `snapshot_keeps_the_longest_d_of_what_it_carries_*`). Dates are per node (first superseder held, below), never earlier than the held set's earliest replacing write, so a node may keep longer, never shorter.

## Bodies
- A body is the commit's one blob (notes/whole-commits.md). Retention's `body_class` still groups its deltas by (reader actor, retention, delete-key indices) for deaths: deaths are kept per part and read over the class, a part drops with the rest of its class, and the blob is rewritten without it. The class depends on what is held (the reader actor, `Core::reader_of`: up the record chain through first-admin links to the nearest actor with secrets of its own), so nothing kept may be keyed by it. Expiry is per commit; purge drops single deltas (the commit is kept without them).
- A held body is judged "held" by the stored blob holding the part (`apply::stores_part`), not by meta alone: during a join the commit is indexed before it is stored, and a rebuild must not count its own delta as held then (order seed 1038). A part still to arrive (`Meta::missing`, a late part) is not held (`retention::held_part`): it takes no death and is not dropped before it arrives, then dies on arrival (walks.rs `open_equals_rebuild_random_histories_unheld_part_dies`).

## Deaths dated per node (`retention/deaths.rs`)
- Only keep-for-D reads deaths (`ROLE_KEEP` and a snapshot's inherited D included): a delta's first death, a snapshot's last, no earlier than the true one; a later date only keeps the body longer (safe; storage). Absolute keep-at-least bounds, purge floors, compaction and host deletes read the clock and `body_keep` alone. So dates may be coarse upward, and which bodies a death credits must be exact (a snapshot's last death decides how long it carries another write's history). issues/retention-modes.md step 3 replaces dating with the held-set rule.
- Tess's decision (spec/commits.md §5), over exact dating with remnants of dropped bodies, deaths windows and their whole-replay triggers: a write's death is the `ts` of the superseder its join met here (`note_deaths`, every valid delta, in order or late: a join reports deaths against the state, so a write arriving after its superseder dies on arrival at the state's winner, and a superseder arriving after a later one changes nothing). That `ts` is an applied superseder's, so never earlier than the earliest (spec/crdt.md §4.8): keep at least is never shortened. A superseder the state holds only as a valid snapshot's copy of a delta invalid here supersedes from the snapshot on: dated at the earliest such snapshot's `ts` (`Parts::date`; the stamp's own `ts` is an invalid commit's and says nothing of when a valid write superseded anything). Limit: once no held valid snapshot holds it (the copy live in the state alone), a recompute dates at the stamp's own `ts`, earlier. Deaths are kept only for bodies with a relative bound (`ix.bounded_held`, `any_bounded`).
- Which bodies a death credits (`Parts::carriers`): the write's own commit (`Core::commit_of`), and every held valid bounded snapshot of the aspect whose part holds a write of the dying stamp and none of the superseder's stamp (`dies_in`, over a stamps index built once per step per snapshot, `Parts::holds`; a snapshot stamped before the write cannot carry it, the HLC). Both halves: a stamp names several writes (an entry's creation and its value, a struct's fields), so the stamp alone would credit a snapshot carrying the creation after it saw the value die by another write it holds (order.rs `snapshot_sharing_stamp_of_unseen_death_not_dated_early_*`); holding any write of the superseder's stamp means its author applied the superseder. The rule is exact for a held valid snapshot: the state holds every write it carries, so a dying write it holds the stamp of is either the write itself or one it saw die by a write it holds, which the reported superseder beat earlier (credited then). Rejected: crediting from the snapshot's frontier without reading its body (it credited a snapshot taken after the write's true death, a later superseder arriving first, with a death it never carried, and dropped it early with live writes); joining the superseder's body into the snapshot's part to see what dies there (the stamp rule answers the same question from the index). A `Death` naming its position was rejected too: joins go through slots and range records.
- A write dies for a body no earlier than the body's `ts` (spec: a snapshot's copies die for it no earlier than its `ts`; an undo or concurrent write its author had not seen; a no-op for own bodies).
- A death a snapshot's own join reports of a write it carries (`Parts::credit` with `me`: a stale copy of a write dead already, or the first copy of one the state superseded) credits that snapshot alone: the write's own commit and the other snapshots were credited at the first superseder's join, and a stale copy dying at a later winner would move their `last` (retention.rs `deaths_alike_after_carried_write_dropped_*`). Deaths of the state's writes by its copies credit the other bodies as any join's. A snapshot arriving also takes the deaths of the writes it carries that the state superseded before it (`note_copies`: `body_deaths` joins its part, a piece at a time, into a copy of the state's part it meets, O(part + state part), its copies of held deltas' writes included: those are left out of its join).
- A write invalid here, or one an un-merge took, has no superseder and no death.
- **Recompute** (`replay_all`): validity flips, an invalid delta arriving, un-merges, `refill` and rebuilds mark the aspect (`e/`, `Core::deaths_due`); the transaction ends with a recompute, except while `Engine::defer_deaths` is on (node-sync steps; reads of deaths or bounds settle first, `settle_deaths`). Held bodies joined in `(ts, ID)` order (invalid writes removed: `Unmerge::bad`, a snapshot's unvouched copies), then the state; each write dies at the first superseder it meets, credited as above, but only to the snapshots joined so far: one stamped after the superseder has not joined yet, so the replay state lacks its view, and its carried writes are judged at its own turn (its copy of a write the replay state superseded dies there, dated at its `ts`). A recompute may date later than the incremental dates (a superseder whose body dropped dates nothing) or earlier (a superseder held later with a lower `ts`), never earlier than the death. Duplicate deaths from a page path repeated in several pieces are harmless: deaths are dated per body, idempotently.
- Text: a creation dies at a delete raised over none or an undelete (deleted again after an undelete it is dated again at the later delete, `last` moving later: upward, so allowed, spec/commits.md §5; the truth, spec/crdt.md §4.8, is the first delete. A node holding the re-delete before the undelete keeps `last` at the first delete: exact), and on arriving where its element is deleted (its own delta after a snapshot's copy of it too). Limit: a snapshot's copy of a creation whose own delta is not held meets only the latest delete the state shows (the flag is the last write; a replay too), so it is dated there, later than its own delta would be.
- Two right holders may then judge one body past its keep-for-D bound at different times. A body a delete removed from a label (`sync::State::deleted`, keyed by label, commit and delta set: a body sharing a delta with it matches, itself or a purge rewrite; keyed by commit it would withhold the commit's other bodies too, sync_review.rs `deleted_body_withholds_only_itself_*`) is not placed there again while its live writes are carried (spec/sync.md §6; else a re-upload per pass until the later node's bound, negative.rs `deletion_across_local_dating_no_churn_*`).
- Rejected: per-write death records (a write is not identified by its stamp); cached decoded bodies (memory O(history)).
- Tests: retention.rs `deaths_dated_from_first_superseder_held_*`, `deaths_never_early_random_*` (random histories with drops against a node keeping every body, `Engine::keep_bodies`; `OSIS_STRAYS_START`/`_SEEDS`), `deaths_alike_nested_in_others_entry_*`, `replay_dates_at_carried_write_in_held_entry_*`, `deaths_alike_after_carried_write_dropped_*`; order.rs `snapshot_after_late_dated_death_not_credited_*`, `snapshot_sharing_stamp_of_unseen_death_not_dated_early_*`, `text_redelete_dated_again_*`, `bound_kept_now` (notes/testing.md).
- Cost (`scale::late_delta_deaths_cost_spec_crdt_4_8`, release, N = 3000, load about 15): a late delta 2.3-2.8 ms whatever the set, 5 in a batch 8.4-9.8 ms, a recent late delta 1.1-1.2 ms, an in-order local write 2.5-3.4 ms; a recompute 10-19 µs on an unbounded set, 77-89 ms on a keep-at-least one (flips and un-merges only; under the 500 ms late-commit budget, notes/testing.md). The stamp rule reads every bounded snapshot's stamps once per step instead of its frontier: no measurable difference (the same numbers back to back).
- Reading a body's deaths (`class_deaths`) costs one record per part of its class, so bounds are judged per body, never per part over the class: node-sync asks `Engine::body_keeps` once per commit judged (every part's keep, by aspect index); a class with no relative bound reads none (it has no records). `Core::death_reads` counts reads (`pass_marks::body_judged_once_per_body_spec_requirements_scale`). 800 parts in one body, release: about 1 s per test where judging per part took 7-31 s.

## Purge on replace (`retention/replace.rs`)
- The writer's cover, the principle's one drop (Tess, issues/retention-modes.md): at the end of every transaction (`Engine::run`, `settle_none`), over the aspects whose purge-on-replace bodies or snapshots changed (`Core::none_due`, filled by `Core::put_meta` for any commit on an aspect holding such a body, `ix.none_held`, by a rebuild, and by a timeline change on the thing: a grant can make this node a writer owing a cover, retention.rs `none_granted_writer_covers_what_it_holds_replaced_*`): a node that may write a snapshot of the aspect (`snapshot_role`, at least write; every writer holds the `writer` right) writes one from its whole state (`snapshot::write_snapshot`, at most one per aspect per transaction) when a held purge-on-replace body is replaced and no held valid snapshot holding the right over it covers it (`coverer`); then every covered purge-on-replace body drops as covered, at every node (readers too: the snapshot's commit holds the right). A function of the held set: the order things came in changes when, never whether.
- **Replaced** (`replaced`): the body is valid, has no expiry (an expiring delta is kept to its T, then covered as a bare leftover), holds a live write by dot the state does not, and joins into the state's part it meets with its canonical bytes unchanged: dead, not un-merged under an invalid record (which would make a snapshot that cannot cover it, every transaction). Not "has a dead write": a root reset dies once a later write implies one yet canonical form keeps it (held-model-4).
- Every replacing write converts into a snapshot: the new body is covered by the snapshot written after it and drops too, so an aspect under purge on replace holds one snapshot and no deltas at the writer after each transaction. Readers keep replaced bodies until a writer's snapshot arrives: some writer must hold both the body and its replacement (the editor need not hold the body, a concurrent write; every writer fetches both in time); with none online they keep them (no timer: a node without write acts on its own only for expiry).
- Cost (spec/requirements.md Scale, the exception the app chooses): every replacing edit costs a snapshot of the aspect, O(aspect state), at every writer holding the replaced body uncovered; in practice the editor alone (it uploads its snapshot with the edit, and a writer receiving both in one batch finds it covered). Duplicates (a writer that applied the edit before the snapshot arrived) are harmless (spec/sync.md §7). Measured in notes/growth.md (purge on replace).
- Rejected: a keep-at-most bound and its timers, carriers written by node-sync before a timed drop, readers dropping at the bound with no carrier, invalid bodies counted dead at their own `ts`: each needed death dating, a live-write judgment per body and a carve-out per kind of body (snapshots dropped live, never received after a flip, oracle excuses); the cover rule needs none of them.
- Rejected: writer-side purge per edit (Tess's fallback if this proves costly): a purge cannot express a partial replacement of a body, a purging commit keeps its blobs `ROLE_KEEP`, every edit leaves a leftover.
- Privacy (spec/commits.md §5): erased data persists in its body at a writer for no transaction, at a reader until the writer's snapshot arrives, at a host until a right holder next syncs. The read state loses it at the kill, and the drop shreds like any.
- Liveness (`retention::live`, `Engine::body_live`) stays for node-sync's host deletes (a body with a live write goes from a label only with a carrier, spec/sync.md §7): judged on the current state, cached per body and forgotten per aspect when its state changes (`Core::forget_live`; `Fragment::holds_owned_write`: an element's or entry's tombstone holds no write of its creator, order seed 64197). A deleted text element's creation stays in the state as its anchor, so a text body fully erased is live too.
- Tests: retention.rs `none_*` (the cover in the replacing transaction, readers dropping on arrival, mixed modes), held.rs model comparison, sim `snapshot_rights`.

## Snapshots: frontiers, covering, inherited retention (`retention/snapshot.rs`)
- Frontiers list the sessions occurring in the state (`osis_crdt::sources_of`; a session with a write left but no contiguous prefix applied gets a 0 entry, spec/crdt.md §8), each covering its counters contiguously applied and valid on the aspect; `Absorbed` lists the commit-form commits occurring in it.
- `covered` judges a held body by the join, with the snapshot's copies under the body's dots stripped first so a differing copy (forged, greater or not) never covers; the body's value-less writes are left out of that strip (`Fragment::valueless_dots`): a snapshot whose writer held a purge this node does not (a fork, or one that fell) holds a live write value-less, and later snapshots hold it valued, so the strict compare would keep that snapshot for as long as the write lives. Tables speak only for one not held (an absent session or a 0 entry says nothing of what joins). Canonical-bytes comparisons there must stay encodings, not `Fragment ==`/`contains`: a deleted text element's char survives a join in memory but not in canonical bytes (deletion::text_partly_erased). An expiring delta is never covered. `receipt::settled` (suspect flags) is any snapshot of the aspect stamped after the commit, valid here.
- A compaction drop needs the snapshot's own commit to hold the right over the body (`purge::snapshot_right`, spec §5 Between the bounds); a snapshot without it still carries the body's live writes and leaves it may-hold, but drops nothing at any node.
- Retention comes from the carried live writes' own deltas' headers (`own_retentions`: each live dot to its commit by `Core::commit_of`; dots whose commit header is not held: the held snapshot bodies carrying them, else every snapshot of the aspect), so any writer snapshots, schema known or not: purge on replace if any carried write's is (as the held model's `snapshot_retention`), else kept for the longest `after` (`osis_commit::retention::snapshot_retention`). A purge-on-replace snapshot cannot keep a carried write's history, so it covers no body that keeps history while that body has a live write (`snapshot::cannot_keep`, in `covered`): on an aspect mixing modes such bodies stay until their writes die and their bound passes, the cost of keeping history there. Liveness changes by a kill, after which a later snapshot covers both, or by a validity change, after which a settled `covered_judged` `false` only keeps the body longer. The aspect's headers are scanned only when some live write's own commit is not found directly. A body whose writes are all live is past a relative keep at least, so the snapshot carrying them keeps that promise, counting D from its last death (undo reads pre-commit values from held bodies, snapshots included); an `until` stays with the original body, which cannot leave before it (inheriting `until` forever would pin every later snapshot).
- Copy indexes (`Core::copy_index`, `copies::copy_index`): a held snapshot's writes by dot, built from what `each_held_piece` reads (its whole body, or its leftover: keys erased, values gone) and cached per commit, forgotten whenever the commit's stored body or leftover changes (`put_commit`, `remove_commit`, `leftover::put`/`remove`) and on rollback. Debug builds check every cache hit against an index built anew (removing the invalidation fails five tier-1 order tests). A stale index vouched for records from a creation copy the strip had erased, and a fresh one did not, so a reopen changed what an un-merge kept (order seed 91703). In the un-merge (`Unmerge::vouches`) a record a valid snapshot holds key-erased keeps every record of its dot, the state's own key-erased record (asked with no key) included: nested writes of other deltas live there. Test: order.rs `settled_seed_91703_live_write_kept_after_reopen_*`.
- Deaths in snapshots (`carriers`): a dying write's death is recorded in its own commit's body (for a `Commit(C)` dot: C), in every held snapshot taken before the death that holds it, and at its `ts` in every one taken at or after it that had not seen its superseder and carries the write the state holds dead (also where the write's own body was never held); dropped bodies get none.
- Known schemas, one per app AK, never from the wire (`Engine::register_schema`, which replaces one of a lower or equal version for each AK it declares, or a local typed write, which replaces only a lower version; kept under `d/` + AK, read at open) serve undo's typed views and large write-back after a restart, not snapshots. `osis::Osis::aspect_key` types only by this session's registrations, so the CLI's `schema rm` untypes.

## Bodies dropped and coming back
- **Never received** (spec/commits.md §5): a body dropped other than as covered (a snapshot a purge request names, a part missing when purged, a delta dropped without a right, spec/permissions.md §6) counts as never received: `retention::takes_late` admits its late copy (not one dropped as covered while a cover carries it, not a memory part, and a value-less one only without a residue), `reopen` clears its `Dropped` (and its `dropped_snapshots` row), the copy joins and `on_late` judges it again (purged or stripped again while the request stands). Nothing fetches it on its own but a part dropped while invalid that turns valid (`valid_again`: missing again, fetched, the aspect flagged `Uncovered`); a holder that judges it must-hold re-uploads it (a fresh encryption past a host tombstone).
- A body dropped as covered whose covers no longer carry it is missing again (`uncover`, spec/permissions.md §5.2) and fetched; the parts harness (`parts::parts_differential_*`) gives a node the commit of any part missing again, as node-sync fetches it (parts seed 79).
- **Dropped snapshot liveness**: a snapshot not held (dropped or never received) is live while the state holds a write stamped at or before its `ts` or one without a `ts` (bare anchors): an over-approximation, so its delete at hosts waits for a carrier (`snapshot_live`; a dropped-as-covered one has its coverer). Test `deletion::dropped_snapshot_waits_for_lost_carrier_spec_sync_7`.
- A drop removes the snapshot from `Core::snapshots` (`apply::forget_snapshot`): an index entry derived from a dropped body goes, since a rebuild cannot read it and an incremental node must not keep them either (order seed 1291; order.rs `dropped_snapshot_rebuilds_alike_*`).
- `drop_part` keeps a dropped part's rotation role, overreach, keysets and shown possession (`Dropped::role`, `overreach`, `keysets`, `shown`), so re-judging it and a rebuild need no body; `reopen` (never received again) forgets them. A bare leftover is never dropped (it stays for un-merge); one holding values (`Meta::valued`) drops as a whole body.

## Leftovers and the value-less list (`retention/leftover.rs`, `w/`)
- An expired or purged part, a snapshot carrying purged values, or a waiting part a request names is held as its leftover (`make_leftover`: its body stripped, in `w/`, `Meta::stripped`): a held body for every reader (`each_held_piece`, `held_parts_at`, `stores_part`: rebuilds, deaths, copy indexes, covering, the rebuild check), no body for node-sync (`Meta::body_gone`). A leftover holds stamps, dots, resets, flags and positions, never a value.
- **Invariant** (`strip_leftover`, the one strip of purge, expiry and arrival): no held body holds a listed write's value, and none loses a value of a write not listed. Its own writes lose their values only when listed (`Fragment::strip`); every copy it carries of another listed write is held value-less (`Fragment::expire`), so a rebuild reads the same with it. A body carrying such a copy (a snapshot, an undelete's char, a map key an entry's record repeats) is held as its leftover with only the copies value-less (`strip_carried`; `rebuild` finds them while the list changed, `drop_gone` on arrival), and a leftover still holding own values (`Meta::valued`, persisted) is a body for keep bounds and deaths (`Meta::bare`: the value-less ones are exempt); node-sync deletes its blob as a dropped body with live writes (snapshot first).
- The value-less list `v/` stays as the one join-time predicate (`apply::strip_valueless`: what joins and what a rebuild gives are stripped by it). Without it an undelete's copy of an expired char or a snapshot's stale copy would reach the state, and deriving it per join would walk the aspect.
- A part dropped as covered and then purged takes its leftover from its valid coverers' copies and is held again from then on. A part missing or dropped for its bounds when purged has no leftover: the list alone (a copy joins stripped and becomes the leftover, `drop_gone`).
- On its purge's fall a stripped part counts as never received: its carrying commits leave the list, the part is missing with its leftover row set aside (`unstrip`), the aspect is rebuilt without its writes and the node refetches (`refetch`, `Changed::undropped`); a copy joins whole (`forget_joined_leftovers` drops the stale row); held value-less again first (a rise, or its expiry), `make_leftover` adopts the row, so it holds what it left as a node that never saw the fall does. An expired part's carrying commits never leave the list.

## Purge (`retention/purge.rs`; spec/commits.md §5 Purge, spec/permissions.md §6)
- Every question about a request comes down to two: does it name a delta (`names`: an item on its aspect reaches it, stamped before the request), and does it have the right? The right has two readings: `holds_right` by the role timeline (demotion frontiers included, so a backdated request by a removed admin drops nothing), which what a request drops follows (`stands`, `rejudge_purges`); and `signers_could`, by roles of any validity at any time (`Held::ever_*`), for what must not turn on arrival (`could_stand`, `counts_as_purged`). A header never names role-class deltas.
- Requests are kept per aspect or per named commit (`p/`, `pn/`), listed before the request's commit joins (`note_requests`), so deltas arriving later are purged on arrival and a body killed by the request's own commit is purged, not dropped: its leftover is bare, so no cover drops it (`kill_and_purge_in_one_commit_keeps_leftover_*`). A body dropped as covered before a request naming it arrived takes its leftover from its coverers (Leftovers). A request by name costs other deltas nothing; an aspect-wide one is judged in one walk of the aspect per class and actor. A named commit never held names nothing yet (its header gives the right and the dots).
- When a role change flips a verdict (`rejudge_purges`): a part a request comes to drop is purged as on arrival; one held value-less for a request that no longer stands counts as never received (above); a snapshot dropped as named by a fallen request is missing again; every body stripped for carrying others' values is asked for again. Cost: the aspect's requests and what they name, its snapshots' views, and with an aspect-wide request a walk of its parts (`scale::purge_rejudge_cost`).
- A purge that dropped a permanent delta on a thing is noted (`z/`, `retention::lasting`): a new node needs the request to hold a copy of the delta that comes back as purged; noted, not walked, since a writer's aspect-wide request naming nothing it may purge would walk the aspect on every pass.
- A memory delta purged has vanished whole and stays gone.

## Waiting parts at expiry or purge (`retention/pending.rs`)
- A part pending at its expiry goes at T (`pending_drops`, `strip_pending` from the T timer; parked copies included) and counts as never received; the commit is examined again without it; a part waiting aside (`pend_aside`) is judged the same way, and only that aspect is flagged suspect (`Why::PendingExpiry`). Expiry cannot strip: a part usually waits for its thing's record at T, and without it the part cannot be decoded.
- A pending part a held purge request names is held value-less, not dropped (`strip_purged`, from `pending_drops`; a purge request's apply wakes the commits on its thing, and holds a part waiting aside of an applied commit it names value-less at once, `purge_pending`): once its thing's record is held it is decoded, stripped by the delta's `Owned`, kept as the part's leftover (`w/`), and its pending slot holds an empty-list delta (`retention::emptied`, never hashing; `examine` trusts it only with the leftover row, joins it as a whole part, `Parts::stripped`, and leaves it out of the stored blob); once joined, `stripped_joined` (before `on_apply`/`on_late`) holds it as its leftover, as purge does an applied part, so every arrival order holds the same state (`shred::purge_of_a_waiting_part_reads_alike_spec_commits_5`, `purge_of_a_part_waiting_aside_reads_alike_spec_commits_5`). No flag. With a page away or parked it cannot be stripped: it goes at once, flagged (`Why::Uncovered`), refetched by `fetch_fallen` if the request fell before the commit applied. Snapshot parts (no own values) wait whole; a memory commit's purged waiting part is dropped as never received (notes/ephemeral.md Divergences left).
- node-sync forgets the blobs of an expired or stripped part at once (`SyncEngine::dropped`, `Engine::spent_copy`): a blob also holding another part is kept while that part differs from the pending copy and is not provably junk (a junk copy may hold its slot, the blob the only true copy); the same for an applied commit with parts waiting aside (else the waiting part is lost for good, `parts_relay::copy_kept_for_a_junk_held_slot_of_an_applied_commit_spec_commits_5`), and every copy of a commit named for redelivery is kept; `redeliver` re-checks the commits it visited. An applied commit with nothing waiting keeps the cheap rule (any part dropped: forget). A blob holds one expiry (one per commit), so the kept-blob case is purge-only. A blob parked whole (its header unreadable by this build) cannot be stripped.

## Undo buffer (`undo/`)
What undoing each of the last `UNDO_DEPTH` (16) local commits per aspect reads (`Fragment::footprint`: struct fields written, the elements a map, set or bag delta writes and the live entries of keys it edits into, along the edit's paths; of a list or text the records holding the delta's dots), in the aspect unit; cleared (shredded) whenever a body of the aspect drops, since a dropped body is not undoable (spec/commits.md §7) and the buffer would keep its content. Undo reads the current state in place along the delta's paths. Undoing another node's commit (admin view) takes the pre-state as the join of the held valid bodies ordered before it (O(aspect history)).
