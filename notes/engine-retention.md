# Engine: retention, deaths and dropped bodies
> Summary: How the engine keeps spec/commits.md §5-§7 (`retention/`): body classes, deaths dated per node and their recompute, keep at most with live writes (0 means 0, carriers at once and their cost, privacy), carrier-only snapshots, snapshot frontiers, covering and inherited retention, bodies dropped and coming back (never received, dropped-snapshot liveness), leftovers and the value-less list, purge, waiting parts at expiry or purge, the undo buffer, and the costs. The spec states the rules; this note keeps reasons, invariants, Tess's decisions, footguns and numbers. Not here: validity and uncovering (notes/engine-validity.md), suspect flags (notes/engine-suspects.md), host deletion judgment (notes/sync.md), memory-class retention (notes/ephemeral.md).

## Bodies
- A body is the commit's one blob (notes/whole-commits.md). Retention's `body_class` still groups its deltas by (reader actor, retention, delete-key indices) for deaths: deaths are kept per part and read over the class, a part drops with the rest of its class, and the blob is rewritten without it. The class depends on what is held (the reader actor, `Core::reader_of`: up the record chain through owner and first-admin links to the nearest actor with secrets of its own), so nothing kept may be keyed by it. Expiry is per commit; purge drops single deltas (the commit is kept without them).
- A held body is judged "held" by the stored blob holding the part (`apply::stores_part`), not by meta alone: during a join the commit is indexed before it is stored, and a rebuild must not count its own delta as held then (order seed 1038). A part still to arrive (`Meta::missing`, a late part) is not held (`retention::held_part`): it takes no death and is not dropped before it arrives, then dies on arrival (walks.rs `open_equals_rebuild_random_histories_unheld_part_dies`).

## Deaths dated per node (`retention/deaths.rs`)
- What each option needs of a death date. Relative keep at least (`ROLE_KEEP` and a snapshot's inherited keep at least included): the body's last death no earlier than the true one; a later date only keeps the body longer (safe; storage). Keep at most 0 after replaced: only whether a write of the body is dead here (the bound is the first death, due whatever the clock, so the date itself never matters); a death credited to a body that does not carry the write drops it early with a carrier, one missed keeps replaced content, the property. Absolute bounds, purge floors, compaction and host deletes read the clock and `body_keep` alone. So dates may be coarse upward, and which bodies a death credits must be exact.
- Tess's decision (spec/commits.md §5), over exact dating with remnants of dropped bodies, deaths windows and their whole-replay triggers: a write's death is the `ts` of the superseder its join met here (`note_deaths`, every valid delta, in order or late: a join reports deaths against the state, so a write arriving after its superseder dies on arrival at the state's winner, and a superseder arriving after a later one changes nothing). That `ts` is an applied superseder's, so never earlier than the earliest (spec/crdt.md §4.8): keep at least is never shortened, keep at most never early. A superseder the state holds only as a valid snapshot's copy of a delta invalid here supersedes from the snapshot on: dated at the earliest such snapshot's `ts` (`Parts::date`; the stamp's own `ts` is an invalid commit's and says nothing of when a valid write superseded anything). Limit: once no held valid snapshot holds it (the last went at a keep-at-most bound with no carrier written, the copy live in the state), a recompute dates at the stamp's own `ts`, earlier. Deaths are kept only for bodies with a relative bound (`ix.bounded_held`, `any_bounded`).
- Which bodies a death credits (`Parts::carriers`): the write's own commit (`Core::commit_of`), and every held valid bounded snapshot of the aspect whose part holds a write of the dying stamp and none of the superseder's stamp (`dies_in`, over a stamps index built once per step per snapshot, `Parts::holds`; a snapshot stamped before the write cannot carry it, the HLC). Both halves: a stamp names several writes (an entry's creation and its value, a struct's fields), so the stamp alone would credit a snapshot carrying the creation after it saw the value die by another write it holds (order.rs `snapshot_sharing_stamp_of_unseen_death_not_dated_early_*`); holding any write of the superseder's stamp means its author applied the superseder. The rule is exact for a held valid snapshot: the state holds every write it carries, so a dying write it holds the stamp of is either the write itself or one it saw die by a write it holds, which the reported superseder beat earlier (credited then). Rejected: crediting from the snapshot's frontier without reading its body (it credited a snapshot taken after the write's true death, a later superseder arriving first, with a death it never carried, and dropped it early with live writes); joining the superseder's body into the snapshot's part to see what dies there (the stamp rule answers the same question from the index). A `Death` naming its position was rejected too: joins go through slots and range records.
- A write dies for a body no earlier than the body's `ts` (spec: a snapshot's copies die for it no earlier than its `ts`; an undo or concurrent write its author had not seen; a no-op for own bodies).
- A death a snapshot's own join reports of a write it carries (`Parts::credit` with `me`: a stale copy of a write dead already, or the first copy of one the state superseded) credits that snapshot alone: the write's own commit and the other snapshots were credited at the first superseder's join, and a stale copy dying at a later winner would move their `last` (retention.rs `deaths_alike_after_carried_write_dropped_*`). Deaths of the state's writes by its copies credit the other bodies as any join's. A snapshot arriving also takes the deaths of the writes it carries that the state superseded before it (`note_copies`: `body_deaths` joins its part, a piece at a time, into a copy of the state's part it meets, O(part + state part), its copies of held deltas' writes included: those are left out of its join).
- A write invalid here, or one an un-merge took, has no superseder: under keep at most 0 after replaced its body counts replaced at its own `ts` (`date_arrival` for an invalid arrival, this part alone, so a forged write costs no walk; in the recompute `date_falls`: invalid parts, and valid own bodies with no death whose part joined into the state would change its canonical bytes, `unmerged`, asked only on an aspect holding an invalid part). The date never matters (0 means 0), so no demotion `ts` is read. Role commits never (`falls`).
- **Recompute** (`replay_all`): validity flips, an invalid delta arriving, un-merges, `refill` and rebuilds mark the aspect (`e/`, `Core::deaths_due`); the transaction ends with a recompute, except while `Engine::defer_deaths` is on (node-sync steps; reads of deaths or bounds settle first, `settle_deaths`). Held bodies joined in `(ts, ID)` order (invalid writes removed: `Unmerge::bad`, a snapshot's unvouched copies), then the state; each write dies at the first superseder it meets, credited as above, but only to the snapshots joined so far: one stamped after the superseder has not joined yet, so the replay state lacks its view, and its carried writes are judged at its own turn (its copy of a write the replay state superseded dies there, dated at its `ts`); then the falls. A recompute may date later than the incremental dates (a superseder whose body dropped dates nothing) or earlier (a superseder held later with a lower `ts`), never earlier than the death. `check_at_most` skips marked aspects and the recompute rechecks them (`recheck_at_most`). Duplicate deaths from a page path repeated in several pieces are harmless: deaths are dated per body, idempotently.
- Text: a creation dies at a delete raised over none or an undelete (deleted again after an undelete it is dated again at the later delete, `last` moving later: upward, so allowed, spec/commits.md §5; the truth, spec/crdt.md §4.8, is the first delete. A node holding the re-delete before the undelete keeps `last` at the first delete: exact), and on arriving where its element is deleted (its own delta after a snapshot's copy of it too). Limit: a snapshot's copy of a creation whose own delta is not held meets only the latest delete the state shows (the flag is the last write; a replay too), so it is dated there, later than its own delta would be.
- Two right holders may then judge one body past its bound at different times. A body a delete removed from a label (`sync::State::deleted`, keyed by label, commit and delta set: a body sharing a delta with it matches, itself or a purge rewrite; keyed by commit it would withhold the commit's other bodies too, sync_review.rs `deleted_body_withholds_only_itself_*`) is not placed there again while its live writes are carried (spec/sync.md §6; else a re-upload per pass until the later node's bound, negative.rs `deletion_across_local_dating_no_churn_*`).
- Rejected: per-write death records (a write is not identified by its stamp); cached decoded bodies (memory O(history)).
- Tests: retention.rs `deaths_dated_from_first_superseder_held_*`, `keep_at_most_zero_late_body_dropped_on_arrival_*`, `deaths_never_early_random_*` (random histories with drops against a node keeping every body, `Engine::keep_bodies`; `OSIS_STRAYS_START`/`_SEEDS`), `deaths_alike_nested_in_others_entry_*`, `replay_dates_at_carried_write_in_held_entry_*`, `deaths_alike_after_carried_write_dropped_*`; order.rs `snapshot_after_late_dated_death_not_credited_*`, `snapshot_sharing_stamp_of_unseen_death_not_dated_early_*`, `text_redelete_dated_again_*`, `bound_kept_now` (notes/testing.md).
- Cost (`scale::late_delta_deaths_cost_spec_crdt_4_8`, release, N = 3000, load about 15): a late delta 2.3-2.8 ms whatever the set, 5 in a batch 8.4-9.8 ms, a recent late delta 1.1-1.2 ms, an in-order local write 2.5-3.4 ms; a recompute 10-19 µs on an unbounded set, 77-89 ms on a keep-at-least one (flips and un-merges only; under the 500 ms late-commit budget, notes/testing.md). The stamp rule reads every bounded snapshot's stamps once per step instead of its frontier: no measurable difference (the same numbers back to back).

## Keep at most and live writes (`retention/bounds.rs`)
- Keep at most is only 0 after replaced or an absolute T (Tess; spec/commits.md §5). Tess's rule: an option keeps its defining property and pays its own cost, so 0 means 0: a body under 0 after replaced goes at its first death, whatever the clock (`at_most_bound`: due by the clock, or under a relative bound at the first death; the bound is past the first death only under a purge request's floor, by the clock). With no live write left it drops in the killing transaction (`judge_at_most`); with one it drops by its keep-at-most timer, put at the kill due at once, and node-sync writes the carrier before running the timers (`Engine::due_live_drops`, `snapshot_first`, run at the start of every sync step, not only a Tick). An absolute bound is judged by its own timer the same way (`abs_timer`). Timers run in rounds (`Engine::tick_round`; `tick` loops them): a round's drops and expiries may mark an aspect's deaths for recompute, and a body whose timer fired while they were marked waits for the recompute, which times it again due at once; node-sync runs the rounds itself with `due_live_drops`' carriers before each. Readers (no write role) drop at the bound with no carrier: the bound wins (spec/sync.md §5).
- Cost, stated in spec/requirements.md Scale as the exception the app chooses: an edit that partly replaces a body under 0 after replaced whose other writes are live costs a snapshot of the aspect (O(aspect state)) at every writer holding the body, once per writer per such edit, unless it already holds a covering snapshot (`snapshot_first` checks `carrier_of` and every held valid snapshot of the aspect by `covered`; a carrier-only snapshot covers none, so under keep at least plus keep at most 0 each writer writes its own). Duplicates are harmless (spec/sync.md §7); the next compaction covers them. A writer lacking the right to write the aspect writes none and drops like a reader. In practice one per edit: the editor writes its carrier in the step of its local commit and uploads it with the kill, and a writer receiving both finds it held and covering at its next step; a duplicate comes only from a writer that applied the kill a step before the carrier arrived. Rejected: only the killing commit's author writes the carrier (it may not hold the body, so no timer fires there, or may vanish after uploading the kill; other writers would then need a wait, an arbitrary value).
- Rejected: keep at most D after replaced as the cheap option (a carrier once per D per aspect). A relative bound in the future is dated per node from the superseder it held first, so two holders' bounds differ by the dating gap: one deletes at hosts while the other still holds the body must-hold and re-uploads it each pass until its own bound. 0 after replaced has no such window (the bound is past when learned, at every node), an absolute T is alike everywhere; D would bring the window back for every body, dead or live, or timed host deletes (what removing D took out: `keep_at_most_due`, the `y/d` rows, `timed_sent`, `Waits::at_most`, `Touched::whole`).
- Rejected: writer-side purge in place of keep at most 0 after replaced. Death dating and carriers stay anyway (relative keep at least, `ROLE_KEEP`, absolute T, compaction), so it removes only about 300 lines of fall dating, while purge names whole deltas (a partly replaced one would lose its live writes), a purging commit keeps its blobs `ROLE_KEEP`, every edit would leave a permanent leftover, and role changes would re-judge O(edits) requests.
- Liveness is judged on the current state (`retention::live`, the tx form of `Engine::body_live`), not frozen at first death (a flag would add state and depend on arrival order): the state is the join of the applied valid, non-undone deltas, so nodes that applied the same commits agree (dropped bodies leave their live writes in the state). Nodes holding different commits can disagree for a while (one already has the last kill and deletes at the host; the other judges the body live and must-hold); the killing commit goes up before the delete in the same pass.
- Cost: `Fragment::holds_owned_write` (a set element's or map entry's tombstone, keyed by the creator's dot, holds no write of it: counting it would keep an undone keep-at-most-0 entry's body as live, order seed 64197) with `Owned::owns_range` (stops at the first hit, never expands range records), cached per body and forgotten per aspect when its state changes (`Core::forget_live` drops every body with a delta on the changed aspect: a body's liveness spans its class, whichever aspect was asked; `body_live_spans_its_class_spec_commits_5`).
- Bounds follow liveness: a validity flip re-runs `check_at_most` for the aspect, and a due keep-at-most timer re-judges instead of dropping blindly (a body that turned live drops only after its carrier); the flip replays the aspect's deaths, so a write revived because its only superseder turned invalid has no death and its body no keep-at-most bound, as at a node that never held that superseder valid.
- A deleted text element's creation stays in the state as its anchor, so a text body fully erased is live too: its carrier carries the anchors (concurrent inserts anchor on them).
- Privacy (spec/commits.md §5 says so): under keep at most 0 after replaced, erased data (a deleted word, a whole erased text) persists in its body only until the node dates the death: at the writer and every reader holding it that is the erasing pass (the erasing edit's undo buffer is cleared when the body drops), at a host until a right holder next syncs after the kill. A writer going offline right after an erasing edit leaves the body at the host until it or another right holder syncs. The read state loses it at the kill, and the drop shreds like any.
- Tests: deletion.rs `erasing_edit_carrier_at_once_*`, `text_partly_erased_carrier_at_once_*`, `text_fully_erased_carrier_at_once_*`, `carrier_by_writer_without_schema_*`; shred.rs `at_most_zero_live_body_dropped_at_bound_*`; local_serve.rs `keep_at_most_body_carried_on_local_label_*`; review.rs `body_is_a_class_*`; retention.rs `death_past_absolute_bound_leaves_drop_to_timer_*`.

## Carrier-only snapshots (spec/commits.md §5, Tess's decision)
- A snapshot whose wire retention keeps at least (above 0) and at most drops no body as covered and covers none for must-hold, judged from its retention alone (`osis_commit::retention::carrier_only`, `Engine::carrier_only`); compaction writes none (node-sync skips it, `sync/local.rs`), only `snapshot_first` does, as the carrier of a body dropped live at its keep-at-most bound. Such aspects are never compacted: their bodies stay until their own bounds (deletion.rs `compaction_skips_carrier_only_aspect_*`, `carrier_only_snapshot_drops_nothing_alike_*`).
- Why not "no snapshot at all" (Tess's literal rule): a delta's bytes are fixed by its header hash, so a body dropped at keep at most with a live write cannot be stripped of its dead ones; only a snapshot can carry the live ones. A carrier cuts no keep at least the dropped body's own keep at most had not already cut, and bodies it would cover stay with their own bounds. A malicious writer can mark its snapshots carrier only, and where its snapshot stands in for headers a node lacks, that node's snapshots too: storage growth, no loss.

## Snapshots: frontiers, covering, inherited retention (`retention/snapshot.rs`)
- Frontiers list the sessions occurring in the state (`osis_crdt::sources_of`; a session with a write left but no contiguous prefix applied gets a 0 entry, spec/crdt.md §8), each covering its counters contiguously applied and valid on the aspect; `Absorbed` lists the commit-form commits occurring in it.
- `covered` judges a held body by the join alone (tables speak only for one not held: an absent session or a 0 entry says nothing of what joins). Canonical-bytes comparisons there must stay encodings, not `Fragment ==`/`contains`: a deleted text element's char survives a join in memory but not in canonical bytes (deletion::text_partly_erased). An expiring delta is never covered. `receipt::settled` (suspect flags) is any snapshot of the aspect stamped after the commit, valid here.
- A compaction drop needs the snapshot's own commit to hold the right over the body (`purge::snapshot_right`, spec §5 Between the bounds); a snapshot without it still carries the body's live writes and leaves it may-hold, but drops nothing at any node.
- Retention comes from the carried live writes' own deltas' headers (`own_retentions`: each live dot to its commit by `Core::commit_of`; dots whose commit header is not held: the held snapshot bodies carrying them, else every snapshot of the aspect), so any writer snapshots, schema known or not; strictest keep at most (an absolute bound passed at the snapshot counts as 0 after replaced: the write outlived it only by being live, so its content goes as it dies), longest relative keep at least (`osis_commit::retention::snapshot_retention`). The aspect's headers are scanned only when some live write's own commit is not found directly. A body whose writes are all live is past a relative keep at least, so the snapshot carrying them keeps that promise (undo reads pre-commit values from held bodies, snapshots included); an absolute one stays with the original body, which cannot leave before it (inheriting `abs` forever would pin every later snapshot).
- Deaths in snapshots (`carriers`): a dying write's death is recorded in its own commit's body (for a `Commit(C)` dot: C), in every held snapshot taken before the death that holds it, and at its `ts` in every one taken at or after it that had not seen its superseder and carries the write the state holds dead (also where the write's own body was never held); dropped bodies get none.
- Known schemas, one per app AK, never from the wire (`Engine::register_schema`, which replaces one of a lower or equal version for each AK it declares, or a local typed write, which replaces only a lower version; kept under `d/` + AK, read at open) serve undo's typed views and large write-back after a restart, not snapshots. `osis::Osis::aspect_key` types only by this session's registrations, so the CLI's `schema rm` untypes.

## Bodies dropped and coming back
- **Never received** (spec/commits.md §5, R1 of notes/convergence-study.md, Tess's decision): a body dropped for its keep bounds (or for carrying a purged value) counts as never received: `retention::takes_late` admits its late copy (not one dropped as covered while a cover carries it, not a memory part, and a value-less one only without a residue), `reopen` clears its `Dropped` (and its `dropped_snapshots` row), the copy joins and `on_late` judges it again (`check_at_most`: dropped at once when dead past its bound; held when a flip made its write live). Nothing fetches it on its own: a holder that judges it must-hold re-uploads it (a fresh encryption past a host tombstone). A node that drops under a possible flip flags the aspect suspect (notes/engine-suspects.md).
  - Order harness: seeds 1053, 1024 (killer invalid), 1085, 1200 (own write invalid); `alike_exchanged` gives both nodes the commits of every body dropped before a flip again and then asserts equality (`drop_flips` picks them, `flip`); `keep_at_most_drop_joins_again_after_flip_spec_commits_5` is Tess's example.
- **Dropped snapshot liveness**: a dropped snapshot's frontiers are gone, so `drop_part` records in its commit record (`Dropped::live`) whether it was live by dot then; `snapshot_live`: dropped dead stays dead (writes leave the state by dying, purge or invalidity; undo and redo write new dots), dropped live counts any live write stamped at or before its `ts` or without one (bare anchors: an over-approximation, the delete waits for a carrier), never held the `ts` rule. A write a validity flip brings back comes from a held body, which carries it at hosts itself, so a dropped-dead snapshot needs no revival (a `ts`-based revival in `rebuild` would mark a snapshot live for a write it never carried, only at a node that saw the flip after the drop). Test `deletion::dropped_snapshot_waits_for_lost_carrier_spec_sync_7`.
- A drop removes the snapshot from `Core::snapshots` and `Core::carried` (`apply::forget_snapshot`): index entries derived from a dropped body go, since a rebuild cannot read them and an incremental node must not keep them either (order seed 1291; order.rs `dropped_snapshot_rebuilds_alike_*`).
- `drop_part` keeps a dropped part's rotation role, overreach, keysets and shown possession (`Dropped::role`, `overreach`, `keysets`, `shown`), so re-judging it and a rebuild need no body; `reopen` (never received again) forgets them. A bare leftover is never dropped (it stays for un-merge); one holding values (`Meta::valued`) drops as a whole body.

## Leftovers and the value-less list (`retention/leftover.rs`, `w/`)
- An expired or purged part, a snapshot carrying purged values, or a waiting part a request names is held as its leftover (`make_leftover`: its body stripped, in `w/`, `Meta::stripped`): a held body for every reader (`each_held_piece`, `held_parts_at`, `stores_part`: rebuilds, deaths, copy indexes, covering, the rebuild check), no body for node-sync (`Meta::body_gone`). A leftover holds stamps, dots, resets, flags and positions, never a value.
- **Invariant** (`strip_leftover`, the one strip of purge, expiry and arrival): no held body holds a listed write's value, and none loses a value of a write not listed. Its own writes lose their values only when listed (`Fragment::strip`); every copy it carries of another listed write is held value-less (`Fragment::expire`), so a rebuild reads the same with it. A body carrying such a copy (a snapshot, an undelete's char, a map key an entry's record repeats) is held as its leftover with only the copies value-less (`strip_carried`; `rebuild` finds them while the list changed, `drop_gone` on arrival), and a leftover still holding own values (`Meta::valued`, persisted) is a body for keep bounds and deaths (`Meta::bare`: the value-less ones are exempt); node-sync deletes its blob as a dropped body with live writes (snapshot first).
- The value-less list `v/` stays as the one join-time predicate (`apply::strip_valueless`: what joins and what a rebuild gives are stripped by it). Without it an undelete's copy of an expired char or a snapshot's stale copy would reach the state, and deriving it per join would walk the aspect.
- A part dropped as covered and then purged takes its leftover from its valid coverers' copies and is held again from then on. A part missing or dropped for its bounds when purged has no leftover: the list alone (a copy joins stripped and becomes the leftover, `drop_gone`).
- On its purge's fall a stripped part counts as never received: its owners leave the list, the part is missing with its leftover row set aside (`unstrip`), the aspect is rebuilt without its writes and the node refetches (`refetch`, `Changed::undropped`); a copy joins whole (`forget_joined_leftovers` drops the stale row); held value-less again first (a rise, or its expiry), `make_leftover` adopts the row, so it holds what it left as a node that never saw the fall does. An expired part's owners never leave the list.

## Purge (`retention/purge.rs`; spec/commits.md §5 Purge, spec/permissions.md §6)
- Every question about a request comes down to two: does it name a delta (`names`: an item on its aspect reaches it, stamped before the request), and does it have the right? The right has two readings: `holds_right` by the role timeline (demotion frontiers included, so a backdated request by a removed admin drops nothing), which what a request drops follows (`stands`, `rejudge_purges`); and `signers_could`, by roles of any validity at any time (`Held::ever_*`), for what must not turn on arrival (`could_stand`, `counts_as_purged`). A header never names role-class deltas.
- Requests are kept per aspect or per named commit (`p/`, `pn/`), so deltas arriving later are purged on arrival. A request by name costs other deltas nothing; an aspect-wide one is judged in one walk of the aspect per class and actor. A named commit never held names nothing yet (its header gives the right and the dots).
- When a role change flips a verdict (`rejudge_purges`): a part a request comes to drop is purged as on arrival; one held value-less for a request that no longer stands counts as never received (above); a snapshot dropped as named by a fallen request is missing again; every body stripped for carrying others' values is asked for again. Cost: the aspect's requests and what they name, its snapshots' views, and with an aspect-wide request a walk of its parts (`scale::purge_rejudge_cost`).
- A purge that dropped a permanent delta on a thing is noted (`z/`, `retention::lasting`): a new node needs the request to hold a copy of the delta that comes back as purged; noted, not walked, since a writer's aspect-wide request naming nothing it may purge would walk the aspect on every pass.
- A memory delta purged has vanished whole and stays gone.

## Waiting parts at expiry or purge (`retention/pending.rs`)
- A part pending at its expiry goes at T (`pending_drops`, `strip_pending` from the T timer; parked copies included) and counts as never received; the commit is examined again without it; a part waiting aside (`pend_aside`) is judged the same way, and only that aspect is flagged suspect (`Why::PendingExpiry`). Expiry cannot strip: a part usually waits for its thing's record at T, and without it the part cannot be decoded.
- A pending part a held purge request names is held value-less, not dropped (`strip_purged`, from `pending_drops`; a purge request's apply wakes the commits on its thing, and holds a part waiting aside of an applied commit it names value-less at once, `purge_pending`): once its thing's record is held it is decoded, stripped by the delta's `Owned`, kept as the part's leftover (`w/`), and its pending slot holds an empty-list delta (`retention::emptied`, never hashing; `examine` trusts it only with the leftover row, joins it as a whole part, `Parts::stripped`, and leaves it out of the stored blob); once joined, `stripped_joined` (before `on_apply`/`on_late`) holds it as its leftover, as purge does an applied part, so every arrival order holds the same state (`shred::purge_of_a_waiting_part_reads_alike_spec_commits_5`, `purge_of_a_part_waiting_aside_reads_alike_spec_commits_5`). No flag. With a page away or parked it cannot be stripped: it goes at once, flagged (`Why::Uncovered`), refetched by `fetch_fallen` if the request fell before the commit applied. Snapshot parts (no own values) wait whole; a memory commit's purged part is dropped (it vanishes whole when applied too).
- node-sync forgets the blobs of an expired or stripped part at once (`SyncEngine::dropped`, `Engine::spent_copy`): a blob also holding another part is kept while that part differs from the pending copy and is not provably junk (a junk copy may hold its slot, the blob the only true copy); the same for an applied commit with parts waiting aside (else the waiting part is lost for good, `parts_relay::copy_kept_for_a_junk_held_slot_of_an_applied_commit_spec_commits_5`), and every copy of a commit named for redelivery is kept; `redeliver` re-checks the commits it visited. An applied commit with nothing waiting keeps the cheap rule (any part dropped: forget). A blob holds one expiry (one per commit), so the kept-blob case is purge-only. A blob parked whole (its header unreadable by this build) cannot be stripped.

## Undo buffer (`undo/`)
What undoing each of the last `UNDO_DEPTH` (16) local commits per aspect reads (`Fragment::footprint`: struct fields written, the elements a map, set or bag delta writes and the live entries of keys it edits into, along the edit's paths; of a list or text the records holding the delta's dots), in the aspect unit; cleared (shredded) whenever a body of the aspect drops, since a dropped body is not undoable (spec/commits.md §7) and the buffer would keep its content. Undo reads the current state in place along the delta's paths. Undoing another node's commit (admin view) takes the pre-state as the join of the held valid bodies ordered before it (O(aspect history)).
