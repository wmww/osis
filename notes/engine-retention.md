# Engine: retention and dropped bodies
> Summary: How the engine keeps spec/commits.md §5-§7 (`retention/`): the one-drop principle, history modes, body classes, when a body was replaced for keep-for-D (a function of the held set), purge on replace as the replacing writer's cover (cost, privacy), snapshot frontiers, covering and inherited retention, invalid bodies, bodies dropped and coming back, leftovers and the value-less list, purge, waiting parts at expiry or purge, the undo buffer, and the costs. The spec states the rules; this note keeps reasons, invariants, Tess's decisions, footguns and numbers. Not here: validity and uncovering (notes/engine-validity.md), suspect flags (notes/engine-suspects.md), host deletion judgment (notes/sync.md), memory-class retention (notes/ephemeral.md).

## Principle
A body drops in one of four ways: as covered (a valid compaction snapshot whose commit holds the right over the body covers it), by expiry, by purge, or as invalid (it gives the state nothing, spec/permissions.md §5.2). A node without write drops nothing on its own but expired and invalid content. Every rule below follows from it:
- keep history (until T, D after replaced) only limits when a covered drop may happen, at every node alike;
- purge on replace is a duty to write the cover: the writer whose transaction replaced the body covers it, and the cover drops it everywhere (`retention/replace.rs`);
- convenient (no history) has neither: right holders compact when they like (node-sync, notes/sync.md);
- a node holds no body it judges invalid past the transaction that judged it (Invalid bodies, below): every read is a function of the valid writes held (a sequence's order included, spec/crdt.md §6.1), so the drop changes no read;
- a purge never drops a snapshot: it has no values of its own, so a request naming one takes nothing, and its copies of purged values go as any carrying body's (held as its leftover). A cover therefore falls only by turning invalid, its author's edit under a demotion that did not list it (spec/permissions.md §5.2 Limit);
- covering is the join and nothing else: a body is covered when the snapshot carries every live write of it. A snapshot carries an expiring delta's values value-less (spec/crdt.md §8), so the join covers such a delta once none of its values lives, before T as after (nothing visible of it can return: its writes are dead); "expiring is never covered" is not a rule, only what the join says while a value lives;
- keep-for-D needs only when the body was replaced, a function of the held set judged when asked (Replaced, below): nothing about it is stored, dated per node or replayed;
- the fixed bounds are keep at least, so they too only delay a covered drop: `ROLE_KEEP` after replaced for role-class bodies, a purge request's floor on its commit's blobs, `nobody` forever;
- expiry and purge are the principle's two exceptions and hold a body as its value-less leftover, which then leaves only as covered (past T) or with its thing; a waiting part keeps its body until it applies (no cover can judge it), and goes before only by those two.
Outside the four: a thing's bodies go whole when the node loses read access to it (spec/permissions.md §3, `retention/forget.rs`), and memory-class bodies vanish when superseded or at a delete here (notes/ephemeral.md Divergences left). There is no keep-at-most bound; the only timers are expiry and a purge request's floor (`T_FLOOR`: a covered purge-on-replace body of a commit carrying a request waits for it, spec/commits.md §5 Purge).

## History modes (`osis_commit::retention::KeepRule`)
The wire carries a history mode (spec/commits.md §5). Keep history is a keep at least: `until` absolute, `after` D after replaced, the later. Purge on replace has no bound. A role-class aspect's wire retention is `admin` with no history; `KeepRule` gives it a relative keep at least of `ROLE_KEEP`, so role bodies are kept as before (compactor policy in the issue's terms, applied by every node).
- Replaced (keep-for-D): a delta's body counts from its earliest write's death, a snapshot's from the latest death among its writes no held valid snapshot stamped later with at least its D carries (one purging on replace or keeping less keeps no such history: `shorter_kept_later_snapshot_carries_no_history_*`) (`osis_commit::retention::body_keep` takes the date): each write's history is kept D after it died by the last snapshot carrying it, so compacting a keep-for-D write while live loses none of it (held-model `snapshot_keeps_the_longest_d_of_what_it_carries_*`, `snapshot_replaced_at_last_death_no_later_snapshot_carries_*`). Counting every carried write instead kept every snapshot of an aspect whose long-lived writes keep dying within D (each carries them all): held snapshots grew with the edit count, not the D window.

## Bodies
- A body is the commit's one blob (notes/whole-commits.md). Retention's `body_class` still groups its deltas by (reader actor, retention, delete-key indices): keep bounds are judged over the class, a part drops with the rest of its class, and the blob is rewritten without it. The class depends on what is held (the reader actor, `Core::reader_of`: up the record chain through first-admin links to the nearest actor with secrets of its own), so nothing kept may be keyed by it. Expiry is per commit; purge drops single deltas (the commit is kept without them).
- A held body is judged "held" by the stored blob holding the part (`apply::stores_part`), not by meta alone: during a join the commit is indexed before it is stored, and a rebuild must not count its own delta as held then (order seed 1038). A part still to arrive (`Meta::missing`, a late part) is not held (`retention::held_part`): it is not judged and not dropped before it arrives (walks.rs `open_equals_rebuild_random_histories_unheld_part_dies`).

## Replaced, for keep-for-D (`retention/bounds.rs` `replaced_at`)
- Tess's rule (issues/retention-modes.md): a body under keep-for-D is not dropped until D after the earliest valid replacing write the node holds, a function of the held set. Judged when asked (node-sync's `body_keeps` per commit judged, `drop_from` before a compaction drop, `Engine::replaced_at`), never stored: no death records, no replays, no crediting. Whether a write is dead, and when, is a function of the held bodies, never of how a part is paged.
- **Which writes died** (`deaths`, `tally`): each held valid part, whole, in one join, into a copy of the state at its positions (`Tx::state_part_at`, `Fragment::part_at`: O(part), not a copy of the state); the writes the join reports superseded (spec/crdt.md §4.8: pruned, beaten, a text element deleted; `dies_in` keeps the part's own writes, not a superseder it holds). None dead: past a relative bound. A delta needs only the set of dead stamps (with the state's superseder date); a snapshot also how many writes of each stamp died (`counts`: a stamp names several writes, an entry's creation and its value, a struct's fields, every value of one write op), since its dating and `carried` are per write. Never joined piece by piece: a page piece repeats the entry creations on its path (`Piece::Page`), so per-piece joins would report those once per page and the counts would depend on paging (`tests_paged::death_on_many_pages_dated_once_*`). Cost: the part decoded whole, O(part) memory for every body judged, live or dead (notes/engine-paged.md Left whole). Every count is of one whole join's reports; the reports stand in for write identity, which they are not, and `carried` can date a snapshot early by it (issues/death-write-identity.md).
- **When** (`first_deaths`): the aspect's other held valid bodies stamped no earlier than the part's earliest dead write join, in `(ts, ID)` order (invalid writes left out as un-merge does), into a scratch fragment holding the part. Supersession is judged against the write alone (§4.8), so the first body whose join kills a write of the part is the earliest held body holding its superseder: the superseder's own commit, or a later snapshot carrying it (dated at the snapshot, later than the write: never earlier than the death). A delta dates there and stops. A snapshot first leaves out the stamps whose every dead write a later held valid snapshot with at least its D carries (`carried`: that snapshot's own `deaths` hold the same death, since a copy of a write dies alike in the state; judged per stamp, so one of a stamp's writes carried and another not keeps the stamp, dating no earlier; later snapshots read in `ts` order until all are carried), and needs each remaining stamp's every counted write dead, and a join reports a death again (a later body's copy of the dead write, a record pair's cross death, spec/crdt.md §5.6), so counting reports dated a snapshot early: reports only bound the date from below, and the count is taken afresh (the part joined into the bodies so far, its deaths counted as `counts` counts them) once the reports could hold them all; the walk stops at the first body by which every stamp is dead, and only when some stamp no held body supersedes does a second walk find the first body by which those dead by the end are (`snapshot_deaths`, `snapshot_replaced_at_latest_death_not_at_copies_*`). Cost: the bodies joined twice and a copy of them per check; counting afresh at every body killing a write was quadratic. A stamp no body of the walk supersedes dates at the state's superseder (`date`; a valid snapshot's copy of a delta invalid here: the earliest held valid snapshot holding it). Holding more bodies dates no later; holding the same dates alike.
- Limit: a snapshot dropped while a later one carried its dead writes is not brought back if that later one turns invalid (its cover falls) before their D passes: those writes' history goes with it, as a body dropped as covered by a snapshot that falls (never received, not fetched).
- Debug check (osis-engine's tests): `deaths` against the part joined into the whole state (`oracle::deaths`).
- `dies_in` reads a stamp index of the part (`Fragment::stamps`, `stamps_of`), built once per part judged: a reset kills every write of a large part, so a walk per death would be quadratic (`retention::tests::reset_judged_walks_parts_once_*` counts the walks, `Core::death_walks`).
- Cost of a snapshot on a frequently edited kept aspect (`scale::kept_snapshots_rejudged_per_death_cost_*`, release, 300 set elements edited hourly under a one-day D, a snapshot every 10 edits, about 700 bodies, deltas never dropped, load 1-5): every held snapshot judged after each death, as `mark_killed` asks, and one past its bound dropped as covered: 3 snapshots held (the D window), about 16 ms each, 49 ms for all at edit 200. Counting every carried write held all 20, 69 ms each, 1.39 s. Still O(bodies from the earliest uncarried dead write): a long-lived write that died before the next snapshot walks from its creation.
- node-sync judges a body again when it may have been replaced: a join's reported deaths mark the dying writes' own commits and the aspect's snapshots (`mark_killed`), a rebuild (flip, un-merge, drop, expiry) every held bounded body of the aspect (`mark_bounded`).
- Rejected: per-node death dates stored per body (dated by arrival order, with crediting of carrying snapshots, recomputes after flips and a text re-delete rule: the class of bugs Tess's rule removes); dating at the superseder the state holds now (the latest: a register's old bodies would stay kept for as long as it keeps changing within D); the minimum over every held superseder pairwise (exact for the model, O(bodies) joins per body and a per-write identity the crdt's death reports lack).
- Cost (`scale::late_delta_deaths_cost_spec_crdt_4_8`, release, N = 3000 set elements, load 9-14): a late delta applied 2.5 ms, plus one body judged 2.9 ms; an in-order local write 2.5-2.7 ms; every one of the 3000 bodies judged 1.57 s, about 0.5 ms each (a body read). Stored dates cost a 75-85 ms recompute per flip on that set and a record read per judgment; judging is paid per node-sync judgment of a bounded body, which reads the body for covering anyway.
- Tests: retention.rs `deaths_and_bounds_*`, `deaths_dated_from_first_superseder_held_*`, `snapshot_replaced_at_latest_death_not_at_copies_*`, `deaths_never_early_random_*` (random histories against a node keeping every body: replaced iff, never earlier; the same judgment on more bodies, not an independent truth; `OSIS_STRAYS_START`/`_SEEDS`); the independent truth is held-model's `replaced_at` (order.rs `arrival_order_kept_*`, `kept_snapshot_replaced_at_latest_death_any_order_*`: no keep bound before D after it), `snapshot_dated_through_*`, `text_delete_replaces_at_earliest_delete_held_*`; order.rs `replaced_alike_in_any_order_*`, `text_redelete_replaced_at_first_delete_*`, `snapshot_after_late_dated_death_not_credited_*`; pass_marks `body_judged_once_per_body_*` (`Engine::replaced_reads`).

## Purge on replace (`retention/replace.rs`)
- The writer's cover, the principle's one drop (Tess, issues/retention-modes.md): at the end of every transaction (`Engine::run`, `settle_none`), over the aspects whose purge-on-replace bodies or snapshots changed (`Core::none_due`, filled by `Core::put_meta` for any commit on an aspect holding such a body, `ix.none_held`, by a rebuild, and by a timeline change on the thing: a grant can make this node a writer owing a cover, retention.rs `none_granted_writer_covers_what_it_holds_replaced_*`): a node that may write a snapshot of the aspect (`snapshot_role`, at least write; every writer holds the `writer` right) writes one from its whole state (`snapshot::write_snapshot`, at most one per aspect per transaction) when a held purge-on-replace body is replaced and no held valid snapshot holding the right over it covers it (`coverer`); then every covered purge-on-replace body drops as covered, at every node (readers too: the snapshot's commit holds the right). A function of the held set: the order things came in changes when, never whether.
- **Replaced** (`replaced`): the body is valid, holds a live write by dot the state does not (or the join reports one dead), and joins into the state's part it meets with its canonical bytes unchanged: dead, not un-merged under an invalid record (which would make a snapshot that cannot cover it, every transaction). Not "has a dead write": a root reset dies once a later write implies one yet canonical form keeps it (held-model-4). A delta with an expiry held with its values is judged against the state with its values value-less, as the cover will carry them: replaced only once none of its values lives, so the duty arises exactly when a snapshot of the state covers it (no snapshot every transaction for a body no snapshot can cover).
- Every replacing write converts into a snapshot: the new body is covered by the snapshot written after it and drops too, so an aspect under purge on replace holds one snapshot and no deltas at the writer after each transaction. Readers keep replaced bodies until a writer's snapshot arrives: some writer must hold both the body and its replacement (the editor need not hold the body, a concurrent write; every writer fetches both in time); with none online they keep them (no timer: a node without write acts on its own only for expiry).
- Cost (spec/requirements.md Scale, the exception the app chooses): every replacing edit costs a snapshot of the aspect, O(aspect state), at every writer holding the replaced body uncovered; in practice the editor alone (it uploads its snapshot with the edit, and a writer receiving both in one batch finds it covered). Duplicates (a writer that applied the edit before the snapshot arrived) are harmless (spec/sync.md §7). Measured in notes/growth.md (purge on replace).
- Rejected: a keep-at-most bound and its timers, carriers written by node-sync before a timed drop, readers dropping at the bound with no carrier, invalid bodies counted dead at their own `ts`: each needed death dating, a live-write judgment per body and a carve-out per kind of body (snapshots dropped live, never received after a flip, oracle excuses); the cover rule needs none of them.
- Rejected: writer-side purge per edit (Tess's fallback if this proves costly): a purge cannot express a partial replacement of a body, a purging commit keeps its blobs `ROLE_KEEP`, every edit leaves a leftover.
- Privacy (spec/commits.md §5): erased data persists in its body at a writer for no transaction, at a reader until the writer's snapshot arrives, at a host until a right holder next syncs. The read state loses it at the kill, and the drop shreds like any.
- Liveness (`retention::live`, `Engine::body_live`) stays for node-sync's host deletes (a body with a live write goes from a label only with a carrier, spec/sync.md §7): judged on the current state, cached per body and forgotten per aspect when its state changes (`Core::forget_live`; `Fragment::holds_owned_write`: an element's or entry's tombstone holds no write of its creator, order seed 64197). A deleted text element's creation stays in the state as its anchor, so a text body fully erased is live too.
- Tests: retention.rs `none_*` (the cover in the replacing transaction, readers dropping on arrival, mixed modes), held.rs model comparison, sim `snapshot_rights`.

## Snapshots: frontiers, covering, inherited retention (`retention/snapshot.rs`)
- Frontiers list the sessions occurring in the state (`osis_crdt::sources_of`; a session with a write left but no contiguous prefix applied gets a 0 entry, spec/crdt.md §8), each covering its counters contiguously applied and valid on the aspect; `Absorbed` lists the commit-form commits occurring in it.
- `covered` judges a held body by the join, with the snapshot's copies under the body's dots stripped first so a differing copy (forged, greater or not) never covers; the body's value-less writes are left out of that strip (`Fragment::valueless_dots`): a snapshot whose writer held a purge this node does not (a fork, or one that fell) holds a live write value-less, and later snapshots hold it valued, so the strict compare would keep that snapshot for as long as the write lives. Tables speak only for one not held (an absent session or a 0 entry says nothing of what joins). Canonical-bytes comparisons there must stay encodings, not `Fragment ==`/`contains`: a deleted text element's char survives a join in memory but not in canonical bytes (deletion::text_partly_erased). An expiring delta held with its values is judged by the join (covered once none of its values lives: retention.rs `dead_expiring_delta_covered_before_its_expiry_*`, `purge_on_replace_covers_dead_expiring_write_*`, ephemeral.rs `dead_expiring_ephemeral_write_covered_before_expiry_*`); one not held and not value-less is not covered (the frontier alone would say it is). `receipt::settled` (suspect flags) is any snapshot of the aspect stamped after the commit, valid here.
- A compaction drop needs the snapshot's own commit to hold the right over the body (`purge::snapshot_right`, spec §5 Between the bounds); a snapshot without it still carries the body's live writes and leaves it may-hold, but drops nothing at any node.
- Retention comes from the carried live writes' own deltas' headers (`own_retentions`: each live dot to its commit by `Core::commit_of`; dots whose commit header is not held: the held snapshot bodies carrying them, else every snapshot of the aspect), so any writer snapshots, schema known or not: purge on replace if any carried write's is (as the held model's `snapshot_retention`), else kept for the longest `after` (`osis_commit::retention::snapshot_retention`). A purge-on-replace snapshot cannot keep a carried write's history, so it covers no body that keeps history while that body has a live write (`snapshot::cannot_keep`, in `covered`): on an aspect mixing modes such bodies stay until their writes die and their bound passes, the cost of keeping history there. Liveness changes by a kill, after which a later snapshot covers both, or by a validity change, after which a settled `covered_judged` `false` only keeps the body longer. The aspect's headers are scanned only when some live write's own commit is not found directly. A body whose writes are all live is past a relative keep at least, so the snapshots carrying them keep that promise, the last carrying a write counting D from its death (undo reads pre-commit values from held bodies, snapshots included); an `until` stays with the original body, which cannot leave before it (inheriting `until` forever would pin every later snapshot).
- Copy indexes (`Core::copy_index`, `copies::copy_index`): a held snapshot's writes by dot, built from what `each_held_piece` reads (its whole body, or its leftover: keys erased, values gone) and cached per commit, forgotten whenever the commit's stored body or leftover changes (`put_commit`, `remove_commit`, `leftover::put`/`remove`) and on rollback. Debug builds check every cache hit against an index built anew (removing the invalidation fails five tier-1 order tests). A stale index vouched for records from a creation copy the strip had erased, and a fresh one did not, so a reopen changed what an un-merge kept (order seed 91703). In the un-merge (`Unmerge::vouches`) a record a valid snapshot holds key-erased keeps every record of its dot, the state's own key-erased record (asked with no key) included: nested writes of other deltas live there. Test: order.rs `settled_seed_91703_live_write_kept_after_reopen_*`.
- Known schemas, one per app AK, never from the wire (`Engine::register_schema`, which replaces one of a lower or equal version for each AK it declares, or a local typed write, which replaces only a lower version; kept under `d/` + AK, read at open) serve undo's typed views and large write-back after a restart, not snapshots. `osis::Osis::aspect_key` types only by this session's registrations, so the CLI's `schema rm` untypes.

## Invalid bodies (`retention::drop_invalid`; spec/permissions.md §6)
- `Core::drop_due` takes every commit with a part held and invalid (`Core::index_validity` for a flip or an arrival, `Core::put_meta` for a late part joining invalid); at the end of `Engine::run`, after `answer_removals` (this node's addenda vouch first) and before `settle_none`, each such part goes (`DropFor::Invalid`, persisted as bit 32 of the dropped flags), each aspect rebuilt once (`run_batched`: the one path; an invalid text body can leave a bare record a rebuild without it lacks, issues/invalid-text-body-leaves-record.md), then held deltas are judged again if the drop marked any (a `keys.0` part's keyset rebuild marks its commits; nothing flips). Kept (`goes_invalid`): role commits whole (the timeline, trust set, disputes and possible holders read every held edge write, applied or not; keeping only the `roles.0` part split a commit a label holds whole, so a writer never promoted an invalid role commit's deposit, sim `link_race`), this node's own commits (`OwnWriteInvalid`: its user may write it again from the body, chat `resend`; its author also uploads it, must-hold, so a re-grant finds it), memory parts (Memory, notes/ephemeral.md Divergences left).
- What the body judged stays in `Dropped` (role, overreach, keysets, shown), as for any drop. An invalid addendum's drop marks nothing stale (it is outside the fixpoint, its list counted for nothing); a `required^.0` part's drop takes its rules out of `ix.required` (a setting not held binds nothing; an invalid one bound nothing already). An invalid snapshot dropped stands in for nothing (`stands_in` only for `DropFor::Other`).
- While invalid a copy arriving again is refused (`takes_late`: only `DropFor::Other` takes one), so a relay costs nothing. Valid again (`rejudge_once` collects the parts rising while `dropped_invalid`, after its debug check), `valid_again` makes each missing (`reopen_to_fetch`, `Changed::undropped`) and flags the aspect `Why::Uncovered`; node-sync turns its entries `Unknown` and fetches them where present (`reopen_undropped`, which also forgets bytes still held when the drop and the rise fell in one step: else nothing re-ingests them).
- Refetch source: no right deletes an invalid body at hosts. node-sync leaves alone (may-hold, `judge` → `None`) any blob holding a part it dropped as invalid and not purged or expired since (`Engine::left_at_hosts`), unless a right dropped another part of it (purge, expiry, cover): that drop wins and the blob goes, the invalid copy with it (else a purged sibling stayed at the host, `purge_beside_invalid_delta_leaves_host_*`). Such a part alone does not make the blob hold nothing (`holds_nothing`) nor its chunks dropped (`dropped_chunks`). So hosts keep what was uploaded while valid, and the author keeps its own. Nobody deletes a never-valid body at hosts either (junk a reader or ex-writer uploads): only its expiry, a purge or the thing leaving the label take it, within the host's per-label cap (issues/invalid-junk-at-hosts.md). A body no holder has stays gone, at every node alike (`regrant_with_no_holder_reads_alike_*`).
- Cost: a part invalid on arrival is stored, then dropped, two rebuilds of its aspect; a fall drops its bodies with a second rebuild (`fall_of_many_one_pass_*`). A part that arrives before what makes it valid (a grant on another label, an addendum) is fetched twice: rare-path brute force.
- Tests: retention.rs `invalid_write_drops_regrant_fetches_it_*`, `revoked_writers_late_edit_goes_*`, `regrant_with_no_holder_reads_alike_*`; required.rs `junk_setting_goes_*`, `junk_reads_nothing_and_goes_*`, `setting_below_a_body_goes_and_comes_back_*` (refetch from the host, its author offline); held-model `Fate::MustDrop(DropWhy::Invalid)`.

## Bodies dropped and coming back
- **Never received** (spec/commits.md §5): a body dropped other than as covered (a part missing when purged) counts as never received: `retention::takes_late` admits its late copy (not one dropped as covered while a cover carries it, not a memory part, and a value-less one only without a residue), `reopen` clears its `Dropped` (and its `dropped_snapshots` row), the copy joins and `on_late` judges it again (purged or stripped again while the request stands). Nothing fetches it on its own; a holder that judges it must-hold re-uploads it (a fresh encryption past a host tombstone).
- A body dropped as covered whose covers no longer carry it is missing again (`uncover`, spec/permissions.md §5.2) and fetched where present; compaction deleted it at the hosts, so it is often held only by readers, who upload nothing, and its live writes are lost (the stated limit, spec/permissions.md §5.2); the parts harness (`parts::parts_differential_*`) gives a node the commit of any part missing again, as node-sync fetches it (parts seed 79).
- **Dropped snapshot liveness**: a snapshot not held (dropped or never received) is live while the state holds a write stamped at or before its `ts` or one without a `ts` (bare anchors): an over-approximation, so its delete at hosts waits for a carrier (`snapshot_live`; a dropped-as-covered one has its coverer). Test `deletion::dropped_snapshot_waits_for_lost_carrier_spec_sync_7`.
- A drop removes the snapshot from `Core::snapshots` (`apply::forget_snapshot`): an index entry derived from a dropped body goes, since a rebuild cannot read it and an incremental node must not keep them either (order seed 1291).
- `drop_part` keeps a dropped part's rotation role, overreach, keysets and shown possession (`Dropped::role`, `overreach`, `keysets`, `shown`), so re-judging it and a rebuild need no body; `reopen` (never received again) forgets them. A bare leftover is never dropped (it stays for un-merge); one holding values (`Meta::valued`) drops as a whole body.

## Leftovers and the value-less list (`retention/leftover.rs`, `w/`)
- An expired or purged part, a snapshot carrying purged values, or a waiting part a request names is held as its leftover (`make_leftover`: its body stripped, in `w/`, `Meta::stripped`): a held body for every reader (`each_held_piece`, `held_parts_at`, `stores_part`: rebuilds, keep bounds, copy indexes, covering, the rebuild check), no body for node-sync (`Meta::body_gone`). A leftover holds stamps, dots, resets, flags and positions, never a value.
- **Invariant** (`strip_leftover`, the one strip of purge, expiry and arrival): no held body holds a listed write's value, and none loses a value of a write not listed. Its own writes lose their values only when listed (`Fragment::strip`); every copy it carries of another listed write is held value-less (`Fragment::expire`), so a rebuild reads the same with it. A body carrying such a copy (a snapshot, an undelete's char, a map key an entry's record repeats) is held as its leftover with only the copies value-less (`strip_carried`; `rebuild` finds them while the list changed, `drop_gone` on arrival), and a leftover still holding own values (`Meta::valued`, persisted) is a body for keep bounds (`Meta::bare`: the value-less ones are exempt); node-sync deletes its blob as a dropped body with live writes (snapshot first).
- The value-less list `v/` stays as the one join-time predicate (`apply::strip_valueless`: what joins and what a rebuild gives are stripped by it). Without it an undelete's copy of an expired char or a snapshot's stale copy would reach the state, and deriving it per join would walk the aspect.
- A part dropped as covered and then purged takes its leftover from its valid coverers' copies and is held again from then on. A part missing when purged has no leftover: the list alone (a copy joins stripped and becomes the leftover, `drop_gone`).
- On its purge's fall a part stripped of its own values counts as never received: its writes leave the list, the part is missing with its leftover row set aside (`unstrip`), the aspect is rebuilt without its writes and the node refetches (`refetch`, `Changed::undropped`); a copy joins whole (`forget_joined_leftovers` drops the stale row); held value-less again first (a rise, or its expiry), `make_leftover` adopts the row, so it holds what it left as a node that never saw the fall does. An expired part's writes never leave the list. A body stripped only for carrying others' values stays held as its leftover through the fall: its value-less copies only hide, the valued copy wins wherever one joins (spec/crdt.md §4.7 Join), and a whole copy arriving replaces it (`takes_late`). Making it missing instead lost the other writes a carrying snapshot covered, deleted at hosts on its strength.

## Purge (`retention/purge.rs`; spec/commits.md §5 Purge, spec/permissions.md §6)
- Every question about a request comes down to two: does it name a delta (`names`: an item on its aspect reaches it, stamped before the request), and does it have the right? The right has two readings: `holds_right` by the role timeline (demotion frontiers included, so a backdated request by a removed admin drops nothing), which what a request drops follows (`stands`, `rejudge_purges`); and `signers_could`, by roles of any validity at any time (`Held::ever_*`), for what must not turn on arrival (`could_stand`, `counts_as_purged`). A header never names role-class deltas.
- Requests are kept per aspect or per named commit (`p/`, `pn/`), listed before the request's commit joins (`note_requests`), so deltas arriving later are purged on arrival and a body killed by the request's own commit is purged, not dropped: its leftover is bare, so no cover drops it (`kill_and_purge_in_one_commit_keeps_leftover_*`). A body dropped as covered before a request naming it arrived takes its leftover from its coverers (Leftovers). A request by name costs other deltas nothing; an aspect-wide one is judged in one walk of the aspect per class and actor. A named commit never held names nothing yet (its header gives the right and the dots).
- When a role change flips a verdict (`rejudge_purges`): a part a request comes to drop is purged as on arrival; one held value-less for a request that no longer stands counts as never received (above); a body stripped for carrying others' values stays held (above). Cost: the aspect's requests and what they name, its snapshots' views, and with an aspect-wide request a walk of its parts (`scale::purge_rejudge_cost`).
- A purge that dropped a permanent delta on a thing is noted (`z/`, `retention::lasting`): a new node needs the request to hold a copy of the delta that comes back as purged; noted, not walked, since a writer's aspect-wide request naming nothing it may purge would walk the aspect on every pass.
- A memory delta purged has vanished whole and stays gone.

## Waiting parts at expiry or purge (`retention/pending.rs`)
- A part pending at its expiry goes at T (`pending_drops`, `strip_pending` from the T timer; parked copies included) and counts as never received; the commit is examined again without it; a part waiting aside (`pend_aside`) is judged the same way, and only that aspect is flagged suspect (`Why::PendingExpiry`). Expiry cannot strip: a part usually waits for its thing's record at T, and without it the part cannot be decoded.
- A pending part a held purge request names is held value-less, not dropped (`strip_purged`, from `pending_drops`; a purge request's apply wakes the commits on its thing, and holds a part waiting aside of an applied commit it names value-less at once, `purge_pending`): once its thing's record is held it is decoded, stripped by the delta's `Owned`, kept as the part's leftover (`w/`), and its pending slot holds an empty-list delta (`retention::emptied`, never hashing; `examine` trusts it only with the leftover row, joins it as a whole part, `Parts::stripped`, and leaves it out of the stored blob); once joined, `stripped_joined` (before `on_apply`/`on_late`) holds it as its leftover, as purge does an applied part, so every arrival order holds the same state (`shred::purge_of_a_waiting_part_reads_alike_spec_commits_5`, `purge_of_a_part_waiting_aside_reads_alike_spec_commits_5`). No flag. With a page away or parked it cannot be stripped: it goes at once, flagged (`Why::Uncovered`), refetched by `fetch_fallen` if the request fell before the commit applied. Snapshot parts (no own values) wait whole; a memory commit's purged waiting part is dropped as never received (notes/ephemeral.md Divergences left).
- node-sync forgets the blobs of an expired or stripped part at once (`SyncEngine::dropped`, `Engine::spent_copy`): a blob also holding another part is kept while that part differs from the pending copy and is not provably junk (a junk copy may hold its slot, the blob the only true copy); the same for an applied commit with parts waiting aside (else the waiting part is lost for good, `parts_relay::copy_kept_for_a_junk_held_slot_of_an_applied_commit_spec_commits_5`), and every copy of a commit named for redelivery is kept; `redeliver` re-checks the commits it visited. An applied commit with nothing waiting keeps the cheap rule (any part dropped: forget). A blob holds one expiry (one per commit), so the kept-blob case is purge-only. A blob parked whole (its header unreadable by this build) cannot be stripped.

## Undo buffer (`undo/`)
What undoing each of the last `UNDO_DEPTH` (16) local commits per aspect reads (`Fragment::footprint`: struct fields written, the elements a map, set or bag delta writes and the live entries of keys it edits into, along the edit's paths; of a list or text the records holding the delta's dots), in the aspect unit; cleared (shredded) whenever a body of the aspect drops, since a dropped body is not undoable (spec/commits.md §7) and the buffer would keep its content. Undo reads the current state in place along the delta's paths. Undoing another node's commit (admin view) takes the pre-state as the join of the held valid bodies ordered before it (O(aspect history)).
